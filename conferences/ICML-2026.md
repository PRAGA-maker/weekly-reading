# ICML 2026 Reading List: bioLM, Virtual Cell and Patient, and the Four Domains

Fifty-two papers from ICML 2026 (Seoul, July 6 to 12), drawn from all 6,341 accepted papers and kept for one of two reasons: they touch bioLM, virtual cell, or virtual patient, or their central move could be stolen into a different field; or they land squarely in one of the four standing domains, Bayesian statistics, learning theory and high-dimensional optimization, causal inference, and information geometry. Fifteen are spotlights. The strongest single current is that flow and bridge matching has become a shared language across cell dynamics, sampling, and geometry.

**How this was built.** Every accepted paper's abstract was triaged against both criteria; roughly 1,200 survivors had their full text deep-read (arXiv or ar5iv where available, abstract otherwise) against a gem decision procedure that rewards a sharp single idea, a reframe, a surprising or negative result, or a cross-domain import that pays rent, and rejects leaderboard, scaling-curve, kitchen-sink, and decorated-method papers; the roughly 120 finalists were then cross-checked against their OpenReview reviews and put through a final adversarial pass that tried to refute each gem.

**On the reviews.** ICML 2026 reviews are public, and each entry below folds in what the reviewers said. Treat that signal as noisy. Not every accepted paper is load-bearing, reviewers vary in rigor, and some reviews may be AI-generated: all reviewers self-affirm the LLM-reviewing policy, which is not proof of anything. Where a paper's own reviewers flagged a real hole, it is named under Reviews and caveats. Weight the moves, not the scores. A few bio papers are not on arXiv and were judged from abstract plus reviews; those are marked.

## A1. Virtual cell and cellular dynamics

### 1. Deep Learning for BioImaging: What Are We Really Learning?
Ivan Svatko, Maxime Sanchez, Ihab Bendidi, Gilles Cottrell, Auguste Genovesio. ICML 2026. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ZgJlDylia4)

- **The move:** Run untrained networks and hand-built structural cell-graph representations as null models and show they match SOTA microscopy foundation models across a large fraction of benchmark settings.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the diagnostic-baseline discipline done right: instead of proposing a better model, it uses a cheap null (untrained nets, plus a domain-specific structural representation of tissue) as a measuring stick and proves that popular microscopy FM benchmarks reward low-level texture rather than any high-level biological abstraction. It builds the muscle of refusing to trust a representation-quality metric until a null model has been run against it, and the structural-graph baseline is a smarter null than generic random features. The lesson generalizes to any field standing up foundation-model leaderboards.
- **Cost:** Low-to-medium read, low prerequisite (no heavy math); high value-per-page as a methodology cautionary tale. Abstract-only in this corpus, so judged from abstract plus four reviews.
- **Reviews and caveats:** Four reviewers, avg rec 5 (6,4,4,6), consensus that the question is important and the simple-baseline comparison is 'excellent' and 'rigorous and thorough across models, datasets, parameter and layer-wise analyses.' No substantive soundness dissent; the only real knock is weak presentation. No hype red flag, praise is specific to the experimental design rather than generic. Honest limit: The originality is bounded: the 'simple baselines beat FMs' critique already exists in the transcriptomics FM world, so this is that genre ported to microscopy imaging rather than a fresh insight. Reviewers scored presentation weak (2,2,3,4) and soundness only moderate (3,3,3,4); the 'comparable on a subset of settings' hedge leaves room for setting selection, and it diagnoses more than it fixes. Verification from abstract plus reviews only.
- **Pairs with:** Sits next to the transcriptomic-FM skeptic line (Kedzierska et al. 2025, Ahlmann-Eltze et al. 2025) that reviewers explicitly flag as the parallel, and the broader untrained-network / random-feature baseline literature.

### 2. Beyond Continuity: Simulation-free Reconstruction of Discrete Branching Dynamics from Single-cell Snapshots
Junda Ying, Yuxuan Wang, Bowen Yang, Peijie Zhou, Lei Zhang. ICML 2026. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=IABjbJlinz) · [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00545v2)

- **The move:** Make the Branching Schrodinger Bridge simulation-free by regressing velocity, growth, and score against a Poisson-Brownian bridge (Brownian bridge in position, log-linear mass) under a mass-weighted conditional score-matching loss.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The real contribution is a single unifying picture: every matching method (OT-CFM, SF2M, WFR-FM, USB) is just a choice of coupling plus conditional path, and USB fills the one empty cell of that table by fusing stochasticity and unbalanced mass at once. The load-bearing import is the branching-process view of mass, which lets the model actually simulate discrete birth-death lineage at single-cell resolution rather than a continuous source term, and removing it collapses the method back to WFR-FM. Builds the muscle of turning an ill-posed variational bridge into a tractable regression by finding the right conditional bridge, and of reading a whole method family through two design knobs.
- **Cost:** High: ~90 min. Needs comfort with Schrodinger bridges, WFR/unbalanced OT, flow/score matching, and branching Brownian motion.
- **Reviews and caveats:** Four reviewers unanimously recommend accept (5,5,5,5) but soundness is only 3,3,4,3 and presentation is weak (3,3,3,2). Praise is specific (simulation-free training formulation named as the strongest technical piece, gap framed clearly), not generic or AI-smelling. The unanimous 5 against middling soundness is a mild hype signal, and reviewers do flag the WFR approximation, but the core idea is sound and honestly scoped. Honest limit: Accuracy is a wash: on the Simulation set it ties WFR-FM at 0.019, and on Mouse hold-out WFR-FM (6.586) beats USB (6.988), so the sell is the branching-simulation capability, not numbers. The BSB-is-RUOT-dual reframe is borrowed from the cited BSB paper, not new here, and the training quietly approximates the intractable RUOT semi-coupling by a second-order WFR expansion, a shortcut buried in the appendix. Branching inference is a decoding scheme layered on the same learned g, so 'discrete' lives more at inference than in training.
- **Pairs with:** SF2M (Tong et al., simulation-free diffusion Schrodinger bridge) and WFR-FM as the two structures it fuses; contrast BranchSBM, which does branching but is not simulation-free.

### 3. A Factorized Low-Rank RNN Framework for Uncovering Independent Neural Latent Dynamics and Connectivity
Chengrui Li, Yunmiao Wang, Yule Wang, Weihan Li, Dieter Jaeger, Anqi Wu. ICML 2026 (spotlight). [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=QvIbmX9jBr) · [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13899v2)

- **The move:** Reformulate a low-rank RNN as a VAE so a group-wise total-correlation penalty enforces partial disentanglement (groups mutually independent, rich within group), interpolating between plain lrRNN at G=1 and full ICA at G=K, and read each group off as a rank-H_g sub-connectivity.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The load-bearing idea is partial disentanglement: instead of the usual dimension-wise independence of ICA/beta-TCVAE, each latent group stays internally entangled while groups are mutually independent, which maps cleanly onto separable low-rank sub-circuits you can name (x vs y motor channels, airpuff oscillation circuits). The rotational-symmetry control on monkey M1, aligning latents to rotated coordinate bases and showing alignment R2 peaks only at the cardinal axes, is a genuine falsification test rather than a decorative plot, and the VAE reframe is what makes both the penalty and the latent-dynamics recursion fall out. It builds the muscle of turning an affine gauge freedom into a structured, testable modeling choice.
- **Cost:** 60-90 min; moderate; needs VAE/ELBO fluency, low-rank RNN and SVD background, and comfort with the Jordan-form argument in Appendix A.1.
- **Reviews and caveats:** Accept (spotlight), rec 5,5,5,3, with specific technical praise, not vague: reviewers single out the lrRNN-as-VAE reformulation as clean and consistent, the orthogonality-vs-independence distinction as clearly motivated, and the bdRNN variant as honestly flagged valid only under identity activation. The lone rec-3 dissent hits presentation (score 1) rather than a soundness hole, so it is outweighed; no hype or AI-smell red flags. Honest limit: Connectivity interpretability holds only for linear encoder/decoder; the nonlinear MLP variant loses the connectivity story and did not even improve disentanglement. Group count and size must be pre-specified, and part of the edge over baselines is that baselines need a combinatorial permutation search to even define groups. Reviewer originality scores are low (mostly 2) since the pieces (VAE, group-TC penalty from the authors' prior work) are borrowed; the reframe and the neuroscience control are what carry it.
- **Pairs with:** LINT (Valente et al. extracting latent dynamics from lrRNNs) and pals2024 SMC inference for lrRNNs, against beta-TCVAE's total-correlation decomposition which this generalizes to groups.

### 4. Identifying Connectivity Distributions from Neural Dynamics Using Flows
Timothy Doyeon Kim, Ulises Pereira Obilinovic, Yiliu Wang, Eric Todd SheaBrown, Uygar Sümbül. ICML 2026. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=AuiUlu3OmT) · [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26506v2)

- **The move:** Because the mean-field lrRNN dynamics depend only on E[n|m,b,d] (law of iterated expectations), fit that conditional mean by ridge/Sylvester regression and fill every unconstrained direction with the maximum-entropy distribution instead of a point estimate.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The spine is the right stance for a degenerate inverse problem: cleanly separate what the data force (a conditional first moment) from what they leave free, then refuse to invent structure in the free part by taking the max-entropy completion. The one-line law-of-iterated-expectations argument, that the K to infinity dynamics see only the first moment of p(n|m,b,d), is genuinely teachable, and the paper is honest that this recasts circuit inference as labeling which features are computationally necessary versus artifacts of underconstraint. Builds the muscle of distinguishing identified from unidentified directions rather than reporting a single fitted network.
- **Cost:** 60-90 min; hard; needs lrRNN mean-field theory, flow matching / CNFs, and identifiability up to linear transforms.
- **Reviews and caveats:** Accept, avg recommendation 4.25, but soundness is the weak axis (3,1,2,3): multiple reviewers praised the identifiability decomposition and the first-moment insight as self-consistent and important, while one flagged a concrete concern that at finite K_obs away from the mean-field limit the per-neuron parameters need not be independent, which the paper downplays. Not hype-driven; the reviews are specific and technical, so the low soundness is a real caveat rather than noise. Honest limit: The second-moment covariance S is left as a free hyperparameter, so 'maximally unbiased' is exact only for the first moment and the rest defaults to Gaussian for tractability; reviewers gave low soundness (one a 1) over whether the mean-field independence holds at finite K_obs, and validation is mostly synthetic ground-truth with a single rat-cortex dataset.
- **Pairs with:** Beiran et al. 2021 mean-field lrRNNs (the dynamics it inverts) and Qian et al. 2024 / Arora and Pillow 2025 MAP estimation of N, which its regression step subsumes as the first-moment case.

### 5. Needles in the Haystack: Addressing Signal Dilution Improves scRNA-seq Perturbation Response Modeling and Evaluation
Gabriel Mateo Mejia, Henry E Miller, Francis J.A. Leblanc, BO WANG, Brendan Swain, Lucas Paulo de Lima Camillo. ICML 2026. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=XsrXLPxBJw)

- **The move:** Show the embarrassing 'mean predictor beats perturbation models' result is a metric artifact of unweighted error under sparse DE signal, then fix evaluation with DEG-weighted MSE and weighted delta-R2 plus calibrated null and positive baselines so the mean sinks to null score, and reuse the same weighting as a training loss to cut mode collapse.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It takes a genuinely puzzling benchmark finding and traces it, via controlled in-silico simulations where sparsity is dialed by hand, to a mechanical artifact: unweighted metrics reward predicting the mean when only a handful of genes actually move. The transferable move is diagnosing a benchmark pathology by construction and defining explicit negative and positive baselines so 'null' has a score, which builds the muscle of distrusting an aggregate error metric whenever the true signal lives in a few coordinates.
- **Cost:** Short-medium read, low-medium difficulty; needs familiarity with the virtual-cell perturbation benchmark discourse (why the mean-baseline debate exists).
- **Reviews and caveats:** Stronger than typical, avg rec 4.25 with soundness uniformly 3; specific technical praise for the controlled-simulation attribution analysis that isolates control bias, perturbation strength, and sampling size before validating on real data, which sanity-checks soundness. One substantive dissent that the weighted metric is unoriginal and the scope narrow. Consensus reads idea-driven, not hype-driven. Honest limit: Abstract-only for me, so I cannot verify the in-silico simulation is not rigged toward the conclusion, and the DEG set is estimated from the same data, raising a real leakage/circularity risk in both the metric and the training loss. One reviewer flags that DEG-based weighting is not itself novel and the contribution is relatively narrow, so the gem is the diagnosis, not the metric.
- **Pairs with:** Sits against the Ahlmann-Eltze 'mean baseline wins' critique and the Replogle22 / Norman19 / scPerturb benchmark line; this is the methodological rebuttal that explains the anomaly rather than just reporting it.

### 6. Interpretable Neural ODEs for Gene Regulatory Network Discovery under Perturbations
Zaikang Lin, Sei Chang, Aaron Zweig, Minseo Kang, Fabian J Theis, Elham Azizi, et al.. ICML 2026. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=nG9bsQq2N6) · [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02409v6)

- **The move:** Bury the GRN inside a neural ODE as a low-rank two-layer network G = A diag(alpha) B with a Hill-equivalent sigmoid and a linear decay term, so one model does both perturbed trajectory inference and interpretable GRN readout and can predict unseen interventions.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The mechanism is load-bearing rather than vocabulary: the logistic sigmoid is chosen for its equivalence to the Hill function on log-expression, the -Wy decay term is a real RNA-turnover model that doubles as a trapping region stabilizing the ODE, and the A diag(alpha) B factorization turns a d-by-d gene matrix into 2dl parameters whose latent modules recover known developmental circuits (validated by GSEA and comparison to Erdos-Renyi nulls). It builds the taste muscle of grounding a black box so its parameters mean something, and the honest 'we tie or lose on accuracy, we win on scale and held-out prediction' framing is a model of how to report a method whose edge is not the leaderboard.
- **Cost:** 60 min; medium; needs neural ODEs, the NO-TEARS/DCDI causal-discovery lineage, and some systems-biology vocabulary.
- **Reviews and caveats:** Split panel, avg rec 3.5 (5/4/3/2), soundness 3/2/4/3. The two higher reviewers give specific technical praise for the unified dynamics-plus-structure framing and scalability; the lone floor-scoring dissent is a substantive novelty complaint, not vague. Acceptance is idea-driven, not hype-driven, but the novelty critique and the placeholder identifiability citation are real caveats. Honest limit: It does not beat DCDI at 400 genes and only ties Bicycle at 100; GEARS still wins the held-out W2 metric per a reviewer. Identifiability is called 'delicate' and rests on an anonymous 'Authors (2025)' placeholder self-citation with no proof for the ODE case, and single-time-point data leaves direct vs indirect effects confounded. One reviewer scored originality and presentation at the floor calling it an incremental neural-ODE-for-perturbation application, which is a fair partial hit.
- **Pairs with:** Bicycle (Rohbeck et al. 2024), which casts the GRN as OU drift and hits a steady state via the Lyapunov equation; PerturbODE is the dynamic-trajectory cousin that scales past the ~100-gene ceiling, and the SCM-from-ODE bridge traces to Mooij et al. 2013.

### 7. Modeling Temporal scRNA-seq Data with Latent Gaussian Process and Optimal Transport
Mehmet Yigit Balik, Harri Lähdesmäki. ICML 2026. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=8RE5i7g4uB) · [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20989v1)

- **The move:** Drop the encoder and the neural ODE/SDE: model latent population dynamics as a heteroscedastic Hilbert-space GP whose decoded snapshots are matched to data purely through optimal transport.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The load-bearing argument is that destructive measurement gives no paired trajectories, so an encoder is forced to double as both compressor and initial-condition setter and breaks under distribution shift, whereas a GP-prior latent trajectory only needs a smooth decoder. The heteroscedastic GP lets biological variance grow at developmental transitions instead of being fixed by an SDE diffusion term, and the homoscedastic ablation is consistently worse, so the uncertainty structure is real not garnish. Builds the muscle of matching model architecture to exactly what the measurement process denies you.
- **Cost:** 60-90 min; moderate; needs GP-prior VAEs, Hilbert-space reduced-rank GP (Solin-Sarkka), and entropic OT / Wasserstein-autoencoder background.
- **Reviews and caveats:** Consensus accept (recs 4,4,4,5) but uniformly modest significance=3 and originality=3, so reviewers read it as solid-not-groundbreaking. Soundness 3,3,1,4: the lone 1 is a red flag but isolated against two 3s and a 4, and the paper reads as internally coherent; no hype-driven or AI-smelling pattern. Honest limit: Five bundled components (heteroscedastic GP, OT, latent time, cell-type Kronecker, gradient perturbation) blur which one carries the gains, and several leaderboard margins over VGFM/scNODE are within noise except at late extrapolation times. The perturbation extension is only a self-consistency check (DE genes shift the target cell fraction, non-DE genes do not) and is never validated against real perturbation data. One of four reviewers gave soundness 1.
- **Pairs with:** Waddington-OT (Schiebinger 2019) and scNODE/MIOFlow as the encoder-plus-neural-dynamics baselines it argues against; contrast the GP prior with PI-SDE's least-action potential.


## A2. Virtual patient and clinical modeling

### 1. MEG-XL: Data-Efficient Brain-to-Text via Long-Context Pre-Training
Dulhan Jayalath, Oiwi Parker Jones. ICML 2026. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=cefix4VmhS) · [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02494v2)

- **The move:** Pre-train MEG on 2.5-minute windows and use a matched-vs-full-context linear probe to prove that exploiting long neural context is a learned prior, not free architectural access: inference context beyond the pre-training window buys nothing.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The keepable content is not the foundation-model leaderboard, it is the matched-vs-full-context probe that surgically separates has-access-to-X from learned-to-use-X, paired with an attention-distance/entropy analysis showing long-context pre-training induces hierarchical near-to-far attention that short-context models never acquire. It also delivers a clean honest negative: on deep single-subject LibriBrain, supervised from-scratch eventually overtakes pre-training past about 2.5 hours, naming exactly where the prior stops paying. Builds the epistemics muscle of designing a control that isolates a learned capability from mere window size.
- **Cost:** 60 min; moderate; needs masked-token pre-training, RVQ tokenizers (BioCodec), and factorized criss-cross attention background.
- **Reviews and caveats:** Three reviews, avg rec 4, uniform soundness 3 and significance 3, originality 2-3. Consensus is a sound, rigorous but not groundbreaking accept; Reviewer 3 gave specific praise for the random-init baseline and the multi-regime dataset design, which is the strongest signal. No hype red flag, but middling originality confirms this is idea-thin outside the one diagnostic. Honest limit: Roughly half the paper is a six-model foundation-model comparison table, and the framing leans hard on the LLM length-generalization analogy. The long-context story is softened by the fine-tuning input being stitched 3s word-windows rather than continuous physiological time, and the task is perceived speech over a 50-word vocabulary, far from clinical use. Reviewers scored originality only 2-3.
- **Pairs with:** Read against d'Ascoli et al. 2025 (sentence-level supervised word decoding, the direct supervised SOTA it beats in low-data) and the 'Are EEG foundation models worth it?' critique it functions as a rebuttal to.

### 2. Training-Free Adversarial Robustness in Computational MRI
Mahdi Saberi, Chi Zhang, Mehmet Akcakaya. ICML 2026. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=NUDNFusCDu) · [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01908v4)

- **The move:** Repurpose cyclic measurement consistency (resample a fresh undersampling pattern, reconstruct, check agreement on the acquired k-space lines) as both a detector and a training-free corrector for adversarial attacks on physics-driven MRI reconstruction.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The mechanism is physically honest and load-bearing: an imperceptible attack barely moves the acquired k-space lines but blows up the unobserved complement Omega^C, so resampling lines from that complement and re-reconstructing exposes the perturbation as a jump in data-fidelity error. Reversing it via a small corrective perturbation optimized for cyclic consistency needs no retraining and no clean reference, which is exactly the right constraint for the medical setting. It builds the muscle of turning a self-consistency structure you already own (the forward operator) into a defense instead of bolting on a trained module.
- **Cost:** 60-90 min, moderate difficulty. Needs PD-DL / unrolled MRI reconstruction (MoDL, SSDU) and adversarial-attack (PGD) background.
- **Reviews and caveats:** Accept, avg 3.75, but soundness 3/3/2/2 and significance 3/2/2/2 are the soft spots. The one piece of consensus specific praise is that using cyclic consistency for adversarial detection/mitigation (rather than training/calibration) is novel and leverages domain structure, which is the actual gem. Lukewarm significance and the empirical-threshold weakness are the honest red flags; no AI-smell. Honest limit: Confined to MRI physics and judged by PSNR/SSIM on fastMRI; detection rests on a dataset-dependent empirical threshold tau with no guarantee, mitigation adds inference-time reverse-PGD cost, and adaptive-attack robustness is not really established. Reviewers rate soundness weakly (two 2s) and significance low across the board, and one questions whether the attack model is realistic for MRI.
- **Pairs with:** Denoised-smoothing / SMUG for the retraining-based defenses it beats, and multi-mask self-supervised MRI (SSDU, Yaman 2022) where the cyclic-resampling trick originated as a training device before being turned here into a detector.

### 3. Automatic Construction of Clinical Scoring Systems with LLM Agents
Silas Ruhrberg Estévez, Christopher Chiu, Mihaela van der Schaar. ICML 2026. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=fFUI2PGaNG) · [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22324v2)

- **The move:** Treat deployability, not interpretability, as the hard modeling constraint: fix the class to unit-weighted N-of-M checklists and split LLM rule-proposal from a deterministic AUROC/Jaccard verify-and-select loop.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The keeper is the conceptual reframe: interpretability does not imply deployability, and a bedside checklist is a deliberately constrained model class chosen for memorability and arithmetic-free execution, not an approximation to a regressor. It builds the taste muscle of picking your hypothesis class from deployment constraints rather than from approximation quality, and the proposal-versus-verification decomposition (LLM never sees patient rows, filter enforces validity) is a reusable template for constrained combinatorial discovery.
- **Cost:** 45 min, low-medium difficulty, needs familiarity with sparse score learning (RiskSLIM/FasterRisk) and clinical AUROC framing.
- **Reviews and caveats:** Four reviews, recommendations 4.75 avg but soundness flat at 3. Specific technical dissent on missing prior art (Zhang 2021 checklists) and no comparison to unconstrained models; genuine praise for the deployability-vs-interpretability distinction. Acceptance is idea-driven (the reframe), not results-driven, since it does not top the table. Honest limit: The method is an LLM-agent pipeline that does not beat decision trees on raw AUROC (0.71 vs 0.73); it only beats the integer-score baselines. Rule discovery is knowledge-bounded by what the LLM can surface, there are no optimization guarantees, and the unit-weighted-checklist model class is partly pre-existing (Zhang 2021), a comparison the authors omit. Soundness scores are uniformly 3 despite high recommendations.
- **Pairs with:** Read against Ustun and Rudin's RiskSLIM to see what the LLM proposal step adds over fixed-feature MIP, and against Zhang et al. 2021 predictive checklists (integer programming), which reviewers note pursues the same goal with a real optimizer and is not compared.


## A3. Biological and molecular foundation models (bioLM)

### 1. Conditionally Site-Independent Neural Evolution of Antibody Sequences
Stephen Zhewen Lu, Aakarsh Vermani, Kohei Sanno, Jiarui Lu, Frederick A Matsen, Milind Jagota, et al.. ICML 2026. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=DcyK4f7ZyU) · [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18982v4)

- **The move:** Parameterize a per-site CTMC rate matrix with a sequence-conditioned neural net, prove it is a first-order approximation to the intractable sequential point-mutation process with error quadratic in branch length, and recover the exact process at sampling via Gillespie.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Reframes antibody modeling from a stationary marginal-distribution problem into a lineage-structured evolutionary-dynamics one, and the borrowed CTMC/phylogenetics structure is load-bearing three times over: it yields an honest O(t^2) error bound, a log-likelihood ratio against a frozen SHM model that disentangles selection from mutation without DASM's heuristic clamping, and a clean transfer of discrete-diffusion classifier guidance to steer Gillespie sampling. It builds the muscle of importing a generative process (not just a loss) from an adjacent field and making its theory earn its keep.
- **Cost:** 2-3 hrs, moderate-hard, needs CTMC/phylogenetics, population-genetics fixation, and discrete-diffusion guidance
- **Reviews and caveats:** Four reviewers, unanimous accept (avg rec 3.75), soundness uniformly 3 with specific praise for the CTMC motivation and the bounded first-order proof; the concrete criticism is the O(t^2) branch-length error, which the authors already acknowledge and study. No hype red flag, no substantive dissent. Honest limit: The exact-sampling guarantee (Lemma 4.2) assumes learned rates equal the true generator, but training uses the factorized likelihood so rates are biased on long branches, undercutting the elegant fix; the model ignores indels and needs equal-length alignments, and VEP gains over DASM are real but modest on several assays.
- **Pairs with:** Direct contrast with DASM+Thrifty (Matsen 2025) for selection/SHM disentangling; Nisonoff 2025 supplies the guidance generator they adapt.

### 2. Autoregressive Boltzmann Generators
Danyal Rehman, Charlie B. Tan, Yoshua Bengio, Joey Bose, Alexander Tong. ICML 2026 (spotlight). [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=75AYDsndHP) · [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27361v1)

- **The move:** Drop the invertibility of normalizing-flow Boltzmann Generators for an autoregressive factorization over Cartesian coordinates, because a homeomorphic flow from a single Gaussian cannot cheaply model disjoint metastable modes but an AR model with exact one-pass likelihood can.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The load-bearing content is a topological argument, not a benchmark: practical NFs preserve the topology of their Gaussian prior, so they are forced into ill-conditioned high-distortion maps to reach separated molecular basins, and abandoning invertibility for AR sidesteps that pathology while keeping exact likelihoods for importance sampling. Proposition 1 cleanly pins the irreducible KL cost of uniform binning to within-bin non-uniformity, and the AR structure enables partial-energy twisted SMC steering that all-at-once flows structurally cannot do. It builds the muscle of asking whether an expensive architectural constraint like invertibility is actually earning its keep.
- **Cost:** 60-90 min; moderate; needs normalizing flows, CNFs, importance sampling, and basic AR/LLM modeling.
- **Reviews and caveats:** Four reviewers all recommend 5 (spotlight) with high-to-mid confidence, but soundness 3-3-4-3 and significance 3-3-4-3 and no review text available; consensus reads as genuine excitement about the reframe with unremarkable rigor scores, not a hype-inflated leaderboard acceptance, though the flat soundness 3s are a mild flag. Honest limit: Heavy SOTA framing around a 60% headline plus per-system temperature tuning, and the flagship twisted-SMC steering only marginally beats plain SNIS (authors admit this, since the base proposal is already good), so the steering capability is more promise than demonstrated payoff. Reviews are unanimous 5/spotlight but with only middling soundness (3s) and no text, so the enthusiasm is idea-driven rather than rigor-driven.
- **Pairs with:** Read against SBG (Sequential Boltzmann Generators) and Prose as the flow-based predecessors it argues against; the topology claim echoes Cornish et al. on relaxing topological constraints of normalizing flows.

### 3. Induction Meets Biology: Mechanisms of Repeat Detection in Protein Language Models
Gal Pomerants, Yaniv Nikankin, Anja Reusch, Tomer Tsaban, Ora Schueler-Furman, Yonatan Belinkov. ICML 2026. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=vPUInIYURl) · [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23179v4)

- **The move:** Reverse-engineer ESM circuits for repeat completion and show the text-LLM induction-head mechanism transports wholesale into protein models, then docks onto biologically specialized neurons whose learned amino-acid similarity independently recapitulates BLOSUM62 substitution scoring.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Two findings pay rent. First, the approximate-repeat circuit functionally subsumes the exact-repeat circuit (cross-task faithfulness over 1.0 in one direction only), so there is one mechanism generalizing rather than two. Second, the PLM's internal similarity function, read off neuron activations, aligns with BLOSUM62 groupings, the exact matrix classical alignment tools use. It builds the taste muscle of asking whether a foundation model has secretly reinvented a known algorithm, and the induction-head-plus-biochemical-neuron decomposition is a clean three-stage story with logit-lens and interaction evidence rather than hand-waving.
- **Cost:** 60-90 min; moderate; needs comfort with circuit discovery (attribution patching, faithfulness, logit lens) and basic protein biochemistry
- **Reviews and caveats:** Strong and idea-driven (recs 4,5,4,6); reviewers give specific technical praise that induction heads in non-causal encoder PLMs had not been shown before and that the reverse-engineering toolkit is a model reference. Soundness is mixed (2,3,2,4) but the low scores attach to the many degrees of freedom and correlational concept-matching, not a fabricated result, so the honest limitations section pre-empts them. ## Threads

### Flow and bridge matching became the lingua franca
The most common machine this year is conditional flow or bridge matching, and it does wildly different jobs. In virtual cell it reconstructs single-cell lineages through a Poisson-Brownian branching bridge (A1, Beyond Continuity), aligns cellular trajectories (A1, CellBRIDGE), and couples latent Gaussian processes to optimal transport (A1). In transferable methods it becomes Riemannian metric matching (A4), tilt matching for sampling and fine-tuning (A4), gauge-fixed population dynamics (A4), two-parameter physics-time flows (A4), and multimarginal OT flows (A4). In geometry it is convex distance-operator transport (B4) and the f-trajectory-balance loss family (B4). The throughline: choose a coupling and a conditional path, and a hard variational problem collapses into a regression. The tension worth holding is that the same expressive machinery that simulates branching lineages also makes it easy to match marginals while missing dynamics, which is exactly the failure the trajectory-inference (B4, Relative Entropy in Function Space) and signal-dilution (A1) papers diagnose.

### Identifiability as a stance, not a theorem
The best papers here share one move: separate what the data force from what they leave free, then stay disciplined about the free part. The connectivity-distributions paper (A1) fits only the conditional first moment the mean-field dynamics can see and fills the rest with a maximum-entropy completion; the factorized low-rank RNN (A1) turns an affine gauge freedom into a testable partial-disentanglement choice; domain transfer becomes identifiable from a single aligned anchor (A4); and the causal section pushes the idea into completeness for counterfactual identification (B3), a holistic account of selection bias (B3), and optimal structure learning with conditional-independence testing (B3). Read together, they argue that identifiability is less a yes-or-no theorem than a habit of labeling which directions are pinned and refusing to invent structure in the ones that are not.

### Null models and the refusal to trust a metric
A strong skeptical current runs through the list: papers whose whole contribution is to kill a reflex. Untrained networks and hand-built structural nulls match microscopy foundation models (A1); the embarrassing mean-predictor-wins result in perturbation modeling is traced to unweighted error under sparse differential-expression signal (A1); Gaussian mean-field variational inference provably overestimates predictive variance in the overparameterized limit (B1); the last layer is asked whether it is even sufficient for uncertainty quantification (B1); sharpness-aware minimization is shown to hallucinate minimizers (B2); and deep sequence models are shown to memorize geometrically for reasons no one can yet fully explain (B2). The generalizable lesson: run the null before you trust the number.

### Geometry as a proof engine
Information geometry earns its section by doing work rather than decorating. The Fisher geometry of softmax is used to probe and steer models (B4); attention is shown to implement the Fisher geometry of exponential families (B4); SVRG is revealed as a special case of Bayesian posterior correction over exponential-family posteriors (B4); weight-space symmetries are exploited to approximate curvature cheaply (B4); and peptide embedding spaces are navigated with an explicit Riemannian metric (A3). Each passes the load-bearing test: remove the geometry and the result collapses. Set against the flow-matching thread, the throughline is that this year's most durable ideas turn a computation you were already doing into a statement about an invariant.

## Synthesis

ICML 2026 reads as a field getting more expressive and more skeptical at the same time. The expressive half is dominated by generative dynamics: flow matching, Schrodinger bridges, and optimal transport now form a shared substrate carrying single-cell biology, sampling, and manifold geometry, and the virtual-cell work in particular has converged on population dynamics as the right object of inference. The skeptical half is a maturing measurement discipline: null models, identifiability stances, and negative results that kill benchmark reflexes. These halves are in productive tension, because the same machinery that matches marginals or fits a connectivity matrix is exactly what the diagnostic papers show can match the wrong thing, and several papers here sit on both sides at once. For the bio reader, the most direct imports are the connectivity-distribution maximum-entropy stance and the gauge-freedom population-dynamics move, both of which are general recipes for degenerate inverse problems wearing a neuroscience or physics costume. For the theory reader, the geometry-as-proof-engine papers and the identifiability results are where the durable ideas live; the leaderboard papers are not. The disagreement worth sitting with is whether foundation-model representations of cells encode biology or texture, since the microscopy-null and signal-dilution papers suggest current benchmarks cannot yet tell. Two questions to carry forward: whether the flow-matching substrate can be made to respect a known conservation law or gene-regulatory constraint rather than only fitting marginals, and whether identifiability-as-a-stance can become a default reporting standard rather than a per-paper virtue. One caveat on the whole list: acceptance and review scores are a weak signal here, so read for the move, not the number.

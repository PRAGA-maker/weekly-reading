# Weekly Reading List, 06-25-26

A geometry-heavy week. The strongest throughline is that the choice made before the data, which symmetry group, which scale prior, which divergence, which estimand, fixes the answer, and several of the best papers are deflationary: they strip a geometric costume off a phenomenon that turns out to be plain statistics. Bayesian and information geometry are unusually entangled this week, so read those two sections together.

Source note: arXiv's API and listing pages returned 403 at the start of the run and came online mid-run; every pick below was verified against its abstract page. Target trial papers were located through the PubMed connector and are linked by DOI.

## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Hierarchical Bayesian Estimation of Covariance Matrices
Daniel Xiang, Malgorzata Bogdan, Jonas Wallin, Daniel Yekutieli. arXiv:2606.24751 (stat.ME), 23 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24751

- **The move:** Shrinkage covariance estimators are exactly the orthogonally O(p)-equivariant rules; demand full GL(p) equivariance and you are trapped on scalar multiples of the sample covariance, so the whole shrinkage literature is recovered as Bayes optimal under the smaller group.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It reframes "why does shrinkage beat the sample covariance" as a fact about which symmetry you insist on. Haff, Ledoit-Wolf, and friends are precisely what orthogonal equivariance buys you over general-linear equivariance. They then derive oracle Bayes rules under Frobenius, Stein, and squared-Stein loss and approximate them with a finite Pólya tree prior on the eigenvalue spectrum. Builds the muscle of reading an estimator class off its invariance group.
- **Cost:** ~50 min, moderate to hard, needs comfort with equivariance and decision-theoretic risk.
- **Pairs with:** the shell-geometry shrinkage paper below, and the Bregman projection tutorial in information geometry.

### 2. Variance or Standard Deviation? Shell Geometry and Global-Scale Priors in High-Dimensional Shrinkage
Wayne Yuan Gao, Zhiheng You. arXiv:2606.23509 (stat.ME, econ.EM), 22 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23509

- **The move:** Putting a flat prior on the variance versus on the standard deviation changes high-dimensional shrinkage risk at first order, because the two allocate different mass on the thin shell near zero scale.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A choice usually treated as notational has a one-unit asymptotic risk consequence near the origin under a radial-power benchmark, with a crossover in the critical regime and second-order equivalence for strong signals. The mechanism is geometric: in high dimensions the prior's near-zero exponent dominates. It retrains you to ask where a prior puts mass rather than what shape it has, and to distrust "default" scale priors.
- **Cost:** ~40 min, moderate, needs high-dimensional asymptotics.
- **Pairs with:** the covariance-equivariance paper above, and Fisher Width in information geometry.

### 3. Strong Likelihood Principle: Strengthening a Principle or Misunderstanding the Likelihood Function
Paul William Vos. arXiv:2606.08975 (stat.OT), 8 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08975

- **The move:** The strong likelihood principle collapses into the weak one once you insist the likelihood is a function on a family of distributions, not on a parameter space.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A contrarian re-reading of the Birnbaum argument that has been litigated for decades. Rather than attacking sufficiency or conditionality, Vos argues the SLP equivocates on the domain of the likelihood, and that proper standardization across a shared-parameter binomial and negative binomial dissolves the paradox. The same conclusion arrives independently from a Fisher-information-metric argument about manifold distance. Foundations done with geometry instead of philosophy.
- **Cost:** ~35 min, moderate, mostly conceptual with light geometry.
- **Pairs with:** the simplex geometry in the Bregman tutorial, and likelihood-principle debates.

### 4. Action-BED: Task-Driven Bayesian Experimental Design with Singly Intractable Objectives
Tom Rossa, Angus Phillips, Tom Rainforth. arXiv:2606.23662 (stat.ML), 22 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23662

- **The move:** Recast Bayesian experimental design as minimizing expected future loss on downstream actions, which turns the usual doubly intractable information objective into a singly intractable one optimizable by stochastic gradients over design and action policies jointly.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Standard BED maximizes expected information gain, doubly intractable and indifferent to what the experiment is for. Going back to decision theory, they show every expected-future-loss objective rearranges into something needing only samples from the joint model and a loss, sidestepping posterior and marginal-likelihood estimation entirely. It removes both the computational bottleneck and the pretense that information, rather than a decision, is the goal.
- **Cost:** ~45 min, moderate to hard, needs decision theory and amortized inference.
- **Pairs with:** target trial estimand specification (what is the experiment for) and the measurement-as-decision thread.

### 5. A Flat Connection: The Pooling Factor and the Geometry of Centring in Hierarchical MCMC
Aidan D. Bindoff. arXiv:2606.19655 (stat.CO, math.ST), 17 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19655

- **The move:** The centring versus non-centring obstruction in hierarchical models is not geometric. The Fisher-induced Ehresmann connection is provably flat for any smooth hierarchical posterior, so the obstruction reduces to one statistical quantity, the classical pooling factor.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A genuine negative result. You might expect the funnel and the centring problem to be curvature felt by the sampler as holonomy. Bindoff proves the connection is flat because its horizontal leaves are level sets of the fiber score, then identifies the real culprit as the prior fraction governing base-fiber dependence. Curvature only re-enters through a sampler's fixed mass matrix, an algorithmic artifact rather than a property of the posterior. It sharpens your sense of which pathologies are geometry and which are statistics, and ships an R package, fibr.
- **Cost:** ~50 min, hard, needs fiber bundles and HMC internals, but the payoff is conceptual.
- **Pairs with:** the information-geometry section below, and anyone who has fought Neal's funnel.

## B. Measurement theory

### 1. When Surveys Become Conversations: Adaptive Matrix Validation for AI-Assisted Interviews
Tyler H. McCormick. arXiv:2606.24244 (stat.ME), 23 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24244

- **The move:** Treat the AI that turns a free-form interview into tabular survey variables as a fallible, versioned, subgroup-dependent measurement instrument, and budget a small randomized set of validation questions to correct its error.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Most "LLM extracts structured data" work ignores that the extraction is a measurement model whose bias varies across people and model versions. McCormick builds estimators that calibrate mapped values using other respondents' validation answers, then correct the residual with the target respondent's own, with planning formulas for how many validation items and what sample size you need, tested on an American Time Use Survey emulation and verbal-autopsy narratives. The taste it builds: see a model output as a measurement with a bias and a variance, not as data.
- **Cost:** ~40 min, moderate, survey-statistics flavored.
- **Pairs with:** the foundation-model validity paper below, and measurement-error correction in causal inference.

### 2. Validity Threats for Foundation Model Research
Gunnar König, Martin Pawelczyk, Ulrike von Luxburg, Sebastian Bordt. arXiv:2606.05029 (cs.LG), 3 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05029

- **The move:** Cast cheap approximations to the ideal foundation-model experiment, proxy experiments, scaling laws, single-run designs, as a causal-inference problem, and read off the validity each one trades away.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It imports the four-validity vocabulary from empirical social science, statistical, internal, external, construct, and shows each compute-saving strategy has a characteristic profile: proxies buy internal validity at the cost of construct and external, observational model studies face confounding and effect heterogeneity, single-run designs suffer interference between treated units. A rare attempt to make ML methodology answerable to measurement theory rather than to leaderboard intuition.
- **Cost:** ~35 min, easy to moderate, conceptual.
- **Pairs with:** the target trial papers (same causal-validity logic) and McCormick above.

### 3. The Unsampled Truth: Psychometrics in SLMs Measure Prompt Artifacts, Not Psychological Constructs
Nils Schwager, Christoph Hau, Simon Münker, Achim Rettinger. arXiv:2606.03357 (cs.CL), 2 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03357

- **The move:** Administer personality scales to small language models and the artifactual variance from personas, item wording, and option symbols routinely overwhelms the supposed trait signal.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A clean negative result with a method attached. By systematically varying the irrelevant surface across 13 models, they show the "trait" you measure is mostly prompt compliance, and they package the variation framework as a construct-validity diagnostic. This is representational measurement biting: if your scale moves under transformations that should not matter, you are not measuring the construct, you are measuring the instrument.
- **Cost:** ~30 min, easy, empirical.
- **Pairs with:** the LLM self-report and IRT papers, and any benchmark you currently trust.

### 4. Item Response Scaling Laws: A Measurement Theory Approach for Efficient and Generalizable Neural Scaling Estimation
Sang Truong, Yuheng Tu, Rylan Schaeffer, Sanmi Koyejo. arXiv:2606.07616 (cs.LG), 29 May 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07616

- **The move:** Fold item response theory into scaling-law estimation so latent model ability and question difficulty factorize, cutting the M-by-N model-question cost to M-plus-N.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Scaling laws usually treat each model-benchmark cell independently and need thousands of checkpoints. Putting an IRT measurement model underneath separates ability from item characteristics, and a one-time calibration lets 50 questions per benchmark recover the same decisions as exhaustive evaluation, with abilities that transfer across benchmarks sharing a measurement objective. The reframe worth keeping: a benchmark score is a measurement of a latent ability, so measurement theory tells you how to spend evaluation budget.
- **Cost:** ~40 min, moderate, needs IRT and scaling-law basics.
- **Pairs with:** the SLM psychometrics critique, and the foundation-model validity paper.

### 5. Multidimensional Item Response Theory under General Latent Distributions
Chengyu Cui, Taoyi Chen, Chun Wang, Gongjun Xu. arXiv:2605.30471 (stat.ME, stat.AP), 28 May 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30471

- **The move:** Replace the Gaussian latent-trait assumption in multidimensional IRT with a normalizing flow, so skew, heavy tails, and multimodality in the latent distribution stop biasing the item parameters.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The genuine psychometric-theory anchor this week. The standard MIRT convenience assumption, Gaussian latent traits, is not innocuous: misspecifying the latent law biases the item parameters you care about. They learn the latent distribution as an invertible transform of a base distribution and approximate the posterior with a conditional flow, estimating item parameters, latent distribution, and posterior jointly. A reminder that the measurement model's prior on the construct is itself an identifying assumption.
- **Cost:** ~45 min, moderate to hard, needs latent-variable models and flows.
- **Pairs with:** Bayesian nonparametric priors, and the SLM psychometrics critique.

## C. Target trial emulation

Located via PubMed; links are DOIs.

### 1. Immortal time bias from selection: a principal stratification perspective
Bronner P. Gonçalves, Etsuji Suzuki. American Journal of Epidemiology, Sep 2025. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwaf146

- **The move:** Recast eligibility-related immortal time bias as a principal stratification problem, so the estimand that conditions on post-baseline eligibility is exactly a contrast over partially overlapping principal strata.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The textbook time-fixed immortal time bias is well worn; the version created by defining eligibility using post-time-zero information is barely discussed. Framing it through principal strata explains precisely why the arms are noncomparable after selection, and shows the conditional outcome probabilities converge to the unconditional potential outcomes as eligibility timing approaches time zero. It teaches you to see a selection bias as a hidden stratum contrast, and suggests principal effects as an alternative estimand to the emulation default.
- **Cost:** ~40 min, moderate to hard, needs potential outcomes and principal stratification.
- **Pairs with:** clone-censor-weight intuition, and the missingness-in-eligibility paper below.

### 2. Missingness in Eligibility Criteria for Target Trial Emulation in EHR With Survival Outcomes
Jenny Shen, Kristin A. Linn, Amy S. Clark, Ronac Mamtani, Rebecca A. Hubbard. Statistics in Medicine, Apr 2026. https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.70500

- **The move:** When eligibility-defining variables are missing, impute before excluding ineligible patients, not after, and use a flexible imputation model such as a random forest.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A small ordering decision with large consequences. Whether you multiply-impute eligibility variables before or after dropping ineligibles changes the bias of the causal estimate, and simulation plus oncology EHR data show flexible imputation before exclusion wins under heavy missingness, beating both complete-case and impute-after. The kind of operational detail target trial protocols gloss over and that silently determines whether the emulation is valid.
- **Cost:** ~35 min, moderate, needs multiple imputation and survival basics.
- **Pairs with:** the principal-stratification immortal time paper, and any EHR emulation.

### 3. Design and Implementation of Observational Studies Emulating a Target Trial
Yan Ren, Yulong Jia, et al. JAMA Network Open, Feb 2026. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.58262

- **The move:** An audit of 237 published target trial emulations finds most skip the parts that make the framework work: only 56% prespecified a protocol, 15% used post-baseline information to define eligibility, and only 17% drew the follow-up diagram that pins down time zero.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The field's own scorecard, and it is unflattering. The value is not the five-step framework they propose but the empirical map of where emulations fail in practice, which doubles as a checklist of the moves that actually matter. Read it as a list of the ways your next emulation will be wrong, then go fix them in advance.
- **Cost:** ~30 min, easy, methodological review.
- **Pairs with:** every applied emulation below, as the rubric.

### 4. Effectiveness of Bedaquiline Use beyond Six Months in Patients with Multidrug-Resistant Tuberculosis
Letizia Trevisi, Miguel A. Hernán, et al. American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine, Jun 2023. https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202211-2125OC

- **The move:** A clone-censor-weight emulation of bedaquiline duration shows the naive analysis manufactures a benefit for longer treatment that disappears once immortal person-time is handled.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The cleanest applied demonstration of the bias the method exists to kill. Naive comparison says 12-plus months beats 6 (ratio 1.09); the properly cloned, censored, and weighted estimate says they are equal (1.01). Same data, opposite clinical conclusion, and the entire difference is immortal time. Keep it as the canonical before-and-after for any treatment-duration question.
- **Cost:** ~30 min, moderate, applied but the methods section is the point.
- **Pairs with:** the duration-effect logic, and the JAMA audit's time-zero findings.

### 5. Nonbenzodiazepine hypnotics and police-reported motor vehicle crash risk among older adults: a sequential target trial emulation
Andrew R. Zullo, et al. American Journal of Epidemiology, Mar 2025. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwae168

- **The move:** A sequential emulation re-run weekly over a decade separates the intention-to-treat and per-protocol estimands for Z-drugs and crashes, and they point in opposite directions.
- **Why it is worth your time:** ITT says initiating a Z-drug does nothing to crash risk (RR 1.06); sustained per-protocol use looks protective (RR 0.83). This is not a contradiction but a lesson in estimand choice: the question you ask, start the drug versus keep taking it, determines the answer, and conflating the two is how a literature argues past itself. The aging-and-driving setting makes the stakes concrete and the design (a fresh trial emulated each week) is worth studying on its own.
- **Cost:** ~35 min, moderate, needs ITT versus per-protocol and inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting.
- **Pairs with:** the bedaquiline duration paper, and the measurement-as-estimand thread.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. A Tutorial on Bregman Projection in Statistics
Gunhee Cho, Jae Kwang Kim, Yumou Qiu. arXiv:2606.21714 (math.ST), 19 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21714

- **The move:** One operation, projecting a reference onto a constrained family under a Bregman divergence, with one Pythagorean theorem, generates GLM fitting, maximum entropy, survey calibration, EM, variational inference, autoencoders, and expectation propagation.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The reframe of the week. The information (e-) projection onto moment-constrained families and the moment (m-) projection onto exponential families are the same theorem read in conjugate coordinates, and under the canonical link the GLM score equation is literally Pythagorean orthogonality. The unification is exact where the families are flat and a controlled approximation where they are not. Once you see it you cannot unsee how many separate-looking methods are a single projection.
- **Cost:** ~60 min, moderate, self-contained convex geometry then statistics.
- **Pairs with:** the covariance-equivariance and shell-geometry Bayesian papers, and the simplex geometry in the likelihood-principle paper.

### 2. On Higher-Order Geometric Refinements of Classical Covariance Asymptotics
Malik Amir, Sourangshu Ghosh. arXiv:2604.12725 (math.ST, cs.LG), 14 Apr 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12725

- **The move:** Add an order n^-2 curvature correction to the Cramér-Rao covariance that splits canonically into a Fisher-Rao Ricci term, an extrinsic second-fundamental-form term, and a Hellinger discrepancy term, and that vanishes exactly for full exponential families.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Classical Fisher asymptotics are first-order and blind to curvature. Treating the model as a Riemannian manifold immersed in L2 through the square-root density map makes the finite-sample deviation a tensor you can name and compute, and they extend it to singular models via resolution of singularities and the real log canonical threshold. The flatness of exponential families gets a precise quantitative meaning, zero correction, and the framework yields curvature-based diagnostics of weak identifiability. Builds intuition for exactly when first-order theory lies.
- **Cost:** ~70 min, hard, needs differential geometry and a little singular learning theory.
- **Pairs with:** the Bregman tutorial (flat families) and weak-identifiability questions in latent-variable models.

### 3. Fisher Width: A Geometric Measure of Complexity on Statistical Manifolds
Vu Khac Ky. arXiv:2606.18306 (cs.LG, stat.ML), 16 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18306

- **The move:** Replace the Euclidean identity in Gaussian width with the Fisher metric's local G(θ)^{1/2}, giving a reparameterization-invariant complexity measure that scales directions by statistical distinguishability rather than ambient length.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A cross-domain import that pays rent. Gaussian width organizes high-dimensional probability, compressed sensing, and learning theory, but is intrinsically Euclidean. Redoing it on the statistical manifold preserves concentration, perturbation stability, and spectral comparison bounds, yields a generalization bound for Fisher-Lipschitz classes, and exposes anisotropy invisible to Euclidean measures. Remove the Fisher rescaling and the invariance and the results collapse, which is the test that the borrowed structure is load bearing.
- **Cost:** ~45 min, moderate to hard, needs high-dimensional probability and the Fisher metric.
- **Pairs with:** the higher-order asymptotics paper, and the shell-geometry shrinkage paper.

### 4. Information-Geometric Optimization on Spheres
Vladimir Jaćimović. arXiv:2606.07588 (cs.NE, cs.LG, math.OC), 27 May 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07588

- **The move:** Ensembles of generalized Kuramoto oscillators on spheres compute natural search gradients, so a dynamical system implements information-geometric optimization in the hyperbolic geometry of Poincaré and Bergman balls.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The cross-pollination the form of this list is built to surface. The natural-gradient flow is not merely analogized to coupled oscillators, it is realized by them, with the Bergman-ball policy linking to quantum decision making. For a reader who thinks in dynamical systems and criticality, this turns an optimization update into a synchronization process, which is a bridge worth crossing. Short and idea-dense.
- **Cost:** ~35 min, moderate, needs natural gradient and a tolerance for oscillator dynamics.
- **Pairs with:** network control and criticality, and natural-gradient methods in Bayesian computation.

### 5. The macroscopic Kähler metric of Geometric Thermodynamics versus the microscopic one on the Event Manifold
Pietro Fré, Alexander S. Sorin, Mario Trigiante. arXiv:2606.09438 (hep-th, cs.IT, math-ph), 8 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09438

- **The move:** Build a metric on the contact manifold of thermodynamic variables whose pullback to equilibrium states is exactly the Fisher Hessian, tying Geometric Thermodynamics to Information Geometry, and use it to integrate exact partition functions on a class of Kähler event manifolds.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The most demanding pick and the one with the longest reach. The claim that the Fisher information metric is the pullback of a Kähler structure from a thermodynamic contact manifold puts statistical estimation and equilibrium thermodynamics on one geometric footing, and the Casimir-driven symmetry breaking they describe is a thermodynamic analogue of spontaneous magnetization. Read it for the bridge, not the special-geometry machinery, and skim the physics-heavy sections. Honest warning: it is heavy and you will not absorb all of it on one pass.
- **Cost:** ~90 min, hard, physics-flavored, needs Kähler geometry and thermodynamics.
- **Pairs with:** thermodynamic and geometric views of estimation, and the criticality thread.

## Threads

### Thread 1: Symmetry and prior geometry decide the estimator
Covariance equivariance (A1), shell-geometry shrinkage (A2), the Bregman projection tutorial (D1), Fisher Width (D3). In each, the answer is fixed before the data by a geometric commitment: which group you demand equivariance under, where the scale prior puts mass on the near-zero shell, which divergence defines projection, which metric rescales directions. The tension is real. The covariance paper wants the smaller orthogonal group precisely to escape the triviality that full general-linear equivariance forces, while the shell paper warns that a seemingly free reparameterization, variance versus standard deviation, is not a symmetry at all and changes risk at first order.

### Thread 2: Is it geometry, or is it statistics wearing a geometric costume
The flat connection (A5), the higher-order curvature correction (D2), the strong likelihood principle (A3). Each tests whether something people call geometric really is. Bindoff proves the hierarchical centring obstruction is flat, so it is statistics (the pooling factor), not curvature. Amir and Ghosh show the finite-sample correction genuinely is curvature and vanishes for flat families. Vos argues the likelihood-principle puzzle is a domain confusion that the simplex's geometry dissolves. Together they police the boundary between a load-bearing geometric cause and decoration, and they disagree on which side the interesting cases fall.

### Thread 3: A score is a measurement, an estimand is a question
Foundation-model validity (B2), item response scaling laws (B4), SLM psychometrics (B3), the immortal-time and Z-drug estimand papers (C1, C5), and Action-BED (A4). Measurement theory and causal inference are asking one question from two sides: what latent thing does this number identify, and under what assumptions. The evaluation papers import validity and IRT to discipline benchmarks; the target trial papers show the estimand, intention-to-treat versus per-protocol, eligibility timing, decides the answer; Action-BED says design the experiment for the decision it serves. The sharpest tension is trust: the SLM critique says many model measurements are artifacts, while item response scaling laws trust the measurement enough to extrapolate it across benchmarks.

### Thread 4: Estimation as a flow or as an equilibrium
Kuramoto natural-gradient optimization (D4), the Fré thermodynamic Kähler metric (D5), with support from Fisher Width (D3). Two papers refuse to treat estimation as static. One makes natural-gradient descent a synchronization process among oscillators; the other makes the Fisher metric the pullback of a thermodynamic Kähler structure on equilibrium states. For work on aging as a dynamical system and on control energy near criticality, these are the candidate load-bearing analogies, and the razor from Thread 2 applies: keep them only if removing the borrowed dynamics or thermodynamics breaks the result.

## Synthesis

This week the four domains converge on a single idea: the choice of geometry or estimand, made before the data, fixes the answer. The Bayesian and information-geometry papers show estimators are determined by symmetry groups, scale-prior mass on a thin shell, and the divergence you project under; the measurement and target trial papers show a number means nothing until you state which latent quantity or counterfactual contrast it identifies. The most useful results are deflationary. The hierarchical centring obstruction is not curvature, the strong likelihood principle is a domain confusion, small-model psychometrics measure prompt artifacts, and longer bedaquiline only looked better because of immortal time. Where the papers disagree is over how much to trust geometric structure: Fré and the Kuramoto paper bet that thermodynamic and dynamical structure is load bearing, while Bindoff and Vos spend their effort stripping false geometry away. Two questions are worth sitting with. First, when is importing a metric or a manifold a genuine reduction rather than a relabeling, and the Fisher-Width test, does the result survive removing the rescaling, is a good razor to carry into the thermodynamic and oscillator papers. Second, whether the measurement-theory framing of model evaluation and the estimand framing of causal inference can be made into one formal object, since both are theories of what a number identifies under stated assumptions. For ongoing work on aging and criticality, the transferable discipline this week is not another manifold but the habit of asking, before estimating, which symmetry and which counterfactual you have already committed to.

# Weekly Reading List — 06-24-26

This week leans hard on one question asked in four dialects: what is the number in front of you actually a number *of*? The Bayesian and measurement sections both interrogate whether a posterior or a benchmark score means what we claim, the information geometry section keeps finding that a metric is secretly an order parameter, and the target trial section keeps showing that the estimand, not the data, is the answer. Network access was confirmed against the arXiv API and PubMed; all 20 picks were verified against their live abstract pages. Target trial metadata is from PubMed.

## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. When Is Generalized Bayes Bayesian? A Decision-Theoretic Characterization of Loss-Based Updating
Kenichiro McAlinn, Kōsaku Takanashi. arXiv:2602.01573, 2026-02. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01573

**The move:** Proves a loss-based posterior coincides with ordinary Bayes if and only if the loss is, up to scale and a data-only term, the negative log-likelihood, and sorts everything else into "belief posteriors" versus "decision posteriors" that are randomized rules, not beliefs.

**Why it is worth your time:** This finally pins down *which kind of object* a Gibbs or generalized posterior is, and the answer bites: outside the log-loss case it is not a belief, so "generalized marginal likelihood" is not evidence and Bayes factors are not even well-defined on it. It builds the reflex of asking what a posterior is a posterior of before you compute with it, and the entropy-penalized variational derivation is clean enough to teach.

**Cost:** 50 min, hard, needs comfort with decision-theoretic axiomatics (Savage, Anscombe-Aumann) and variational representations.

**Pairs with:** Knoblauch, Jewson, and Damoulas on generalized variational inference, as the constructive counterpart to this foundational audit.

### 2. Predictively Oriented Posteriors
Yann McLatchie, Badr-Eddine Cherief-Abdellatif, David T. Frazier, Jeremias Knoblauch. arXiv:2510.01915, 2025-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01915

**The move:** Builds a posterior whose concentration is driven by predictive ability rather than parameter recovery, so under misspecification it stops concentrating and stabilizes toward a predictively optimal model average whose residual width *is* the degree of misspecification.

**Why it is worth your time:** Standard Bayes and Gibbs posteriors both concentrate whether or not the model is right; this reframes that as a bug and turns irreducible posterior width into a readable diagnostic of model error. The result that these posteriors predictively dominate classical and generalized Bayes up to log factors, plus a runnable mean-field Langevin sampler, makes it a rare new-principle-you-can-also-run paper. It trains the instinct that uncertainty should answer to prediction, not to a fiction of recoverability.

**Cost:** 50 min, hard, needs posterior-contraction rates and some mean-field Langevin background.

**Pairs with:** Fong, Holmes, and Walker on martingale posteriors (arXiv:2103.15671), the other major predictive-first reconstruction of Bayesian inference.

### 3. When can a posterior predictive check identify the learning rate? Exact degeneracy in Gaussian models
Nam Anh Le. arXiv:2606.07169, 2026-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07169

**The move:** A clean kill-shot: for the Gaussian linear model with a reference prior, the log-likelihood PPC p-value used to select the generalized-Bayes learning rate depends only on (n, d, η) and not on the data, so the selector's output is fixed before you see a single observation.

**Why it is worth your time:** It takes a recent, sensible-looking selector and shows that on the canonical Gaussian case a pivotality property makes it data-free and systematically over-tempering. It builds the muscle of suspecting that a diagnostic might be secretly deterministic, the finite-sample χ²-pivot argument is short enough to redo by hand, and the honest scoping (the degeneracy disappears under informative priors) keeps it from overclaiming.

**Cost:** 30 min, moderate, comfortable with χ² pivots and reference priors suffices.

**Pairs with:** Pick A1, since both interrogate what the tempered generalized posterior actually licenses.

### 4. Bayesian model criticism using uniform parametrization checks
Christian T. Covington, Jeffrey W. Miller. arXiv:2503.18261, 2025-03. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18261

**The move:** Exploits the exact fact that a posterior draw, when parameters come from the prior and data from the likelihood, is distributed as the prior, then reparametrizes every random element into independent uniform u-values so departures become classical tests of non-uniformity or dependence over arbitrary subsets.

**Why it is worth your time:** One sharp, obvious-in-hindsight idea executed cleanly, and the u-value reparametrization is what makes it operational: you can localize which data points or parameters break the model, not just that it breaks. It builds the taste for turning a distributional identity into a practical, aggregable diagnostic, and the core fact fits on one slide.

**Cost:** 35 min, moderate, basic probability-integral-transform fluency.

**Pairs with:** the calibrated posterior predictive p-value literature, which fixes the non-uniformity of ppp-values from the complementary direction.

### 5. Connecting classical finite exchangeability to quantum theory *(timeless cross-domain gem, chosen on merit over a fourth recent paper)*
Alessio Benavoli, Alessandro Facchini, Marco Zaffalon. arXiv:2306.03869, 2023-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03869

**The move:** Shows the *finite* exchangeability case, where de Finetti's mixture-of-iid representation famously fails, recovers a clean representation theorem once you allow quasi-probabilities, and the resulting object is formally equivalent to quantum theory with boson-symmetric density matrices.

**Why it is worth your time:** A cross-domain import that pays rent: the borrowed structure (quantum states, signed measures) is exactly what repairs the gap between finite and infinite exchangeability, and removing it collapses the result. It builds the foundations muscle of taking the failures of de Finetti seriously rather than waving at the infinite limit, and the payoff (a de Finetti-style theorem for finite exchangeable sequences plus a notion of entanglement for indistinguishable bosons) is the kind of strange, durable connection you will not have seen elsewhere.

**Cost:** 60 min, hard, needs comfort with density matrices, tensor-product Hilbert spaces, and signed-measure representations.

**Pairs with:** Diaconis and Freedman's classic finite-exchangeability bounds, the probabilistic backdrop this paper reframes.

## B. Measurement theory

### 1. Measuring What AI Systems Might Do: Towards A Measurement Science in AI
Konstantinos Voudouris, Mirko Thalmann, Alex Kipnis, José Hernández-Orallo, Eric Schulz. arXiv:2603.00063, 2026-02. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00063

**The move:** Capabilities and propensities are dispositions, counterfactual context-to-output relations, and both benchmark averages and item-response theory skip the three steps that measuring a disposition actually requires.

**Why it is worth your time:** This takes "is this even a measurement" seriously for AI evaluation and answers with a teachable apparatus: hypothesize the causally relevant context, operationalize it independently, then map its effect on behavior probability. It trains the muscle for spotting when a number purporting to measure a capability is really just conditioned performance on one elicitation regime, and the dispositional framing is a genuine import from philosophy of science rather than decoration.

**Cost:** 70 min, medium, conceptual with light formalism.

**Pairs with:** B2, same disease and a complementary diagnosis (dispositions versus construct validity).

### 2. The Benchmarking Epistemology: Construct Validity for Evaluating Machine Learning Models
Timo Freiesleben, Sebastian Zezulka. arXiv:2510.23191, 2025-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23191

**The move:** A benchmark score only measures performance on one dataset; turning it into a claim about a theoretical task requires explicit construct-validity conditions, which they name and stress-test on ImageNet, WeatherBench, and Fragile Families.

**Why it is worth your time:** The three case studies are the payload, each making the hidden inferential leap from score to scientific claim concrete and showing exactly which assumption snaps. It builds the habit of asking what the intended inference is and what would have to be true for this number to support it. Cleanly written, no kitchen sink, durable framing.

**Cost:** 60 min, medium.

**Pairs with:** the nomological-network line on LLM capability benchmarks, for the Cronbach/Meehl-vs-Messick-vs-Borsboom taxonomy.

### 3. Comparison requires valid measurement: Rethinking attack success rate comparisons in AI red teaming
Alexandra Chouldechova, A. Feder Cooper, Solon Barocas, Abhinav Palia, Dan Vann, Hanna Wallach. arXiv:2601.18076, 2026-01. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18076

**The move:** Most attack-success-rate comparisons in red teaming are apples-to-oranges because the conditions under which two quantified system attributes can be *meaningfully* compared are almost never met.

**Why it is worth your time:** It takes a single fashionable number and applies measurement-theoretic meaningfulness to show when the comparison is empty, a sharp negative result with immediate operational consequences. The jailbreaking running example keeps the abstraction honest, and it teaches you to ask whether two scores are on the same construct, the same scale, and the same population of items before trusting any delta.

**Cost:** 45 min, easy to medium.

**Pairs with:** B5 (Ferrante et al.), the formal underpinning of meaningful comparison.

### 4. Measurement and Fairness *(timeless classic, included deliberately)*
Abigail Z. Jacobs, Hanna Wallach. arXiv:1912.05511 (FAccT 2021), 2019-12. https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05511

**The move:** Many fairness harms are construct-validity failures, mismatches between a contested theoretical construct and its operationalization, and the same measurement-modeling toolkit that diagnoses them also explains why fairness debates are really about rival theoretical understandings, not rival metrics.

**Why it is worth your time:** This is the load-bearing bridge that carried construct reliability and validity from political science, education, and psychology into machine learning, and it remains the cleanest statement of why "we operationalized it differently" and "we mean different things" get confused. Reading it teaches the reliability-versus-validity distinction precisely enough to reuse everywhere else on this list.

**Cost:** 60 min, medium, dense but lucid.

**Pairs with:** everything above; it is the shared vocabulary.

### 5. Towards Meaningful Statements in IR Evaluation: Mapping Evaluation Measures to Interval Scales *(representational-measurement classic)*
Marco Ferrante, Nicola Ferro, Norbert Fuhr. arXiv:2101.02668, 2021-01. https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.02668

**The move:** Most popular IR metrics are merely ordinal, not interval-scaled, so averaging and differencing them is formally meaningless, and they construct interval-scale versions to repair it.

**Why it is worth your time:** This is representational measurement theory (Stevens scale types, meaningfulness of statistics) drawing actual blood in a mature empirical field, with the claim that decades of experiments may have used improper methods. It shows the abstract permissible-transformations machinery changing what you are allowed to compute, and the community debate it triggered is itself instructive. Build the reflex of asking what scale a number is on and whether its mean is even defined.

**Cost:** 50 min, medium, some measure-theoretic notation, conceptually self-contained.

**Pairs with:** B3, meaningfulness theory generalized beyond IR to any compare-two-systems claim.

## C. Target trial emulation

*Article metadata in this section is from PubMed.*

### 1. Using Big Data to Emulate a Target Trial When a Randomized Trial Is Not Available
Miguel A. Hernán, James M. Robins. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2016-03. PMID 26994063. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwv254). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26994063/

**The move:** Reframes every observational comparative-effectiveness analysis as a flawed attempt to emulate one explicit hypothetical randomized trial, and makes the protocol the unit of criticism.

**Why it is worth your time:** This is the source text for the whole register; it gives you the seven protocol components (eligibility, treatment strategies, assignment, time zero, outcome, contrast, analysis) as a checklist to interrogate any study with. The muscle it builds is design-before-analysis discipline, locating where a published study silently deviates from its target trial, which is where the bias lives. Read it even if you think you know it, because most "target trial" papers only namecheck it.

**Cost:** 45 min, moderate.

**Pairs with:** C3, the same theory caught in the wild.

### 2. Where Do Target Trials Come From? Specifying the Protocol of a Target Trial When Repurposing Data for Causal Inference
Miguel A. Hernán, Barbra A. Dickerman, Sonja A. Swanson, Issa J. Dahabreh. Epidemiology, 2026-03. PMID 41921517. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000001951). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41921517/

**The move:** Argues the target trial is not prespecified but co-produced by the data: investigators iterate from an "index trial" (the question they wanted) to a "target trial" (the question the data can answer), so full prespecification is generally impossible.

**Why it is worth your time:** This is the spiky update to the 2016 framework and the exact register to aim for. It surfaces a methodological honesty problem the field has mostly papered over: which data peeks are legitimate, how to report each adaptation, and whether the adaptation rules themselves can be prespecified. It builds the muscle of separating the question you asked from the question you can defensibly answer, and reporting the gap.

**Cost:** 30 min, moderate, conceptual and light on math.

**Pairs with:** read directly after C1; it is the sequel.

### 3. Avoidable Flaws in Observational Analyses: An Application to Statins and Cancer
Barbra A. Dickerman, Xabier García-Albéniz, Roger W. Logan, Spiros Denaxas, Miguel A. Hernán. Nature Medicine, 2019-10. PMID 31591592. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-019-0597-x). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31591592/

**The move:** The definitive applied flip: emulating a target trial on 733,804 EHR records collapses a strongly protective statin-cancer effect to null, showing the protection was design artifact, not biology.

**Why it is worth your time:** This is the paper to teach from when someone insists observational data "showed" an effect; it runs the naive analysis and the target-trial emulation side by side on the same data, so the bias is isolated rather than asserted. The muscle is diagnostic, recognizing that immortal time and prevalent-user selection, not confounding, generate the most seductive spurious effects.

**Cost:** 35 min, moderate.

**Pairs with:** C4 for the underlying bias mechanism.

### 4. Immortal Time Bias in Pharmaco-epidemiology
Samy Suissa. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2007-12. PMID 18056625. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwm324). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18056625/

**The move:** Names, quantifies, and derives the single bias that target trial emulation exists to defeat, the span of immortal follow-time created by an exposure definition, across time-, event-, and exposure-based cohort designs.

**Why it is worth your time:** It predates the target-trial vocabulary but is its load-bearing prerequisite; you cannot judge a time-zero choice without internalizing it. Suissa shows the bias grows with the duration of immortal time and worsens under decreasing hazards (Weibull versus exponential), giving intuition for *when* a flawed design lies most. The foundational slot: timeless, mechanistic, and still under-read by people who cite target-trial papers downstream of it.

**Cost:** 30 min, moderate, some hazard-function algebra.

**Pairs with:** C3, essentially this bias caught in the wild.

### 5. Nonbenzodiazepine Hypnotics and Police-Reported Motor Vehicle Crash Risk Among Older Adults: A Sequential Target Trial Emulation
Andrew R. Zullo, Marzan A. Khan, Melissa R. Pfeiffer, Seth A. Margolis, Brian R. Ott, Allison E. Curry, Thomas A. Bayer, Melissa R. Riester, Nina R. Joyce. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2025-03. PMID 38957996. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwae168). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38957996/

**The move:** A sequential target trial where the intention-to-treat estimand says no effect (RR 1.06) but the per-protocol estimand for sustained use says protective (RR 0.83): the estimand choice is the answer.

**Why it is worth your time:** This is what a teachable applied emulation looks like, weekly re-emulated trials on a genuinely novel linkage (driver licensing, police crash reports, Medicare claims) with inverse-probability censoring to recover the per-protocol effect. The muscle is reading the ITT and per-protocol divergence as information about adherence and effect heterogeneity rather than a contradiction, and seeing how a non-pharma outcome keeps the design honest.

**Cost:** 40 min, moderate to hard, sequential trials plus inverse-probability weighting.

**Pairs with:** C2, a concrete instance of choosing which trial to emulate from the data you have.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. Information-Geometric Decomposition of Generalization Error in Unsupervised Learning
Gilhan Kim. arXiv:2604.12340, 2026-04. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12340

**The move:** The generalized Pythagorean theorem on an e-flat manifold splits KL generalization error into exactly three orthogonal non-negative pieces (model error, data bias, variance), and that orthogonality is the whole result.

**Why it is worth your time:** The cleanest example here of dually flat structure doing genuine work: remove the Pythagorean decomposition and the three-way split collapses into an inseparable mess. The worked ε-PCA case turns the abstract identity into a closed-form phase diagram with a Marchenko-Pastur edge, so you watch the geometry produce a falsifiable prediction rather than decorate one. It builds the muscle for spotting when an "exact decomposition" is actually a geometric orthogonality in disguise.

**Cost:** 60 min, moderate, needs comfort with e- and m-flat duality and the Pythagorean identity.

**Pairs with:** D5 (Crooks), the other face of the same Fisher/Pythagorean machinery.

### 2. Information geometry and synchronization phase transition in Kuramoto model
Artem Alexandrov, Alexander Gorsky. arXiv:2211.15617, 2022-11. https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15617

**The move:** All-to-all Kuramoto dynamics is literally the gradient flow of a KL divergence on a statistical manifold, and the Fisher metric diverges at the synchronization threshold, so the metric component is an order parameter.

**Why it is worth your time:** A cross-domain import that pays rent: the borrowed Fisher metric is not ornamental, it is what detects criticality, and the divergence is the punchline rather than a corollary. Short and reframe-driven (a dynamical system is secretly a divergence gradient flow), it trains you to ask whether a known dynamics is an information-geometric flow in disguise. An under-cited sleeper worth chasing the references of.

**Cost:** 40 min, moderate, light on machinery, the payoff is the identification.

**Pairs with:** D4 (Ito), the same divergence-gradient-flow idea in stochastic thermodynamics; and aging-as-a-dynamical-system work where criticality is the quantity of interest.

### 3. Fidelity susceptibility and geometric response in flux-tuned Dirac systems: exact results from a low-energy two-level reduction
C. A. S. Almeida. arXiv:2606.08280, 2026-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08280

**The move:** A controlled two-level reduction yields a closed-form Bures metric that is a universal Lorentzian in flux, peaking as m⁻², a power-law critical divergence built purely from spectral curvature, with no Berry phase or topology involved.

**Why it is worth your time:** The honest insistence that the response is independent of Berry curvature and topological invariants is the spiky part; it isolates the quantum-information metric as the sole mechanism and resists the usual topological hand-wave. The exact Lorentzian is a clean derivation you could teach as a canonical example of fidelity susceptibility tracking a quantum critical point, and it sharpens the instinct to separate metric-geometric effects from curvature and topology ones.

**Cost:** 50 min, moderate to hard, needs the Bures metric and a little Dirac/Aharonov-Bohm fluency.

**Pairs with:** the relativistic thermodynamic-curvature line, for the classical analogue of sign-of-curvature criticality.

### 4. Stochastic thermodynamic interpretation of information geometry
Sosuke Ito. arXiv:1712.04311, 2017-12. https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04311

**The move:** An information-geometric inequality is reread as a thermodynamic uncertainty relation between dynamical speed and thermodynamic cost: geometry and dissipation are the same bound.

**Why it is worth your time:** This is the reframe (information geometry is secretly stochastic thermodynamics) that seeded a whole subsequent literature, including the optimal-transport links, so reading the source is more durable than reading the descendants. It is a Letter: one idea, executed tightly, with a biochemical example to keep it honest. It builds the taste for recognizing a foundational move before it becomes a field.

**Cost:** 30 min, moderate, short, but the speed-cost duality rewards a slow read.

**Pairs with:** D5 (Crooks), the equilibrium ancestor of this speed-dissipation geometry.

### 5. Measuring thermodynamic length *(timeless classic, included on durability)*
Gavin E. Crooks. arXiv:0706.0559, 2007-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.0559

**The move:** Thermodynamic length is the Fisher-Rao metric distance between equilibrium states, and, the surprise, Bennett's classic acceptance-ratio free-energy estimator was secretly measuring that length all along.

**Why it is worth your time:** The timeless gem of the set: it nails the equivalence of thermodynamic length, Fisher information, and Jensen-Shannon divergence in a few pages, then lands a genuinely surprising you-were-already-computing-this result. The kind of clean, short derivation you can teach from and that reframes a decades-old numerical method as geometry. It earns the slot over recent options on durability and the quality of the punchline.

**Cost:** 30 min, easy to moderate, accessible and rereadable.

**Pairs with:** D4 (Ito), the out-of-equilibrium continuation of the dissipation-as-length bound.

## Threads

**1. What is this number a number *of*?** McAlinn-Takanashi (A1), Voudouris et al. (B1), Freiesleben-Zezulka (B2), and Chouldechova et al. (B3) are the same audit run in two fields. The Bayesian paper asks what a generalized posterior is a posterior of and finds that, off the log-loss case, it is a decision rule wearing the costume of a belief. The measurement papers ask what a benchmark score is a measurement of and find that, without explicit validity conditions, it is performance on one dataset wearing the costume of a capability. Both refuse to let a computed object inherit an interpretation it did not earn. The tension worth sitting with: McAlinn-Takanashi wants a sharp if-and-only-if characterization, while the measurement crowd argues validity is irreducibly contestable and theory-laden, so the same impulse lands as a theorem in one field and as an epistemology in the other.

**2. The diagnostic that was secretly determined before the data arrived.** Le (A3), Suissa (C4), Dickerman et al. (C3), and Ferrante et al. (B5) are all negative results about quantities that look data-driven but are not. Le shows a learning-rate selector whose output is fixed by (n, d, η) before any observation. Suissa shows immortal time bias is baked in by the exposure definition, and Dickerman shows it manufacturing a protective effect from nothing. Ferrante shows that an ordinal metric makes the *mean itself* meaningless, so the average was never measuring what its decimal places implied. The throughline: design fixes the answer, and the data is often along for the ride.

**3. The metric is the order parameter.** Alexandrov-Gorsky (D2), Almeida (D3), Ito (D4), and Crooks (D5), with Kim (D1) on the Bayesian-flavored edge, all make a geometric quantity do the detecting. A diverging Fisher metric marks synchronization, a peaking Bures metric marks a quantum critical point, and a Fisher-Rao length lower-bounds dissipation. Remove the geometry in any of them and the result collapses, which is the test the spec asks for. This is the thread that touches aging-as-a-dynamical-system and control-energy-at-criticality work most directly: if the metric tensor is the order parameter, then estimating curvature is estimating how close a system sits to a transition.

**4. Identification is a choice, not a discovery.** McAlinn-Takanashi (A1) and Hernán et al. (C1, C2) converge from opposite ends of statistics on one point: you do not find the estimand, you choose it, and most confusion comes from pretending otherwise. "Where do target trials come from" makes this almost confessional, admitting the target trial is co-produced by the data rather than prespecified, which is the causal-inference echo of asking what a posterior is a posterior of before computing with it.

## Synthesis

The week's strongest current is suspicion of objects that arrive pre-interpreted. A posterior is assumed to encode belief, a benchmark score to encode capability, a selector to be data-driven, a cohort effect to be biology; four of these papers pull the interpretation off and show what was underneath. McAlinn-Takanashi and the measurement cluster agree that the cure is to name the inference you intend before you compute, and they disagree productively about whether that naming can ever be made sharp: one wants an iff theorem, the other insists validity stays contestable. The target trial papers say the same thing in causal language, with Hernán's 2026 piece conceding that even the estimand is negotiated with the data rather than fixed in advance, which is a more honest and less comfortable position than the 2016 framework implied. The information geometry papers run orthogonal to all this but rhyme with the ongoing dynamical-systems work: if a diverging metric is what flags criticality, then the geometry is not decoration on the model, it is the observable. Two questions worth carrying: if a generalized posterior is a randomized decision rule rather than a belief, what is the right notion of evidence for it, and does the answer look more like a target trial estimand than like a Bayes factor? And if both validity in measurement and identification in causal inference are choices rather than discoveries, is there a single geometry of admissible choices, or are we just noticing the same humility twice in different notation?

# Weekly Reading List — 07-08-26

A negative-results week. Four of the twenty pick a fight with a practice people trust: flat minima that do not generalize, exchangeability that forbids fitting a frequency spectrum, clone-censor-weight that quietly breaks for rare initiators, and a relaxation identity whose failure measures dissipation. The through line is symmetry used as leverage, sometimes to identify, sometimes to constrain. Network access confirmed, all sources reachable; every finalist verified against its abstract page.

## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Posterior uncertainty for kernel density estimates
Dennis Christensen, Torjus Svardal, Leiv Rønneberg, Emil A. Stoltenberg. arXiv:2607.03927, 2026-07-04. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03927

- The move: the ordinary KDE is the point estimate of a predictive Bayesian measure, so it comes with a posterior you can band.
- Why it is worth your time: they read the KDE as a one-step predictive rule, iterate it into a sequence of resampled measures, and prove weak almost-sure convergence to a random limiting density even though the construction breaks conditional exchangeability. That hands a tool everyone already uses a native uncertainty quantification with no likelihood and no explicit prior. It builds the reflex of seeing a familiar frequentist estimator as a martingale-posterior object.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate, needs comfort with predictive (martingale) posteriors and weak convergence of measures.
- Pairs with: the Fong-Holmes-Walker martingale posterior program, which supplies the predictive-Bayes machinery.

### 2. Asymptotic regimes for maximum likelihood estimation in the Ewens-Pitman model: When the strength parameter matters
Filippo Ascolani, Mario Beraha, Stefano Favaro. arXiv:2606.13554, 2026-06-11. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13554

- The move: infinite exchangeability welds the cluster count to the shape of the frequency spectrum, and the only escape is to let the strength parameter grow with n.
- Why it is worth your time: a foundations result with a negative core. A whole class of species and clustering models cannot fit realistic spectra because exchangeability ties two quantities you wanted to control separately, and the paper charts four MLE regimes that pin down exactly when the strength parameter stops being asymptotically inert. The fix, scaling theta with sample size, is a clean diagnosis of what the symmetry costs. It sharpens the instinct to ask what an assumption silently forbids.
- Cost: 60 min, hard, needs random partitions and Pitman-Yor background.
- Pairs with: Ascolani's companion work on posterior concentration for Dirichlet process mixtures, the same tension seen from inside the posterior.

### 3. Merging of Bayes and quasi-Bayes empirical Bayes procedures for Poisson compound decisions
Stefano Favaro, Sandra Fortini. arXiv:2607.02340, 2026-07-02. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02340

- The move: the cheap quasi-Bayes recursion (Newton's algorithm) and the principled Dirichlet-process empirical Bayes solution provably merge in excess Bayes risk, so the shortcut is not a compromise.
- Why it is worth your time: a surprising equivalence. A one-pass predictive recursion with no coherent prior behind it lands at the same regret rate as the full nonparametric-Bayes machine for compound Poisson decisions, shown through matched concentration and regret bounds rather than a benchmark. It tunes the taste for when a computational heuristic is secretly asymptotically optimal.
- Cost: 50 min, hard, needs empirical Bayes and Dirichlet-process posterior asymptotics.
- Pairs with: the Fortini-Petrone predictive-characterization line, the theoretical parent of the quasi-Bayes side.

### 4. Geometric Causal Models
Eli N. Weinstein, David M. Blei. arXiv:2607.05153, 2026-07-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05153

- The move: group symmetry plus ergodic theory for amenable groups makes a single dependent dataset causally identifiable, with the symmetry doing the work a second independent sample normally would.
- Why it is worth your time: a cross-domain import that pays rent. Strip out the group invariance and the ergodic averaging and identification collapses, so the borrowed structure is load-bearing, not decorative. It extends the exchangeable-array intuition to richer symmetry groups and pairs it with scalable Bayesian estimation, giving a genuine route to causal effects in the one-big-correlated-blob regime. It builds the muscle of treating invariance as an identification resource rather than a regularizer.
- Cost: 75 min, hard, needs group actions and ergodic theory plus causal identification; skim the genomics application, the mechanism is in the theorems.
- Pairs with: the Aldous-Hoover exchangeable-array representation, the de Finetti-style precursor this generalizes.

### 5. Default priors for Gaussian processes (older sleeper)
Rui Paulo. arXiv:math/0505603, 2005-05-27 (Ann. Statist. 33(2):556-582). https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0505603

- The move: derive the Jeffreys, independence-Jeffreys, and reference priors for GP correlation and variance parameters, and prove which actually yield proper posteriors instead of reaching for a convenient flat prior.
- Why it is worth your time: everyone fits Gaussian processes and quietly picks range and nugget priors by habit, and this shows those choices are not innocent. Some standard defaults give improper posteriors, and the information-geometry-derived reference prior is the one with propriety guarantees and sane frequentist behavior. A clean derivation you can teach from, and under-cited relative to how ubiquitous GPs became. It builds the reflex to check whether your objective GP prior is even a probability distribution.
- Cost: 90 min, hard, needs Fisher-information and reference-prior construction with GP likelihoods.
- Pairs with: Berger, De Oliveira and Sansó on objective Bayesian analysis of spatial models.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. Stable Minima of ReLU Neural Networks Suffer from the Curse of Dimensionality: The Neural Shattering Phenomenon
Tongtong Liang, Dan Qiao, Yu-Xiang Wang, Rahul Parhi. arXiv:2506.20779, 2025-06-25 (rev 2026-01-11). https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20779

- The move: flat minima do not generalize in high dimension; they hit low loss by shattering the input into sparse, large-weight activations, and that trick decays exponentially with dimension.
- Why it is worth your time: a clean negative result aimed at the flat-minima-generalize folklore, with matching upper and lower bounds and a packing argument instead of a slogan. The named mechanism, sparse activation plus large weight norm, is the payload, and the separation from weight-decay solutions is where it draws blood. It trains the muscle of asking when an abstraction (flatness implies generalization) is actually valid.
- Cost: 2-3 hours, moderate-hard, needs ReLU function-space or bounded-variation norms and minimax lower bounds.
- Pairs with: Conflicting Biases at the Edge of Stability (2505.21423) on the norm-versus-sharpness tension.

### 2. Implicit Bias of SGD in Multivariate ReLU Networks: Effective Width Collapse
Shuang Liang, Tom Jacobs, Guido Montúfar. arXiv:2607.03613, 2026-07-03. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03613

- The move: train an infinitely wide two-layer ReLU net with noisy SGD and the stationary solution collapses to finitely many weight directions, bounded by the number of linear dichotomies of the data.
- Why it is worth your time: implicit bias with a counted mechanism, not another norm bound. The collapse count ties to the combinatorial geometry of the training set, each surviving direction owning a distinct ternary activation pattern, so overparameterization provably buys sparsity rather than redundancy. It builds the instinct to look for the structure that survives, and to count it.
- Cost: 3 hours, hard, needs the mean-field or stationary-measure view of noisy SGD and ReLU activation regions.
- Pairs with: neuron-splitting saddle analyses of two-layer nets, for how directions merge and separate during training.

### 3. Phase Transitions in Attention: A Bayesian Theory of Copy Head Emergence
Itay Lavie, Kirsten Fischer, Andrey Lekov, Frederic Van Maele, Zohar Ringel, Moritz Helias. arXiv:2606.12058, 2026-06-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12058

- The move: the abrupt appearance of copy and induction heads is a first-order phase transition in a closed-form Bayesian posterior, and softmax is what makes the jump discontinuous while linear attention gives only a second-order one.
- Why it is worth your time: it reduces a real, widely observed training phenomenon, sudden circuit emergence, to a few order parameters and shows why the softmax nonlinearity is load-bearing. This is physics-of-learning done honestly on a minimal copy task, not an LLM pipeline dressed as theory. It builds taste for degenerate and critical regimes that turn out to be informative, and for statistical-mechanics imports that pay rent.
- Cost: 3-4 hours, hard, needs partition-function analysis, order parameters, and softmax attention.
- Pairs with: sharp feature-learning transitions in extensive-width networks (2605.10395).

### 4. A theory of learning data statistics in diffusion models, from easy to hard
Lorenzo Bardone, Claudia Merger, Sebastian Goldt. arXiv:2603.12901, 2026-03-13 (rev 2026-06-10). https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12901

- The move: diffusion models learn pairwise correlations at linear sample complexity but need cubic samples for fourth-order cumulants, with a single diffusion information exponent governing the ordering.
- Why it is worth your time: a sharp sample-complexity result with a mechanism, not a scaling curve. The exponent unifies the easy-to-hard progression, and the exception, shared latent structure dropping the hard statistic back to linear, is a clean derivation you can teach from. It builds the muscle of finding the invariant that predicts how many samples a given statistic costs.
- Cost: 2-3 hours, moderate, needs Hermite and information-exponent single-index intuition plus basic score matching.
- Pairs with: the single-index and multi-index gradient-flow literature for the information-exponent lineage.

### 5. Outliers with Opposing Signals Have an Outsized Effect on Neural Network Optimization (older gem)
Elan Rosenfeld, Andrej Risteski. arXiv:2311.04163, 2023-11-07. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04163

- The move: progressive sharpening and edge-of-stability oscillation are driven by paired outliers carrying strong opposing signals that force the net into a narrow balancing valley, which then oscillates.
- Why it is worth your time: a concrete, falsifiable microscopic cause for two phenomena usually described only phenomenologically, connecting onward to grokking, simplicity bias, and SAM without overclaiming a grand theory. It is a mechanism paper that lets you predict training behavior, and it stays under-cited next to the more formal edge-of-stability dynamics work. It rewards honest framing plus a mechanism that explains a real training phenomenon.
- Cost: 1.5-2 hours, moderate, mostly empirical and mechanistic; needs familiarity with edge of stability and Hessian sharpness.
- Pairs with: the central-flows account of time-averaged edge-of-stability dynamics (2410.24206) for the formal picture.

## C. Target trial emulation

### 1. Comparison of the Test-negative Design and Cohort Design With Explicit Target Trial Emulation for Evaluating COVID-19 Vaccine Effectiveness
Guilin Li, Hanna Gerlovin, Arin L. Madenci, James M. Robins, Marc Lipsitch, Miguel A. Hernán, Barbra A. Dickerman, et al. Epidemiology 35(2):137-149, 2024. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000001709 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38109485/)

- The move: run the test-negative design and an explicit target-trial cohort on the same VA data, and show they agree when confounder information is rich but diverge once the data thin out, with the cohort the more principled default.
- Why it is worth your time: a rare head-to-head that treats a beloved shortcut, the test-negative design, as a sampling scheme nested inside a real target trial, then uses negative controls and an RCT benchmark to adjudicate. The payoff is a diagnosis of when the two designs part company, not a vague "results were similar." It builds the habit of asking what a convenient design is implicitly assuming.
- Cost: 50 min, moderate, needs vaccine-effectiveness designs and negative-control logic.
- Pairs with: the Pearce, Cerqueira-Silva, Vandenbroucke reply in Epidemiology 2024, which contests the framing.

### 2. Reflection on Modern Methods: trial emulation in the presence of immortal-time bias (older gem)
Camille Maringe, Sara Benitez Majano, Aimilia Exarchakou, Bernard Rachet, Aurélien Belot, Clémence Leyrat, et al. International Journal of Epidemiology 49(5):1719-1729, 2020. https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyaa057 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32386426/)

- The move: a five-step clone-censor-weight tutorial that quantifies the damage numerically. Naive Kaplan-Meier overstates the one-year surgery benefit by 22 points, cloning without censoring weights still overstates by 17, correct weighting lands at 11.
- Why it is worth your time: the cleanest teach-from-it derivation of why the informative censoring you introduce by design must be weighted away, with R and Stata code. It makes immortal-time bias and its fix concrete in one worked example rather than in the abstract. A quiet, load-bearing companion to the Hernán canon.
- Cost: 40 min, low-moderate, needs survival basics and IPCW intuition.
- Pairs with: the Maringe group's IJE reply on clone-censor-weight versatility.

### 3. Can metformin prevent cancer relative to sulfonylureas? A target trial emulation accounting for competing risks and poor overlap via double/debiased machine learning estimators
Lucia Xu, Chi Zheng, Bang Zheng, Zach Shahn, et al. American Journal of Epidemiology 194(2):512-523, 2025. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwae217 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39030720/)

- The move: recast the metformin-cancer question as an overlap-weighted separable direct effect so pre-cancer death is a competing event rather than a nuisance, then estimate it with double/debiased ML to survive poor overlap.
- Why it is worth your time: a rare applied emulation where the estimand choice, separable effects, is the load-bearing decision rather than decoration, and where DML earns real rent against overlap failure and misspecification. You learn how competing risks silently change the causal question inside a target trial. It sharpens the instinct that the estimand, not the estimator, is where the thinking happens.
- Cost: 70 min, hard, needs separable effects, influence-function or DML estimators, and competing risks.
- Pairs with: finalist 5 below; both interrogate what clone-censor-weight and overlap can and cannot deliver.

### 4. Illustrating an Adaptive Prespecification Framework for Observational Research: Target Trial Emulations Comparing Immunomodulator Treatments for COVID-19
Keith Weckstein, Jenna Frajzyngier, Andrew R. Vititoe, Catherine Gatto, et al. Epidemiology 36(6):791-801, 2025. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000001901 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40996066/)

- The move: preregister an intentional multiphase approach where diagnostic thresholds and contingencies are set in advance and released under staged blinding, so data-driven edits to the target trial happen by rule rather than by whim.
- Why it is worth your time: it confronts the field's dirty secret, that rigid prespecification is a fiction for EHR and claims emulation, and imports adaptive-trial machinery to make the inevitable mid-course edits auditable. The illustration honestly documents which contingencies fired and where the secondary objective was stopped. A reframe with limits stated plainly, co-authored with FDA reviewers.
- Cost: 55 min, moderate, needs propensity and IPCW workflow plus preregistration norms.
- Pairs with: the "Against Resignation on Prespecification" commentary in Epidemiology 2026, the companion argument.

### 5. Methodological Challenges of Emulating a Target Trial to Assess Timing of PCSK9 Inhibitor Initiation Post Myocardial Infarction
Camilla Cars, Stefan Gustafsson, Jasper J. Brugts, M. Alan Brookhart, et al. Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety 35(4):e70354, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1002/pds.70354 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41902372/)

- The move: show that clone-censor-weight cleanly removes immortal-time bias yet fails to balance covariates when initiators are rare and censoring is strongly informative, so the "unbiased" method quietly breaks for newly launched drugs.
- Why it is worth your time: a negative, honest result that names a specific failure mode of the fashionable clone-censor-weight workflow, high censoring plus tiny treated groups, and shows weight truncation only trades bias for imbalance. It is the corrective footnote every timing study should cite. It builds distrust of methods that are unbiased in expectation but unstable in the finite sample you actually have.
- Cost: 35 min, low-moderate, needs clone-censor-weight basics and covariate-balance diagnostics.
- Pairs with: positive-control clone-censor-weight emulations where treated groups are large, as the counterpoint.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. Information-Geometric Signatures of Nonconservative Driving
Andrea Auconi, Sosuke Ito. arXiv:2605.03757, 2026-05-05. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03757

- The move: under detailed balance the KL divergence toward equilibrium accelerates at exactly twice the temporal Fisher information, and the failure of that identity, the relaxation gap, is a computable lower bound on steady-state entropy production.
- Why it is worth your time: it turns a second-derivative identity into a detector of broken detailed balance, so a purely observational relaxation curve reveals that a system is being driven and how much dissipation that costs. Remove the Fisher-information and KL geometry and no bound survives, which is the rent test passed cleanly. Honest about where the equality holds and where it breaks.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate, needs stochastic thermodynamics, Fisher information, and KL divergence.
- Pairs with: Ito's earlier "Stochastic thermodynamic interpretation of information geometry" as the parent frame.

### 2. Quantum Fisher Information for Entropy of Gibbs States
Francis J. Headley. arXiv:2603.16456, 2026-03-17 (rev 2026-04-07). https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16456

- The move: the quantum Fisher information for estimating a Gibbs state's entropy equals the inverse heat capacity, dual to the temperature Fisher information being heat capacity over T squared.
- Why it is worth your time: heat capacity is the most routinely computed quantity in thermal physics, and this shows it is secretly a metrological precision limit read off the dually-flat exponential-family structure in thermodynamic coordinates. The payoff is an entropy-temperature uncertainty relation in which every system-specific quantity cancels and only temperature survives, the kind of clean invariant that signals the geometry is doing real work. A crisp reframe of something you thought you understood.
- Cost: 30 min, moderate, needs quantum Fisher information, exponential families, and Legendre duality.
- Pairs with: finalist 1, the estimation-side view of the same dually-flat structure.

### 3. Exponential families from a single KL identity
Marc Dymetman. arXiv:2604.28036, 2026-04-30. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28036

- The move: one elementary KL identity plus KL at least zero regenerates the Pythagorean theorem, Legendre duality, the variational principle, and the exponential-tilting formula behind entropy-regularized RL, all by substitution and rearrangement.
- Why it is worth your time: the rare paper whose value is the derivation itself, collapsing a scattered pile of standard results into one anchor you could teach an afternoon from. It is self-contained and algebra-first, which makes the logical skeleton of dually-flat geometry unusually visible. Take it as the accessible on-ramp of this set.
- Cost: 30 min, easy-to-moderate, needs basic exponential families and no differential geometry to start.
- Pairs with: finalist 2, since it makes the exponential-family duality that Headley exploits fall out in one line.

### 4. Efficient coding under constraint drives neural systems towards criticality and sloppiness
He Xiao, Xinyue Zhao, Haijun Zhou, Weikang Wang. arXiv:2605.22598, 2026-05-21 (rev 2026-06-03). https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22598

- The move: maximizing Fisher information in a Gaussian population code under a resource constraint forces soft modes, diverging correlation length, and critical slowing down, so brain criticality and sloppiness become consequences of efficient coding rather than assumptions.
- Why it is worth your time: the constrained Fisher-information optimization is the mechanism, not a label, and it unifies the statistical reading of criticality (diverging correlations) with the dynamical one (critical slowing) that usually get argued separately. It also ties criticality to the sloppy-eigenspectrum picture, linking two literatures through one geometric objective. Strong cross-pollination with criticality where the geometry is load-bearing.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate, needs population coding, the Fisher information matrix, and some RG or criticality intuition.
- Pairs with: Crosser and Brinkman on information geometry of spiking network behavior, for the sloppy-manifold view in spiking models.

### 5. Asymmetric Relaxations Through the Lens of Information Geometry (older sleeper)
Alessandro Bravetti, Miguel Ángel García-Ariza, Pablo Padilla. arXiv:2402.14267, 2024-02-22 (rev 2025-02-19). https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14267

- The move: recast Newtonian cooling as a gradient flow on a statistical manifold and the sign of the Amari-Chentsov third-order tensor becomes a criterion predicting when heating and cooling relax asymmetrically, including Mpemba-like orderings.
- Why it is worth your time: the third-order tensor that most treatments carry as decoration is here the actual predictor of a physical asymmetry, so deleting the geometry deletes the result. It spans classical and quantum ideal gases and connects to thermodynamic uncertainty relations, a lot of reach from one geometric object. The under-cited older gem of the set, worth surfacing against the 2026 crowd.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate, needs dual connections and the Amari-Chentsov tensor, gradient flows, and basic thermodynamics.
- Pairs with: the Bravetti group's 2026 follow-up generalizing past the Hessian case.

## Threads

### Thread 1: What a symmetry forbids, and what it buys
Geometric Causal Models (A4), Ewens-Pitman regimes (A2), and Effective Width Collapse (B2) are three faces of one question: what does an invariance assumption do to identification. Weinstein and Blei use group symmetry as a substitute for a second sample, buying causal identification from one dependent dataset. Ascolani, Beraha and Favaro show the other edge of the same knife, where exchangeability rigidly welds cluster count to spectrum shape and forbids a fit you wanted. Liang, Jacobs and Montúfar sit between them, letting the dichotomy geometry of the data count the directions SGD keeps. The tension worth holding: symmetry is an identification resource in A4 and a straitjacket in A2, and the difference is whether the symmetry is one you chose or one the model imposed.

### Thread 2: Negative results that indict a fashionable method
Neural shattering (B1), the PCSK9 clone-censor-weight failure (C5), and the Ewens-Pitman impossibility (A2) each kill a comfortable belief. Flat minima do not generalize in high dimension, clone-censor-weight is unbiased in expectation but unstable when initiators are rare, and a standard clustering prior cannot match realistic spectra. All three fail not through pathology but through a mechanism that arises in the regime you actually work in: high dimension, small treated group, growing sample. Read together they argue that the useful negative result is not a counterexample but a scaling story about when the method's guarantee evaporates.

### Thread 3: Fisher information as a thermodynamic and dynamical object
The relaxation gap (D1), Gibbs-state entropy QFI (D2), efficient coding to criticality (D4), and asymmetric relaxations (D5) all make Fisher information carry physical weight rather than statistical bookkeeping. Auconi and Ito read dissipation off a broken second-derivative identity; Headley reads a precision limit off heat capacity; Xiao and colleagues derive criticality from constrained Fisher optimization; Bravetti and colleagues predict a relaxation asymmetry from the third-order tensor. The throughline is estimation geometry as physics, and it connects directly to the phase-transition view of learning in B3, where the same partition-function machinery governs when a circuit appears. This is the cluster closest to aging as a driven, near-critical dynamical system: D1 and D4 both say that observable relaxation and correlation structure encode how far a system sits from equilibrium and from criticality.

### Thread 4: Shortcuts that are secretly the real thing
The KDE posterior (A1), quasi-Bayes merging (A3), and the KL-identity derivation of exponential families (D3) share a stance: a cheap or familiar object is, on inspection, the principled one. Christensen and colleagues show the KDE is a predictive Bayesian measure, Favaro and Fortini show a one-pass recursion matches the full Dirichlet-process machine in regret, and Dymetman shows the whole scaffolding of dually-flat geometry falls out of one inequality. The taste being built is suspicion of the boundary between heuristic and principle, and a habit of checking whether the shortcut you reach for already carries the guarantee you thought you needed the machinery for.

## Synthesis

This week reads as an argument that the interesting content of a method lives in its failure boundary and its hidden geometry, not in its average-case behavior. The negative results (B1, C5, A2) converge on a single lesson: a guarantee that holds in expectation or in the limit can dissolve in the high-dimensional, small-sample, or misspecified regime where you actually stand, and the way it dissolves is itself a mechanism worth naming. Against that, the symmetry papers (A4, B2) show the upside of the same structural thinking, where an invariance you can name turns an underdetermined problem into an identified one. The Fisher-information cluster (D1, D2, D4, D5) and the attention phase transition (B3) are the week's deepest agreement: estimation geometry and statistical-mechanics criticality are the same mathematics, and both let you read how far a system sits from equilibrium off its relaxation and correlation structure. Where they disagree is temperament. The Bayesian shortcuts (A1, A3, D3) say trust the cheap object, it is secretly principled; the target-trial papers (C1, C5) say distrust the convenient design, it is secretly assuming something. Two questions worth sitting with. First, when symmetry buys identification (A4), what is the finite-sample cost of the ergodic averaging that replaces the missing second sample, and does it degrade the way clone-censor-weight does in C5. Second, if criticality and sloppiness are forced by constrained efficient coding (D4), is a driven, aging system near criticality by selection or by the same constraint, and would the relaxation-gap detector of D1 tell the difference from data alone.

## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Beyond Laplace: Closed-form wrapped Gaussian posterior approximations on statistical manifolds
Marcelo Hartmann, Luu Hoang Phuc Hau, Anton Mallasto, Albert Kjøller Jacobsen, Georgios Arvanitidis, Søren Hauberg, Håvard Rue, Mark Girolami. arXiv:2607.01909, 2026-07-02. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01909

- The move: the Laplace approximation can only ever return an ellipse, and the skewness and heavy tails it discards are recoverable in closed form once you wrap a Gaussian onto the Fisher-Rao manifold instead of the tangent space.
- Why it is worth your time: the interesting claim is not that a manifold approximation is more expressive, everyone expects that, but that the exp and log maps come from contrast-function theory in closed form rather than ODE solvers, so the fix is orders of magnitude cheaper than earlier wrapped-Gaussian schemes. It trains the muscle of asking what geometric structure a routine approximation silently flattens, and whether recovering it is expensive or just unexamined.
- Cost: 60 min, hard, needs the Fisher-Rao metric, exp and log maps, and the standard Laplace approximation.
- Pairs with: finalist A5, the inner-product geometry of the same Bayesian objects.

### 2. Exact computation of posterior distribution of mixture weights in hierarchical Bayesian models
Georgy Meshcheryakov. arXiv:2607.05692, 2026-07-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05692

- The move: the mixture-weight posterior everyone reaches for a sampler to get is exactly a finite mixture of Betas, computable by an O(n log squared n) FFT dynamic program with no MCMC and no EM.
- Why it is worth your time: a clean believed-hard-is-easy result. It kills the reflex to run a sampler on two- and three-component hierarchical mixtures and hands closed-form summaries that beat EM and Laplace exactly in the small-n regime where those approximations quietly mis-cover or collapse to a boundary. It builds the taste for spotting when a marginalization you assumed was intractable has a tractable recursion hiding in it.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate, needs Dirichlet and Beta conjugacy and comfort with dynamic programming.
- Pairs with: Favaro and Fortini on merging of Bayes and quasi-Bayes empirical Bayes for Poisson compound decisions, another shortcut that turns out exact.

### 3. A causal modeling perspective on decision theory
Arvid Sjölander. arXiv:2606.29911, 2026-06-29. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29911

- The move: recast decision theory inside nonparametric structural equation models, define personal decision theory as maximizing a subjective model of one's own counterfactual utility, and the evidential-versus-causal fight along with Newcomb and the smoking lesion dissolves into a question about which counterfactual object the agent is modeling.
- Why it is worth your time: the payoff is translation, not another position in the philosophical argument. Once the competing decision theories are written in SEM notation you can read off exactly which identifying assumption each one is quietly making, and the paradoxes stop being paradoxes. It sharpens the instinct to turn an impasse into a well-posed identification problem.
- Cost: 50 min, moderate, needs potential-outcomes or SEM fluency; a philosophy background helps but is not required.
- Pairs with: the reading-log entry on when generalized Bayes is Bayesian, the decision-theoretic-characterization companion.

### 4. Calibrated Probability Forecast Sequences and Measure-Valued Martingales
Thomas Wilkinson, Christopher Ferro. arXiv:2606.31621, 2026-06-30. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31621

- The move: for a forecaster who revises predictions as information arrives, auto-calibration of the whole sequence is equivalent to the random forecast measures forming a martingale, so you test calibration by testing the martingale property.
- Why it is worth your time: the measure-valued martingale is the exact characterization, not decoration, and it delivers the first calibration test for updating forecasts on any Borel outcome space rather than the usual single-shot setting. It trains you to recognize when consistency over time is really a martingale in disguise, which is a reflex that transfers well beyond forecasting.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate-hard, needs martingales and conditional expectation on general spaces.
- Pairs with: the log entry on when a posterior predictive check can identify the learning rate, an adjacent calibration-degeneracy result.

### 5. On the geometry of Bayesian inference (older sleeper)
Miguel de Carvalho, Garritt L. Page, Bradley J. Barney. arXiv:1701.08994, 2017-01-31 (rev 2018-05-23). https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.08994

- The move: read the marginal likelihood literally as an inner product between prior and likelihood, so prior-likelihood compatibility becomes an angle and prior sensitivity, expert agreement, and evidence all become projections of one Hilbert-space object.
- Why it is worth your time: three things usually treated separately, marginal likelihood, prior sensitivity, and expert pooling, collapse into geometry you could teach in an afternoon, with estimators computable straight from posterior draws. Under-cited relative to how often people recompute these quantities piecemeal. It builds the reframe reflex of seeing a scalar you calculate all the time as secretly a geometric quantity.
- Cost: 35 min, easy-to-moderate, needs only inner-product spaces and basic Bayes.
- Pairs with: finalist A1, the same manifold objects put to a complementary use.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. The Multiscale Single-Index Model: A Stylized Model for Hierarchical Feature Learning
Joan Bruna. arXiv:2607.03347, 2026-07-03. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03347

- The move: build a minimal nested single-index target and prove online SGD recovers the full hierarchy at n = Õ(d^{K-1}) samples, the same order as a linear model, despite genuine depth.
- Why it is worth your time: the mechanism is concrete rather than a bound. A Wiener-chaos and Edgeworth expansion shows the nonlinearity perturbs the target at order d^{-1/2} into a spiked-tensor structure with characteristic singular-value plateaus, and that plateau structure is exactly why SGD dodges the expected exponential cost. It is a stripped model built to isolate one phenomenon, which is the right way to earn a sample-complexity claim.
- Cost: 2.5 hours, hard, needs high-dimensional probability, Hermite and Wiener-chaos tools, and single-index SGD intuition.
- Pairs with: Ben Arous, Gheissari and Jagannath on online SGD from high-dimensional inference, the information-exponent ancestor this generalizes.

### 2. Muon learns balanced solutions in matrix factorization without slow saddle-to-saddle dynamics
Mark Rhee, Jamie Simon, Dhruva Karkada. arXiv:2606.30509, 2026-06-29. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30509

- The move: explain why the Muon optimizer is fast by showing it learns all top modes at equal rates, bypassing the slow saddle-to-saddle staircase that small-init gradient descent is stuck on, and stays stable above the sharpness threshold.
- Why it is worth your time: this is mechanism over leaderboard, done on matrix factorization where everything is exactly analyzable rather than on a benchmark. The sharp observation is that Muon and gradient descent conserve different matrix quantities yet both land on balanced solutions, which is a reframe you can put on a board in one session. It builds the habit of explaining a hyped training trick by finding the conserved quantity rather than reporting the win.
- Cost: 90 min, moderate, needs linear algebra, gradient-descent dynamics, and spectral matrix-factorization implicit-bias basics.
- Pairs with: the small-init saddle-to-saddle line for deep linear networks, the dynamics Muon is escaping.

### 3. Sharp description of local minima in the loss landscape of high-dimensional two-layer ReLU neural networks
Jie Huang, Bruno Loureiro, Stefano Sarao Mannelli. arXiv:2604.09412, 2026-04-10 (rev 2026-05-29). https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09412

- The move: give an exact low-dimensional summary-statistic description of the population-loss local minima of two-layer ReLU nets and identify those minima with the attractive fixed points of one-pass SGD.
- Why it is worth your time: landscape geometry usually gets cataloged in isolation from the dynamics that reach it, and here the minima are precisely the fixed points SGD converges to, with overparameterization shown to shrink the basin of the spurious ones. The paper is honest about the cost of its own abstraction, closing by naming the limits of the summary-statistic and population-loss assumptions. It builds the instinct to connect where the loss is flat to where the training actually lands.
- Cost: 2 hours, hard, needs replica- or DMFT-style summary statistics, high-dimensional SGD, and teacher-student setups.
- Pairs with: finalist B5, the older account of why those minima are singular in the first place.

### 4. Broken Ergodicity and the Violation of the Fluctuation-Dissipation Theorem Lead to Generalization Beyond Overfitting in Machine Learning
Chan Li, Nigel Goldenfeld. arXiv:2607.04135, 2026-07-05. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04135

- The move: derive double descent as a genuine phase transition in the stochastic field theory of training, signaled by a breakdown of the fluctuation-dissipation theorem tied to broken ergodicity, with computed critical exponents and scaling functions.
- Why it is worth your time: this is a cross-domain import that pays rent. Remove the dynamical-mean-field and fluctuation-dissipation machinery and the result collapses to a curve fit, whereas keeping it buys an actual order parameter and superconductivity-style scaling. Read it skeptically for grand-theory overreach, but the specific claim, that an FDT-violation diagnostic marks where generalization kicks in, is concrete and falsifiable, which is the opposite of a scaling-curve paper.
- Cost: 3 hours, hard, needs DMFT, fluctuation-dissipation and response theory, and critical-phenomena vocabulary.
- Pairs with: finalist D2, the other paper this week where nonequilibrium physics sets the geometry.

### 5. Local minima and plateaus in hierarchical structures of multilayer perceptrons (older classic)
Kenji Fukumizu, Shun-ichi Amari. Neural Networks 13(3):317-327, 2000. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0893-6080(00)00009-5 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10937965/)

- The move: every critical point of the H-1 unit network embeds as a whole line of critical points in the H unit network, so the global optimum of the smaller model becomes a local minimum or saddle of the larger one, with a singular Hessian.
- Why it is worth your time: this is a degenerate regime that turns out to be informative, written twenty-five years before the current singular-learning wave rediscovered it. The Hessian is singular precisely because a set of parameters shares the error value, so ordinary second-order tests fail and plateaus become structural rather than accidental. It is the true ancestor of the monomial-network and learning-coefficient papers, under-read by the crowd citing the modern versions, and you can lecture straight from it.
- Cost: 2 hours, moderate, needs patience with older notation and basic differential geometry of parameter space.
- Pairs with: the reading-log entry on singular learning and Occam's razor in deep monomial networks, the modern descendant in algebraic-geometry language.

## C. Target trial emulation

### 1. Concordance between target trial emulation and randomised controlled trials: systematic review and meta-analysis
Canlong Wang, Dongfeng Tang, Peter von Dadelszen, Chengsheng Ju, Lu Liu, Yanzhong Wang, Laura A. Magee. BMJ 393:e086810, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2025-086810 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42156120/)

- The move: pool 107 emulation-and-trial pairs and measure when an emulation actually agrees with the trial it copies, rather than asserting that the method works.
- Why it is worth your time: the audit the field needed, and it is directional. Overall concordance is only moderate, correlation 0.59, but it jumps to 0.83 once design emulation is tight, and the spiky findings are that claims-data emulations systematically underestimate effects and that agreement is outcome-dependent, underestimating thromboembolism and cardiovascular effects while overestimating respiratory ones. It names the conditions under which emulation quietly breaks instead of settling for results were similar.
- Cost: 40 min, low-moderate, needs comfort reading meta-analytic agreement metrics such as ratio of ratios and standardized difference.
- Pairs with: finalist C2, the single prospective case that this aggregate view is built from.

### 2. Prospective benchmarking of an observational analysis in the SWEDEHEART registry against the REDUCE-AMI randomized trial
Anthony A. Matthews, Issa J. Dahabreh, Conor J. MacDonald, Bertil Lindahl, Robin Hofmann, David Erlinge, Troels Yndigegn, Anita Berglund, Tomas Jernberg, Miguel A. Hernán. European Journal of Epidemiology 39(4):349-361, 2024. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10654-024-01119-3 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38717556/)

- The move: specify and run the emulation before the trial reports, so benchmarking tests only whether the two protocols align, with the trial result unavailable to contaminate the comparison.
- Why it is worth your time: a real reframe of what benchmarking is for. It shifts the exercise from can we reproduce a known answer, which is corrupted by researcher degrees of freedom, to a blinded pre-registered wager with a planned postmortem if it fails. That epistemics-first honesty about failure modes is exactly the register this list wants, and it reads as a template for validating a real-world data source rather than a one-off.
- Cost: 35 min, moderate, needs target-trial protocol vocabulary and per-protocol estimands.
- Pairs with: finalist C1, the aggregate evidence for what this single case exemplifies.

### 3. Observational data for comparative effectiveness research: an emulation of randomised trials of statins and primary prevention of coronary heart disease (older classic)
Goodarz Danaei, Luis A. García Rodríguez, Oscar Fernández Cantero, Roger Logan, Miguel A. Hernán. Statistical Methods in Medical Research 22(1):70-96, 2013. https://doi.org/10.1177/0962280211403603 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22016461/)

- The move: the original worked example, building intention-to-treat, per-protocol, and as-treated analogues from observational data by inverse-probability weighting, with annotated SAS.
- Why it is worth your time: the paper the whole emulate-a-trial literature quietly stands on, cited far less than the later conceptual pieces. The teachable jolt is that a naive current-user-versus-never-user comparison gives a hazard ratio of 1.31, statins apparently harmful, while the emulation recovers a protective 0.79 to 0.89, a clean demonstration that the design choice, not the data, was the bug. You can lecture from it line by line, which is rare for an applied causal paper.
- Cost: 60 min, moderate-hard, needs time-varying confounding and inverse-probability weighting.
- Pairs with: the reading-log entry on using big data to emulate a target trial, the conceptual companion.

### 4. Studying When to Add Biological Therapy to Cytotoxic Therapy in Advanced Cancer Care Using Real World Data: How to Avoid Design Choices That Generate Immortal Time
Xabier Garcia-Albeniz, Julia Vila-Guilera, Manuel Zamparini, Helena Oliveres, Vicente Alonso-Orduna, Jaime Feliu, et al. (incl. Barbra A. Dickerman, Joan Maurel). Cancer Medicine 15(6):e71950, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1002/cam4.71950 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42321955/)

- The move: separate the two distinct mechanisms that manufacture immortal time, one through eligibility and selection, one through treatment-strategy misclassification, and fix each with clone-censor-weight and with sequential emulation on the same data.
- Why it is worth your time: the teaching value is the contrast. The biased analyses produce an implausible six-month risk ratio of 0.13, a fake early survival benefit, while the correct designs land at no effect. Most applied papers blur the two sources of immortal time together, and this one pulls them apart and shows clone-censor-weight and sequential emulation side by side, so you see which design choice is doing the damage. A sharp applied case where the framing changes the answer.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate, needs grace-period and clone-censor-weight basics.
- Pairs with: Hernán and colleagues on specifying a target trial to prevent immortal time bias, the conceptual scaffold.

### 5. Practical elements to consider when emulating a target trial
Viet-Thi Tran, Raphaël Porcher, Elodie Perrodeau, Sally Yaacoub, Isabelle Boutron, Philippe Ravaud. Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 194:112205, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112205 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41765347/)

- The move: argue that trial-and-emulation discrepancies persist beyond bias, arising from differences in population, intervention definition, outcome measurement, and the estimand itself, then give eleven design considerations.
- Why it is worth your time: the reframe worth stealing is the split between our emulation is biased and our emulation targets a genuinely different estimand than the trial, a distinction that dissolves a lot of confused real-world-data-versus-RCT debate. It is concrete where it matters, on grace periods that must reflect logistical delay while holding assignment at time zero, and on concomitant and geographic comparability. Slightly checklist-leaning, but the estimand-mismatch framing earns the slot.
- Cost: 25 min, low-moderate, minimal prerequisites.
- Pairs with: finalist C1, the empirical measure of how large these discrepancies actually get.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. Madelung hydrodynamics and Poisson geometry of wave functions
Boris Khesin, Klas Modin. arXiv:2607.01024, 2026-07-01. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01024

- The move: the Madelung transform is a Kähler map identifying the infinite-dimensional Fubini-Study geometry of quantum states with Fisher-Rao geometry, realized as a momentum map generating prequantum bundles.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the X is secretly Y geometry reframe at its cleanest, and the whole result is the isometry, so removing the information geometry removes the theorem. It extends to non-simply-connected configuration spaces and to wave functions with zeros, where the transform becomes a symplectomorphism to coadjoint orbits and throws new light on the Wallstrom quantization condition. Two serious geometers executing one structural idea, which is the cross-domain-import test passed without slack.
- Cost: 2-3 hours, hard, needs symplectic and Kähler geometry, momentum maps, and comfort with infinite-dimensional diffeomorphism groups.
- Pairs with: von Renesse on an optimal-transport view of the Schrödinger equation, the companion reframe from the transport side.

### 2. Wasserstein-2 gradient flows and the geometry of entropy production in classical and quantum stochastic thermodynamics
Olga Movilla Miangolarra, Ralph Sabbagh, Artemy Kolchinsky. arXiv:2606.00698, 2026-05-30. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00698

- The move: formulate relaxation to equilibrium, for overdamped diffusions, detailed-balanced Markov chains, and dissipative Lindblad dynamics alike, as a gradient flow under generalized Wasserstein-2 metrics, so the second law endows nonequilibrium transformations with a genuine Riemannian geometry.
- Why it is worth your time: the geometry is load-bearing. One metric bounds dissipation, recovers thermodynamic length from linear response, and links counterdiabatic protocols, unifying the classical and quantum cases that are usually treated with separate machinery. It shows how inertial or coherent processes reduce dissipation, extending the Riemannian view past equilibrium and past the overdamped regime, which is where the cross-pollination with thermodynamics stops being decorative.
- Cost: 2-3 hours, hard, needs stochastic thermodynamics, optimal transport and Wasserstein gradient flows, and some open-quantum-systems background.
- Pairs with: finalist B4, the other paper where nonequilibrium physics does the real work behind a learning or relaxation phenomenon.

### 3. Newton's Algorithm as a Gradient Flow: A Geometric Framework for Recursive Mixture Estimation
Bernardo Flores. arXiv:2604.13341, 2026-04-14. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13341

- The move: identify Newton's classical recursive mixture estimator as a discrete-time approximation of a Fisher-Rao gradient flow on the space of probability measures, then run the logic backward to recover the variational objective the estimator was implicitly solving.
- Why it is worth your time: a clean, teachable reframe that turns a decades-old recursion into Fisher-Rao gradient descent, and removing the geometry removes both the convergence characterization and the bridge to variational Bayes. Single-author and under-cited, with one sharp idea executed cleanly. It builds the reflex of asking what implicit objective an existing algorithm is descending, rather than only the reverse.
- Cost: 90 min, moderate, needs exponential families, gradient flows on measures, and basic variational Bayes.
- Pairs with: Newton, Quintana and Zhang on nonparametric Bayes by predictive updating, the recursive estimator being reinterpreted.

### 4. Information Geometry of Absorbing Markov-Chain and Discriminative Random Walks
Masanari Kimura. arXiv:2602.08185, 2026-02-09. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08185

- The move: treat class-specific hitting-time laws on an absorbing Markov chain as a statistical manifold, show each seed node's Fisher matrix is rank-one, and quotient out the null space to get a globally flat low-dimensional manifold of identifiable directions.
- Why it is worth your time: a crisp reframe that turns random-walk hitting times into dually flat geometry and then extracts a perturbation-sensitivity score the geometry actually earns, since the rank-one Fisher structure is what makes the flat quotient and the sensitivity bound possible. Strip the geometry and the identifiability argument evaporates. It is honest about what is and is not identifiable, and it puts the sensitivity score to work choosing which unlabeled nodes to label.
- Cost: 75 min, moderate, needs absorbing Markov chains, Fisher information, and basic dually flat geometry.
- Pairs with: Callut, Françoisse and Saerens on semi-supervised classification from discriminative random walks, the method being geometrized.

### 5. Defining the Curvature of a Statistical Problem (with Applications to Second Order Efficiency) (older classic)
Bradley Efron. Annals of Statistics 3(6):1189-1242, 1975. https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176343282

- The move: define statistical curvature, zero for exponential families and positive otherwise, measuring how far a one-parameter family bends away from being a straight line in the space of distributions, and tie it to second-order efficiency.
- Why it is worth your time: the origin of curved-exponential-family geometry, seeding Amari's whole program, yet under-read by the modern geometry-and-learning crowd who cite the textbooks instead. The object still does real work: curvature as a breakdown-of-exponentiality diagnostic tells you when the good properties of exponential families survive and when they fail. Concrete and teachable, and it beats several recent options on depth, which is the point of surfacing a classic.
- Cost: 2 hours, moderate, needs MLE asymptotics and exponential families; the prose is classical but clear.
- Pairs with: finalist B5, the same singular-geometry idea, curvature and degeneracy of a model family, arriving in learning theory a quarter century later.

## Threads

### Thread 1: Fisher-Rao as the hidden skeleton
Beyond Laplace (A1), the geometry of Bayesian inference (A5), Newton's algorithm as a gradient flow (D3), the Madelung map (D1), and Efron's curvature (D5) are all one claim applied to different objects: a thing people compute without thinking is secretly a point, flow, inner product, or curvature in Fisher-Rao geometry. A1 wraps a posterior onto the manifold, D3 reads a recursion as descent on it, A5 reads the marginal likelihood as an inner product on it, and D1 shows the Schrödinger equation lives on it. The tension worth holding is between exploitation and obstruction. Madelung and Newton exploit exact flatness or Kähler structure to get closed forms, while Efron's whole point is that curvature, the failure of flatness, is itself the quantity you want to measure. Flatness is a gift in D1 and D3 and a thing to be quantified in D5.

### Thread 2: Training and relaxation are the same dynamics
Broken ergodicity and FDT violation (B4), Wasserstein-2 entropy production (D2), Muon's saddle avoidance (B2), and the Fukumizu-Amari plateaus (B5) all treat learning as a physical relaxation process on a metric space. B4 makes double descent a phase transition with an order parameter, D2 makes relaxation a Wasserstein gradient flow with a dissipation bound, B2 explains optimizer speed as escaping a saddle staircase, and B5 explains why the staircase has flat steps in the first place, namely singular Hessians along embedded critical lines. The sharp disagreement is about how much physics you need. B4 imports the full dynamical-mean-field and critical-phenomena apparatus and risks grand-theory overreach, while B2 extracts the same why is it fast answer from plain linear algebra and a conserved quantity. Read together they ask whether the statistical-mechanics framing is load-bearing or expensive scaffolding for results simpler tools would reach.

### Thread 3: Degeneracy is where the information is
Fukumizu-Amari (B5), Kimura's rank-one Fisher (D4), Efron's curvature (D5), the sharp ReLU minima (B3), and Bruna's multiscale model (B1) all locate the content of a problem in a degenerate or low-dimensional structure rather than in the full parameter space. A singular Hessian marks a plateau (B5), a rank-one Fisher matrix collapses to a flat quotient of identifiable directions (D4), a spiked-tensor perturbation of order d^{-1/2} sets the sample complexity (B1), and the local minima admit an exact summary-statistic description (B3). The throughline is that degeneracy is not pathology to be regularized away but the object to be analyzed, since it is exactly where the identifiable directions, the plateaus, and the learnable statistics live. D5 is the oldest statement of the same instinct: curvature measures how the model degenerates away from the exponential-family straight line.

### Thread 4: When does the shortcut match the real thing
The concordance meta-analysis (C1), prospective benchmarking (C2), the Danaei statin emulation (C3), and the Garcia-Albeniz immortal-time paper (C4) are the empirical mirror of the Bayesian closed-form results (A1, A2). The target-trial papers measure when a cheap observational design agrees with the expensive randomized ground truth, and their answer is a failure boundary: emulation matches only when the design is tight (C1), a naive comparison can flip the sign of the effect (C3), and a single bad eligibility choice manufactures a fake early benefit (C4). The Bayesian side runs the same test in reverse, showing a closed form or a wrapped Gaussian recovers what a sampler would, and exactly where the cheap approximation mis-covers instead. Both sides insist the interesting question is not does the shortcut work on average but under what conditions does it break.

## Synthesis

This week argues that the useful content of a method lives in its geometry and its failure boundary, not in its average behavior. The Fisher-Rao cluster (A1, A5, D1, D3, D5) says that objects you compute by habit are secretly geometric, and that the payoff of noticing is either a closed form or a diagnostic, depending on whether the geometry is flat or curved. The relaxation cluster (B2, B4, B5, D2) says training and thermodynamic relaxation are the same dynamics on a metric space, though it disagrees sharply with itself about whether you need heavy statistical-mechanics machinery to see it or whether linear algebra suffices. The degeneracy thread (B1, B3, B5, D4, D5) is the deepest agreement across domains: singular Fisher and Hessian structure is where identifiability, sample complexity, and learning plateaus are decided, and Efron's 1975 curvature turns out to be the ancestor of a claim the learning theorists are still sharpening. Against all of this, the target-trial section (C1 through C5) keeps the list honest by refusing to trust geometry alone, insisting on the empirical measurement of when an emulation matches its trial and when it does not. The genuine tension is between the two temperaments. The geometry and Bayesian papers trust structure, a cheap object is secretly the principled one, while the target-trial papers trust auditing, a convenient design is secretly assuming something you must check against ground truth. Two questions worth sitting with. First, if double descent is a real phase transition with an FDT-violation diagnostic (B4), does the same nonequilibrium geometry that governs Wasserstein relaxation (D2) predict where it sits, and would that make the physics load-bearing rather than analogical. Second, if degeneracy is where the information lives (B5, D4, D5), the target-trial failures (C1, C4) are degeneracies too, overlap collapse and immortal time, so is there a curvature-style diagnostic that would tell you a priori when an emulation is near its own singular boundary.

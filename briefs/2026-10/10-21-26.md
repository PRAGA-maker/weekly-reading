## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Bagged Martingale Posteriors: Calibrated Uncertainty Quantification for Predictive Resampling
Hui Wang, Edwin Fong, David T. Frazier. arXiv:2609.30622, 2026-09. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30622
- The move: the fashionable martingale posterior has dishonest uncertainty, its credible sets undercover, and the fix is to start predictive paths from bootstrap resamples instead of the observed sample.
- Why it is worth your time: martingale posteriors are the object everyone is building on right now and almost nobody asked whether the sets they produce are calibrated. This shows they usually are not, diagnoses why in terms of the variance the predictive fails to inject, and repairs it at zero extra simulation cost. It builds the muscle of distrusting a method's uncertainty even when its point estimate is fine.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate, needs predictive resampling and bootstrap intuition.
- Pairs with: the c.i.d. classic below, which is the convergence theory this calibration argument leans on.

### 2. Statistical models as natural transformations: meaningfulness, coherence and priors as states in Markov categories
Francesco Vaccarino. arXiv:2609.22929, 2026-09. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22929
- The move: a statistical model is a natural transformation between functors into the Kleisli category of the Giry monad, and from naturality alone de Finetti exchangeability, Kolmogorov consistency, and "ridge regression is Bayesian inversion of the Gaussian linear model" drop out.
- Why it is worth your time: this is a cross-domain import that passes the rent test. Coherence over insertions is exactly Kolmogorov consistency, coherence over injections adds exactly the exchangeability of the categorical de Finetti theorem, and Jeffreys' rule is shown to fail coherence while the location-scale analogue passes. Remove the category theory and none of these identifications survive. It trains the muscle of seeing which "axioms" of inference are really one structural condition in disguise.
- Cost: 90 min, hard, needs monads, Kleisli categories, and some patience with Baues-Wirsching cohomology.
- Pairs with: the improper-prior paper below, another look at when a prior is coherent or permissible rather than merely proper.

### 3. Optimal Priors for Betting Tests with Bounded Payoffs: An Exact Efficiency Characterization
Francesco Orabona. arXiv:2610.25821, 2026-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25821
- The move: the efficiency of an F-weighted testing-by-betting portfolio equals the logarithmic decay index of the prior F near zero, so choosing the prior is a question about its tail at the origin, not its bulk.
- Why it is worth your time: a clean necessary-and-sufficient result that collapses prior choice in the e-value program to one analytic quantity, with a surprising and teachable punchline: Beta priors have infinite efficiency, Robbins-type priors attain efficiency exactly one. It sharpens taste for where in a prior the action actually lives.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate, needs e-values and game-theoretic testing basics.
- Pairs with: the betting and portfolio testing entries already in your log; this is the prior-selection theory underneath them.

### 4. Inconsistency between Frequentist and Bayesian inference, approximation of improper priors and permissible priors
Pierre Druilhet, Erwan Saint Loubert Bié. arXiv:2610.26422, 2026-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26422
- The move: a proper posterior from an improper prior is not safe. Strong inconsistency provably blocks convergence of any proper-prior approximation, so permissible priors cannot be strongly inconsistent.
- Why it is worth your time: it exposes a hidden assumption behind everyday objective-Bayes practice and replaces the folklore with a convergence theory where the answer depends on which compact exhaustion you pick to approximate the improper prior. It unifies the Flatland and Fraser-Monette-Ng paradoxes in one discrete framework. The taste it builds is suspicion of limits that look harmless.
- Cost: 60 min, hard, needs measure-theoretic priors and the improper-prior paradox literature.
- Pairs with: the Markov-categories paper above on coherence, and the marginalization-paradox tradition.

### 5. Limit theorems for a class of identically distributed random variables (classic)
Patrizia Berti, Luca Pratelli, Pietro Rigo. Annals of Probability 32(3):2029-2052, 2004. https://doi.org/10.1214/009117904000000676 (preprint arXiv:math/0410105)
- The move: defines conditionally identically distributed (c.i.d.) sequences, where each future value is identically distributed given the past, proves their limit theory, and shows a sequence is exchangeable iff it is stationary and c.i.d.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the quiet probabilistic backbone the entire martingale-posterior and predictive-resampling wave stands on, item 1 above included. c.i.d. is the precise weakening of exchangeability that predictive updating needs, and it decouples "the predictive limit exists" from "the sequence is exchangeable." Reading it makes every modern predictive-Bayes paper legible.
- Cost: 75 min, hard, needs martingale convergence and measure-theoretic probability.
- Pairs with: the martingale-posterior line in your log and item 1 here.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. Learning a Group Element from Its Action: Galois Correspondences, Optimal ERM, and an Abel-Ruffini Threshold for the Natarajan Dimension
Xiaoyu Li. arXiv:2610.18154, 2026-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18154
- The move: the multiclass learning complexity of recovering a group element from its action is the group's subgroup and Galois structure, and the Natarajan-versus-DS gap first opens exactly at A5/S5, where the quintic stops being solvable by radicals.
- Why it is worth your time: the reframe of the week. DS, graph, Littlestone, and teaching dimensions become concrete group invariants, and radical-solvability becomes a literal dividing line for a learning-theoretic quantity. Remove the algebra and the result is gone. Almost nobody will have this single-author paper on their radar, and it reorganizes how you think about what makes a class hard.
- Cost: 90 min, hard, needs group actions and basic Galois theory.
- Pairs with: the weak-signal paper below, a second route from identifiability structure to learning difficulty.

### 2. Benign Loss Landscapes Can Coexist with Worst-Case Hardness
Zach Furman, Stephan Wäldchen, Yangda Bei, Liam Hodgkinson. arXiv:2609.13057, 2026-09. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13057
- The move: bad local minima are not what separates learnable from hard. Using tree tensor networks they prove every minimum-norm local minimum is global, yet the model still hides poly-size, gradient-descent-unlearnable targets.
- Why it is worth your time: it kills the folk intuition that a benign landscape means easy optimization, and relocates the obstruction to high-order degenerate saddles created by rank-deficiency, with parity as the clean case study. The surrogate model generalizes deep linear nets and Tucker decompositions, so it is not a pathology. It builds the muscle of separating landscape geometry from computational hardness.
- Cost: 60 min, medium-hard.
- Pairs with: singular learning theory and degenerate-minima work in your log; the degenerate saddles here are the same phenomenon wearing a hardness hat.

### 3. Sample complexity of weak-signal latent-variable models
Amnon Balanov, Tamir Bendory, Dan Edidin. arXiv:2610.24770, 2026-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24770
- The move: if a parameter first becomes globally identifiable at order r in a weak-signal expansion, you need about beta^(-2r) samples, so identifiability order reads off the statistical rate directly.
- Why it is worth your time: it turns a qualitative property, "identifiable at which order," into an exact exponent, with a likelihood-separation stability condition that is necessary and, under regularity, sufficient for both MLE and method of moments to hit the rate. Tested on additive-noise channels and exponential families, with a cryo-EM companion as the concrete payoff. It sharpens the instinct that identifiability is a graded quantity, not a yes/no.
- Cost: 60 to 75 min, medium-hard.
- Pairs with: the multi-reference alignment entry in your log, and the Galois paper above.

### 4. The Multiple Timescales of Gradient Descent on the Edge of Stability: A Perturbative Derivation of the Central Flow
Raphaël Berthier. arXiv:2609.01034, 2026-09. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01034
- The move: it turns the heuristic central-flow model of edge-of-stability training into a clean singular-perturbation derivation, with fast oscillation, intermediate self-stabilization, and a slow central flow falling out as three separated timescales.
- Why it is worth your time: writing the objective as f = g + eps*h and taking eps to zero, gradient descent provably limits to the gradient flow of h constrained to minimizers of g with sharpness at most 2/eta, and self-stabilization appears as the next-order term. It handles the realistic multiple-eigenvalue-at-the-edge case and explains why fluctuations persist. A mechanism for a real training phenomenon, derivable on a board.
- Cost: 60 min, medium, needs method of multiple scales.
- Pairs with: the edge-of-stability and quadratic-model entries in your log, and the natural-gradient geometry papers in section D.

### 5. Living on the edge: Phase transitions in convex programs with random data (classic)
Dennis Amelunxen, Martin Lotz, Michael B. McCoy, Joel A. Tropp. arXiv:1303.6672, 2013 (Information and Inference, 2014). https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.6672
- The move: it defines the statistical dimension as the canonical extension of subspace dimension to convex cones, then proves intrinsic volumes concentrate sharply around it, which pins the exact location and width of recovery phase transitions.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the load-bearing classic behind "how few measurements suffice." Every statistical-dimension calculation for sparse and low-rank recovery, demixing, and random cone programs rests on it, and it is still under-read outside compressed sensing. A clean derivation importing conic integral geometry into high-dimensional statistics.
- Cost: 60 min for the core, 2 to 3 hours full, hard, needs conic geometry.
- Pairs with: the thermodynamic and criticality papers in section D; same phenomenology, a sharp transition located by one intrinsic geometric quantity.

## C. Information geometry

### 1. How Inefficient Is Natural Gradient Descent? From Exact Optimality to Theta(sqrt(log d)) Divergence
Guni Sharon, Alan Kuhnle. arXiv:2610.07228, 2026-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07228
- The move: natural gradient is provably not geodesic, and the gap is unbounded in dimension. On dually flat families NGD on forward-KL follows the mixture geodesic, which is often strictly longer than the Fisher-Rao geodesic.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the opposite of the "natural gradient made it faster" antipattern. The geometry is the whole claim, Fisher length versus Fisher-Rao distance, and it overturns a dogma with three clean regimes: exact optimality for quadratic-potential or 1D families, a dimension-free bound under bounded skewness, and Theta(sqrt(log d)) blowup for products of scale families like Gaussian covariances. A tensor criterion you could teach from, with experiments matching all three.
- Cost: 90 min, moderate, needs dual affine coordinates.
- Pairs with: the central-flow paper in section B; both ask what the correct limiting trajectory of an optimizer on a manifold actually is.

### 2. The Dually Flat Geometry of Planning as Inference
Nikola Milosevic, Asaki Kataoka, Nicolas Hinrichs, Kenji Doya, Nico Scherf. arXiv:2609.04005, 2026-09. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04005
- The move: planning-as-inference is secretly a dually flat manifold, the achievable visitation measures carry two affine charts dual under conditional entropy, and each planning iterate is exactly one natural-gradient step.
- Why it is worth your time: a reframe that pays rent in two fields at once. It recasts the TD error as a marginal-utility estimate and generalizes planning from linear rewards to nonlinear functionals of the visitation, which the flat geometry makes natural rather than ad hoc. It builds taste for when a known algorithm is secretly geometric.
- Cost: 2 hours, moderate-to-hard.
- Pairs with: item 1 above; one says each planning step is a natural-gradient step, the other says natural-gradient steps are not geodesic, a productive tension.

### 3. Fisher Information Metric as a model-free measure of proximity to criticality in neural systems
Yuewei Du, Alberto Liardi, Hardik Rajpal, Henrik Jeldtoft Jensen. arXiv:2609.07624, 2026-09. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07624
- The move: use the Fisher information metric as a generalized susceptibility to read off distance to criticality, even when the control parameter is unknown, by using the observed branching ratio instead.
- Why it is worth your time: critical-exponent estimation from avalanches is notoriously model-dependent, and this gives a continuous, model-free readout where the FIM peaks at criticality with peak width and height carrying the information, validated from branching processes up to whole-brain models. The geometry is the mechanism, not decoration, and the paper is honest about the unknown-control-parameter failure mode and how to route around it.
- Cost: 60 min, easy-to-moderate.
- Pairs with: aging as a dynamical system and control-energy-near-criticality work; this is a clean estimator for "how close to the edge."

### 4. Finite-resolution exhaustive traversal of thermodynamic state spaces has divergent thermodynamic length
Satori Tsuzuki. arXiv:2606.29751, 2026-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29751
- The move: covering a whole control region, rather than going endpoint to endpoint, forces thermodynamic length to diverge, from a covering argument, with required length growing at least as eps^(1-d).
- Why it is worth your time: a genuinely surprising "you cannot win" result. Reframing state-space mapping as an epsilon-coverage problem, Hilbert and Peano traversals attain the exponent, fixed by the curve's codimension rather than the scan, and with a friction tensor this becomes a slow-driving resource law with excess work scaling as eps^(2(1-d)). Honest about the finite-resolution cutoff doing the work.
- Cost: 90 min, moderate.
- Pairs with: the thermodynamic-length entries in your log, and the statistical-dimension classic in section B on sharp geometric thresholds.

### 5. Entropy differential metric, distance and divergence measures in probability spaces: A unified approach (classic)
Jacob Burbea, C. Radhakrishna Rao. Journal of Multivariate Analysis 12(4):575-596, 1982. https://doi.org/10.1016/0047-259X(82)90065-3
- The move: it derives Riemannian differential metrics from alpha-order entropy functionals and builds the distance and divergence apparatus on probability simplices in one framework, a decade before "information geometry" was a common phrase.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the under-cited root of Rao-distance geometry, feeding both Amari's and Cencov's later programs yet rarely read in the original. It is the historical anchor for half the recent papers above and still a clean derivation you can teach from.
- Cost: 2 hours, moderate, classic notation.
- Pairs with: the Cencov-characterization and Rao-distance entries in your log; this predates and underwrites them.

## D. Target trial emulation

Thin for brand-new 2026 method papers that are not already in the log, so four sharp 2025 pieces and one under-cited operational classic. All verified via Crossref.

### 1. A Structural Description of Biases That Generate Immortal Time
Miguel A. Hernán, Jonathan A.C. Sterne, Julian P.T. Higgins, Ian Shrier, Sonia Hernández-Díaz. Epidemiology 36(1):107-114, 2025. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000001808
- The move: the bias is not the immortal time. The real culprit is either selection on post-assignment eligibility or misclassification of treatment assignment, and immortal time is only a symptom.
- Why it is worth your time: it is a reframe that renames the problem correctly, with a clean structural taxonomy of how each mechanism arises and why synchronizing eligibility, assignment, and time-zero prevents it. It builds the habit of asking which structural bias a named artifact is standing in for.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate, needs selection and misclassification diagrams.
- Pairs with: the landmark-analysis and immortal-time entries in your log, which this reorganizes.

### 2. The Target Trial Framework for Causal Inference From Observational Data: Why and When Is It Helpful?
Miguel A. Hernán, Issa J. Dahabreh, Barbra A. Dickerman, Sonja A. Swanson. Annals of Internal Medicine 178(3):402-407, 2025. https://doi.org/10.7326/ANNALS-24-01871
- The move: the originators demarcate what the framework fixes, design errors, from what it does not, unmeasured confounding and measurement error, and say where emulation actually adds value over a careful analysis.
- Why it is worth your time: a rare piece that pushes back on its own method's hype, exactly the honest-framing pattern. It is useful precisely because it refuses to oversell, and it gives you the language to tell a real emulation from a namecheck.
- Cost: 30 min, easy.
- Pairs with: item 1 above; one names the biases design fixes, the other names the biases it cannot.

### 3. Causal inference in multi-cohort studies using the target trial framework to identify and minimize sources of bias
Marnie Downes, Meredith O'Connor, Craig A. Olsson, David Burgner, Sharon Goldfeld, Elizabeth A. Spry, George Patton, Margarita Moreno-Betancur. American Journal of Epidemiology 194(9):2685-2697, 2025. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwae405
- The move: a single target estimand becomes the fixed reference against which cohort-specific and pooling-induced biases are each audited, which disciplines when to pool and how to read discordant replications.
- Why it is worth your time: one sharp idea cleanly executed, a reframe of multi-cohort and meta-analytic practice as bias bookkeeping around a fixed estimand rather than another drug emulation. It builds taste for using the estimand as an accounting device.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate.
- Pairs with: the generalizability and transportability entries in your log.

### 4. Effectiveness of Metformin in Preventing Herpes Zoster Among Japanese Patients With Type 2 Diabetes: A Target Trial Emulation
Shunsuke Hiroki, Toshiki Fukasawa, Kasumi Yokogawa, Koji Kawakami. Journal of Medical Virology 97(9):e70606, 2025. https://doi.org/10.1002/jmv.70606
- The move: a clean negative result. An active-comparator, new-user, per-protocol emulation against DPP-4 inhibitors finds a null effect and attributes an earlier reported metformin benefit to confounding by indication and an unspecified estimand.
- Why it is worth your time: a compact applied case where correct framing flips the answer, with the methodological lesson, not the virology, doing the work. It shows what "specify the estimand and the comparator first" buys you in practice.
- Cost: 25 min, easy.
- Pairs with: the avoidable-flaws and active-comparator entries in your log.

### 5. Electronic medical records can be used to emulate target trials of sustained treatment strategies (sleeper classic)
Goodarz Danaei, Luis A. García Rodríguez, Oscar Fernández Cantero, Roger W. Logan, Miguel A. Hernán. Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 96:12-22, 2018. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2017.11.021
- The move: the actual recipe for emulation that the famous 2016 big-data paper overshadowed. It works through three distinct target trials, derives the observational analogues of ITT and per-protocol effects, and gives two IPW approaches for adherence.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the paper that teaches you to build the estimator, not just name the estimand, with annotated detail on single-versus-none, joint-versus-none, and head-to-head designs. Under-cited relative to its usefulness.
- Cost: 60 min, moderate-hard, needs marginal structural models and IPW.
- Pairs with: the sustained-treatment and g-formula entries in your log.

## Threads

### Thread 1: Identifiability sets the price
Papers: B3 (weak-signal sample complexity), B1 (Galois learnability threshold), A4 (improper-prior inconsistency), D1 and D2 (target trial identifiability).
In every domain the binding constraint is not estimation effort but whether the target is identified at all, and the structure of the parameter space fixes either the rate or the bias. B3 makes this quantitative, identifiability order r gives rate beta^(-2r), and B1 makes it algebraic, the learnability dimension is a group invariant with a solvability threshold. A4 shows a Bayesian posterior can be proper yet fail to identify a frequentist-coherent answer. D1 and D2 treat it as a design property, align time-zero or the estimand is not identified. The tension worth holding: learning theory treats identifiability as a graded, continuous exponent, while target trial practice treats it as binary, aligned or not. The weak-signal view suggests the binary design question has a hidden order parameter.

### Thread 2: Optimization as geometry, and the limits of the natural metric
Papers: C1 (NGD not geodesic), C2 (planning-as-inference is dually flat), B4 (central flow on the edge of stability).
Training dynamics live on a statistical manifold, and the right object is a geodesic or a constrained flow, not a raw step. C2 is the optimistic pole, every planning update is exactly a natural-gradient step on a dually flat space. C1 is the corrective, natural gradient is provably not geodesic and the shortfall grows with dimension, so the natural metric is both the organizing principle and a trap. B4 sits between them, the true limiting object is a gradient flow constrained to a minimizing manifold, not the discrete natural-gradient iteration. Read together they argue that "use the Fisher metric" is a good instinct and a bad literal recipe.

### Thread 3: Criticality, thermodynamics, and the cost of covering a space
Papers: C3 (Fisher as proximity to criticality), C4 (divergent thermodynamic length), B5 (statistical-dimension phase transitions), B2 (degenerate saddles and hardness).
Fisher information doubles as a generalized susceptibility, so it peaks at criticality (C3) and sets the thermodynamic length and dissipation of moving through state space (C4). B5 gives the same signature in convex recovery, a sharp phase transition whose location and width are fixed by one intrinsic geometric quantity. B2 is the dissonant note: a landscape can be benign and still computationally critical in a different sense, with the hardness hidden in degenerate saddles rather than in a susceptibility peak. The shared question is when a single geometric scalar controls a sharp change in behavior, and when it misses the real obstruction. This is the thread closest to aging as a dynamical system and control energy near criticality.

## Synthesis

This week rewards suspicion. The strongest results are negative: martingale-posterior sets undercover (A1), a proper posterior need not be coherent (A4), natural gradient is not geodesic (C1), a benign landscape can still be unlearnable (B2), and a reported drug benefit evaporates under a specified estimand (D4). The common move is to take a default everyone trusts and show the trust was unearned, then either repair it cheaply or name the exact condition under which it fails. That is more useful than most positive theorems because it changes defaults.

Underneath the suspicion is a single organizing idea across all four domains: geometry decides difficulty. Identifiability order sets a sample-complexity exponent (B3), group structure sets a learnability dimension with a radical-solvability threshold (B1), the statistical dimension of a cone sets a recovery transition (B5), and the Fisher metric sets both proximity to criticality (C3) and the cost of traversing a control space (C4). The papers disagree about whether the right geometric object is clean. Planning-as-inference says the manifold is dually flat and the algorithm is a geodesic step (C2), while the natural-gradient and improper-prior papers say the natural object misleads once you leave the simplest families (C1, A4). The Burbea-Rao and c.i.d. classics (C5, A5) are here to remind you that both the entropy-metric apparatus and the exchangeability-weakening that predictive Bayes needs were worked out decades ago and are worth reading in the original.

Two questions worth sitting with. First, the weak-signal result (B3) turns identifiability into a continuous order parameter; does the binary "aligned time-zero or not" of target trial emulation (D1) have the same hidden order, so that near-misalignment degrades identification smoothly rather than catastrophically? Second, C3 and C4 both read criticality off the Fisher metric, but B2 shows an obstruction that no susceptibility peak would detect; for a system you actually care about, aging or a controlled network, is the quantity that matters the critical susceptibility or the computational degeneracy, and can one masquerade as the other?

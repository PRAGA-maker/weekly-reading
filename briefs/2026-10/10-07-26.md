## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Isotropic Gaussian Processes Improve Vanilla Bayesian Optimization in High Dimensions
Wei-Ting Tang, Madhav Muthyala, Joel A. Paulson. arXiv:2610.05780 (stat.ML/cs.LG), 2026-10-05. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05780

- **The move:** For radial kernels the marginal likelihood sees per-coordinate ARD lengthscales only through weighted pairwise distances, so when you have fewer points than dimensions whole ARD directions are literally invisible to the data. Drop ARD, use one shared lengthscale, do better.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This quietly kills the near-universal "ARD by default" reflex with a one-line identifiability argument you can rederive yourself, and it is the rare negative result that is also a simplification: fewer knobs, better behavior. The value is the diagnostic instinct it trains, to ask whether the likelihood can even resolve a hyperparameter before you tune it. A flexible prior that is unidentified is not flexibility, it is noise in the posterior over hyperparameters.
- **Cost:** 30 min, easy to moderate, needs GP marginal-likelihood fluency.
- **Pairs with:** item A2, the same "can the model see this parameter" question in a different guise.

### 2. When Noise Estimation Hides Basis Misspecification in Repeated Bayesian Inverse Problems
Rares Dimitrie Grozavescu, Mark Girolami. arXiv:2609.37762 (stat.ME), 2026-09-29. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37762

- **The move:** When the prior lives in a restricted basis and the truth has out-of-basis components, the maximum-likelihood noise estimate absorbs that energy into the model-range complement, so residual and observation-coverage checks stay nominal while the field coverage silently collapses.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It names a specific, common failure of a practice everyone uses, estimate the noise variance then trust your coverage diagnostics, and shows exactly why the standard checks pass while the reconstruction is wrong. The sphericity-style test it offers is the useful artifact, but the lasting lesson is that a well-fitting noise model can be laundering misspecification. Build the instinct to distrust a coverage check that never fails.
- **Cost:** 40 min, moderate, needs comfort with linear Gaussian inverse problems and coverage.
- **Pairs with:** the Bayesian inverse-problem and model-criticism veins already in the log.

### 3. Statistical models as natural transformations: meaningfulness, coherence and priors as states in Markov categories
Francesco Vaccarino. arXiv:2609.22929 (math.ST), 2026-09-19 (v3 2026-10-03). https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22929

- **The move:** Cast McCullagh-style statistical models as natural transformations into the Kleisli category of the Giry monad, and "coherent prior" becomes a naturality condition that forces Kolmogorov consistency and a categorical de Finetti exchangeability. Priors are states, incoherence is a vanishing-cohomology obstruction.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the spikiest item of the week and a cross-domain import that pays rent: the category theory is load-bearing, not decoration, because it recovers de Finetti, derives when "natural" quantities admit corrections via cohomology, and reads ridge regression as Bayesian inversion of a coherent Gaussian model. Read it for the "priors as states" reframe even if you black-box the proofs. It will either change how you think about what a prior is or annoy you, and both are useful.
- **Cost:** 90 min, hard, needs functors and monads or willingness to black-box them.
- **Pairs with:** McCullagh, "What is a statistical model?" (Ann. Statist. 2002), its direct ancestor and still sharper than most descendants.

### 4. Semiparametric Bernstein-von Mises theorems from Stein's method
Paul Rosa. arXiv:2609.37939 (math.ST), 2026-09-29. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37939

- **The move:** Prove semiparametric BvM by Stein's method instead of Laplace or integrated-likelihood arguments, characterizing the prior's influence through the prior-weighted divergence of a chosen vector field over the model, which gives explicit non-asymptotic bounded-Lipschitz bounds and a built-in bias-correction recipe.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A genuinely different proof technology for a classical object turns "does the posterior look Gaussian" into local differential quantities you can compute, and reaches regimes where the usual approach fails. The transferable move is Stein plus a little information geometry for an asymptotic normality statement, and the payoff is seeing which quantities actually control the limit. This builds taste for how the choice of proof method reveals the load-bearing structure.
- **Cost:** 75 min, hard, needs BvM background and comfort with Stein's method.
- **Pairs with:** the white-noise and histogram-density examples inside the paper, which are the cleanest teaching cases.

### 5. A Correspondence Between Bayesian Estimation on Stochastic Processes and Smoothing by Splines
George S. Kimeldorf, Grace Wahba. Ann. Math. Statist. 41(2):495-502, 1970. https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177697089

- **The move:** The smoothing spline is exactly the posterior mean of a Gaussian-process prior. Penalized least squares and Bayesian estimation under a GP are the same object.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This six-page paper is the load-bearing identity under the entire GP equals RKHS equals spline equals representer-theorem edifice that the modern ML crowd keeps rediscovering and under-citing. Reading the original is the clearest way to internalize that a regularizer is a prior, which is the reframe that makes half the "deep kernel uncertainty" literature feel like 1970 in new clothes. The timeless sleeper of the week.
- **Cost:** 30 min, moderate, needs RKHS and penalized-regression basics.
- **Pairs with:** M. Unser, "Generalized Splines and Gaussian Processes" (arXiv:2608.28446, 2026), which extends the correspondence to infinite dimensions.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. Principal Component Regression Dominates all Monotone Spectral Filters for Linear Regression
Juno Kim, Hengyu Fu, Peter Bartlett, Jason D. Lee, Jingfeng Wu. arXiv:2609.39440 (stat.ML), 2026-09-30. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39440

- **The move:** Optimally-tuned PCR, a hard spectral cutoff, is never worse than any monotone spectral filter by more than a constant, and beats gradient descent and ridge by polynomial sample-complexity factors.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It collapses the usual "which shrinkage should I use" question into one instance-wise, finite-sample dominance statement rather than a minimax rate, and the proof, sharp per-instance bounds across the whole filter family, is the reusable artifact. The surprise is directional: the crudest filter, truncate rather than shrink, wins, which inverts the folklore that smooth shrinkage like ridge or GD is the safe default. Star-author paper, so watch that it does not go viral, but the theorem itself is clean and teachable, not a release-cycle artifact.
- **Cost:** 2 to 3 h, moderate, random-matrix and spectral comfort helps.
- **Pairs with:** item B2, the companion question of what the optimizer lands on rather than what the estimator class allows.

### 2. How abundant are good interpolators?
August Y. Chen, Ahmed El Alaoui. arXiv:2606.06469 (math.ST), 2026-06-04. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06469

- **The move:** Via large deviations over the set of margin-kappa interpolators under Gaussian-mixture and logistic data, the typical interpolator is characterized, and efficient algorithms such as GD and LP are shown to find atypically good ones.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It reframes "why do interpolators generalize" as a question about the measure of the solution set, cleanly separating what an average zero-training-error classifier does from what your optimizer actually selects. The payoff is a concrete gap: good generalization is a property of the algorithm's selection bias, not of interpolation itself. The LDP computation is clean enough to teach from.
- **Cost:** 2.5 h, moderate to hard, needs large-deviations comfort.
- **Pairs with:** item B5, the 1988 volume-of-solutions calculation it modernizes.

### 3. A Flatness-Generalization Relation in the Teacher-Student Tree-Committee Machine
Brandon Livio Annesi, Davide Straziota, Enrico Maria Malatesta. arXiv:2609.31101 (cond-mat.dis-nn), 2026-09-25. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31101

- **The move:** Solve a teacher-student committee machine exactly, via zero-temperature Gibbs and the Edwards-Jones Hessian, and show the flatness-to-generalization link holds for regression and heavily overparametrized classification but reverses underparametrized.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It takes a heuristic everyone cites, flat minima generalize, and turns it into an analytically characterized Hessian-spectrum calculation where you can watch the correlation flip sign as you cross the parameter-to-data ratio. This is a believed-practice-is-conditional result with the exact phase boundary, not another empirical sharpness-versus-accuracy scatterplot. Living in cond-mat.dis-nn, it is a paper an ML reader would not otherwise surface.
- **Cost:** 3 h, hard, replica and stat-mech machinery.
- **Pairs with:** item B4, and Hochreiter-Schmidhuber "Flat Minima" (1997), the source of the heuristic it conditions.

### 4. Broken scale symmetries in undercomplete linear autoencoders
Farhad Pashakhanloo, Jacob A. Zavatone-Veth. arXiv:2610.03640 (cs.LG), 2026-10-02. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03640

- **The move:** Finite-step SGD breaks the scale symmetry that gradient flow preserves, and the gradient noise rectifies into a directed drift along the degenerate solution manifold that systematically favors large decoder weights.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It is a genuine optimization-dynamics mechanism, not a landscape snapshot, with a tractable slow-timescale effective theory for where on a flat manifold SGD ends up. It also carries an honest wrinkle: the selected solutions are "sharper," but different sharpness metrics disagree in sign, which connects directly to the flatness debate in B3. A tidy minimal model of how stochastic noise, not the loss, picks the solution.
- **Cost:** 2 h, moderate, helps to know symmetry and SGD-noise arguments.
- **Pairs with:** item B3, the flatness side of the same coin.

### 5. The space of interactions in neural network models
Elizabeth Gardner. J. Phys. A: Math. Gen. 21(1):257, 1988. https://doi.org/10.1088/0305-4470/21/1/030

- **The move:** Compute the fractional volume of weight space that stores random patterns and get the critical capacity alpha_c = 2 as that volume shrinks to zero, via a replica saddle point over couplings.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the original "learning is the geometry of the solution volume" calculation, quietly load-bearing under every modern interpolation and overparametrization result including B2, yet rarely cited by the ML audience that keeps reinventing it. Reading it once makes the LDP-over-interpolators machinery feel inevitable rather than clever. Short, self-contained, and timeless.
- **Cost:** 2 h, hard (replica method) but compact.
- **Pairs with:** item B2, its direct modern descendant.

## C. Target trial emulation

Picks verified via PubMed metadata; DOIs are the working links. According to PubMed.

### 1. A Structural Description of Biases That Generate Immortal Time
Miguel A. Hernán, Jonathan A. C. Sterne, Julian P. T. Higgins, Ian Shrier, Sonia Hernández-Díaz. Epidemiology 36(1):107-114, 2025 (e-pub 2024-11-04). https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000001808

- **The move:** "Immortal time bias" is a misnomer. The bias is always generated by either selection, post-assignment eligibility criteria, or misclassification, strategies indistinguishable at time zero, and immortal time is only the symptom.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It separates two distinct structural mechanisms that the literature blurs under one label, and shows which analytic fix each demands: synchronize eligibility, assignment, and time zero for the selection case, clone and reweight longitudinally for the misclassification case. This is the short, teach-from-it reframing that changes how you diagnose a design rather than just naming the disease. The authorship is the field's core, but the paper is recent and still under-circulated next to the 2016 classic.
- **Cost:** 30 min, moderate.
- **Pairs with:** item C4, which measures how often the misclassification fix is botched in practice.

### 2. Emulating a target trial in case-control designs: an application to statins and colorectal cancer
Barbra A. Dickerman, Xabier García-Albéniz, Roger W. Logan, Spiros Denaxas, Miguel A. Hernán. Int. J. Epidemiol. 49(5):1637-1646, 2020. https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyaa144

- **The move:** Impose a target-trial protocol on case-control sampling and the famous "statins prevent cancer" signal collapses. The conventional case-control analysis gave OR 0.57, the emulation gave a null hazard ratio near 1.00.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It is a clean worked demonstration that the case-control design is not the source of the protective finding, the design choices that cannot be mapped to any trial are, and that correctly-sampled case-control data reproduce the cohort emulation exactly. The framing literally changes the answer, and it extends target-trial thinking to a design most people assume is incompatible with it. A good antidote to "case-control studies are inherently biased."
- **Cost:** 40 min, moderate.
- **Pairs with:** item C1, the structural account of why the unmappable design choices inject bias.

### 3. Diagnosing Covariate Balance Across Levels of Right-Censoring Before and After Application of Inverse-Probability-of-Censoring Weights
John W. Jackson. Am. J. Epidemiol. 188(12):2213-2221, 2019. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwz136

- **The move:** Build love-plot-style covariate-balance diagnostics for right-censoring and dependent censoring, so you can actually see whether IPCW removed the selection bias it was meant to, a validity check for per-protocol effects rather than just a point estimate.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Everyone doing clone-censor-weight or per-protocol emulation leans on IPCW, but almost nobody inspects whether the weights worked. This gives the concrete, interpretable tool and shows the weighted-versus-unweighted balance evolving over time. It is a genuinely load-bearing, rarely-cited methods contribution that makes the invisible assumption of "informative censoring removed" checkable. The under-cited sleeper of this domain.
- **Cost:** 45 min, harder, notation-dense.
- **Pairs with:** item C4, the review that catalogs CCW implementations that never run this check.

### 4. Use of clone-censor-weight to avoid immortal-time bias: a systematic methodological review
Aldrine Manzanilla, Clément Brunetta, François Peyre-Pradat, Stefan Michiels, Thomas Filleron, Noémie Simon-Tillaux. J. Clin. Epidemiol. 199:112468, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112468

- **The move:** Audit 113 published CCW studies and find that about 20% still carry immortal-time bias, because they excluded patients on post-time-zero criteria before cloning, defeating the method they invoked.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The negative result is the teaching: using the right method name does not buy you the right estimand, and the single most common error is a sequencing mistake, filter-then-clone instead of clone-then-filter. It also surfaces how wildly implementation varies in weighting, censoring events, and outcome models, which is useful ammunition when reviewing or reporting a CCW analysis. This is the rare review that adds a load-bearing finding instead of restating the discourse.
- **Cost:** 30 min, easy to moderate.
- **Pairs with:** items C1 and C3, the mechanism it confirms and the diagnostic most of its studies skip.

### 5. Emulating target trials of postexposure vaccines using observational data
Christopher Boyer, Marc Lipsitch. Am. J. Epidemiol., 2024. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwae350

- **The move:** For postexposure vaccination the incubation period and the timing of the dose redefine eligibility and time zero, so you must specify several distinct target trials indexed by timing window rather than one, illustrated with mpox.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It is a tidy estimand-definition problem that is invisible until you write the protocol, showing how "optimal timing" questions from observational vaccine data map onto a family of trials with different eligible populations. The move generalizes well beyond vaccines to any exposure with a latency between eligibility and effect. Honest about the identification conditions each trial in the family needs.
- **Cost:** 35 min, moderate.
- **Pairs with:** item C1, since both turn on getting time zero and eligibility to coincide.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. The Loss Floor of Denoising Score Matching: Fisher Geometry from Schrödinger Bridges
Avinash Raju, Kai Zhang. arXiv:2608.23916 (cs.LG), 2026-08-24. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23916

- **The move:** The irreducible excess in the denoising-score-matching loss is exactly the trace of the Fisher-Rao metric of the conditional-endpoint family, integrated along the diffusion trajectory.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It derives the floor from a Schrödinger-bridge variational principle, so the geometry people keep seeing in diffusion latent spaces turns out to be an intrinsic term in the training loss rather than an artifact. The sharp practical payoff is a negative result: raw losses under different noise ranges or weightings carry different additive Fisher floors and need not rank models consistently, a quiet indictment of loss-based diffusion comparisons. Remove the geometry and there is no floor and no explanation.
- **Cost:** 45 min, moderate, needs SDE diffusions and relative entropy.
- **Pairs with:** item D3, the other pick where a dually flat or Fisher quantity is the controlling object in a learning dynamic.

### 2. Learning the exogenous rate but not the distance to criticality in nearly unstable heavy-tailed Hawkes processes
Mauricio Herrera-Marín. arXiv:2609.38268 (math.PR), 2026-09-29. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38268

- **The move:** In the nearly-unstable rough-Volterra limit the asymptotic information geometry is singular in the exogenous direction, an atom at zero, so you can consistently estimate the exogenous rate mu while the distance-to-criticality 1 minus rho that it is coupled to is provably not uniformly locally consistent, even as the event count diverges.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A surprising negative result where the geometry is the whole explanation: the relevant Fisher content is governed by a quantity that stays bounded while events explode. It is a clean lesson that more data is not more information when the manifold degenerates, and it imports rough-volatility and criticality machinery that pays rent. The strongest cross-pollination of the week with dynamical systems and criticality.
- **Cost:** 1 h, hard, Volterra and rough-process asymptotics, LAN-style arguments.
- **Pairs with:** items A1 and A2, the identifiability-degeneracy thread, and your standing interest in distance-to-criticality.

### 3. The Dually Flat Geometry of Planning as Inference
Nikola Milosevic, Asaki Kataoka, Nicolas Hinrichs, Kenji Doya, Nico Scherf. arXiv:2609.04005 (cs.AI), 2026-09-03. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04005

- **The move:** The achievable visitation measures of an MDP form a dually flat manifold whose two affine charts, visitation probabilities and log-policies, are dual under conditional entropy, so each planning-as-inference iterate is literally one natural-gradient step.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A reframe that earns its structure: it generalizes planning from linear rewards to nonlinear functionals of the visitation and hands the TD error a crisp identity as a marginal-utility estimate. The dual flatness is what makes the natural-gradient iteration exact, so the geometry is doing the work rather than decorating it. The RL-to-theoretical-neuroscience bridge is the kind of import worth stealing.
- **Cost:** 50 min, moderate, RL occupancy measures and exponential-family duality.
- **Pairs with:** item D1, both making an integrated or dual geometric quantity the controlling object.

### 4. The Advective Fisher-Rao Geometry of Deterministic Measure Transport
Benjamin Gess, Johannes Müller. arXiv:2608.12111 (math.OC), 2026-08-12. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12111

- **The move:** One advective Fisher-Rao metric for measures evolving under the continuity equation falls out of three independent derivations: the rescaled zero-noise limit of Fisher-Rao on path measures, the expected second variation of the Freidlin-Wentzell rate functional, and the Hessian of the Benamou-Brenier action.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A teachable convergence: three lineages, information geometry, large deviations, and dynamic optimal transport, name the same object, and that object is the metric giving optimal descent directions for density-fitting, in contrast to Gauss-Newton, which optimally fits velocity fields instead. Rigorous and well-authored, and the "X, Y, and Z are secretly one metric" structure is exactly a gem pattern. The distinction between fitting densities and fitting velocities is a useful knife.
- **Cost:** 50 min, moderate to hard, continuity equation and some large-deviations background.
- **Pairs with:** item D1, Fisher-Rao as the intrinsic cost along a transport path.

### 5. Logarithmic divergences: geometry and interpretation of curvature
Ting-Kam Leonard Wong, Jiaowen Yang. arXiv:1906.09103 (math.DG), 2019 (GSI 2019). https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.09103

- **The move:** The logarithmic L-alpha-divergence is a conformal deformation of the Bregman divergence whose statistical manifold has constant sectional curvature minus alpha, and it is the canonical divergence of a new optimal-transport problem.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the load-bearing, under-cited root of the current optimal-transport-meets-information-geometry wave, with the Bregman-Wasserstein line as its follow-up. It gives a concrete geometric reading of curvature, how primal and dual geodesics separate relative to the dually flat zero-curvature case, via an explicit affine immersion. One sharp idea you can teach from in an afternoon, and it reframes Bregman and dually flat as the alpha-to-zero flat limit of a curved family. The geometry sleeper of the week.
- **Cost:** 40 min, moderate, dually flat geometry and a little convex analysis.
- **Pairs with:** Amari and collaborators' escort-coordinate flattening, the same conformal trick run the other direction.

## Threads

### Thread 1: More data is not more information
Papers: A1 (ARD invisible to the likelihood), A2 (noise estimate absorbs out-of-basis energy), B2 (good generalization is selection, not abundance), C3 (did IPCW actually remove dependent censoring), D2 (Fisher information bounded near criticality). The throughline is degeneracy: a direction the model cannot resolve, an estimand the sampling design cannot see, a parameter the asymptotics cannot pin even as n diverges. Each paper's honest move is the same, build the diagnostic that checks whether the information you assumed is present actually is. The tension worth holding: A1 and D2 treat invisibility as a wall, while B2 treats it as the thing that helps, since the optimizer's refusal to sample the typical solution is exactly why it generalizes.

### Thread 2: A regularizer is a prior, a filter is an estimator, a prior is a state
Papers: A5 (spline equals GP posterior mean), A3 (priors as states in a Markov category), B1 (PCR dominates the whole monotone filter family), D5 (dually flat as the flat limit of a curved divergence family). These are reframes that collapse a menu of choices into one object. Kimeldorf-Wahba make a penalty and a prior the same thing, Vaccarino makes a prior a state and coherence a naturality condition, Kim et al. make an entire family of spectral filters dominated by one cutoff, Wong-Yang make Bregman the zero-curvature special case of a curved family. Reframes compound, so this is where the week's durable value sits.

### Thread 3: Where stochastic dynamics settle on a flat manifold
Papers: B3 (flatness-generalization flips sign at a phase boundary), B4 (SGD noise rectifies into drift on a degenerate manifold), D3 (planning as inference is natural gradient on a dually flat manifold), D1 (the score-matching loss floor is integrated Fisher-Rao). This connects learning-theory optimization geometry to information geometry directly: the first two ask where on a flat set the dynamics land and whether flatness even means what we think, the second two give the geometry that makes a learning update an exact geometric step or an intrinsic cost. The tension is sharp: B3 and B4 show flatness is a slippery, metric-dependent, regime-dependent notion, while D1 and D3 show that when you pick the right geometry the relevant quantity is exact and coordinate-free. The lesson is to distrust ad hoc sharpness measures and look for the intrinsic one.

### Thread 4: Time zero and the discipline of the estimand
Papers: C1, C2, C4, C5, connected to A3 and A4. The target-trial papers all insist the protocol defines the estimand before any method runs, and that most "bias" is a failure to make eligibility, assignment, and time zero coincide. That discipline rhymes with the Bayesian foundations picks: Vaccarino's demand that a model admit a coherent natural extension, and Rosa's localization of exactly how the prior influences the limit, are the same insistence on specifying the object before trusting the machinery. The disagreement across the two literatures is only vocabulary. Both say the method cannot rescue a question that was never pinned down.

## Synthesis

This week's papers converge on a single discipline: find the object the data can actually resolve, name it exactly, and refuse to trust any method that runs before that naming. Half the list is degeneracy results, and they are not pessimistic. An unresolvable ARD direction, an unidentifiable distance to criticality, a censoring weight that did nothing, each is a precise statement about where information lives and where it does not, and each comes with a check you can run. The other half is reframes that compound, a spline is a prior, a filter family has a dominating member, a divergence menu is one curved family, and these are the moves that pay rent for years rather than a news cycle. The strongest cross-current is between the flatness papers and the geometry papers: B3 and B4 show that sharpness is metric-dependent and regime-dependent and quietly unreliable, while D1 and D3 show that the right intrinsic geometry makes the controlling quantity exact. Read together they argue that most of our training-dynamics folklore is coordinate-dependent and should be rederived in Fisher-Rao or a dually flat chart before being believed. The Hawkes result is the one to sit with, because it says proximity to criticality can be fundamentally unlearnable from the process itself even with unbounded data, which should temper any program that hopes to estimate distance-to-criticality in aging or other near-unstable systems from observation alone. The target-trial thread lands in the same place from the causal side: the bias is almost never exotic, it is a failure to synchronize time zero, and the fix is specification, not cleverness. Two questions worth carrying: when a quantity is provably unidentifiable in the degenerate limit, is there a reparametrization in which a nearby, genuinely estimable quantity captures what you cared about, and is there a single intrinsic sharpness, built from the Fisher metric rather than the raw Hessian, that would make the flatness-generalization relation hold without the sign flips.

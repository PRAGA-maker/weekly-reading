## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Overlap, Not Moments: Finite-Sample Theory for Bridge Sampling
Giorgio Micaletto. arXiv stat.ME, 2026-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17403

**The move:** The optimal bridge-sampling estimator of a marginal likelihood is exactly a logistic classifier separating posterior draws from proposal draws, and its accuracy is pinned within a factor of two to that classifier's Bayes error.

**Why it is worth your time:** This converts a workhorse evidence estimator into a two-sample classification problem, which is why the usual delta-method standard error can lie to you. You get a finite-sample, moment-free, dimension-uniform answer to "is my run long enough": draws needed scale inversely with overlap, the same way importance sampling cost scales exponentially in KL. It builds the habit of seeing a Monte Carlo estimator as a statistical learning object whose hardness is a classifier's error rate.

**Cost:** 45 min, moderate. Needs bridge and importance sampling, logistic regression, Berry-Esseen intuition.

**Pairs with:** Any evidence-estimation workflow; contrast with the stacking and predictive-prior line in earlier lists.

### 2. Bagged Martingale Posteriors: Calibrated Uncertainty Quantification for Predictive Resampling
Hui Wang, Edwin Fong, David T. Frazier. arXiv stat.ME, 2026-09. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30622

**The move:** Martingale posteriors can systematically undercover for general functionals, and the fix is to launch each predictive path from a bootstrap resample rather than the observed sample, restoring calibration at zero extra simulation cost.

**Why it is worth your time:** This is a clean negative result aimed at a fashionable method. It shows the credible sets are not automatically trustworthy and isolates the cause: every predictive path is anchored at the same empirical start, so concentration is not calibration. The bootstrap-start remedy is almost embarrassingly cheap, and the diagnosis is the part that transfers. Good antidote to "likelihood-free equals assumption-free."

**Cost:** 1 hr, moderate. Needs martingale posteriors (Fong-Holmes-Walker), coverage, bootstrap.

**Pairs with:** The c.i.d. classic below, which is the structural theory it leans on.

### 3. Synthetic Priors
Nick Polson, Vadim Sokolov. arXiv stat.ME, 2026-02. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00347

**The move:** Elicit a GLM prior in observable space as Beta beliefs about response probabilities at chosen design points, then use Good's imaginary-observations device to turn that prior into a literal binomial likelihood on synthetic data, giving an exact Polya-Gamma Gibbs sampler with no tuning and no Metropolis step.

**Why it is worth your time:** It reframes what a prior is: not a distribution on uninterpretable log-odds but pseudo-data in the same exponential family, so elicitation and computation collapse into one object. It operationalizes the old conjugate-prior-as-likelihood identity into a tuning-free workflow you can actually run. The muscle it builds is eliciting where humans have beliefs and treating the prior as data.

**Cost:** 40 min, easy to moderate. Needs GLMs, conjugacy, Polya-Gamma augmentation.

**Pairs with:** Distance-based and conditional-means prior elicitation work.

### 4. Limit theorems for a class of identically distributed random variables (under-cited classic)
Patrizia Berti, Luca Pratelli, Pietro Rigo. arXiv math.PR, 2004-10. Annals of Probability 2004. https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0410105

**The move:** Introduces conditionally identically distributed (c.i.d.) sequences, where future observations are identically distributed given the past but the sequence need not be exchangeable, and shows exchangeable equals stationary plus c.i.d.

**Why it is worth your time:** This is the quiet foundation under the entire predictive-resampling and martingale-posterior wave. It is the exact weakening of exchangeability that lets you define a coherent predictive without a prior, which is why papers like Bagged Martingale Posteriors even make sense. Most people cite this second-hand without reading it. Read the key sections and the modern predictive-Bayes literature stops looking like magic.

**Cost:** 1.5 hr for the core, harder, measure-theoretic. Needs filtrations, exchangeability, martingale CLTs.

**Pairs with:** Bagged Martingale Posteriors (a direct descendant); de Finetti entries in earlier lists.

### 5. Three Homoscedastic Gaussian Components Have at Most Four Modes
Rintaro Okahara, Kei Kobayashi. arXiv math.ST, 2026-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15369

**The move:** Closes an old gap by proving that a mixture of three equal-covariance Gaussians has at most four modes in every dimension, dropping to three when the whitened means are collinear or form a right or obtuse triangle, replacing a loose upper bound of eight.

**Why it is worth your time:** The proof is the gem: modes correspond to local minima of a separable free-energy functional on the simplex, which reduces a geometric mode-counting problem to counting branches of a scalar inverse function. Mixture multimodality is what wrecks Bayesian mixture posteriors through label switching and MCMC trapping, so a sharp mode bound is load-bearing rather than cute. A derivation you could teach from.

**Cost:** 1 hr, moderate, clean but detailed. Needs Gaussian mixtures and a variational free-energy viewpoint.

**Pairs with:** Bayesian density-estimation inconsistency and BNP mixture entries.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. Averaged Annealed Markov Chains Achieve the Algorithmic Threshold for Tensor PCA, Part I: Glauber Dynamics
Aniket Das, Tselil Schramm. arXiv stat.ML, 2026-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16705

**The move:** A purely local Markov chain (Glauber) with SNR annealing and ergodic trajectory averaging matches the algorithmic threshold for tensor PCA from a random start, reaching where global spectral and sum-of-squares methods live.

**Why it is worth your time:** It overturns the standard belief that local sampling dynamics are strictly inferior to global algorithms on information-computation-gap problems and only work a polynomial factor above threshold. The fix is two cheap, well-understood ingredients, not a bespoke estimator, which makes the result both surprising and mechanistically clean. This is the rare "conventional wisdom was wrong and here is exactly why" paper.

**Cost:** careful afternoon, high. Needs tensor PCA, information-computation gaps, Markov-chain mixing.

**Pairs with:** The high-dimensional inference entries in earlier lists; the Bayesian sampling-as-classification paper above for the opposite view of what a chain is doing.

### 2. The hidden advantage of mask resampling: a theory of masked autoencoders
Jorge Medina Moreira, Lorenzo Bardone, Lenka Zdeborova. arXiv stat.ML, 2026-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01578

**The move:** In a solvable high-dimensional model, masked linear reconstruction recovers the latent feature at linear sample complexity in regimes where unmasked reconstruction, which is just PCA, provably fails, and the paper quantifies why resampling K masks per example lowers sample complexity.

**Why it is worth your time:** It gives a mechanism, not a bound, for why masking beats reconstruction, and the load-bearing reframe is that unmasked reconstruction is PCA and PCA is blind here. It then cashes out on real pipelines with a falsifiable prediction: random cropping and flipping can silently destroy the mask-resampling advantage by renewing the task. Clean contact between a stat-mech toy and actual self-supervised training.

**Cost:** 2 hr, moderate to high. Replica and state-evolution fluency helps but the main results are readable.

**Pairs with:** Advani-Saxe below, the linear-model-as-microscope lineage.

### 3. Symmetry-Aware Feature Learning: A Polynomial Separation for Multi-Index Models
Jivan Waber, Vanessa Piccolo, Yatin Dandi, Florent Krzakala. arXiv stat.ML, 2026-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08420

**The move:** Under online SGD on multi-index models with a cyclic symmetry orbit, weight-tying and full-group data augmentation both reach weak recovery at d^(p-1) samples while the symmetry-agnostic learner needs r times d^(p-1), a provable factor-r separation with matching upper and lower bounds.

**Why it is worth your time:** It makes the folklore "weight sharing is like data augmentation, and both beat ignoring symmetry" precise and two-sided, not a one-directional bound. The mechanism lives in the SGD feature-learning dynamics through the information exponent p, so it explains a real training choice rather than an abstract capacity. A rare clean statement that two different ways of exploiting symmetry are equivalent and strictly better.

**Cost:** careful read, high. Needs multi-index models, information exponents, online-SGD dynamics.

**Pairs with:** The masked-autoencoder paper above for the feature-learning-dynamics lens.

### 4. Generic Local Identifiability Does Not Imply Generic Global Identifiability in Linear Networks
Anders Hansson. arXiv math.OC, 2026-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18228

**The move:** An explicit 12-node, 42-coefficient linear network that is generically locally identifiable yet admits exactly two compatible parameter vectors on an open set, with exact rational-number certificates, kills the conjecture that local identifiability implies global.

**Why it is worth your time:** A negative result, the rarest and most list-worthy archetype, aimed at a shortcut practitioners lean on. The construction is concrete and certificate-checked rather than an asymptotic pathology, and the rational transformation that swaps the two solutions is the whole mechanism. Short, surgical, and the kind of thing you remember as a cautionary tale about trusting a local rank condition.

**Cost:** 1 hr, low to moderate. Needs linear-systems and transfer-function identifiability basics.

**Pairs with:** The target trial identifiability-failure paper below; both are about a nice-looking condition that does not deliver what you assumed.

### 5. High-dimensional dynamics of generalization error in neural networks (under-cited classic)
Madhu S. Advani, Andrew M. Saxe. arXiv stat.ML, 2017-10. Later in Neural Networks. https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.03667

**The move:** An average-case, exactly solvable analysis using random matrix theory and a linear model of gradient-descent generalization in the regime where parameters are at least as many as examples, showing the dynamics naturally protect against overtraining and produce non-monotone train and test curves.

**Why it is worth your time:** This laid out the mechanism behind what the field later rebranded double descent, two years before the famous papers, and it is routinely skipped in favor of them. The derivation is clean enough to teach from, and the n comparable to p framing is exactly where modern over-parameterization theory lives. Reading it reframes a trendy phenomenon as something already understood dynamically.

**Cost:** 1.5 hr, moderate, very readable. Needs random matrix theory and linear-regression dynamics.

**Pairs with:** The masked-autoencoder and symmetry papers above, which use the same solvable-model method on newer questions.

## C. Target trial emulation

Sources for this section were found through the PubMed connector. DOIs are linked per entry.

### 1. Towards a Clearer Causal Question Underlying the Association Between Cancer and Dementia
L. Paloma Rojas-Saunero, Kimberly D. van der Willik, Sanne B. Schagen, M. Arfan Ikram, Sonja A. Swanson. Epidemiology, 2024-05. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000001712)

**The move:** Takes the famous inverse cancer-dementia association and shows the estimate swings from a risk ratio of 0.71 to 1.1 depending purely on how you specify the causal question and the proxy, here Pin1 expression, before any data issue enters.

**Why it is worth your time:** This is the "Where do target trials come from" register made concrete. The contribution is interrogating what question the proxy is even answering, not producing a clinical number, and it does so on a real Rotterdam cohort with the identification assumptions and selection-bias-by-death written out. A worked example where question specification, not analysis, is the whole game.

**Cost:** moderate. Needs IPW, competing-risk-by-death, proxy-exposure reasoning.

**Pairs with:** The Dissanayake identifiability paper below.

### 2. Challenges in Estimating Effects of Hypothetical Interventions on Resources Patterned by Structural Racism: An Example in a Rural North Carolina Medicaid Population
Mekhala V. Dissanayake, John W. Jackson, Chantel L. Martin, Rachel Peragallo Urrutia, Michele Jonsson Funk, Mollie E. Wood. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwaf072)

**The move:** An honest identifiability-failure paper. The hypothetical intervention that equalizes resources cannot be emulated from the data because marginalized groups never experienced the resource levels privileged groups did, so there is no common support.

**Why it is worth your time:** This is a negative result done right. Instead of reporting a weighted estimate, the authors stop and show why the target intervention is unidentifiable, then generalize the lesson to causal health-equity work broadly. It treats positivity and common support not as a nuisance diagnostic but as the finding, which is the discipline most emulations skip.

**Cost:** moderate. Needs mediator-weighting and positivity concepts; the equity framing is accessible.

**Pairs with:** The Hansson local-versus-global identifiability counterexample in the learning-theory section.

### 3. Initiator Types and the Causal Question of the Prevalent New-User Design: A Simulation Study (under-cited sleeper)
Michael Webster-Clark, Rachael K. Ross, Jennifer L. Lund. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2021-07. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwaa283)

**The move:** Decomposes initiators into new users, direct switchers, and delayed switchers, then shows by simulation that the standard fix, time-conditional propensity-score matching, still gives a biased effect unless you condition on full treatment history, and clarifies that the prevalent new-user design answers a genuinely different causal question.

**Why it is worth your time:** The sleeper of this batch. It sharpens what estimand a popular design actually targets instead of treating it as a bias-reduction trick. The three-initiator taxonomy is a reusable mental model and the simulation makes the residual bias concrete. It pays rent every time someone reaches for a prevalent new-user cohort.

**Cost:** moderate. Needs pharmacoepi design vocabulary and PS-matching familiarity.

**Pairs with:** The Nicolaisen clone-censor-weight paper below on who-to-include decisions.

### 4. When to Start, How to Classify, and Whom to Include: A Methodological Assessment and Illustrative Example of the Clone-Censor-Weight Approach for Evaluating Intervention Timing in Observational Studies
Sia Kromann Nicolaisen, Reimar Wernich Thomsen, Lars Pedersen. Clinical Epidemiology, 2026-09. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2147/CLEP.S618946)

**The move:** Walks the clone-censor-weight machinery for treatment-timing questions and is candid that the censoring weights must simultaneously represent the target strategies and the intended population, a tension the primers gloss over.

**Why it is worth your time:** This covers the clone-censor-weight leg of the field as a methods assessment rather than a cheerleading tutorial, using a clean four-strategy diabetes-timing example to show how diagnosis-as-index-date resolves misalignment. The honest-limits framing, flagging that weights may fail to reflect either the strategy or the population, is the skeptical register you want. Practical enough to implement from.

**Cost:** moderate. Needs IPCW and clone-censor mechanics; the worked example is followable.

**Pairs with:** Webster-Clark above; the Limozin inference paper below.

### 5. Inference Procedures in Sequential Trial Emulation With Survival Outcomes: Comparing Confidence Intervals Based on the Sandwich Variance Estimator, Bootstrap and Jackknife
Juliette M. Limozin, Shaun R. Seaman, Li Su. Statistical Methods in Medical Research, 2025-07. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/09622802251356594)

**The move:** Shows the ubiquitous sandwich variance estimator is conservative for sequential trial emulation in exactly its usual regime, small samples, rare events, low treatment prevalence, and adapts the linearized-estimating-function bootstrap as a faster, better-covering alternative.

**Why it is worth your time:** The unglamorous but real question of whether your confidence intervals mean what you think. Most applied sequential emulations default to the sandwich interval without knowing it is mis-sized; this quantifies the cost and gives regime-specific guidance, with the LEF bootstrap for small and rare and the sandwich only when samples are large and events common. The recommendation table is usable without the proofs.

**Cost:** high. Needs IPW and marginal-structural-model survival machinery and influence-function and bootstrap theory.

**Pairs with:** Any applied sequential emulation; the bridge-sampling finite-sample paper above for the theme of standard errors that lie.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. How Inefficient Is Natural Gradient Descent? From Exact Optimality to Theta(sqrt(log d)) Divergence
Guni Sharon, Alan Kuhnle. arXiv cs.LG, 2026-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07228

**The move:** Quantifies exactly how far natural-gradient descent on the forward-KL objective strays from the true Fisher-Rao geodesic through an inefficiency ratio, and bounds its growth as Theta(sqrt(log d)) in dimension.

**Why it is worth your time:** The rare natural-gradient paper that is pure geometric insight rather than "it trains faster." It proves natural gradient follows the mixture geodesic, not the shortest path, and a clean tensor criterion pins the families where the ratio is one, fixed-covariance Gaussians and quadratic potentials, exactly. Remove the geometry and the claim disappears, and it directly rebuts the folklore that natural gradient is the optimal metric descent.

**Cost:** 1.5 hr, medium. Needs dually flat and alpha-connection basics and KL-versus-Fisher-Rao length.

**Pairs with:** The score-matching and transport papers below; the tensor-PCA sampling paper for another "which dynamics are actually optimal" result.

### 2. The Loss Floor of Denoising Score Matching: Fisher Geometry from Schrödinger Bridges
Avinash Raju, Kai Zhang. arXiv stat.ML, 2026-08. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23916

**The move:** The irreducible excess in the denoising score-matching objective is shown to be exactly the trace of the Fisher-Rao metric of the conditional-endpoint family, integrated along the diffusion trajectory.

**Why it is worth your time:** A genuine reframe. A quantity practitioners watch daily, the training loss floor of diffusion models, is secretly a Fisher-Rao arc length. The conditional-variance decomposition is exact under mild regularity for general corruption kernels, so the geometry does load-bearing work rather than labeling a method. A clean bridge between score matching, Schrödinger bridges, and information geometry.

**Cost:** 2 hr, medium to high. Needs diffusion and score-matching, some stochastic-bridge background.

**Pairs with:** The advective-transport paper below; both recast transport and diffusion objectives as Fisher-Rao structure.

### 3. The Advective Fisher-Rao Geometry of Deterministic Measure Transport
Benjamin Gess, Johannes Müller. arXiv math.OC, 2026-08. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12111

**The move:** Introduces an advective Fisher-Rao metric on paths of measures under the continuity equation and shows it arises identically from three independent routes.

**Why it is worth your time:** The triple coincidence is the gem. The same metric is the rescaled zero-noise limit of Fisher-Rao on path measures, the expected second variation of the Freidlin-Wentzell large-deviation rate functional, and the Hessian of the Benamou-Brenier action. That three unrelated derivations land on one object is the import that pays rent, and it also yields provably optimal descent directions. Strong cross-pollination of large deviations, dynamic optimal transport, and information geometry.

**Cost:** 3 hr, high. Needs large-deviations and dynamic-optimal-transport fluency.

**Pairs with:** The score-matching paper above.

### 4. The Dually Flat Geometry of Planning as Inference
Nikola Milosevic, Asaki Kataoka, Nicolas Hinrichs, Kenji Doya, Nico Scherf. arXiv cs.LG, 2026-09. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04005

**The move:** Embeds the RL planning criterion into a resetting process so its stationary visitation measure lives on a dually flat manifold whose two affine charts, visitation probabilities and log-policies, are dual under conditional entropy.

**Why it is worth your time:** The dually flat structure does the work rather than decorating it. It is what lets planning-as-inference generalize from linear rewards to nonlinear functionals of the visitation, each step a single natural-gradient update. A cross-domain import of RL control into exponential-family geometry that changes what you can solve, and clean enough to teach the e-chart and m-chart duality from.

**Cost:** 2 hr, medium. Needs dually flat geometry, e- and m-connections, RL occupancy measures.

**Pairs with:** The synthetic-priors and c.i.d. entries above on inference as the organizing object.

### 5. Statistical Inference, Occam's Razor and Statistical Mechanics on the Space of Probability Distributions (under-cited classic)
Vijay Balasubramanian. arXiv cond-mat, 1996-01. Neural Computation 1997. https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9601030

**The move:** Casts parametric model selection as low-temperature statistical mechanics on the space of distributions, where the natural measure is the Fisher-information volume and the Bayesian posterior series makes Occam's razor fall out automatically.

**Why it is worth your time:** A sleeper that pays rent today. It derives model complexity as the log Fisher-information volume, the Jeffreys-prior volume, from a partition function, pre-dating and undergirding modern MDL and Bayesian-complexity intuitions, yet it is far less cited than its reach warrants. The geometry is indispensable: the razor emerges only because the measure on model space is the intrinsic Fisher volume. A beautifully teachable derivation connecting geometry, thermodynamics, and inference.

**Cost:** 2 hr, medium, self-contained. Needs Bayesian model selection and a Laplace or low-temperature expansion.

**Pairs with:** The three-Gaussians mode-counting paper above, which also reduces a Bayesian problem to a free-energy functional.

## Threads

**Thread 1: the estimator was a geometry or a classifier all along.** Bridge sampling (A1), the denoising score-matching loss floor (D2), and the advective transport metric (D3) each take a number you already compute and rewrite it as a geometric or learning object: a Bayes error, a Fisher-Rao arc length, a Hessian of an action. The throughline is that the rewrite is not cosmetic, it changes the error analysis. Bridge sampling stops trusting its delta-method standard error once you see it as classification, and the score-matching floor stops looking like a nuisance once you see it as arc length. The tension worth holding: A1 says the hard part is overlap between two measures, while D2 and D3 say the hard part is curvature along a path, and these are not the same notion of difficulty.

**Thread 2: nice-looking conditions that do not deliver.** Bagged martingale posteriors (A2), the local-versus-global identifiability counterexample (B4), and the structural-racism intervention (C2) are three negative results across three fields with one shape: a property you assumed transfers, calibration, global identifiability, a well-defined intervention, simply does not. Set against them, the tensor-PCA result (B1) is the inverse surprise, a property everyone assumed failed, local dynamics reaching the global threshold, that actually holds. Read together they are a lesson in not trusting the direction of folklore, and the natural-gradient paper (D1) sits between them by showing a method that is neither optimal nor useless but off by a precise, bounded amount.

**Thread 3: specify the object before you compute.** Synthetic priors (A3) and the c.i.d. classic (A4) are about what a prior is or whether you even need one; the cancer-dementia (C1) and prevalent-new-user (C3) papers are about what causal question a proxy or a design actually answers; planning as inference (D4) recasts control as an inference object on a statistical manifold. The common discipline is that the estimand or the prior is a modeling choice to be stated and interrogated first, and the computation follows. The best pairing across domains is A4 with C1: both say the coherent object exists before the data, one for predictive Bayes without a prior, one for a causal question without a trial.

**Thread 4: high-dimensional structure governs what is learnable.** The masked-autoencoder (B2), symmetry-separation (B3), and double-descent-dynamics (B5) papers are all solvable high-dimensional models that pin a sample-complexity or generalization phenomenon to a mechanism, and the three-Gaussians mode bound (A5) and Balasubramanian's Fisher-volume complexity (D5) are their Bayesian and geometric cousins, reducing a posterior or a model-selection problem to a free-energy functional. The throughline is that counting, of modes, of samples, of effective parameters, is controlled by a geometry or a phase structure rather than by raw dimension.

## Synthesis

This week's papers keep performing the same operation from different sides: take an object you treat as a black box, a marginal-likelihood run, a training loss, a prior, a causal estimate, and expose the structure that was deciding its behavior. Three of them do it by failing on purpose, which is the more valuable mode, because a clean negative result retires a practice while a clean reframe only reorganizes one. The geometry papers and the learning-theory papers quietly agree that difficulty in high dimension is curvature or overlap or phase, not parameter count, and the natural-gradient result is the sharpest version of that claim because it measures exactly how far the convenient metric sits from the correct one. Where they disagree is instructive. Thread 1 cannot decide whether the hard part of inference is separating two measures or traversing a curved path, and that disagreement is not semantic, it points at different diagnostics and different stopping rules for a run. The causal papers and the Bayesian foundations papers converge on a single demand, state the object first, which is less a method than a standard of care. Two questions worth sitting with. First, if bridge sampling is a classifier and planning is a dually flat inference, how much of applied Bayesian computation is secretly a geometry problem we are solving with the wrong coordinates, and would the natural-gradient inefficiency bound tell us when it matters. Second, the martingale-posterior and identifiability failures both come from a hidden anchoring, every path starting at the same empirical point, every local condition read as global, so the open question is whether there is one diagnostic for anchoring bias that covers predictive Bayes, system identification, and causal positivity at once.

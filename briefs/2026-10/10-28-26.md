## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Beyond Brown's radial tests: admissibility, parabolicity, and a doubling-Poincaré prior that neither test decides
Oleg Roshka. arXiv 2610.34309 (stat), 2026-10-25. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34309

- The move: reads Brown's admissibility theory for the normal mean as potential theory on the weighted space (R^m, f* dx), then builds an explicit prior that silences both of Brown's radial tests yet is decided inadmissible by a ball-volume growth criterion.
- Why it is worth your time: Brown (1971) tied admissibility of a generalized Bayes estimator to recurrence of a diffusion attached to the prior's marginal, and gave two tests that can both go silent. This paper imports modern recurrence-on-manifolds machinery and shows that under doubling plus a scale-invariant Poincare inequality the sphere test is already complete, so geometry buys new decidable cases only outside that class, and it constructs them. It trains the muscle of seeing a decision-theoretic property as the large-scale geometry of a measure, and of knowing exactly where the classical tests stop.
- Cost: 90 min, hard, needs comfort with generalized Bayes, parabolicity/recurrence, and weighted Poincare inequalities.
- Pairs with: Efron's statistical curvature below, and the diameter-constrained information geometry of Cai and Li. All three make optimality a geometric property of the model, not of the estimator formula.

### 2. On the asymptotic behaviour of alpha-divergence generalized posteriors
Ioar Casado-Telletxea, Omar Rivasplata. arXiv 2610.35330 (math.ST), 2026-10-26. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35330

- The move: a Bernstein-von Mises story for generalized variational posteriors regularized by an alpha-divergence, where KL (alpha = 1) is the unique member whose limit is Gaussian at the n^{-1/2} scale.
- Why it is worth your time: for alpha in (0,1) and small dimension the rescaled posterior converges in L1 to a Student-t and shrinks faster than the sampling error of the empirical risk minimizer; for alpha > 1 it converges to a compactly supported Pearson type-II law and shrinks slower. The single knob people turn for robustness quietly changes both the shape of the limit and the rate, which directly breaks frequentist coverage of credible regions. This is a clean, teachable warning about what generalized Bayes actually does to uncertainty.
- Cost: 50 min, moderate, needs Bernstein-von Mises and basic variational inference.
- Pairs with: Bagged martingale posteriors below, on the theme that non-standard Bayesian updates often miscalibrate in opposite directions.

### 3. Bagged Martingale Posteriors: Calibrated Uncertainty Quantification for Predictive Resampling
Hui Wang, Edwin Fong, David T. Frazier. arXiv 2609.30622 (stat.ME), 2026-09-24. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30622

- The move: shows martingale-posterior credible sets systematically undercover for general functionals unless the predictive is carefully tuned, then fixes it by starting each predictive path from a bootstrap resample rather than the observed sample.
- Why it is worth your time: predictive resampling is spreading because it is cheap and sidesteps the likelihood-prior pair, but its calibration was largely unstudied. The paper gives the concentration and calibration analysis, locates the failure, and the bagging remedy costs nothing extra in simulation while delivering conservative coverage, scaling to sparse high-dimensional regression and nonparametric quantiles. Good example of diagnosing a method's hidden failure mode and repairing it without new machinery.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate, needs martingale posteriors and the bootstrap.
- Pairs with: the alpha-divergence result above and, across domains, the natural-gradient inefficiency result, as three cases of a trusted shortcut paying a quiet price.

### 4. Posterior Frequencies and the Cost of Not Fitting: A Correction to a Memorization Lower Bound
Zhenisbek Assylbekov, Rustem Takhanov. arXiv 2610.20402 (stat.ML), 2026-10-14. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20402

- The move: an exact three-point counterexample to Feldman's memorization lower bound, then an exact replacement coefficient built from full-sample posterior probabilities under exchangeability.
- Why it is worth your time: Feldman's frequency coefficient is widely cited as the reason label memorization is necessary. Using independent fair labels and a permutation-equivariant learner, the authors make the ratio of the proposed penalty to the actual excess risk grow exponentially, ruling out any universal constant-factor repair, and then show the original coefficient was the posterior-mean form of Good's exact frequency formula all along. It is a precise lesson in how an occupancy-weighted average can look like a bound but fail to be one.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate, needs exchangeability and the Good-Turing frequency picture; the memorization literature helps.
- Pairs with: Barzilai on learned versus fixed features, since both are about what fitting the data actually costs and buys.

### 5. On the Consistency of Bayes Estimates (classic)
Persi Diaconis, David Freedman. The Annals of Statistics 14(1):1-26, 1986. https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176349830

- The move: the paper that shows a Bayesian can be consistent in finite dimensions yet inconsistent in infinite dimensions, with the prior concentrating the posterior on the wrong answer no matter how much data arrives.
- Why it is worth your time: every current paper on Bayesian nonparametric inconsistency, martingale-posterior calibration, and prior sensitivity is standing on this. The construction and the discussion section remain the cleanest statement of why tail-free and Dirichlet priors behave and generic priors need not, and why consistency is a joint property of prior and parameter space rather than a free lunch. Read it before trusting any nonparametric posterior. It beats the fifth-best recent Bayesian paper this week outright.
- Cost: 2 h with the discussion, hard, needs measure-theoretic probability.
- Pairs with: the Bagged martingale posterior paper, which is a modern instance of the same lesson about where Bayesian uncertainty goes wrong.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. Learning a Group Element from Its Action: Galois Correspondences, Optimal ERM, and an Abel-Ruffini Threshold for the Natarajan Dimension
Xiaoyu Li. arXiv 2610.18154 (cs.LG), 2026-10-13. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18154

- The move: casts learning an unknown group element from action examples (x, gx) as a Galois correspondence, turning multiclass learning dimensions into group invariants, and locating the Natarajan-DS gap exactly at the quintic.
- Why it is worth your time: the DS and graph dimensions become the orbit height, the Littlestone dimension the longest irredundant sequence, the teaching dimension the minimal base size. For finite Galois extensions the Natarajan dimension equals a coset-cube number, so the DS-Natarajan dimensions coincide for every solvable Galois group and first separate at A5 and S5, the groups of the unsolvable quintic. A learning-theoretic gap turning out to be Abel-Ruffini solvability by radicals is the rare cross-domain import where removing the borrowed structure collapses the result. This is the gem of the week.
- Cost: 2 h, hard, needs Galois theory and multiclass learning dimensions (Natarajan, DS, Littlestone).
- Pairs with: the Efron curvature classic, as another case where an invariant of the problem, not the algorithm, sets the achievable rate.

### 2. Dimension-Free Scaling Laws for Size Generalization: An Exponential Separation
Behrooz Tahmasebi, Melanie Weber. arXiv 2610.19279 (cs.LG), 2026-10-13. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19279

- The move: a minimax theory of size generalization showing polynomial source decay gives polynomial in-domain sample complexity but only (log n)^{-Theta(1)} error across sizes, an exponential separation, with matching lower bounds and no dimension dependence.
- Why it is worth your time: everyone trains on small graphs or sets and hopes it transfers to large ones. This quantifies the statistical cost of that hope for the first time, identifies the exponential source condition that restores polynomial complexity, and makes the whole thing dimension-free. It gives you a sharp way to think about when size transfer is cheap and when it is hopeless, rather than a vague invariance argument.
- Cost: 75 min, hard, needs source conditions, kernel/minimax rate theory.
- Pairs with: network control theory and graph-size scaling, and with the central-flow paper below on what the slow structure of a problem controls.

### 3. Singular Geometry of Low-Rank Adaptation
Kamillo Ferry, Anthea Monod, Keisuke Yano. arXiv 2610.32854 (stat.ML), 2026-10-23. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32854

- The move: treats LoRA as a singular statistical model and computes its real log canonical threshold, showing a forward-backward covariance of activations and backpropagated sensitivities decides which low-rank directions are statistically observable.
- Why it is worth your time: LoRA's factorization is non-identifiable, so its learning complexity cannot be read off the parameter count. The paper locates the geometry locally through observable tangent directions and a residual singularity, and globally through affine sections of determinantal varieties, and finds the global complexity can be set by a singularity on a different rank stratum than the ground truth. This is singular learning theory touching a method people actually run, with a curvature-corrected RLCT estimator to match. Experiments show layer-dependent geometry, so ambient covariance degeneracy does not imply degeneracy along the adapted directions.
- Cost: 90 min, hard, needs singular learning theory (RLCT) and reduced-rank regression.
- Pairs with: Diaconis and Freedman on why parameter dimension is the wrong complexity measure, and Watanabe-style sBIC work.

### 4. The Multiple Timescales of Gradient Descent on the Edge of Stability: A Perturbative Derivation of the Central Flow
Raphael Berthier. arXiv 2609.01034 (cs.LG), 2026-09-01. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01034

- The move: derives the empirically accurate central flow of Cohen et al. as the leading term of a singular-perturbation expansion, with self-stabilization appearing at next order and three clean timescales.
- Why it is worth your time: the central flow matched experiments but its derivation was heuristic. Writing the loss as g + epsilon h and sending epsilon to zero, gradient descent converges to the gradient flow of h constrained to the minimizers of g of sharpness at most 2/eta, and the method of multiple scales separates the fast oscillation, the intermediate self-stabilization, and the slow drift along the minimizer manifold. It explains why fluctuations persist with several eigenvalues at the edge, and it is the right template for turning an observed training phenomenon into a controlled asymptotic model.
- Cost: 70 min, moderate to hard, needs singular perturbation theory and edge-of-stability background.
- Pairs with: non-Euclidean edge-of-stability work and, in geometry, the gradient-flow and non-metric-geodesic papers.

### 5. A Distribution-Free Separation Between Learned and Fixed Features
Daniel Barzilai. arXiv 2610.25335 (cs.LG), 2026-10-18. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25335

- The move: settles the COLT open question of Feldman, Kamath and Srebro by constructing hypothesis classes a two-layer ReLU network learns under any input distribution, which no polynomial-dimension fixed feature map can represent.
- Why it is worth your time: prior proofs that learned features beat fixed ones leaned on a specific input distribution, leaving open whether the advantage was an artifact of that choice. This makes the separation distribution-free: for any small error and large runtime budget R there is a class learnable in time R under every distribution while the dimension complexity of any fixed feature map is superpolynomial in R. It is a crisp, foundational reason representation learning is not reducible to a fixed kernel.
- Cost: 60 min, hard, needs dimension complexity and the kernel-versus-feature-learning literature.
- Pairs with: the memorization-bound correction above, both on the genuine cost and payoff of fitting rather than reading off fixed structure.

## C. Target trial emulation

Source: PubMed (Epidemiology, American Journal of Epidemiology). A strong methodological week, so all five are recent and each teaches a lesson rather than reporting a clinical result.

### 1. Structural Limitations of Early-Late Treatment Comparisons Under Target Trial Emulation: A Simulation Study
Atsushi Takayama, Shiro Tanaka, Koji Kawakami. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag208

- The move: a structural simulation showing that clone-censor-weighting for early-versus-late initiation can produce window-dependent risk ratios, including direction reversals, even when the true marginal contrast is exactly 1.
- Why it is worth your time: E-L comparisons are widely treated as the safe, immortal-time-free use of CCW. This shows that under ideal implementation the window-defined strategy contrast is shaped by risk accumulated before initiation within the specified windows, and the distortion is not explained by weight instability or lack of overlap. It is a genuine negative result about a method people reach for by default, and it tells you to interrogate the initiation windows before trusting an E-L estimate.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate, needs clone-censor-weighting and the target-trial framing.
- Pairs with: the PNU-versus-CCW paper below, on how emulation design choices silently redefine the estimand.

### 2. Nesting a Target Study within a Target Trial: A Framework for Evaluating Intervention Effects on Disparities
Xinyi Sun, Theodore J. Iwashyna, Emmanuel F. Drabo, Deidra C. Crews, Kadija Ferryman, John W. Jackson. Epidemiology 37(6):727-737, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000002024

- The move: nests a target study inside a target trial, using an allowability-based enrollment plan to make social groups distributionally similar on allowable covariates before randomizing intervention strategies within each group.
- Why it is worth your time: measuring whether an intervention reduces a disparity requires first deciding what a fair baseline comparison is, an ethical choice this framework makes explicit through allowability and then anchors to a causal estimand. It extends semiparametric G-computation to continuous stochastic interventions and counterfactual disparities in time-to-event outcomes, and the pulse-oximeter-bias application shows it working. This is the estimand-specification register at its best: the hard part is defining the question, and they do.
- Cost: 60 min, moderate to hard, needs G-computation, stochastic interventions, and the disparities-estimand literature.
- Pairs with: causal fairness work and the exposure-mixture estimand paper below, both on designing the contrast before estimating it.

### 3. Adapting the prevalent new-user study design and clone-censor-weighting approach to evaluate drug discontinuation
Qoua Her, Til Sturmer, Robert J. Glynn, Emilie Duchesneau, Tiansheng Wang, Charles Poole, Jennifer Leigh Lund, Michael Webster-Clark. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag238

- The move: lays the prevalent new-user design and clone-censor-weighting side by side for drug discontinuation and shows they emulate different target trials, so their estimands diverge under treatment-effect heterogeneity even when both are unbiased for their own target.
- Why it is worth your time: both methods are promoted for discontinuation studies and both looked interchangeable until you ask which target trial each one emulates. In the statin-discontinuation application the mortality rate ratios land at 1.41 versus 1.55, a gap driven by who each design implicitly enrolls. The lesson, that the design is the estimand, is exactly the kind of distinction the target-trial tradition exists to force.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate, needs CCW and the new-user design.
- Pairs with: the E-L structural-limits paper above, same theme that the emulation you choose fixes the question you answer.

### 4. Everything All at Once: On Choosing an Estimand for Multi-component Environmental Exposures
Kara E. Rudolph, Shodai Inose, Nicholas T. Williams, Ivan Diaz, Lucia Calderon, Jacqueline M. Torres, Marianthi-Anna Kioumourtzoglou. Epidemiology 37(6):807-818, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000002022

- The move: a shift-based estimand for multivariate exposure mixtures that defines a flexible shift of one or more continuous components, assesses and modifies the shift to stay inside data support, and estimates the effect fully nonparametrically.
- Why it is worth your time: most mixture analyses bolt a parametric model onto a non-randomized problem and call a coefficient causal. This reframes the target as a modified treatment policy whose shift is chosen to minimize extrapolation, gives the estimand a main-effect-style interpretation including interactions between components, and fits everything with machine learning. The pesticide-and-hypertension application and the step-by-step code make it immediately usable, and the positivity-aware shift design is the transferable idea.
- Cost: 55 min, moderate to hard, needs modified treatment policies, positivity, and nonparametric estimation.
- Pairs with: the target-study-in-target-trial framework above, both on constructing a supported, interpretable contrast for non-binary exposures.

### 5. Estimate Optimal Dynamic Treatment Regimes with Partial Compatibility
Chloe Si, David A. Stephens, Erica E. M. Moodie, Ivan Marbaniang, Vidya Mave, Haridas Prasad. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag254

- The move: two adaptive weighting schemes, generalized compatibility weighting and bootstrap compatibility windowing, let near-compatible trajectories contribute to dynamic-treatment-regime value estimation in small samples, with the information borrowing vanishing as n grows.
- Why it is worth your time: strict regime compatibility throws away most trajectories and leaves inverse-probability-weighted DTR value estimates noisy and threshold estimates unstable. Allowing almost-compatible paths to contribute, with diminishing weight as the sample grows, stabilizes the estimated optimal thresholds without biasing the large-sample answer. The HIV-cohort application shows it recovering reliable CD4-and-weight decision rules where standard IPW does not, a practical fix for the small-sample reality of observational DTR estimation.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate, needs dynamic treatment regimes and IPW value estimation.
- Pairs with: longevity and adaptive-treatment modeling, and the bias-variance theme running through the Bayesian picks.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. How Inefficient Is Natural Gradient Descent? From Exact Optimality to Theta(sqrt(log d)) Divergence
Guni Sharon, Alan Kuhnle. arXiv 2610.07228 (stat.ML), 2026-10-05. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07228

- The move: measures how far natural gradient descent strays from optimal by the ratio of the Fisher length of the mixture geodesic it follows to the Fisher-Rao distance, and classifies the answer into three regimes by a tensor criterion.
- Why it is worth your time: idealized NGD on the forward KL objective follows the mixture geodesic, which is generally longer than the shortest Fisher-Rao path, and nobody had quantified the gap. The inefficiency ratio is exactly 1 only for quadratic-potential or one-dimensional families such as fixed-covariance Gaussians; bounded third-order skewness with finite diameter gives a dimension-free bound; and products of scale families, including Gaussian covariances and Gamma rates, make it grow as sqrt(log d), unbounded. Under a per-step budget this is a real step-count penalty, so the paper reframes a universally used method as geometrically suboptimal with a precise, checkable rate.
- Cost: 60 min, moderate to hard, needs dually flat families, Fisher-Rao geometry, and alpha-connections.
- Pairs with: Brown admissibility and Efron curvature, all three locating optimality in the geometry of the model manifold.

### 2. Stochastic Processes as Non-Metric Geodesics in Information Geometry
T. Koide, A. van de Venn. arXiv 2609.08870 (cond-mat.stat-mech), 2026-09-08. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08870

- The move: a one-to-one correspondence between alpha-connection geodesics on the Gaussian manifold and continuous stochastic processes with constant noise, where the physical restoring force of the resulting Ornstein-Uhlenbeck relaxation fixes the connection parameter alpha.
- Why it is worth your time: it gives a concrete physical observable, a restoring force, that pins down which affine connection you are on, and shows that force can be attributed either to scalar curvature or purely to non-metricity, a direct analogy to the geometrical trinity of gravity. The slow-driving work-minimizing protocol in stochastic thermodynamics turns out to be an expectation geodesic of the non-metric manifold. This is cross-pollination with gravity and thermodynamics where the borrowed structure does real work rather than decorating the method.
- Cost: 60 min, moderate to hard, needs alpha-connections, stochastic thermodynamics, and some differential geometry.
- Pairs with: thermodynamic length and control-energy views of estimation, and the central-flow edge-of-stability paper on slow optimal dynamics.

### 3. Exact Asymptotic Efficiency under zCDP: Diameter-Constrained Information Geometry
T. Tony Cai, Yicheng Li. arXiv 2609.16895 (math.ST), 2026-09-15. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16895

- The move: under zero-concentrated differential privacy the governing object is not the Fisher information matrix but a diameter-constrained information region, and exact local minimax risk is an inverse-information variational functional over that set.
- Why it is worth your time: privacy forces statistics to have bounded diameter, which replaces the single information matrix with the set of information matrices achievable under that constraint, and efficiency becomes set-valued and loss-dependent. A mixed information region then yields one unified efficiency constant covering both the privacy regime and classical Fisher efficiency, and the optimal estimator releases a nearly optimal bounded statistic with Gaussian noise and inverts its moment map. This is a genuine reframe of efficiency theory, and the import from differential privacy is load-bearing rather than cosmetic.
- Cost: 75 min, hard, needs local asymptotic minimax theory and differential privacy.
- Pairs with: the NGD inefficiency paper above and Efron curvature below, on how the relevant geometry stops being a single point once you add a constraint or go to second order.

### 4. G-Exponential Families through Probability Coordinates
Manuela-Simona Cojocea. arXiv 2609.03208 (math.ST), 2026-09-02. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03208

- The move: builds generalized exponential families by transporting a classical family through a probability-coordinate chart, changing the geometry while leaving the exponential function untouched, which is the opposite of the algebraic deformations in Tsallis and Kaniadakis.
- Why it is worth your time: because the construction is geometric rather than algebraic, tails are inherited from the chart, the sufficient statistic is bounded, maximum likelihood reduces to moment matching in probability coordinates, and the estimator has a bounded influence function even when every family member has infinite mean. Robustness arises from geometric compactification, and transport preserves the information geometry. It is a clean, teachable alternative route to heavy-tailed exponential-family-like models that keeps the usual machinery working.
- Cost: 50 min, moderate, needs exponential families, information geometry, and influence functions.
- Pairs with: robust generalized Bayes and the alpha-divergence posterior paper, on tails and robustness from changing the geometry rather than the likelihood.

### 5. Defining the Curvature of a Statistical Problem, with Applications to Second Order Efficiency (classic)
Bradley Efron. The Annals of Statistics 3(6):1189-1242, 1975. https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176343282

- The move: the paper that defines statistical curvature, shows a family is locally like an exponential family to first order, and ties the curvature to the second-order deficiency of the maximum likelihood estimator.
- Why it is worth your time: every modern paper in this section that talks about where the Fisher-metric picture is exact and where it needs a correction is a descendant of this. Efron's curvature is the invariant that says how far a model bends away from flatness, hence how much information a first-order method loses, which is exactly the quantity the NGD-inefficiency and zCDP-efficiency papers are recomputing under new constraints. It is under-read by machine-learning people and beats the marginal recent option this week.
- Cost: 2 h, hard but rewarding, needs exponential families and asymptotic efficiency.
- Pairs with: the NGD inefficiency and diameter-constrained efficiency papers above, and Brown admissibility in the Bayesian section.

## Threads

### Thread 1: Optimality lives in the manifold, not in the estimator
Papers: Brown admissibility (A1), Efron curvature (D5), diameter-constrained efficiency (D3), NGD inefficiency (D1). Each says that whether a procedure is optimal is a geometric fact about the model, admissibility as recurrence of a diffusion on a weighted space, second-order efficiency as curvature, private efficiency as a variational problem over an information region, natural-gradient speed as a geodesic-length ratio. The tension is in what counts as the geometry: Efron and Brown work with the local, point-wise curvature of the model, while Cai and Li and the NGD paper show the relevant object becomes non-local once you add a privacy constraint or ask about a whole optimization path, so the single-matrix Fisher picture is the special case, not the rule.

### Thread 2: Singular and non-regular regimes are the real ones
Papers: LoRA singular geometry (B3), alpha-divergence posteriors (A2), G-exponential families (D4), and the undecidable zone in Brown admissibility (A1). The regular Gaussian n^{-1/2} Bernstein-von Mises default assumes non-degeneracy, and all four live where that assumption fails. LoRA's non-identifiability forces an RLCT rather than a parameter count, non-KL divergences give Student-t or Pearson limits at the wrong scale, chart-transported families keep working with infinite means, and Brown's tests simply go silent. The shared move is to find the invariant that actually governs behavior in the degenerate regime instead of pretending the regular theory still applies.

### Thread 3: Trusted methods that silently fail, and how to catch them
Papers: E-L target-trial limits (C1), Bagged martingale posteriors (A3), the memorization-bound correction (A4), and the alpha-divergence coverage result (A2). A target-trial estimand reverses sign with the initiation window, martingale-posterior credible sets undercover, a cited memorization lower bound admits a three-point counterexample, and switching the divergence quietly breaks credible-region coverage. Across epidemiology, Bayesian nonparametrics, and learning theory the pattern is identical: a method or bound people deploy without checking misbehaves under a clean adversarial example, and in every case the fix comes from naming the exact quantity that was being assumed well-behaved. Read together they are a short course in adversarial self-checking.

## Synthesis

The week's throughline is that the clean cases everyone computes with, regular models, point-wise Fisher information, Gaussian posterior limits, compatible trajectories, strict feature maps, are the exceptions, and the interesting mathematics is in naming what governs the degenerate case. Li's Galois reframe and Efron's curvature are the two poles of this: both say an invariant of the problem, not a cleverness of the algorithm, sets what is achievable, one through solvability by radicals and one through how far a family bends from flat. The information-geometry papers agree and push further, arguing that once you add a constraint such as privacy, or follow a full optimization path, the governing object stops being a single matrix and becomes a region or a geodesic. Where they disagree is instructive: the NGD and zCDP results treat non-locality as the new normal, while Efron and Brown still read everything off local curvature and recurrence, and it is not yet clear whether the set-valued picture is a genuine generalization or a different problem wearing the same words. The negative results cut across all of this and should change practice now: clone-censor-weighting for early-versus-late comparisons, martingale-posterior credible sets, and non-KL generalized posteriors each fail quietly, and each failure was invisible until someone built the adversarial case. Two questions worth sitting with. First, the recurring appearance of a slow manifold, the central flow's minimizers of g, the DTR's near-compatible trajectories, the mixture geodesic natural gradient is stuck on, suggests a single question about what dynamics do when confined to a degenerate set, and whether the singular-learning RLCT is the right complexity measure for it. Second, if optimality is geometric, the practical lever is choosing the geometry, chart, divergence, connection, enrollment plan, and the open problem is when that choice is a modeling freedom and when it is smuggling in the answer.

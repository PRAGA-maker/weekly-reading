# Weekly Reading List — 11-25-26

A strong week. The spine is identification: what a design, a prior, or a divergence actually pins down, and what it only appears to. Bayesian foundations get a clean generalization of exchangeability with exact predictive limit laws; learning theory lands a resolution of the weak sample-compression conjecture and a sharp memory-versus-query price; target trial emulation gets a blunt negative result showing the label prevents nothing; information geometry supplies three reframes of estimation as divergence choice. ## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Limit theorems for almost conditionally identically distributed random variables
Marco Battiston, Lorenzo Cappello. arXiv:2611.28967, 2026-11-21. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28967

- **The move:** Introduces a scalar sequence $\xi$ that measures departure from the conditionally-identically-distributed property and proves SLLN and three CLTs (predictive, empirical, directing) that degrade gracefully as $\xi$ grows.
- **Why it is worth your time:** c.i.d. is the right weakening of exchangeability for recursive algorithms and heteroskedastic streams, but until now the asymptotics were all-or-nothing. Parametrizing the failure and getting necessary-and-sufficient conditions for absolute continuity of the directing measure is the move that turns a binary property into a dial. The payoff is honest asymptotic credible intervals for predictive inference when the data are not quite exchangeable.
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate-to-hard, needs comfort with directing random measures and martingale CLTs.
- **Pairs with:** its own ancestor below (Berti-Pratelli-Rigo), and martingale-posterior predictive inference.

### 2. Rational v. Radical: Trajectory Properties of Iterated Bayesian Updates
Clive Cenxin Aw, Lynette Zhong Ling Wang, Peter Sidajaya. arXiv:2611.24430, 2026-11-18. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24430

- **The move:** Treats iterated Jeffrey updating on fixed evidence as a dynamical system and runs it backward, showing reverse iteration is prior hacking that drives almost every interior prior to certainty while worsening fit at every step.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It names and characterizes "radicalization" as a generic attractor of reversed updating, partitions priors into Bayes phases separated by M-projection fixed points, and proves you can always pick evidence that herds every interior prior to one conclusion without controlling which. This is a genuinely unusual way to think about where updating sends you, with a phase-diagram picture you can carry around.
- **Cost:** 35 min, moderate, light on machinery and heavy on geometry of the simplex.
- **Pairs with:** information-geometric projection (Eguchi below), and the opinion-dynamics literature it quietly inverts.

### 3. Asymptotics of Gibbs Posterior Measures on Infinite-Dimensional Hypothesis Spaces
Terje Mildner. arXiv:2611.23214, 2026-11-17. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23214

- **The move:** Proves almost-sure contraction and consistency for Gibbs posteriors under dependent data, nonadditive and time-varying losses, misspecification, and unattainable minimizers, using Lorentz spaces and real interpolation instead of testing or variational arguments.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Standard contraction theory leans on sieves, tests, or ELBO-style variational bounds, all of which break at this level of generality. Swapping in Laplace transforms over interpolation spaces is a new proof technology for generalized and fractional Bayes, and it delivers rates for Dirichlet-process Gibbs posteriors under a Wasserstein-metric loss as a concrete payoff.
- **Cost:** 90 min, hard, needs functional analysis (interpolation theory) more than statistics.
- **Pairs with:** generalized-Bayes and loss-based updating from prior weeks.

### 4. Greedy Empirical Bayes for Density Estimation
Taole Sha, Shibo Yu, Edwin Fong. arXiv:2611.22796, 2026-11-17. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22796

- **The move:** Builds a Dirichlet-process prior smoothed over a data-chosen partition so that posterior sampling collapses to a Bayesian bootstrap, and selects the partition by greedily maximizing a closed-form marginal likelihood into an adaptive tree.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The theoretical result is the interesting part: the greedy tree lands in an admissible family with high probability, splitting every identifiable signal while refusing to over-split noise, which buys adaptive contraction under weak checkable assumptions. It is a rare case where a cheap, scalable algorithm comes with the contraction guarantee you would want from full nonparametric Bayes.
- **Cost:** 45 min, moderate, needs Dirichlet-process and posterior-bootstrap basics.
- **Pairs with:** martingale posteriors (Fong's line), and the compression/admissibility thread below.

### 5. Limit theorems for a class of identically distributed random variables (classic)
Patrizia Berti, Luca Pratelli, Pietro Rigo. Ann. Probab. 32(3):2029–2052, 2004. https://doi.org/10.1214/009117904000000676 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0410105)

- **The move:** Defines conditionally identically distributed sequences relative to a filtration, proves exchangeability is c.i.d. plus permutation invariance, and establishes a.s. and $L^1$ convergence of the mean with three centerings for the CLT, one of them the Bayesian predictive mean.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the load-bearing sleeper under pick 1 and under most of the current martingale-posterior and predictive-inference work. Reading it shows you the exact point where exchangeability can be dropped without losing a limit theory, and why $E[X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal G_n]$ is the natural object once you condition rather than permute. Twenty years on it is still cited mostly within one small school.
- **Cost:** 50 min, moderate, clean probability writing.
- **Pairs with:** pick 1 (the a.c.i.d. generalization) and de Finetti-style foundations.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. Mirror Descent at the Edge of Stability
Vincent X. Wang, Vikas Garg. arXiv:2611.29043, 2026-11-21. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29043

- **The move:** Defines mirror sharpness as curvature with respect to the mirror potential, shows it hovers just above $2/\eta$ empirically, and derives a dual central-flow ODE for the onset of edge of stability in non-Euclidean descent.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The honest part is the admission that the usual self-stabilization argument for why sharpness parks at $2/\eta$ does not transfer from gradient descent to mirror descent in general. That failure is more informative than a clean extension: it localizes which piece of the edge-of-stability story is Euclidean-specific and which is geometric. A mechanism paper that marks its own boundary.
- **Cost:** 45 min, moderate, needs edge-of-stability background and Bregman geometry.
- **Pairs with:** Fisher-geometric sharpness and flat-minima implicit bias from prior weeks.

### 2. Exponential memory/query-complexity tradeoffs in high-accuracy feasibility problems
Jialin Yu, Moïse Blanchard. arXiv:2611.29320, 2026-11-22. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29320

- **The move:** First nontrivial lower bound in the high-accuracy regime: any algorithm with $d^{1+\delta}\ln(1/\varepsilon)$ memory bits needs at least $(d\ln(1/\varepsilon))^{\widetilde\Omega(d^{(1-\delta)/8})}$ separation-oracle calls.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It explains a previously strange fact, that the best known low-memory feasibility algorithms pay an exponential-in-$d$ query cost, by proving the cost is forced rather than an artifact. This is the memory-constrained-optimization story at its sharpest: not a bound in search of a phenomenon, but the phenomenon pinned down. The surprise is that insufficient memory is catastrophic exactly in the regime where you most want accuracy.
- **Cost:** 60 min, hard, needs information-based complexity and cutting-plane intuition.
- **Pairs with:** the compression/admissibility thread below, and streaming lower bounds.

### 3. Near-Quadratic Sample Compression for All VC Classes
Yang Cai, Vineet Gupta, Yanchen Jiang, Christopher Liaw, Aranyak Mehta, Shay Moran, Grigoris Velegkas, Di Wang, Manfred K. Warmuth. arXiv:2611.25011, 2026-11-18. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25011

- **The move:** Proves the weak sample-compression conjecture by constructing a compression scheme of size $O(d^2\log^2 d)$ for every VC class, replacing the exponential-in-$d$ bound that had stood since 2016.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is a real dent in a forty-year-old problem about why keeping a small part of the data implies generalization. The prior best (Moran-Yehudayoff) was exponential; going to polynomial is the kind of result the best people in learning theory will read. If you only read one learning-theory paper this quarter, it is defensibly this.
- **Cost:** 90 min, hard, needs VC theory and some combinatorial geometry; the statement alone is worth ten minutes.
- **Pairs with:** the Floyd-Warmuth and Littlestone-Warmuth compression lineage it finally advances.

### 4. Attention Head Specialization: Phase Transitions in Sample Complexity
Léo Catteau, O. Duranthon, Lenka Zdeborová. arXiv:2611.26228, 2026-11-19. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26228

- **The move:** Shows in a solvable high-dimensional multi-head attention model that head specialization is a genuine phase transition in sample complexity, a non-analyticity in test error, and separates this statistical transition from the dynamical one seen over training time.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It reframes a phenomenon usually told as a training-time story (heads specialize as you train) into a data story (heads specialize as you add samples), and gets a cascade of transitions that recovers weaker latent features in order, a statistical form of simplicity bias. The disentangling of statistical from dynamical origins is the taste muscle here: two different clocks produce the same picture.
- **Cost:** 50 min, moderate-to-hard, needs replica/statistical-physics-of-learning fluency.
- **Pairs with:** Seung-Sompolinsky-Tishby below, and singular-learning phase transitions.

### 5. Statistical mechanics of learning from examples (classic)
H. Sebastian Seung, Haim Sompolinsky, Naftali Tishby. Phys. Rev. A 45:6056–6091, 1992. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.45.6056

- **The move:** Casts learning as a Gibbs distribution over networks and derives learning curves, a discontinuous transition from poor to perfect generalization for nonsmooth realizable rules, inverse-power-law asymptotics for smooth ones, and genuine spin-glass phases for unrealizable rules.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the source code for the entire "phase transitions in learning" program that pick 4 and much of singular learning theory now extend. Reading it shows you where the vocabulary of transitions, degenerate minima, and learning curves came from, and it is sharper than most of its descendants. The spin-glass phase for unrealizable rules is exactly the degenerate-minima picture modern loss-landscape work keeps rediscovering.
- **Cost:** 2 hr, hard, long and physics-heavy, but skimmable for the transitions and learning curves.
- **Pairs with:** pick 4 (tension: discontinuous jump vs a cascade of smoother transitions) and information geometry of criticality.

## C. Target trial emulation

Discovered via PubMed; DOIs linked.

### 1. Laundered Survival: When Target Trial Emulation Doesn't Emulate — Implications for GLP-1 Receptor Agonists and Cancer Outcomes
James M. Brophy. J. Clin. Epidemiol., 2026-11-20. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112595

- **The move:** Shows by Monte Carlo under a true null that two published GLP-1-and-cancer studies invoking target trial emulation reproduce their large protective hazard ratios (0.72 down to 0.39) purely from misaligned eligibility-assignment-time-zero, and that enforcing alignment recovers HR = 1.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the negative result of the week. It makes concrete that the phrase "target trial emulation" prevents nothing; the only thing doing work is EAT0 alignment, and defining exposure on post-baseline information (two prescriptions) smuggles immortal time back in through both misclassification and survivor selection. The simulated HRs landing on the published numbers is the kind of demonstration that changes how you read a methods section.
- **Cost:** 30 min, easy-to-moderate, no heavy math, just the structure of time zero.
- **Pairs with:** the systematic review below (how often this still happens) and Hernán 2008.

### 2. Adapting the prevalent new-user study design and clone-censor-weighting approach to evaluate drug discontinuation
Am. J. Epidemiol., 2026-10. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag238

- **The move:** Spells out that the prevalent new-user design and clone-censor-weighting, applied to drug discontinuation, emulate two different target trials and answer two different questions, then shows both are unbiased for their own estimand.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Discontinuation is where estimand sloppiness hides, because "the effect of stopping" is ambiguous until you say among whom and from when. The paper's value is the explicit mapping from method to target trial: same data, same drug, two legitimate but non-interchangeable causal contrasts. It is a clean lesson in reading the estimand off the design rather than off the outcome.
- **Cost:** 35 min, moderate, needs clone-censor-weight and new-user design basics.
- **Pairs with:** pick 1 (both turn on what the design actually emulates) and prior per-protocol estimand work.

### 3. Informing sample size and power calculations for clone-censor-weight analyses: a Monte Carlo study of the variance inflation factor relative to the underlying target trial
J. Clin. Epidemiol., 2026-10. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112576

- **The move:** Quantifies, by simulation, the variance inflation that cloning-censoring-weighting incurs relative to the target trial it emulates, giving a usable design-stage factor for power and sample size.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Almost all clone-censor-weight writing is about bias; this one is about cost. Cloning creates artificial censoring and correlated pseudo-observations, so effective sample size is below the headcount, and nobody had a clean rule of thumb for how far below. Framing it as inflation relative to the hypothetical trial keeps the benchmark honest and makes the method's price visible before you run it.
- **Cost:** 30 min, easy-to-moderate, mostly simulation design.
- **Pairs with:** picks 2 and 4 (the practical economics of clone-censor-weight).

### 4. Use of clone-censor-weight to avoid immortal-time bias: a systematic methodological review
Aldrine Manzanilla et al. (Noémie Simon-Tillaux, senior). J. Clin. Epidemiol. 199:112468, 2026-11. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112468

- **The move:** Reviews 113 clone-censor-weight studies and finds that 20% still carry immortal-time bias, almost all from excluding patients on post-time-zero criteria before cloning.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the empirical complement to pick 1: not "here is how the bias sneaks in" but "here is how often it still does, in print, under the TTE banner." The specific failure mode, post-baseline eligibility, is the same one Brophy simulates, which makes the pair unusually tight. Also a useful map of how inconsistently weighting and censoring are actually implemented and reported.
- **Cost:** 40 min, easy-to-moderate, a review you read for the failure taxonomy.
- **Pairs with:** pick 1 directly, and reporting-guideline debates.

### 5. Observational studies analyzed like randomized experiments: hormone therapy and coronary heart disease (classic)
Miguel A. Hernán, Alvaro Alonso, Roger Logan, Francine Grodstein, Karin B. Michels, Walter C. Willett, JoAnn E. Manson, James M. Robins. Epidemiology 19(6):766–779, 2008. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0b013e3181875e61

- **The move:** Reconstructs the Nurses' Health Study as a sequence of nested trials emulating the Women's Health Initiative, and shows the famous hormone-therapy-and-CHD discrepancy largely dissolves once time since menopause and follow-up length are aligned, with ITT and inverse-probability-weighted per-protocol estimates both reported.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the paper that launched target trial emulation as a program, and it remains the cleanest worked example of the framing changing the answer. Reading it alongside the three recent picks shows how little the operational discipline has changed and how often it is still skipped. The ITT-versus-adherence-adjusted contrast is a mini-course in what intention-to-treat costs you.
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate, applied but methodologically dense.
- **Pairs with:** picks 1-4 (the standard they are measured against) and the estimand-selection literature.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. Characterizing M-estimators using canonical divergences from information geometry
Paul Navas. arXiv:2611.18141, 2026-11-13. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18141

- **The move:** Redefines the estimation residual as the inverse exponential map of an affine connection, whose geodesic integral is the Ay-Amari canonical divergence, and uses a geometric Osband principle to decide which losses each connection admits on the simplex.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The sharp consequences are the draw: for flat connections every strictly convex generator gives a consistent Bregman loss, but for the curved $\alpha$-connections only half the squared Fisher-Rao distance works for the Fréchet mean, and for generic $\alpha$ no consistent loss exists, leaving only method of moments. The residual is integrable only at $\alpha=0$. This is a reframe that tells you exactly when "pick a loss" and "pick a geometry" are the same choice and when they collide.
- **Cost:** 60 min, hard, needs exponential/mixture connections and scoring-rule theory.
- **Pairs with:** Eguchi below (its direct ancestor) and consistent scoring rules.

### 2. The Bayesian Learning Rule Beyond KL Geometry
Sophia Sklaviadis, Wu Lin, Andre Martins, Mario Figueiredo, et al. arXiv:2610.31308, 2026-10-22. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31308

- **The move:** Refutes the belief that only KL preserves the additive Bayesian learning rule by deriving the same additive updates for mixed-space Bregman and Fenchel-Young discrepancies, which yield generalized max-entropy families with dual coordinates.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Prior work with $f$-divergences suggested KL was special; this shows the special structure is Bregman duality, not KL, and the generalization buys genuinely new algorithms. The Tsallis-negentropy instance gives bounded-support and heavy-tailed $q$-Gaussian posteriors with a Newton-like update, so the geometry change is not cosmetic. A clean case of removing a load-bearing assumption and finding it was not load-bearing.
- **Cost:** 50 min, moderate-to-hard, needs natural-gradient and exponential-family variational inference.
- **Pairs with:** pick 1 and the generalized-Bayes thread in Bayesian statistics.

### 3. Dually flat structure of von Mises-Fisher distributions
Behzad Najafi, Morteza Sharifi-Najjar, Ryu Ueno. arXiv:2610.39249, 2026-10-29. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39249

- **The move:** Works out the full dually flat geometry of the von Mises-Fisher family: exponential and mixture autoparallel submanifolds, the classification of doubly autoparallel ones, and explicit KL-divergence minimizers with projection theorems.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The vMF distribution is the sphere's answer to the Gaussian, and having its autoparallel submanifolds and projections in closed form is the kind of reference result you reach for when doing directional-data inference or anything on $S^{d-1}$. It is short, concrete, and teachable, with the projection theorems doing the real work rather than decorating it.
- **Cost:** 35 min, moderate, needs dually-flat and autoparallel-submanifold definitions.
- **Pairs with:** the curved-exponential cylindrical-distribution work and directional statistics.

### 4. Thermodynamic geometry of three coupled Ising spins: exact curvature, frustration and bond anisotropy
Zahra Ebadi, Nayyere Einali Saghavaz, Omid Yahyayi Monem, Hosein Mohammadzadeh. arXiv:2611.08072, 2026-11-05. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08072

- **The move:** Computes the Fisher-Rao (Ruppeiner) scalar curvature of three Ising spins in closed form and shows it stays finite and negative down to $T=0$ in the frustrated regime, with any bond anisotropy destroying the plateau below a logarithmically small crossover.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is thermodynamic curvature tested outside the thermodynamic limit, where it can be solved exactly and where the few-spin cluster is itself physical (molecular trimers, few-qubit simulators). The payoff is that curvature becomes an extremely sensitive detector of exact ground-state degeneracy rather than of criticality, and the near-zero behavior is analytic, not critical scaling. A cross-domain import where the geometry genuinely diagnoses the physics.
- **Cost:** 45 min, moderate, needs Ruppeiner geometry and basic statistical mechanics.
- **Pairs with:** criticality and sloppiness in neural systems, and the phase-transition thread.

### 5. Geometry of minimum contrast (classic)
Shinto Eguchi. Hiroshima Math. J. 22:631–647, 1992. https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.hmj/1206128508

- **The move:** Shows that any contrast (divergence) function on a manifold induces a Riemannian metric and a dual pair of affine connections, and studies the minimum-contrast leaf and its second fundamental form.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the construction every recent pick in this section quietly stands on: divergence first, geometry second. Reading it makes picks 1 and 2 legible, because the canonical-divergence and Fenchel-Young stories are both "run Eguchi's machine with a different contrast." It is the cleanest statement of why choosing a discrepancy is choosing a dual geometry, and it predates the vocabulary now used to rediscover it.
- **Cost:** 50 min, moderate-to-hard, differential-geometric but self-contained.
- **Pairs with:** picks 1 and 2, and Efron's and Amari's curvature work from earlier weeks.

## Threads

### Thread 1: Phase transitions and degeneracy carry information
Papers: B4 (attention head specialization), B5 (Seung-Sompolinsky-Tishby), D4 (Ising thermodynamic geometry). All three read non-analyticity and degeneracy as signal rather than pathology: a head specializes at a sample-complexity threshold, a network's generalization jumps or crawls depending on realizability, and curvature spikes precisely at exact ground-state degeneracy. The productive tension is the shape of the transition. SST 1992 gets a discontinuous jump for nonsmooth realizable rules, while B4 gets a cascade of smoother transitions that recover latent features in order. Same statistical-physics lens, opposite verdicts on whether learning is a cliff or a staircase.

### Thread 2: What a design identifies versus what it seems to
Papers: A1 (a.c.i.d. limit laws), A5 (Berti-Pratelli-Rigo), C1 (Laundered Survival), C5 (Hernán 2008). The Bayesian pair asks what object a weakened-exchangeability sequence actually pins down (the directing measure, the predictive mean), and the causal pair asks what estimand a design actually emulates. The throughline is that identification lives in the alignment, not the label: conditioning rather than permuting changes which limit exists, and aligning eligibility-assignment-time-zero rather than invoking "target trial" changes whether the hazard ratio is one or one-half. Getting the directing measure or the estimand right is the whole game; everything downstream is bookkeeping.

### Thread 3: Choosing a divergence is choosing a geometry is choosing an estimator
Papers: D1 (M-estimators as canonical divergences), D2 (Bayesian learning rule beyond KL), D5 (Eguchi), with A2 (iterated updates toward M-projection fixed points) as the Bayesian echo. Eguchi's 1992 construction is the grammar: a contrast function generates a metric and a dual connection pair. D1 and D2 are two conjugations of that verb, one deciding which losses each connection admits and finding Fisher-Rao forced for the curved case, the other showing the additive learning rule survives any Bregman discrepancy, not just KL. A2 then shows updating itself flows toward the M-projection fixed points this geometry defines. The tension with Thread 2 is worth holding: here the geometry is a free choice, there the estimand is a fact you must not get wrong.

### Thread 4: How little suffices, and the price of too little
Papers: B3 (near-quadratic sample compression), B2 (memory/query tradeoffs), A4 (greedy empirical Bayes admissible tree). Each is about keeping only part of the information and still succeeding, or failing. B3 says a small compressed subset implies generalization, polynomially in VC dimension. A4 says a greedy tree that keeps every identifiable signal and no noise attains adaptive contraction. B2 says the opposite tail: too little memory forces an exponential query price in exactly the high-accuracy regime you care about. Read together they bracket the question of sufficiency, with B3 and A4 on the optimistic side and B2 on the cost side.

## Synthesis

The week's center of gravity is identification, and it splits cleanly by field in a way worth noticing. In Bayesian statistics and target trial emulation the object to be identified is a fact about the world: a directing measure, an estimand, the trial you are actually emulating. Get it wrong and no amount of machinery recovers it, which is the explicit lesson of Laundered Survival and the implicit one of the a.c.i.d. limit theorems. In information geometry the divergence is a free choice, and the recent papers are about understanding the consequences of that choice rather than being trapped by one. The two postures sit in tension: Thread 3 celebrates the freedom to pick a geometry, Thread 2 insists some things are not yours to pick. Learning theory this week is unusually concrete, with a real dent in the sample-compression conjecture and a sharp memory-query lower bound, both of which say something durable about how little information suffices and what too little costs. The phase-transition thread is where the fields rhyme: head specialization, spin-glass learning transitions, and thermodynamic curvature all treat non-analyticity as information, and they disagree productively about whether the transition is a cliff or a staircase. That disagreement connects to the criticality-and-degeneracy questions worth sitting with: when a system parks at the edge of stability or on a line of degenerate ground states, is the relevant object the sharpness, the curvature, or the sample count, and are these three clocks or one. The honest note of the week comes from Mirror Descent at the Edge of Stability, which shows a favorite stabilization argument does not transfer off the Euclidean case, a reminder that mechanisms are often narrower than the phenomena they explain. Two questions to carry forward: whether the alignment discipline that fixes causal estimands has a geometric analogue that would tell you when a divergence choice is a real choice versus a disguised misspecification, and whether the cascade-versus-cliff distinction in learning transitions is a property of the data or of the clock you measure it with.

# Weekly Reading List, 11-11-26

Two spines this week. One is a cluster of negative results, in Bayesian testing, target trial emulation, and information geometry, that all say the same thing: the number you compute by default moves when you re-slice the space, so the representation is doing work you think the data is doing. The other is exponential-family duality running from a 1979 characterization of conjugacy to a 2026 geometry of reinforcement learning. Network access confirmed against the arXiv API, PubMed, and journal DOIs; every arXiv id and DOI below was re-verified on its own abstract or Crossref record before inclusion. Target trial emulation was not thin this week, so no classic was used as filler there.

## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Functional inequalities from the fundamental limits of Bayesian estimation
Shuchen Li, Ilias Zadik. arXiv:2611.05831 (math.ST/math.PR), 2026-11-04. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05831

- **The move:** A log-concave measure satisfies a Φ-Sobolev inequality with constant C if and only if the Bayes risk of estimating f(X) from the Gaussian channel Y = √t X + G is at least a 1/(1+Ct) fraction of the trivial estimator, for every f.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This reads an analytic inequality as an estimation-impossibility statement and then runs the equivalence in the hard direction to get constants. From the one identity they reprove the Lee-Vempala O(n^{1/2}) Poincaré bound with a static information argument and no stochastic localization, and push the log-Sobolev constant to K²n^{1/4}, beating the previous O(K²n^{1/2}). The payoff is the low-degree MMSE recasting of the recent KLS proofs: remove the Bayesian channel and the results genuinely collapse, which is the test for an import that pays rent.
- **Cost:** 90+ min, hard, needs log-concave geometry, Φ-entropy and Sobolev inequalities, and I-MMSE.
- **Pairs with:** the KLS-conjecture proof wave it reframes, and the Wasserstein-Cramér-Rao entry in the geometry section as the other "estimation limit as geometry" paper.

### 2. Species sampling priors through exact augmentation
Ramsés H. Mena, Christos Merkatas, Theodoros Nicoleris, Carlos E. Rodríguez. arXiv:2611.13323 (stat.ME), 2026-11-09. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13323

- **The move:** Every proper species sampling process has an exact two-stage finite representation, a finite index that picks atoms followed by a reweighted draw, and inverting it lets you build nonparametric priors from an index law plus finite allocation probabilities instead of specifying infinite weights.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is a representation theorem used as a construction kit, not a new sampler. Instead of wrestling with stick-breaking weights you prescribe a visibility schedule and an index distribution and read off the partition and predictive laws, with total-variation bounds that separate exact averaging from fixed-level truncation. It yields genuinely new size-biased-invariant families, for example a normalized gamma completely random measure combined with a compound Poisson index, which is a different place to put the modeling choice.
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate-hard, needs species sampling and exchangeable partition machinery.
- **Pairs with:** Kingman's partition structures and Pitman-Yor, the natural reference-chase ancestors.

### 3. Heavy tails in Gaussian-process surrogates: process-level profiling degeneracy versus observation-level identifiability
Neelesh S. Upadhye, Rohit Kumar Mani. arXiv:2611.11188 (stat.ME), 2026-11-08. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11188

- **The move:** For process-level scale mixtures with a free amplitude, Student-t and α-stable processes, the profile likelihood of the mixing law is literally data-free and is maximized by the Gaussian, so you cannot learn the tails from a single realization; only observation-level heavy noise is identifiable.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It quietly kills a common practice. People swap a Gaussian process for a Student-t process believing they are learning heavier tails from their data, and this shows the plug-in predictive is just a data-free transform of the Gaussian predictive with no consistent estimator for the mixing law along one path. The constructive flip side is that symmetric α-stable observation noise is identifiable, with a cross-fitted estimator that hits the efficiency bound under fixed-domain asymptotics even for Matérn-5/2 paths. It trains the reflex to ask where in the hierarchy a parameter lives before trusting its estimate.
- **Cost:** 50 min, moderate-hard, needs Gaussian-process regression and fixed-domain asymptotics.
- **Pairs with:** the Zhang and Stein microergodicity literature on what is estimable from one process path.

### 4. Prior Probabilities for Constrained Hypotheses: Invariance, Neutrality, and Model Search
Joris Mulder. arXiv:2611.13781 (stat.ME), 2026-11-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13781

- **The move:** Assigning equal prior probabilities to constrained hypotheses makes the posteriors depend on how you partition the parameter space, so a hypothesis can end up with more posterior mass than a hypothesis that contains it.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is a sharp "your default breaks a logical law" result that most practitioners of informative-hypothesis testing have never confronted. The fix is a single encompassing prior that induces complexity-weighted hypothesis probabilities, which restores partition-invariance and nestedness and reconciles constrained testing with exploratory model search, at the honest cost that inequality constraints no longer get a free posterior boost. It builds the habit of running coherence checks on model-space priors, not only on parameter priors.
- **Cost:** 35 min, moderate, needs Bayes factors and variable-selection priors.
- **Pairs with:** the Klugkist and Hoijtink encompassing-prior Bayes factors, and the target trial window-dependence result in section C as the same pathology in a different field.

### 5. Conjugate Priors for Exponential Families (timeless)
Persi Diaconis, Donald Ylvisaker. Annals of Statistics 7(2):269-281, 1979. https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176344611

- **The move:** Conjugate priors for an exponential family are characterized by the property that the posterior mean of the mean parameter is linear in the data, and the paper pins down exactly which linear posterior expectations are achievable.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Everyone uses conjugacy and almost no one can state what it is beyond algebraic convenience. This is the clean answer: conjugacy is linear shrinkage of the natural parameter, with the hyperparameters reading as a prior sample size and location, which is what makes "the prior is a pseudo-dataset" precise rather than a slogan. It is reference-chased as the load-bearing ancestor behind this week's design and species-sampling machinery, and the derivation is short enough to teach in one sitting.
- **Cost:** 45 min, moderate, needs exponential families and basic measure theory.
- **Pairs with:** the dually flat planning paper and the Pistone-Sempi manifold in section D, which carry the same natural-versus-expectation parameter duality.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. Benign population landscapes do not transfer to empirical risk minimization in nonsmooth optimization
Penghao Wang, Mateo Díaz, Liwei Jiang. arXiv:2611.01384 (math.OC), 2026-11-01. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01384

- **The move:** The standard recipe, prove the population loss has a benign landscape and then transfer by concentration, provably breaks in the nonsmooth weakly-convex setting: robust phase retrieval has a benign population objective yet its empirical objective keeps sharp spurious local minima with probability bounded away from zero even as n goes to infinity.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It kills a move people use constantly, and it does so on a highly structured problem rather than a pathological construction, so you cannot wave it away. The failure-probability lower bound is dimension-independent, which is the detail that makes it bite. This is the cleanest "when is the abstraction valid" answer of the week, and it should change how much you trust population-landscape arguments for strict-saddle and active-saddle methods.
- **Cost:** 60 min, hard, needs weakly-convex and nonsmooth landscape analysis and phase retrieval.
- **Pairs with:** the Auer-Herbster-Warmuth classic below, its direct ancestor, and the surrogate-trust paper as the applied version of the same warning.

### 2. Singular Geometry of Low-Rank Adaptation
Kamillo Ferry, Anthea Monod, Keisuke Yano. arXiv:2610.32854 (stat.ML), 2026-10-23. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32854

- **The move:** Treat LoRA as the singular statistical model it actually is and compute its real log canonical threshold, showing the learning complexity is controlled by a forward-backward covariance, activations times backpropagated sensitivities, that decides which low-rank directions are statistically observable.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is singular learning theory aimed at something everyone runs rather than a toy. The sharp observation is that ambient covariance degeneracy does not imply degeneracy along the adapted low-rank directions, and the global learning coefficient can be set by singularities on a different rank stratum than the ground truth, which is a genuinely non-obvious statement about effective dimension. It also ships a curvature-corrected multi-temperature RLCT estimator you could actually use.
- **Cost:** 75 min, hard, needs RLCT and singular learning theory basics and reduced-rank regression.
- **Pairs with:** the Dead Directions geometric singular learning line already in the log, and the Fisher-information-flow speed limit in section D as the dynamical account of the same effective-dimension story.

### 3. Symmetry-Aware Feature Learning: A Polynomial Separation for Multi-Index Models
Jivan Waber, Vanessa Piccolo, Yatin Dandi, Florent Krzakala. arXiv:2610.08420 (cs.LG), 2026-10-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08420

- **The move:** A clean polynomial sample-complexity separation: architectural weight sharing and full-group data augmentation both recover a cyclically symmetric teacher in Θ̃(d^{p-1}) samples, while the symmetry-agnostic learner needs Θ̃(r·d^{p-1}), through a two-stage mechanism where initialization fluctuations pick one orbit direction and localized growth amplifies it.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It settles, with matching bounds, the folklore that augmentation is as good as baking in the symmetry, and quantifies the exact factor of r you pay for ignorance. The two-stage symmetry-breaking account is the kind of mechanism that generalizes past the specific model, and the information-exponent condition p ≥ 3 tells you precisely when the separation is real and when quadratics collapse it. This is a separation with a mechanism, not another bound.
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate-hard, needs multi-index and online-SGD staircase intuition and Hermite and information-exponent machinery.
- **Pairs with:** the feature-learning-order results in the log, and the symmetry-breaking order parameter in the quantum many-body geometry literature.

### 4. When May a Model Replace the Experiment? Audits, Licenses, and the Price of Trust in Surrogate-Driven Design
Shuangxiu Ma, Wenhe Zhao. arXiv:2608.01378 (cs.LG), 2026-08-02. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01378

- **The move:** In surrogate-driven search over structured design spaces, predictive accuracy cannot anchor trust, near-perfect R² is compatible with worst-possible selection, and the right certificate is rank preservation plus selection-aware audits, with a quantified selection tax and matching upper and lower bounds on the winner you screened for.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It answers the in-scope question of when a cheap model may stand in for the real evaluation, and gives an architectural rule that is both sufficient with no distributional assumptions and necessary: predictions may propose and train freely, but every certified conclusion must rest on true evaluations, because admitting predictions into certification opens a deterministic self-confirmation failure. The empirical side is unusually honest, with an audit statistic tracking regret at Spearman 0.80 to 0.99 while R² drops to 0.33. It is the structured-latent-search entry and the one paper here you are least likely to have stumbled on.
- **Cost:** 50 min, moderate, mostly selection and query-complexity reasoning with light prerequisites.
- **Pairs with:** Bayesian optimization over chemical and other structured latents, and the optimizer's-curse and post-selection-inference literature, where the selection tax is the same object.

### 5. Exponentially many local minima for single neurons (timeless)
Peter Auer, Mark Herbster, Manfred K. Warmuth. NeurIPS (NIPS) 1995. https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/1995/hash/3806734b256c27e41ec2c6bffa26d9e7-Abstract.html

- **The move:** A single sigmoidal neuron under squared loss can have exponentially many distinct local minima in the input dimension, generated from n well-separated examples, so the simplest possible object already has a maximally bad landscape.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the original and under-cited cautionary tale against benign-landscape optimism, and it reads as the direct ancestor of this week's nonsmooth non-transfer result. Chasing it surfaces how much of the modern "overparameterization makes landscapes nice" discourse quietly assumes away a phenomenon that already exists at the one-neuron level. It is short, exact, and surprising in a way that recalibrates intuition before you open any 2026 landscape paper.
- **Cost:** 30 min, moderate, an elementary but careful combinatorial-geometry construction with no modern prerequisites.
- **Pairs with:** entry 1 above, the 2026 continuation of the same lesson.

## C. Target trial emulation

### 1. Structural Limitations of Early-Late Treatment Comparisons Under Target Trial Emulation: A Simulation Study
Atsushi Takayama, Shiro Tanaka, Koji Kawakami. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026-08-28 (article kwag208). https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag208

- **The move:** Standard clone-censor-weight emulation of an early-versus-late initiation contrast can produce window-dependent risk ratios, including outright direction reversals across window specifications, even when the true marginal causal contrast is exactly 1 by construction.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The field treats clone-censor-weighting as the fix for immortal time in early-versus-late comparisons, and this shows by structural simulation that the estimand itself is contaminated by risk accumulated before initiation inside the chosen windows, with the artifact not explained by weight instability or poor overlap. It teaches you to distrust the estimand, not just the estimator, whenever the strategies being compared are defined by initiation windows. According to PubMed, the abstract states the window-defined strategy contrast can be strongly shaped by pre-initiation risk, which is exactly the subtle-bias surfacing the register prizes.
- **Cost:** 45 min, moderate, needs clone-censor-weight mechanics and marginal risk contrasts.
- **Pairs with:** the Austin power-calculation paper below as its design-side companion, and Mulder's partition-dependence result in section A as the same pathology in Bayesian testing.

### 2. Effects conditional on post-treatment events generated by independent mechanisms
Marco Piccininni, Mats J. Stensrud. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026-09-28 (article kwag241). https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag241

- **The move:** Survivor average causal effects and conditional separable effects can be identified without measuring the common causes of the post-treatment event and the outcome, provided treatment and the unmeasured outcome-causes generate the event through independent mechanisms.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This loosens an assumption that almost everyone treats as mandatory, the requirement to measure all common causes of the intermediate and the outcome. The independent-mechanisms condition is a clean, checkable structural reframe, and the paper stress-tests it on truncation by death, differential nonadherence, and the birth-weight paradox, which are exactly the cases where conditioning on a post-treatment variable usually destroys causal meaning. According to PubMed, the result is that these measurements are not always necessary, which is the kind of identification statement that changes what you bother to collect.
- **Cost:** 60 min, hard, needs separable effects and single-world intervention graphs and competing-events framing.
- **Pairs with:** the Stensrud-group vaccine-waning test below, and the heavy-tails identifiability result in section A, both about where identification actually lives.

### 3. A simple and powerful test of vaccine waning
Gellért Perényi, Matias Janvin, Mats J. Stensrud. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026-09-03, 195(9):2469-2475 (article kwag118). https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag118

- **The move:** Reframe "does efficacy wane" from an estimation problem, whose causal estimands only yield uselessly wide bounds, into a hypothesis test of whether the individual-level treatment effect is constant over time, which is both more powerful and valid under interpretable assumptions.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is an estimand-versus-test reframe that flips a real conclusion. According to PubMed, reanalyzing the BNT162b2 randomized trial, the test rejects the null of no waning where a prior analysis could not, and two of the three test statistics run on summary data alone. It makes explicit why the naive waning comparisons lack causal meaning, and it is honest about the bounding limitation it routes around rather than overselling the estimand it declines to estimate.
- **Cost:** 40 min, moderate, needs survival analysis and hypothesis-testing comfort.
- **Pairs with:** entry 2 above from the same group, and the vaccine-efficacy estimand papers already in the log.

### 4. Informing sample size and power calculations for clone-censor-weight analyses: a Monte Carlo simulation study of the variance inflation factor relative to the underlying target trial
Peter C. Austin. Journal of Clinical Epidemiology, 2026-10 (article 112576). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112576

- **The move:** Supply the missing design-phase tool for clone-censor-weight studies, a variance inflation factor relative to the randomized trial you are emulating, since the inverse-probability-of-censoring weights are only known at analysis and not at design.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It addresses a real and under-served gap, since nobody knows how to power a clone-censor-weight study a priori, and the mildly surprising result is that with modest confounding and around ten covariates the analysis can be more precise than the underlying randomized trial, with the inflation factor rising as the covariate count grows. According to PubMed and Crossref it is single-author and concrete, with public R code, so it is immediately usable. Read it next to entry 1 to see both why the method is indispensable and exactly where its estimand can go wrong.
- **Cost:** 30 min, easy-moderate, practical rather than theoretical.
- **Pairs with:** entries 1 and 5, the structural-bias and historical-origin ends of the same clone-censor-weight arc.

### 5. Correcting for noncompliance and dependent censoring in an AIDS Clinical Trial with inverse probability of censoring weighted (IPCW) log-rank tests (timeless)
James M. Robins, Dianne M. Finkelstein. Biometrics, 2000, 56(3):779-788. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0006-341x.2000.00779.x

- **The move:** The applied birth of clone-censor-weight logic: artificially censor subjects when they stop or switch therapy, then re-weight by the inverse probability of censoring using time-varying prognostic factors to recover the per-protocol effect.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the under-cited ancestor that every clone-censor-weight and per-protocol paper leans on, and it is the cleanest existing example of the framing flipping the answer. According to PubMed, the intent-to-treat comparison in ACTG 021 found no survival difference between bactrim and aerosolized pentamidine at p = .32, while the IPCW analysis exposed a real bactrim survival benefit masked by heavy crossover and discontinuation. Reading it alongside entries 1 and 4 shows both why the method is indispensable and exactly where it can mislead.
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate-hard, needs survival analysis and the logic of dependent censoring.
- **Pairs with:** entries 1 and 4 above, the modern clone-censor-weight cluster that descends from it.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. Speed limits for information acquisition in stochastic learning dynamics
Shuta Kobayashi, Andreas Dechant. arXiv:2609.08219 (cond-mat.stat-mech), 2026-09-08. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08219

- **The move:** Cast stochastic gradient descent as a Markov process and derive a Fisher-information-flow speed limit that bounds how fast the trainable parameters can acquire information about the data-generating parameters, decomposed exactly into a drift and a noise contribution.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the information-geometry, thermodynamics, and learning-dynamics bridge done with a real inequality instead of a slogan. The bound predicts the order in which different data-generating parameters get encoded and their characteristic timescales, verified in basis-function linear regression, so it answers which properties are learned, in what order, and how fast. Strip out the Fisher-information flow and there is no bound, which is the test for geometry doing load-bearing work.
- **Cost:** 75 min, hard, needs stochastic thermodynamics and speed-limit inequalities and the SGD-as-SDE picture.
- **Pairs with:** the singular-geometry LoRA paper and the symmetry-aware feature-learning paper in section B, the statistical and dynamical accounts of the same order-of-learning phenomenon.

### 2. The Dually Flat Geometry of Planning as Inference
Nikola Milosevic, Asaki Kataoka, Nicolas Hinrichs, Kenji Doya, Nico Scherf. arXiv:2609.04005 (cs.AI), 2026-09-03. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04005

- **The move:** The achievable visitation measures of a reinforcement-learning problem form a dually flat statistical manifold whose two affine charts, visitation probabilities and log-policies, are dual under conditional entropy, so planning-as-inference becomes a single natural-gradient step and the temporal-difference error becomes a marginal-utility estimate.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is genuine Amari-Chentsov dually flat structure doing real work in control rather than being named in passing, because the dual coordinates are the occupancy and the policy, and the duality is what lets planning generalize from linear rewards to nonlinear functionals of the visitation measure. It reframes the temporal-difference error geometrically and connects to the theoretical-neuroscience line on planning in the brain. It is cross-pollination with control that pays rent.
- **Cost:** 75 min, moderate-hard, needs dually flat and natural-gradient geometry and Markov-decision-process occupancy measures.
- **Pairs with:** the Diaconis-Ylvisaker conjugacy characterization in section A and the Pistone-Sempi manifold below, the other two faces of exponential-family duality this week.

### 3. Compositional Information Geometry of Probabilistic Programs
Jacek Karwowski, Frank Nielsen, Sam Staton. arXiv:2610.17886 (cs.PL), 2026-10-13. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17886

- **The move:** Build a contravariant lax monoidal functor that, for a probabilistic program X → Y, pulls back a possibly degenerate Riemannian metric on Y to X, specializing to the Fisher metric for standard models and to ordinary pullback for deterministic programs.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the most unusual-in-how-it-thinks paper of the week, because it makes information geometry compositional and intensional, so the geometry depends on how the program is written rather than only on the measures it realizes, and it then honestly bounds the gap to the non-compositional geometry of the realized measures with a three-level inequality against the Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao metric. Nielsen on the geometry side and Staton on the categorical-probability side is the cross-disciplinary pairing that produces a reframe rather than a decoration. It builds taste for when the presentation of a model, not just the model, carries geometric content.
- **Cost:** 2 hr, hard, needs monoidal-functor category theory, the Fisher metric, and probabilistic-programming semantics.
- **Pairs with:** Markov-category probability and the Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao and unbalanced-transport line.

### 4. Learning by Confusion Locates a Parametrization-Dependent Fisher Information Maximum
George Issa, Richard Scalettar. arXiv:2610.34769 (cond-mat.stat-mech), 2026-10-26. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34769

- **The move:** The machine-learning "learning by confusion" phase detector is shown to actually locate a maximum of the Fisher information, and the finite-size pseudocritical trajectory it reports depends on whether you grid the data in temperature, log-temperature, or inverse temperature.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It does two things the rubric rewards at once: it identifies what a popular unsupervised method is really measuring, a Fisher-information maximum, and it exposes an honest negative result, that the answer is parametrization-dependent and so partly an artifact of how you sampled the control axis. That lands it squarely in the reformulation-invariance conversation already in the log, now with a concrete criticality example. It is the kind of small, surprising result that changes how you read every confusion-based phase-detection plot.
- **Cost:** 45 min, moderate, needs Fisher information and basic finite-size-scaling and criticality.
- **Pairs with:** Mulder's partition-dependence in section A and the Takayama window-dependence in section C, the same representation-dependence pathology in three different fields.

### 5. An Infinite-Dimensional Geometric Structure on the Space of all the Probability Measures Equivalent to a Given One (timeless)
Giovanni Pistone, Carlo Sempi. Annals of Statistics 23(5):1543-1561, 1995. https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176324311

- **The move:** Construct a genuine Banach manifold, with exponential Orlicz-space charts, on the set of all strictly positive densities, giving nonparametric information geometry its foundational object with the exponential family as a chart rather than a model.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Almost every modern statistical-manifold paper silently assumes a finite-dimensional parametric model, and this is the paper that showed how to do the geometry nonparametrically. It is load-bearing for infinite-dimensional information geometry, the Orlicz-manifold and weak-information-geometry threads, and the nonparametric reading of this week's metric-geometry results, yet it stays quietly cited outside the specialist community. The construction is a climb through Orlicz spaces but it is written clearly enough to teach from.
- **Cost:** 2 hr, hard, needs Orlicz spaces and functional analysis.
- **Pairs with:** the dually flat planning paper above and the Čencov and Campbell characterizations already in the log, the finite-dimensional endpoints this generalizes.

## Threads

**Thread 1: The default contrast answers a question you did not ask.**
Papers: Target trial 1 (Takayama), Target trial 3 (vaccine waning), Bayesian 4 (Mulder), Information geometry 4 (Issa-Scalettar). Takayama shows a clone-censor-weight early-versus-late risk ratio reverses sign depending on the initiation windows, even when the true contrast is 1; Perényi, Janvin, and Stensrud show the naive waning comparison has no causal meaning and replace it with a test; Mulder shows equal prior probabilities make a constrained-hypothesis posterior depend on the partition, so a hypothesis can beat one that contains it; Issa and Scalettar show the confusion method's pseudocritical point moves with whether you grid in T, log T, or β. Four fields, one pathology: a convenient representation, a window, a partition, a temperature axis, silently sets the answer, and the repair in every case is to write down the estimand or the invariant first.

**Thread 2: Counting and ordering the learnable directions.**
Papers: Learning 2 (singular LoRA), Learning 3 (symmetry-aware feature learning), Information geometry 1 (Kobayashi-Dechant). Ferry, Monod, and Yano make the effective dimension of LoRA the count of low-rank directions made observable by the forward-backward covariance; Waber and colleagues recover teacher features one orbit at a time through a two-stage symmetry-breaking mechanism with a sharp polynomial separation; Kobayashi and Dechant bound the rate and fix the order in which parameters are learned with a Fisher-information-flow speed limit. The agreement is that learning happens direction by direction and the geometry counts which directions are alive. The tension is in the language: a singular-model log canonical threshold, an online-SGD information exponent, and a stochastic-thermodynamic speed limit are three dialects pointing at one clock.

**Thread 3: Exponential-family duality, from conjugacy to planning.**
Papers: Bayesian 5 (Diaconis-Ylvisaker), Information geometry 2 (dually flat planning), Information geometry 5 (Pistone-Sempi). Diaconis and Ylvisaker characterize conjugacy as linearity of the posterior mean of the natural parameter, the pseudo-sample reading of a prior; Milosevic and colleagues make planning a natural-gradient step between the occupancy and log-policy charts of a dually flat manifold; Pistone and Sempi build the nonparametric exponential-family manifold on which both the finite-dimensional conjugate story and the dual-connection machinery live. Read in sequence they show the natural-versus-expectation parameter pair is the same working object whether you are updating a conjugate posterior, planning a policy, or coordinatizing all positive densities at once.

**Thread 4: The stand-in is not the target, and the gap has a price.**
Papers: Learning 1 (benign landscapes), Learning 4 (surrogate trust), Information geometry 3 (compositional programs), Bayesian 1 (Li-Zadik). Wang, Díaz, and Jiang show a benign population landscape does not survive the passage to the finite-sample empirical objective; Ma and Zhao show a high-accuracy surrogate can rank the true optimum last and quantify the selection tax you pay for trusting it; Karwowski, Nielsen, and Staton show the geometry of a program as written differs from the geometry of the measures it realizes and bound that gap; Li and Zadik show the Bayes-estimation floor is exactly a functional inequality, the one case where the convenient analytic object and the estimation target coincide. The first three warn that the tractable stand-in and the real object come apart, and the last says when geometry lets them be two names for one thing.

## Synthesis

The week's recurring instinct is suspicion of the default. Mulder, Takayama, Perényi, and Issa and Scalettar independently catch a standard procedure returning a conclusion that flips when you re-slice the space it lives on, and in every case the fix is the same discipline: name the estimand, the invariant, or the quantity being maximized before you compute it. Against that negative mood sits a constructive one built almost entirely on exponential-family duality, where Diaconis and Ylvisaker, Milosevic and colleagues, and Pistone and Sempi show the natural-versus-expectation coordinate pair is what makes conjugate updating, policy planning, and nonparametric geometry one machine seen from three angles. The learning-theory and geometry papers add the dynamical counterpart, that learnability arrives direction by direction and that a singular log canonical threshold, an information exponent, and a Fisher-information-flow speed limit may be three clocks reading the same time. The clearest disagreement is about surrogates: Li and Zadik find a case where the convenient analytic object is exactly the estimation target, while Wang and colleagues, Ma and Zhao, and the compositional-geometry paper insist the tractable stand-in and the real object come apart and make you pay a measurable tax for pretending otherwise. The causal-inference cluster supplies the sharpest single arc, from Robins and Finkelstein inventing clone-censor-weighting to recover a hidden survival benefit, to Takayama showing its estimand can invert under an innocent window choice, to Austin finally telling you how to power the thing. Two questions worth sitting with. First, is the order-of-learning set by Kobayashi and Dechant's speed limit the same order set by a singular model's learning coefficient, and if so what is the shared geometric clock. Second, partition-dependence in Bayesian testing, window-dependence in target trial emulation, and parametrization-dependence in a phase detector look like one failure of reparametrization invariance wearing three costumes, and it is worth asking whether the invariance principle that would cure all three is the same object that decides which estimands an emulation can identify at all.

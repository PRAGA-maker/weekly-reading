## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. A mechanistic characterization of missingness at random
Maya B. Mathur, Willi Zhang, Shaun Seaman, Rohit Bhattacharya, Ilya Shpitser. arXiv:2611.22022 (stat.ME), 2026-11-17. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22022

**The move.** It translates MAR from a statement about abstract conditional independences into a statement about paths in a DAG whose edges switch off when a variable goes missing, and then uses that to prove you should sometimes drop complete auxiliaries from your imputation model.

**Why it is worth your time.** The reframe, "context-specific causal irrelevance," is the load-bearing idea: MAR holds iff the modified graph has no MAR-violating path, and without CSCI this collapses to the familiar d-separation condition. The practical payload is a genuine surprise against standard advice, that adding a complete auxiliary like income can create an MAR violation that was not there before, demonstrated on the Stockholm Public Health Cohort. It builds the muscle of checking whether an assumption you invoke is actually implied by the mechanism you believe.

**Cost.** 50 min, moderate, needs comfort with causal DAGs and the ID literature.

**Pairs with.** The interventional-score-geometry and causal-estimand threads from earlier weeks, and with any workflow where multiple imputation is treated as a black box.

### 2. The random analyst
Gérard Biau. arXiv:2611.21081 (math.ST), 2026-11-16. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21081

**The move.** It shows that searching over many candidate analyses can make your reported estimate more replicable across independent studies while converging to the wrong number, and that the size of this displacement is governed by the Gaussian width of the set of distinguishable analyses, not by how many analyses you nominally tried.

**Why it is worth your time.** This is the garden of forking paths recast as a concentration-of-measure problem, with metric entropy and generic chaining doing the real work. The sharp and uncomfortable consequence is that numerical agreement between replications is not evidence of a stable analytic path or of proximity to the truth. The selection-induced pseudotruth persists when squared Gaussian width is of the same order as sample size, which is exactly the regime of modern high-dimensional pipelines.

**Cost.** 45 min, moderate to hard, needs empirical-process geometry.

**Pairs with.** The CCW window-dependence result in the target trial section, which is the same disease in a different host: a well-defined-looking procedure returning a systematically displaced answer.

### 3. Asymptotics of Gibbs Posterior Measures on Infinite-Dimensional Hypothesis Spaces
Terje Mildner. arXiv:2611.23214 (math.ST), 2026-11-17. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23214

**The move.** It proves almost-sure contraction rates for Gibbs and fractional-Bayes posteriors in full generality, dependent data, nonadditive and time-varying losses, misspecification, unattainable minimizers, by abandoning the usual testing and variational machinery and instead pushing Gibbs measures through Lorentz spaces, real interpolation, and Laplace transforms.

**Why it is worth your time.** The interest is almost entirely in the method of proof. Testing-based contraction theory breaks in this generality, and the paper shows an interpolation-theoretic route that does not need it, which is the kind of technique that gets reused. It also closes a real gap for generalized and misspecified Bayes, where "what does the posterior concentrate on" has been murky.

**Cost.** 90 min, hard, needs functional analysis and empirical Bayes asymptotics.

**Pairs with.** The generalized-Bayes line (when is generalized Bayes Bayesian, predictively oriented posteriors) and this week's canonical-divergence M-estimator paper in information geometry.

### 4. Species sampling priors through exact augmentation
Ramsés H. Mena, Christos Merkatas, Theodoros Nicoleris, Carlos E. Rodríguez. arXiv:2611.13323 (math.ST), 2026-11-09. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13323

**The move.** Every proper species sampling process admits an exact two-stage finite representation: a finite index picks a set of atoms, then a draw is made from explicitly reweighted probabilities, which lets you build nonparametric priors from an index distribution and finite allocation probabilities instead of specifying infinite weights.

**Why it is worth your time.** This inverts the usual BNP construction. Rather than start from a stick-breaking or CRM recipe and derive the predictive law, you start from the finite index law you can reason about and recover the infinite-dimensional object exactly, with total-variation control over truncation. It is a clean example of making an infinite-dimensional prior tractable by finding the right finite shadow, and it yields new heavy-tailed and multimodal SSP families with explicit partition and predictive laws.

**Cost.** 60 min, moderate, needs familiarity with Dirichlet processes and size-biased permutation.

**Pairs with.** Diaconis and Ylvisaker on conjugacy, and the Bregman exponential-family geometry paper this week, both about finding the parametrization in which an object becomes simple.

### 5. Statistical Theory: The Prequential Approach (classic)
A. P. Dawid. Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series A, 147(2):278-292, 1984. https://doi.org/10.2307/2981683

**The move.** Judge a probability forecaster only by the sequence of forecasts it actually issued against the outcomes that actually occurred, and most of the apparatus of calibration, model assessment, and even parameter estimation follows from that one prequential commitment.

**Why it is worth your time.** This is the under-cited taproot of a large amount of current work: conformal prediction, martingale posteriors, test-by-betting, and the predictively-oriented-posterior line all live downstream of Dawid's prequential principle and his calibration criterion. Reading it makes clear how much of modern "predictive Bayes" is a rediscovery, and the writing is a model of stating a principle plainly and following it to its consequences. The weak prequential principle, that assessment should depend on the model only through its forecasts, is exactly the stance several of this week's papers adopt without naming it.

**Cost.** 70 min, moderate, a discussion paper so read the rejoinder too.

**Pairs with.** The predictive-discrepancy objective-Bayes work and The random analyst above, and with the betting and anytime-valid inference literature.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. The Manifold-Index Model: provably and efficiently avoiding the statistical and computational curse of dimensionality
Yantao Wu, Mauro Maggioni. arXiv:2611.20275 (stat.ML), 2026-11-15. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20275

**The move.** When predictors fill a tube around an unknown m-dimensional manifold and the response depends only on the nearest-point projection, you can estimate tangent planes by multiscale PCA of points with similar responses and get both the statistical rate n^{-4/(m+4)} and polynomial computation, so the curse is beaten on both axes at once.

**Why it is worth your time.** Most manifold-regression results beat the statistical curse and quietly pay for it in computation, or vice versa. The mechanism here is specific: neighborhoods narrow tangentially while keeping full normal fibers, and tangent planes are refined by pooling outer products of local slopes, which is what makes the estimator both consistent at the intrinsic rate and tractable. This is a good template for what a real joint statistical-computational guarantee looks like.

**Cost.** 75 min, hard, needs geometric measure theory basics (reach, projections) and nonparametric rates.

**Pairs with.** Single-index and multi-index model literature, and the diffusion-on-manifolds and hitting-probability examples at the end connect to dynamical-systems work.

### 2. High-dimensional limit theorems for adaptive variants of SGD I: Ballistic limits
Aukosh Jagannath, Varnan Sarangian. arXiv:2611.19059 (stat.ML), 2026-11-14. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19059

**The move.** The non-Markovianity that Adam and Muon introduce through exponential moving averages and preconditioning can be removed by the method of averaging, giving a clean Markovian effective dynamics in high dimensions, and from it an infinity-Laplacian analogue that sharply characterizes the edge of stability.

**Why it is worth your time.** This is the first treatment I have seen that puts Adam and Muon on the same high-dimensional-dynamics footing as plain SGD and then makes quantitative algorithm-to-algorithm comparisons. The cross-over phenomenon, online SGD generalizing better than Adam early before the curves cross and Adam takes over, is the sort of concrete prediction this machinery should produce and usually does not. The edge-of-stability characterization via an infinity Laplacian is a genuinely new object.

**Cost.** 90 min, hard, needs SDE limits of SGD and some PDE vocabulary.

**Pairs with.** The attention-indexed dynamics paper below and the Seung-Sompolinsky-Tishby classic, all three being the statistical physics of learning dynamics.

### 3. High-Dimensional Learning Dynamics of Attention-Indexed Models
Yizhou Xu, Margarita Sagitova, Lenka Zdeborová, Florent Krzakala. arXiv:2609.03858 (cs.LG), 2026-09-03. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03858

**The move.** For a broad attention-indexed model, the population landscape is captured by finitely many trace order parameters while online SGD obeys an infinite hierarchy of matrix moments that truncates exponentially well, and in that limit the attention parametrization itself acts as an implicit bias: direct optimization of S gets stuck, tied S=WW^T breaks symmetry automatically and recovers in Theta(d^2 log d) samples.

**Why it is worth your time.** This is the sharpest statement I have seen that how you parametrize attention, not just the loss, selects what gets learned, with an explicit fast-slow mechanism for the untied case. It is a mechanism paper, not a bound, and it isolates why a modeling choice everyone makes (tied projections) has a learning-theoretic consequence. The order-parameter reduction is also a clean instance of extensive-rank dynamics being finite-dimensional after all.

**Cost.** 80 min, hard, needs statistical-physics order parameters and online-SGD analysis.

**Pairs with.** Phase-transitions-in-attention and copy-head-emergence work from earlier weeks, and the ballistic-SGD paper above.

### 4. A Distribution-Free Separation Between Learned and Fixed Features
Daniel Barzilai. arXiv:2610.25335 (cs.LG), 2026-10-18. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25335

**The move.** It settles the COLT open question of Feldman, Kamath, and Srebro by constructing hypothesis classes that a two-layer ReLU network learns efficiently under any input distribution while no fixed feature map of polynomial dimension can represent them, so the advantage of learned over fixed features survives the removal of distributional assumptions.

**Why it is worth your time.** Every prior separation between feature learning and kernels leaned on a specific input distribution, which always left open whether the gap was an artifact of that choice. Removing the distribution is the whole point, and the construction is the rare learning-theory result where the pathology is exactly the thing being characterized rather than an obstacle. This is the clean answer to "do learned features really help, distribution-free," and the answer is yes.

**Cost.** 60 min, hard, needs dimension-complexity and kernel lower-bound machinery.

**Pairs with.** The random-features and kernel-vs-deep separation literature, and the distribution-free-separation-between-learned-and-fixed-features question in representation theory.

### 5. Statistical mechanics of learning from examples (classic)
H. S. Seung, H. Sompolinsky, N. Tishby. Physical Review A, 45:6056-6091, 1992. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.45.6056

**The move.** Treat the training set as quenched disorder and the weights as thermal degrees of freedom, and learning curves, generalization, and the transitions between memorization and generalization fall out of a free-energy calculation.

**Why it is worth your time.** This is the paper the extensive-width, order-parameter, and phase-transition lines in this list are all descended from, and reading the original makes the modern replica and dynamical-mean-field results legible as refinements rather than novelties. It is where the idea that generalization has sharp thresholds, and that they are computable, is laid out with unusual clarity. If you want to understand why three of this week's learning-theory picks keep saying "order parameter," start here.

**Cost.** 2 h, hard, needs statistical mechanics, but skimmable for the phenomenology.

**Pairs with.** Ballistic-SGD and attention-indexed dynamics above, and the singular-learning and phase-transition threads.

## C. Target trial emulation

*(Three recent methodological papers plus two foundational classics. The recent PubMed output this period is dominated by applied clinical emulations with little new methodology, so this domain does not force a fifth recent paper.)*

### 1. Structural Limitations of Early-Late Treatment Comparisons Under Target Trial Emulation: A Simulation Study
Atsushi Takayama, Shiro Tanaka, Koji Kawakami. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag208

**The move.** Even under ideal implementation, clone-censor-weighting for an early-versus-late initiation contrast can return risk ratios that vary with, and reverse direction across, the arbitrary choice of initiation window, in settings where the true marginal causal contrast is exactly 1 by construction.

**Why it is worth your time.** CCW for early-versus-late comparisons is widely treated as the safe, immortal-time-free default. This paper shows the window-defined strategy contrast is partly an artifact of risk accumulated before treatment under the chosen windows, and that the artifact is not explained by weight instability or poor overlap, the usual suspects. It is a structural negative result that should change how you read any early-versus-late emulation, including your own.

**Cost.** 40 min, moderate, needs CCW and estimand definitions.

**Pairs with.** The random analyst in the Bayesian section, same failure mode, a clean procedure yielding a systematically displaced answer, and the landmark-analysis and immortal-time literature.

### 2. What phases of the drug development framework can epidemiological studies emulate?
Stephen Burgess, Benjamin Woolf, Emily Bassett, David Robertson, Sofía Villar, Jeremy A. Labrecque. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag194

**The move.** It maps emulations onto trial phases: a late-phase target trial aims at a directly actionable estimand, while a natural experiment or quasi-random comparison is better understood as an early-phase target trial that buys validity by giving up direct applicability.

**Why it is worth your time.** This is the "where do target trials come from" register applied to a question people usually skip: not whether you emulated a trial well, but which kind of trial is even emulable from your data. The early-phase framing legitimizes Mendelian randomization and instrument-style comparisons as proof-of-concept rather than policy estimands, and it gives language for the validity-versus-applicability trade that most papers leave implicit. Useful for deciding what claim you are actually entitled to make.

**Cost.** 30 min, easy to moderate, conceptual.

**Pairs with.** Mendelian randomization and natural-experiment identification work, and the ideal-trial and estimand-anatomy papers from earlier weeks.

### 3. Informing sample size and power calculations for clone-censor-weight analyses: a Monte Carlo simulation study of the variance inflation factor relative to the underlying target trial
Peter C. Austin. Journal of Clinical Epidemiology, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112576

**The move.** It quantifies the variance inflation factor of a CCW analysis relative to the RCT it emulates, and finds the counterintuitive result that with low-to-moderate confounding and around ten baseline covariates, CCW can be more precise than the underlying trial, with the VIF rising as covariate count grows.

**Why it is worth your time.** Power calculation for CCW has been a blank spot because the censoring weights are only known at analysis time, so people either skip it or borrow the RCT number. This gives a concrete, reusable VIF with public R code, and the finding that cloning can buy precision rather than only cost it is the kind of fact that reorders design intuition. It teaches the right mental model for where CCW's variance actually comes from.

**Cost.** 35 min, moderate, needs IPCW and basic power calculation.

**Pairs with.** The CCW structural-limitations paper above, the design counterpart to its identification critique.

### 4. Specifying a target trial prevents immortal time bias and other self-inflicted injuries in observational analyses (classic)
Miguel A. Hernán, Brian C. Sauer, Sonia Hernández-Díaz, Robert Platt, Ian Shrier. Journal of Clinical Epidemiology, 79:70-75, 2016. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2016.04.014

**The move.** Most biases in observational comparative-effectiveness analyses are self-inflicted, created by failing to align eligibility, treatment assignment, and start of follow-up at a single time zero, and naming the target trial forces that alignment.

**Why it is worth your time.** This is the short, sharp statement of the whole program, and it is the paper the two recent CCW papers above are arguing within. Rereading it next to the structural-limitations result is instructive: the 2016 paper says alignment prevents immortal time bias, and the 2026 paper shows alignment via CCW is necessary but not sufficient for a well-defined estimand. The framing of bias as self-inflicted, and therefore preventable by design rather than fixable by analysis, is the durable idea.

**Cost.** 25 min, easy, foundational.

**Pairs with.** Everything in this section, and the hormone-therapy emulation below as its applied origin.

### 5. Observational studies analyzed like randomized experiments: an application to postmenopausal hormone therapy and coronary heart disease (classic)
Miguel A. Hernán, Alvaro Alonso, Roger Logan, Francine Grodstein, Karin B. Michels, Walter C. Willett, Joann E. Manson, James M. Robins. Epidemiology, 19(6):766-779, 2008. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0b013e3181875e61

**The move.** By conceptualizing the Nurses' Health Study as a sequence of trials and emulating the Women's Health Initiative's design and intention-to-treat analysis, it reconciles the notorious hormone-therapy discrepancy as an artifact of differing time-since-menopause distributions and follow-up length, not a contradiction between observational and randomized evidence.

**Why it is worth your time.** This is the applied case that made target trial emulation credible, the clean example where reframing the question changed the answer and dissolved a decade-long controversy. The sequence-of-trials device and the move from intention-to-treat to adherence-adjusted inverse-probability-weighted effects are both here in their first serious outing. It is the proof that the framework earns its keep on a real, consequential disagreement.

**Cost.** 50 min, moderate, needs IPW and ITT-versus-per-protocol distinctions.

**Pairs with.** The self-inflicted-injuries paper above, and the effect-timing and eligibility-alignment literature.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. How Inefficient Is Natural Gradient Descent? From Exact Optimality to Theta(sqrt(log d)) Divergence
Guni Sharon, Alan Kuhnle. arXiv:2610.07228 (stat.ML), 2026-10-05. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07228

**The move.** Idealized natural gradient descent on the forward-KL objective follows the mixture geodesic, not the shorter Fisher-Rao geodesic, and the resulting inefficiency ratio is exactly 1 only for quadratic-potential families, while for products of scale families it grows as Theta(sqrt(log d)), unbounded in dimension.

**Why it is worth your time.** This is the reframe that natural gradient is not geometrically optimal in the way it is usually sold: it is optimal for the KL geometry it descends, but that is the mixture connection, and the gap to the true Fisher-Rao shortest path is real and dimension-growing. The tensor criterion that pins down the R=1 case (quadratic potential, that is, essentially fixed-covariance Gaussians) is clean and the three-regime classification is the kind of result that corrects a widely held intuition. The structure does real work; remove the dual-connection geometry and there is no statement left.

**Cost.** 55 min, moderate to hard, needs dually flat geometry and geodesics.

**Pairs with.** The ballistic-SGD paper in learning theory (preconditioned dynamics) and the quantum-natural-gradient audit from the broader optimization-geometry literature.

### 2. Characterizing M-estimators using canonical divergences from information geometry
Paul Navas. arXiv:2611.18141 (math.ST), 2026-11-13. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18141

**The move.** Define the residual of an M-estimator as the inverse exponential map of an affine connection; its integral along geodesics is exactly the Ay-Amari canonical divergence, and a geometric Osband's principle then decides which losses each connection admits, yielding the sharp fact that for the curved alpha-connections with alpha not in {-1,0,1} no consistent loss for the Frechet mean exists and only the method of moments survives.

**Why it is worth your time.** This connects elicitability and scoring-rule theory, which lives in the frequentist forecasting world, to the dual-connection geometry of the simplex, and the connection pays rent: it sharpens Ay and Amari's integrability claim by showing the residual is integrable only at alpha=0. The result that whole families of functionals have no strictly consistent loss, only moment conditions, is a genuine negative result with teeth for anyone designing calibration or compositional-forecast objectives. Beautifully organized.

**Cost.** 70 min, hard, needs affine connections, exponential maps, and elicitability basics.

**Pairs with.** The Gibbs-posterior and Dawid prequential picks (loss-based updating and forecast evaluation), and the Bregman exponential-family paper below.

### 3. Compositional Information Geometry of Probabilistic Programs
Jacek Karwowski, Frank Nielsen, Sam Staton. arXiv:2610.17886 (cs.PL), 2026-10-13. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17886

**The move.** It builds a contravariant lax monoidal functor that, for a probabilistic program of type X to Y, pulls a Riemannian metric on Y back to X, specializing to the Fisher metric for standard statistical models and to ordinary pullback for deterministic programs, so a program carries an intrinsic geometry determined by its syntax.

**Why it is worth your time.** This is the unusual one. Information geometry is almost always done on a fixed statistical model; here the object with geometry is the program, and the compositional structure means the geometry of a composite is determined by its parts. The three-level inequality relating the score-velocity metric of the program, its output-conditioned metric, and the Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao metric of the realized family is the technical heart, and it quantifies exactly how much the syntactic presentation can differ from the geometry of what the program actually computes. A real cross-domain import, category theory and PPL semantics into information geometry, that does load-bearing work.

**Cost.** 80 min, hard, needs categorical semantics and a tolerance for functorial language.

**Pairs with.** The dually-flat and canonical-divergence papers here, and the probabilistic-programming and semantics literature.

### 4. Bregman geometry in regular exponential families: dual representations and relative smoothness
Mame Diarra Fall, Éric Barat, Nicolas Dobigeon. arXiv:2611.18480 (stat.ML), 2026-11-13. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18480

**The move.** It systematically writes regular exponential-family log-densities as ordinary Bregman divergences in dual coordinate systems, shows conjugate priors inherit the same dual-Bregman form and preserve conjugacy, and uses these dual geometries as reference measures to establish sharp relative-smoothness bounds for the primal representation.

**Why it is worth your time.** The exponential-family-as-Bregman-divergence fact is old, but it is usually left as a remark; this paper turns it into a working characterization in the expectation parametrization and extracts something practical, relative-smoothness constants that are sharp in the scalar and separable cases and that justify reference geometries for mirror-descent-style algorithms. The Poisson worked example quietly explains several scattered results in the literature as instances of one geometry. Good clean-derivation reading.

**Cost.** 55 min, moderate, needs convex duality and Bregman divergences.

**Pairs with.** Diaconis and Ylvisaker on conjugacy, the species-sampling prior construction, and relative-smoothness results in first-order optimization.

### 5. An Infinite-Dimensional Geometric Structure on the Space of all the Probability Measures Equivalent to a Given One (classic)
Giovanni Pistone, Carlo Sempi. The Annals of Statistics, 23(5):1543-1561, 1995. https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176324311

**The move.** It makes the set of all probability measures equivalent to a reference measure into a genuine Banach manifold, modeled on an Orlicz space built from exponentially integrable functions, so that nonparametric families get a bona fide differential structure rather than a finite-dimensional analogy.

**Why it is worth your time.** This is the under-cited foundation of nonparametric information geometry, and it is the reason phrases like "nonparametric exponential manifold" and "statistical manifold in infinite dimensions" are not hand-waving. The choice of Orlicz space is the whole trick: ordinary L^p is too small to hold the exponential families you want, and this paper identifies the right ambient space. Anyone working with infinite-dimensional statistical manifolds, Stein geometry, or tractable pseudo-metrics on exponential manifolds is standing on this.

**Cost.** 90 min, hard, needs functional analysis (Orlicz spaces, Banach manifolds).

**Pairs with.** The Gibbs-posterior-on-infinite-dimensional-spaces paper in the Bayesian section, and the nonparametric-exponential-manifold and Stein-geometry threads from earlier weeks.

## Threads

**Thread 1: a well-defined procedure can converge to the wrong number.** The random analyst (Bayesian), the CCW structural-limitations paper (target trial), and the natural-gradient inefficiency result (information geometry) are three versions of one phenomenon. Biau shows search makes estimates replicable yet displaced; Takayama et al. show CCW makes an early-versus-late contrast look identified yet window-dependent and sign-unstable; Sharon and Kuhnle show natural gradient faithfully follows a geodesic that is simply the wrong, longer one. In all three the machinery runs correctly and the output is systematically off because the procedure optimizes a proxy (distinguishable-analysis geometry, window-defined strategy, mixture connection) that diverges from the target. The tension worth holding: replicability, apparent identification, and geometric optimality are each individually reassuring and jointly insufficient.

**Thread 2: find the finite shadow, or the right ambient space.** Species sampling priors through exact augmentation (Bayesian), the Manifold-Index Model (learning theory), the attention-indexed order-parameter reduction (learning theory), and Pistone-Sempi (information geometry) are all about replacing an intractable infinite object with the correct finite or Banach handle. Mena et al. find the finite index law behind an infinite SSP; Wu and Maggioni find the intrinsic m-dimensional structure inside D dimensions; Xu et al. collapse an infinite moment hierarchy to finitely many order parameters; Pistone and Sempi find the Orlicz space in which infinite-dimensional families actually live. The throughline is that tractability comes from identifying the right coordinates, not from approximation.

**Thread 3: the statistical physics of learning dynamics, old and new.** Seung-Sompolinsky-Tishby (classic), the ballistic limits of adaptive SGD, and the attention-indexed dynamics paper form a direct lineage. The 1992 paper computes learning curves as free energies; the 2026 papers compute effective dynamics for Adam and Muon and for attention training, and both recover phase-transition-like phenomena (edge of stability via an infinity Laplacian, symmetry-breaking recovery at Theta(d^2 log d)). The disagreement to notice: the classic treats generalization thresholds as equilibrium properties, while the adaptive-SGD paper insists the interesting structure is in the non-equilibrium trajectory, including a cross-over where the ranking of two optimizers flips mid-training.

**Thread 4: geometry as the arbiter of what loss or estimator is admissible.** The canonical-divergence M-estimator paper and the Bregman exponential-family paper (information geometry), read with Dawid's prequential principle and the Gibbs-posterior contraction result (Bayesian), say that the choice of connection or divergence is not cosmetic, it determines which losses yield consistent estimators and which forecasts can be scored. Navas shows curved alpha-connections admit no consistent loss for most functionals; Fall et al. show the dual-Bregman geometry fixes the reference measure for relative smoothness; Dawid shows assessment should depend on the model only through its forecasts. Together they push toward treating the loss or scoring rule as a geometric commitment rather than a free hyperparameter.

## Synthesis

The strongest current in this week's list is distrust of procedures that look correct from the inside. Three papers across three domains (The random analyst, CCW structural limitations, natural-gradient inefficiency) independently show that replicability, identification, and geometric optimality can each certify an answer that is systematically wrong, because each certifies faithfulness to a proxy rather than proximity to the target. That is a useful sharpening of a worry that is usually vague: the problem is not noise or finite samples, it is that the proxy and the target come apart in a structured, dimension-growing, window-dependent way. Set against this, a second group of papers is quietly optimistic about tractability, arguing that infinite and intractable objects collapse to finite or Banach handles once you find the right coordinates, whether that is the finite index behind a species sampling prior, the intrinsic manifold inside high-dimensional predictors, or the Orlicz space under nonparametric families. The two currents are not in conflict so much as in sequence: first find the right coordinates, then check that optimizing in them actually tracks what you care about.

The learning-theory picks and their 1992 ancestor suggest the field is re-deriving statistical mechanics with better tools and a new insistence that the trajectory, not just the equilibrium, carries the physics. The information-geometry picks make the complementary claim that the choice of connection or divergence is a substantive commitment that decides which estimators and losses are even admissible, which is the same message Dawid delivered in 1984 about forecasts. Where they disagree is on whether geometry is prescriptive or merely descriptive: the M-estimator paper treats the connection as fixing what is consistent, while the natural-gradient paper treats a particular connection as a mistake to be corrected toward Fisher-Rao.

Two questions worth sitting with. First, the proxy-target gap in Thread 1 is quantified by a complexity measure in each case (Gaussian width, window-induced pre-treatment risk, the inefficiency ratio); is there a single geometric quantity that unifies them, and if so does it connect to the control-energy and criticality language from the dynamical-systems side of your work. Second, if learning dynamics really are best read as non-equilibrium trajectories with cross-overs and broken conservation laws, then the equilibrium intuitions borrowed from singular learning theory and from aging-as-a-dynamical-system may be describing only the endpoints; the Eyring-Kramers-for-singular-wells line and the ballistic-SGD line are both reaching for the transition structure between them, and that is where the next real gems likely are.

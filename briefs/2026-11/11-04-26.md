# Weekly Reading List, 11-04-26

This week leans on reframes and negative results. The spine is a single question asked four ways: when is the object you are estimating actually well defined, and what geometry is secretly governing it. Four recent arXiv or journal picks plus one under-cited classic per domain. Network was fine; arXiv API, PubMed, and Crossref all reachable, and every finalist was verified against its abstract or bibliographic record before it went in.

## A. Bayesian statistics

### A Bayesian-Geometric Reading of Classical Model-Based Optimal Design
Mohamed Tarek. arXiv:2611.04450, 2026-11-03. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04450
- The move: the classical design criteria (D, T, L, C, A optimality) are not a grab bag of functions of the Fisher matrix; each is the leading term of an expected prior-to-posterior divergence, with Fisher information entering only through Bernstein von Mises.
- Why it is worth your time: the standard motivation routes everything through Cramer Rao and leaves you with five named scalars and no sense of what they measure. Deriving D optimality as expected KL from posterior to prior and T optimality as the reverse KL tells you exactly which information each criterion is buying, and makes the whole catalog one object. This is the kind of reframe that changes how you read a design paper, because it collapses a taxonomy into a principle. Solo preprint, not yet refereed, so trust the derivation over the pedigree.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate, needs Bernstein von Mises and divergence asymptotics.
- Pairs with: the information geometry picks below, especially Amari and the NGD inefficiency paper, since all three turn a Fisher-matrix computation into a statement about divergence geometry.

### Optimal Priors for Betting Tests with Bounded Payoffs: An Exact Expected-Time Characterization
Francesco Orabona. arXiv:2610.25821 (v2), 2026-10-19. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25821
- The move: for testing by betting with bounded scalar payoffs, there is a sharp closed form for expected rejection time that holds uniformly over all bounded alternatives, which turns prior choice from folklore into a solved optimization.
- Why it is worth your time: many priors in a universal portfolio test hit the optimal rate at a fixed alternative, so the usual rate analysis cannot tell them apart. The interesting action is near the null, where those priors behave very differently, and this paper is what pins that behavior down. It is a clean answer to a question the betting literature kept waving at, from an author who writes without hype.
- Cost: 1 hr, moderate to hard, needs e-processes and universal portfolios.
- Pairs with: the concentration game betting line already in the log (2608.18061), and the Blackwell Dubins merging result below, which is the limiting statement about where the wealth process is headed.

### Inconsistency between Frequentist and Bayesian inference, approximation of improper priors and permissible priors
Pierre Druilhet, Erwan Saint Loubert Bié. arXiv:2610.26422, 2026-10-19. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26422
- The move: approximating an improper prior by a sequence of proper priors can give a posterior limit that depends on which compact sets you used, so the improper posterior is not the well defined object objective Bayes treats it as.
- Why it is worth your time: posterior propriety is the usual licence for improper priors, and this shows propriety is not enough for coherence. The authors introduce monotone q-vague convergence and expected-KL and expected-TV notions, then exhibit continuous and discrete cases where the limit genuinely moves with the approximating compacts. It exposes a hidden assumption in a routine practice and ties it to the older permissible-prior and frequentist-coherence question.
- Cost: 1 hr, moderate, measure-theoretic but the counterexamples are concrete.
- Pairs with: the deep Gaussian process identifiability paper below, the other case this week where a Bayesian object you assumed was pinned down turns out not to be.

### Canonical Layer Inference for Deep Gaussian Processes via Symmetry-Breaking Augmentation
Yichen Zhu, Hengrui Luo. arXiv:2610.23842, 2026-10-16. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23842
- The move: deep GP intermediate layers carry an adjacent-layer symmetry, so the posterior need not concentrate on any prescribed intermediate representation even when the composed regression map is perfectly estimable, and a fractional-posterior augmentation that penalizes identity-deviation breaks the symmetry to recover a canonical layer.
- Why it is worth your time: people read intermediate layers of a deep GP as if they meant something, and this says the inferential object is symmetry-degenerate and can smear across decompositions regardless of sample size. The construction that fixes it is as instructive as the obstruction, because it shows the symmetry breaking has to be non-saturating to work. The general lesson is about when compositional latent representations are identifiable at all.
- Cost: 1 hr, moderate to hard, needs GP priors and posterior contraction.
- Pairs with: Singular Geometry of Low-Rank Adaptation in the learning-theory section, which is the same non-identifiability story told through singular learning theory rather than GP priors.

### Merging of Opinions with Increasing Information
David Blackwell, Lester Dubins. The Annals of Mathematical Statistics 33(3):882-886, 1962. https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177704456
- The move: if two priors are mutually absolutely continuous, their predictive distributions merge almost surely as information accrues, and the merging is uniform over all future events, not just pointwise.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the clean theorem under the slogan that data wash out the prior, and it is sharper than the slogan. Merging is in the sup-over-events metric and it fails exactly when absolute continuity fails, which is the doorway to the Diaconis Freedman inconsistency world. Most readers know the result exists and have never read the four-page proof, which is a mistake, because the proof is where the absolute-continuity condition earns its role.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate, classic measure-theoretic probability, very readable.
- Pairs with: the Orabona betting paper above, and with the improper-prior paper, which is a modern case where the absolute-continuity bookkeeping that Blackwell and Dubins made load bearing quietly breaks.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### Information-Dense Synthesis for Molecular Discovery
Kasper K. Jakobsen, Eli N. Weinstein. arXiv:2610.08495, 2026-10-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08495
- The move: treat molecular search as a measurement-complexity problem, design and assay complex mixtures as pools rather than one molecule at a time, then deconvolute the molecule-to-activity map, and optimize the synthesis itself to encode maximal information.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the rare paper that fills the search-over-a-chemical-latent-space slot while actually being an information and sample-complexity result rather than another model. The spiky claim is that pooled, algorithmically controlled stochastic synthesis provably cuts the number of experiments to find rare-property molecules, in exactly the sparse regime where existing design-make-test loops barely beat random. It builds the muscle of reading experimental design as channel coding.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate, needs information theory and experimental-design intuition, light chemistry.
- Pairs with: the target trial power paper below, since both ask how much information a given design extracts relative to the ideal, and with Bayesian experimental design.

### Beyond Multi-Index Models: Learning Intersections of Degree-2 PTFs
Ilias Diakonikolas, Daniel M. Kane, Mingchen Ma, Lisheng Ren, Trung Tran. arXiv:2611.05114, 2026-11-03. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05114
- The move: the first PAC algorithm for intersections of k degree-two polynomial threshold functions under Gaussian marginals whose dimension exponent does not depend on the accuracy, matched by an SQ lower bound.
- Why it is worth your time: the previous polynomial-regression route paid a dimension exponent that blew up with 1/eps, and pulling eps out of the exponent even at k equal to two is a qualitative change in what counts as tractable. The matching statistical-query lower bound makes it a two-sided result, which is what separates a real complexity statement from one more upper bound. Diakonikolas and Kane write crisply and state their assumptions plainly.
- Cost: 45 min, hard, needs PAC learning and SQ lower-bound machinery.
- Pairs with: the statistical-mechanics classic below, which is where the phase-transition intuition behind these sharp thresholds comes from.

### Singular Geometry of Low-Rank Adaptation
Kamillo Ferry, Anthea Monod, Keisuke Yano. arXiv:2610.32854, 2026-10-23. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32854
- The move: LoRA is a singular statistical model because its low-rank factorization is non-identifiable, and its learning complexity is governed not by parameter count but by a real log canonical threshold that depends on the parameterization and the pretrained network through a forward-backward covariance of activations and backprop sensitivities.
- Why it is worth your time: this takes one of the most-used objects in practice and reads its degeneracy as the thing that sets its effective complexity, which is exactly the singular-learning claim that non-identifiability is informative rather than a nuisance. The RLCT computation gives a concrete handle on why LoRA generalizes the way it does, tied to quantities you can actually form from a trained network. It is a fresh and teachable instance of singular learning theory hitting a regime that genuinely arises.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate to hard, needs RLCT and singular learning basics.
- Pairs with: the deep GP identifiability paper above, the Bayesian mirror of the same non-identifiability, and the Amari classic, since both read complexity off a degenerate geometry.

### Optimal Explicit and Implicit Regularization Strengths in High-Dimensional Continual Linear Regression
Gilad Karpel, Daniel Soudry. arXiv:2610.20804, 2026-10-14. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20804
- The move: a closed-form generalization loss for fixed-step gradient descent across a sequence of tasks, showing that the implicit regularization of the step size is asymptotically equivalent to optimally tuned L2, with the optimal fixed step decaying like log T over T in the number of tasks.
- Why it is worth your time: implicit equals explicit claims are usually asserted through a bound, and here there is an exact expression, so the mechanism is visible and the prediction is falsifiable. The continual-learning setting makes the step-size-as-regularizer story concrete, and the log T over T scaling is the kind of sharp, testable statement you can carry into an experiment. Clean Soudry-group craft.
- Cost: 35 min, moderate, high-dimensional linear regression asymptotics.
- Pairs with: the NGD inefficiency paper in the geometry section, another case where the step and the metric you optimize in decide the effective regularization.

### Statistical mechanics of learning from examples
H. S. Seung, H. Sompolinsky, N. Tishby. Physical Review A 45:6056-6091, 1992. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.45.6056
- The move: the foundational replica treatment of generalization, computing learning curves and exhibiting genuine phase transitions in learning as a function of the ratio of examples to parameters, in the thermodynamic limit.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the ancestor of every DMFT and statistical-physics-of-learning paper you read now, and it is where the order-parameter and free-energy view of generalization starts. It makes the case, still the cleanest, that learning has first-order and continuous transitions, which is the intuition behind this week's sharp sample-complexity thresholds. The log already carries Saad and Solla on online soft-committee dynamics; this is the complementary equilibrium landmark.
- Cost: 2 to 3 hr, hard, replica method and statistical mechanics. A sit-down read.
- Pairs with: the degree-two PTF complexity paper above and the lattice field theory geometry paper below, both of which live on the same distinguishability and phase-transition axis.

## C. Target trial emulation

### Comparing causal estimands from sequential nested versus single point target trials: a simulation study
Catherine Wiener, Chase D. Latour, Kathleen E. Hurwitz, Xiaojuan Li, Catherine R. Lesko, Alexander Breskin, M. Alan Brookhart. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026-09-25. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag227
- The move: sequential nested and single-point emulations silently target different estimands, and they diverge exactly when the treatment effect is modified by a time-varying severity, because the sequential design mixes person-time across disease stages instead of anchoring to one time zero.
- Why it is worth your time: this is sensitivity to the target-trial choice done carefully, with a Monte Carlo study that shows both a calendar-indexed and a decision-point-indexed sequential design match the single-point trial under no effect modification and split from it under severity-modified effects, even after confounding adjustment. The ATT suffers more than the ATE because treated indexes concentrate in high severity, and standardizing to the first-visit severity distribution removes the bias at the cost of variance. The lesson is that the emulation design is an estimand choice, not a nuisance knob.
- Cost: 30 min, moderate, needs sequential emulation and effect modification.
- Pairs with: the CATE transportability paper below, the other place this week where effect-modifier structure decides whether a causal quantity travels.

### Informing sample size and power calculations for clone-censor-weight analyses: a Monte Carlo simulation study of the variance inflation factor relative to the underlying target trial
Peter C. Austin. Journal of Clinical Epidemiology, 2026-10-30. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112576
- The move: a variance inflation factor that says how much precision a clone-censor-weight analysis loses or gains against the RCT it emulates, with the counterintuitive finding that CCW can be more precise than the underlying trial when confounding is low to moderate.
- Why it is worth your time: design-phase power for CCW has been a blind spot, because the censoring weights only exist at the analysis stage, so you could not plan sample size the way you can for a trial. Austin maps 270 scenarios over sample size, treatment prevalence, the early-treatment threshold, and covariate-treatment associations, across risk difference and relative risk, and ships R code to read off a study-specific VIF. The negative inflation at low confounding, turning into growth as covariates multiply, is a usable surprise rather than a leaderboard number.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate to hard, needs IPC weighting and simulation design.
- Pairs with: the information-dense synthesis paper in learning theory, since both quantify how much information a design extracts relative to the ideal experiment.

### Use of clone-censor-weight to avoid immortal-time bias: a systematic methodological review
Aldrine Manzanilla, Clément Brunetta, François Peyre-Pradat, Stefan Michiels, Thomas Filleron, Noémie Simon-Tillaux. Journal of Clinical Epidemiology, 2026-08-15. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112468
- The move: an audit of 113 published CCW analyses that finds about 20 percent still carry immortal-time bias, almost always because eligibility was assessed on post-time-zero criteria before the cloning step.
- Why it is worth your time: this is not a literature tour, it is a validity audit with a pointed empirical finding, that the method people reach for to kill immortal-time bias is routinely applied in a way that reintroduces it. The breakdown of how weights are estimated and where time zero goes undefined doubles as a checklist of how CCW fails in the wild. Read it as the error-mode companion to the power paper above.
- Cost: 35 min, moderate.
- Pairs with: the Hernán 2016 classic below, which is the principle this review catches people violating.

### Generalizing Conditional Average Treatment Effects From Nested Randomized Trials to Trial-Eligible Individuals
Lan Wen, Issa J. Dahabreh, Yu-Han Chiu. Statistics in Medicine 45(23-24):e70767, 2026-10. https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.70767
- The move: influence-function estimation of the conditional, not just the average, treatment effect transported from a nested randomized trial to all trial-eligible individuals, with pseudo-outcomes from conditional influence functions regressed on pre-specified effect modifiers by local linear smoothing.
- Why it is worth your time: the nested-trial design is the formal backbone of transportability, and this is its heterogeneity-aware extension, giving the CATE function over chosen modifiers rather than a single transported average. The construction is honest semiparametrics, with cross-fitting conditions under which first-stage nuisance error is negligible, demonstrated on the Coronary Artery Surgery Study. It is the technical step up from the ATE-focused generalizability work already in the log.
- Cost: 50 min, hard, needs influence functions, semiparametric efficiency, and cross-fitting.
- Pairs with: the sequential versus single-point paper above, which is the same concern about effect modifiers seen through design rather than estimation.

### Specifying a target trial prevents immortal time bias and other self-inflicted injuries in observational analyses
Miguel A. Hernán, Brian C. Sauer, Sonia Hernández-Díaz, Robert Platt, Ian Shrier. Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 79:70-75, 2016. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2016.04.014
- The move: frame immortal time bias and several other common errors as self-inflicted injuries that follow from failing to align eligibility, treatment assignment, and start of follow-up at time zero.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the pointed, five-page statement of the align-everything-at-time-zero discipline, and it is the origin of the self-inflicted-injuries language the field now uses. The log already carries the 2016 Big Data emulation paper and Avoidable Flaws, but not this one, which is the more teachable version of the same principle. It is the right fixed point to read the CCW review against.
- Cost: 25 min, easy to moderate.
- Pairs with: the CCW systematic review above, which is empirical proof that the injury is still being inflicted a decade later.

## D. Information geometry

### How Inefficient Is Natural Gradient Descent? From Exact Optimality to Theta(sqrt(log d)) Divergence
Guni Sharon, Alan Kuhnle. arXiv:2610.07228, 2026-10-05. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07228
- The move: idealized natural gradient descent on the forward KL objective follows the mixture geodesic, which is generally longer than the Fisher-Rao geodesic, and the inefficiency ratio diverges like the square root of log dimension except for families with a quadratic potential or dimension one, which a tensor criterion identifies as the exactly geodesic cases.
- Why it is worth your time: the folklore that natural gradient is steepest descent in the Fisher metric hides that the path it actually traces is not the Fisher-Rao shortest path. This measures the gap and proves it grows with dimension, with fixed-covariance Gaussians as the clean exact case. The geometry is fully load bearing, since the whole result is a statement about mixture-geodesic length against Fisher length, and it is a useful corrective to a method people treat as obviously optimal.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate, needs dually flat geometry and geodesics.
- Pairs with: the continual-regression paper in learning theory and the Tarek design paper, all three about how the metric you move in decides what you actually optimize.

### Estimators from Convex Geometries of the Simplex
Paul Navas. arXiv:2610.21551, 2026-10-15. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21551
- The move: one potential-generation principle produces both the Bregman and dually flat world and the Pal and Wong logarithmic and projectively flat world, each carrying a maximum-entropy to minimum-divergence duality and a Pythagorean projection that yields an estimator reducing to maximum likelihood in the undeformed limit.
- Why it is worth your time: this shows two separately developed estimator families, the Eguchi style U-estimators and the Wong Zhang lambda-deformed estimators, are the convex and the exponentially concave faces of a single construction. It is a compounding reframe, the kind that makes two literatures snap into one picture, and the Pythagorean projection does the real work rather than decorating the method. Single author, clean enough to teach from.
- Cost: 1 hr, moderate to hard, needs convex duality and divergence geometry.
- Pairs with: the Amari classic below, which is the dually flat half of exactly this picture, and the Tarek Bayesian design paper, another divergence-first reading of a standard construction.

### The Bayesian Learning Rule Beyond KL Geometry
Sophia Sklaviadis, Wu Lin, André Martins, Mário Figueiredo, Thomas Möllenhoff, Mohammad Emtiyaz Khan. arXiv:2610.31308, 2026-10-22. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31308
- The move: the received claim that only KL divergence yields additive Bayesian-learning-rule updates is false, and the whole class of mixed-space Bregman or Fenchel-Young discrepancies preserves the additive natural-gradient computation, which also repairs the undefined-objective pathology of KL on families with parameter-dependent support.
- Why it is worth your time: prior f-divergence work suggested KL was special, and this is the surprising correction, showing which discrepancy geometries admit additive information-geometric updates and why. It earns its keep by fixing a concrete failure mode rather than chasing a benchmark, and it sits in a serious lineage combining Khan's learning-rule program with Fenchel-Young losses. The geometry is the result, not the vocabulary.
- Cost: 50 min, moderate, needs variational inference and exponential-family Bregman duality.
- Pairs with: the NGD inefficiency paper above, since both are about what the natural-gradient update is really doing once you stop assuming KL is the only game.

### Information geometry of continuum limits in lattice field theory: The Gross-Neveu model with Wilson fermions
Akio Tomiya. arXiv:2610.34277, 2026-10-25. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34277
- The move: treat lattice actions as a statistical manifold whose Fisher metric counts the measurements needed to distinguish nearby actions, so the continuum limit becomes a statement about Fisher distinguishability and dimensional transmutation appears as an exponential relation between a renormalization-fixed expectation coordinate and a natural coordinate.
- Why it is worth your time: this is physics cross-pollination where the geometry reveals something, not decorates it, because the renormalization condition literally fixes an expectation coordinate and the approach to the continuum is read off the Fisher metric. The solvable large-N setting means the claims are checkable rather than hand-waved. It is the opposite of a method that name-drops a manifold.
- Cost: 1 hr, hard, needs lattice QFT and exponential-family geometry, a physics-literate read.
- Pairs with: the Seung Sompolinsky Tishby classic in learning theory, the other place this week where order parameters and distinguishability govern a limit.

### Differential Geometry of Curved Exponential Families, Curvatures and Information Loss
Shun-ichi Amari. The Annals of Statistics 10(2):357-385, 1982. https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176345779
- The move: the text that makes the curved exponential family a geometric object, introducing the alpha-connections and the exponential and mixture duality, and reading the information loss of an estimator off the embedding curvatures, generalizing Efron's statistical curvature and second-order efficiency.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the dual-connection machinery that every geometry paper here quietly stands on, and reading it after the convex-geometries and NGD papers shows you where their dually flat and geodesic language was forged. The log carries Efron's 1975 curvature paper, which defined the quantity; this is where that curvature becomes a full geometry with the loss-of-information theorem attached. Dense notation, but the payoff is seeing the foundation whole.
- Cost: 2 hr, hard, the original differential-geometric notation rewards slow reading.
- Pairs with: Estimators from Convex Geometries above and Singular Geometry of Low-Rank Adaptation, both of which read statistical complexity or loss off a dual or degenerate geometry.

## Threads

### Estimators and designs are divergence geometry, and the metric you move in is not always the one you named
Tarek (Bayes), Estimators from Convex Geometries (IG), How Inefficient Is NGD (IG), and Amari 1982 (IG) are one argument. Tarek turns the classical design criteria into prior-to-posterior divergences, Navas unifies two estimator families as faces of one convex-duality construction, and Amari is the foundation both rest on. The tension is the NGD paper: naming the Fisher metric does not mean you move along its geodesics, and the gap grows with dimension. Read together, they say the geometry is real and load bearing, but the operational question is always which path in it you actually traverse.

### When is the inferential object even well defined
Improper priors (Bayes), Canonical Layer Inference for deep GPs (Bayes), Singular Geometry of LoRA (LT), and the sequential versus single-point trial paper (TTE) all find that a quantity people treat as pinned down is not. The improper posterior depends on the approximating compacts, the deep-GP intermediate layer is symmetry-degenerate, the LoRA factorization is non-identifiable, and the sequential emulation targets a different estimand than the single-point one. The disagreement worth noticing is in the response: LoRA treats degeneracy as the thing that sets effective complexity and keeps it, while the improper-prior and deep-GP papers treat non-identifiability as a trap to break, and the trial paper treats it as a design choice to make on purpose. Same pathology, three opposite attitudes.

### Statistical physics, order parameters, and distinguishability
Seung Sompolinsky Tishby (LT), the lattice field theory geometry paper (IG), and the degree-two PTF complexity result (LT) sit on one axis. The 1992 classic computes learning curves and phase transitions from order parameters, Tomiya makes the continuum limit a statement about Fisher distinguishability of actions, and the Diakonikolas result gives a sharp two-sided complexity threshold with the same phase-transition flavor. The throughline is that both learning and continuum limits are governed by how distinguishable nearby models are, and Fisher information and replica order parameters are two accountings of the same quantity. The open question is whether the sharp PTF threshold has an order-parameter story as clean as the replica one.

### Information accrual to a decision
Orabona (Bayes), Blackwell Dubins (Bayes), the CCW power paper (TTE), the CATE transportability paper (TTE), and the information-dense synthesis paper (LT) all ask how fast and how reliably information builds toward a decision. Blackwell and Dubins give the clean limit, opinions merge when absolute continuity holds, Orabona gives the sharp expected time to reject, Austin measures how much precision a CCW design extracts against the ideal trial, Weinstein and Jakobsen optimize the synthesis to extract the most per experiment, and Wen and Dahabreh ask when a treatment effect built from one population transports. The tension is between the Bayesian limit results, which assume the accrual is well posed, and the design and transport results, which spend their effort on exactly the cases where it is not.

## Synthesis

The strongest current this week is that well definedness is not free. Four papers across three domains find that an estimand, a posterior, a layer, or a factorization that everyone treats as fixed is actually contingent on a choice you did not know you were making, whether the approximating compacts, the emulation design, or the parameterization. The geometry papers give the other half: once you take Fisher and divergence structure seriously, classical design criteria, estimator families, and even continuum limits in field theory turn out to be the same kind of object, and the one caution is that moving in a geometry is not the same as naming it, which the NGD result makes quantitative. The disagreement worth sitting with is about degeneracy. Singular learning theory, through the LoRA paper, says non-identifiability is informative and should be measured, not removed, while the improper-prior and deep-GP papers say it is a coherence failure to be broken. Both can be right, and the question is whether the difference is intrinsic or just a matter of whether a canonical representative is available. On the causal side, the trial papers push the same lesson into practice: the design is an estimand choice, effect modifiers decide both divergence between emulations and transportability, and a decade after Hernán named the self-inflicted injuries, a fifth of CCW analyses still inflict them. Two questions to carry forward. First, does the sharp complexity threshold for degree-two PTFs have an order-parameter description that would tie it to the replica and Fisher-distinguishability picture. Second, when non-identifiability is unavoidable, is there a principled test for whether to measure it like an RLCT or break it like a symmetry, rather than deciding case by case.

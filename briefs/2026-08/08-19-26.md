## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. The concentration game: Bayesian updating, regret, and information
Akshay Balsubramani. arXiv:2608.18061 (cs.LG/math.ST), 2026-08-18. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18061

- **The move:** Bayesian updating is the unique Bellman equalizer of a two-player zero-sum game, and exponential-weights regret plus a wide class of concentration bounds fall out of one exact three-term decomposition of that game's value.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It hands you a single variational object from which Gibbs/Bayes weights, log-partition value functions, and the standard variance and bounded-range regret proxies emerge as special cases or loose relaxations, so the usual quadratic-variation surrogate is exposed as an approximation to something exact. The regret splits into per-round information loss, a retempering drift for changes of measurement scale, and the comparator's relative entropy to the prior, with both players' optimal strategies read off term by term. It quietly unifies bandits, posterior sampling, aggregation, boosting, and classical large deviations as one comparator-class geometry.
- **Cost:** 60-90 min, hard, needs online learning and exponential weights, convex duality, relative-entropy variational forms.
- **Pairs with:** the Grünwald-Dawid pick below, its direct intellectual ancestor.

### 2. Bessel-Debiased Pseudo-Marginal MCMC for Generalised Bayesian Inference
Yingkai Lu, Jeong Eun Lee, Geoff K. Nicholls. arXiv:2608.16573 (stat.ME), 2026-08-17. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16573

- **The move:** Exponentiating an unbiased loss estimate silently changes the Gibbs target, and a Bessel factor built from the block sample variance exactly cancels the Gaussian exponential bias, turning a naive n^2 per-proposal budget into a tractable signed pseudo-marginal sampler.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It names a real and under-appreciated failure mode in generalized Bayes computation, that plugging a Monte Carlo loss estimate into exp{-beta*loss} does not sample what you think when beta grows like n, then derives an exact correction instead of a heuristic. Pairing the Bessel debiasing with randomized quasi-Monte-Carlo blocks drops the sufficient budget from n^2 to n^{2/alpha}. It keeps honest books, separating sign efficiency of the signed estimator from the residual finite-block target error, which most method papers skip.
- **Cost:** 60 min, hard, needs pseudo-marginal MCMC, unbiased-estimator and sign-problem intuition, some QMC.
- **Pairs with:** "Inverse Probability Weighting in a Post-Bayesian World" (2606.28685) already in the log.

### 3. Induction and the rule of succession through a possibilistic inferential model lens
Ryan Martin, Shih-Ni Prim, Jonathan P. Williams. arXiv:2608.11935 (stat.ME/math.ST), 2026-08-12. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11935

- **The move:** It takes the classic criticisms of the Bayesian answer to Hume's problem of induction head on and shows the possibilistic inferential-model framework escapes exactly those criticisms while keeping a Laplace-style rule of succession, with frequentist reliability guarantees Bayes cannot match.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Foundations with a sharp edge, not a survey. It walks both the well-known and the lesser-known failures of the Bayesian induction story, using the sunrise problem as a concrete testbed against Laplace's rule, and shows a non-additive uncertainty calculus buys validity guarantees precisely where the additive prior forces awkward commitments. That reframes what a prior is actually doing when it claims to solve induction, and Martin's IM program is one of the few serious live alternatives to Bayes.
- **Cost:** 45 min, moderate, needs foundations of inference; some familiarity with imprecise and possibility measures helps.
- **Pairs with:** "Strong Likelihood Principle" (2606.08975) in the log, for adjacent foundations sparring.

### 4. NP-LEAP: Nonparametric Latent Exchangeability Prior for Model-Lean Borrowing from Historical Data
Ethan M. Alt, Miheer Dewaskar, Jacob M. Maronge, Yuelin Lu, Matthew A. Psioda. arXiv:2608.16688 (stat.ME), 2026-08-17. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16688

- **The move:** Instead of a parametric outcome model, it does individual-level exchangeability assessment and induces Bayesian model averaging over every partition of the historical subjects into exchangeable and non-exchangeable subsets.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Dynamic borrowing usually smuggles in a triply misspecified survival model, parametric baseline hazard, proportional hazards, and blanket exchangeability, and this replaces all of it with a kernel-based, outcome-agnostic exchangeability judgment, a genuinely different place to put the assumption. Framing how much to borrow as posterior averaging over exchangeable partitions is a real use of de Finetti-flavored structure that most borrowing methods only gesture at, and they prove posterior consistency rather than leaving it empirical.
- **Cost:** 45-60 min, moderate, needs Bayesian nonparametrics, exchangeability, some survival vocabulary.
- **Pairs with:** the Ewens-Pitman asymptotics paper (2606.13554) in the log, for the partition-prior machinery underneath.

### 5. Game theory, maximum entropy, minimum discrepancy and robust Bayesian decision theory (timeless)
Peter D. Grünwald, A. Philip Dawid. Annals of Statistics 32(4):1367-1433, 2004. arXiv:math/0410076. https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0410076

- **The move:** Maximizing entropy and minimizing worst-case expected loss are the same zero-sum game between Decision Maker and Nature, and the whole thing generalizes past Shannon entropy to an arbitrary loss with its own generalized entropy and matching divergence.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the load-bearing ancestor of the game-theoretic Bayes revival, including entry 1, and reading it makes the modern regret-decomposition papers legible as descendants rather than novelties. The duality gives a rationale for MaxEnt and a constructive tool for finding robust Bayes acts at once, and the extension of the redundancy-capacity theorem to arbitrary divergences is a derivation you can teach a whole seminar from. Twenty years on it is still under-read outside a small circle.
- **Cost:** 2-3 hrs, hard, needs convex duality, minimax game theory, information theory; skimmable to the main duality theorem in about 40 min.
- **Pairs with:** entry 1 above, the direct modern continuation.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. The Phase Transition in Online PCA Depends on n/d log(d), not n/d
Apratim Dey. arXiv:2607.23914 (v2), 2026-07-27. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23914

- **The move:** The classic constant-aspect-ratio n/d BBP threshold is the wrong scaling for streaming PCA; Oja's algorithm has its sharp recovery transition at n/(d log d), so constant n/d gives zero overlap.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It nails a precise threshold and even the critical-window law: at n = γ* d log d + ηd the overlap converges to an explicit random limit driven by a single Gaussian. The extra log d factor is a genuine surprise that separates online from batch estimation, and the criticality analysis is a sharp asymptotic you can teach from. A clean recovery-threshold result, not another bound.
- **Cost:** ~2 hrs, hard, needs high-dimensional probability, spiked covariance and BBP, stochastic approximation for Oja's algorithm.
- **Pairs with:** the extensive-width scaling paper below for the shared sharp-threshold theme.

### 2. A Defense of the Quadratic Model
Alexandru Meterez, Pranav Ajit Nair, Depen Morwani, Cengiz Pehlevan, Sham Kakade, Alex Damian. arXiv:2607.21716, 2026-07-23. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21716

- **The move:** It directly tests when the simplest optimization abstraction, a local quadratic Taylor model, is actually valid, and finds it predicts real 150M-param LLM training dynamics over windows up to 10% of training.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the is-the-abstraction-justified question answered empirically and mechanistically instead of assumed. They use deep-probe Lanczos quadrature to read the Hessian spectrum far into the tail and find batch-size, preconditioner, and time-dependent structure, then show LLM pretraining sits at a stochastic edge of stability whose character is set by batch size. It licenses theory to use the quadratic model where it earns its keep and flags where it breaks.
- **Cost:** ~2-3 hrs, medium-hard, needs edge of stability, Hessian spectral estimation, basic optimization dynamics.
- **Pairs with:** the near-zero Hessian eigenvalue paper below, which explains the bulk structure this one measures.

### 3. Sharp feature-learning transitions and Bayes-optimal neural scaling laws in extensive-width networks
Minh-Toan Nguyen, Jean Barbier. arXiv:2605.10395, 2026-05-11. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10395

- **The move:** It derives neural scaling laws from an actual mechanism: teacher features become recoverable one at a time through discontinuous phase transitions, and effective width is the count of currently learnable features.
- **Why it is worth your time:** In the extensive-width regime where width scales with input dimension, a leave-one-out decoupling yields sharp fixed-point equations for Bayes-optimal error and per-feature overlaps. Two apparently distinct scaling regimes collapse to the single law ε = Θ(k_c d/n), and Adam near k_c is shown empirically to hit it. This is a scaling-law paper that supplies the missing mechanism instead of a fitted curve.
- **Cost:** ~3 hrs, hard, needs replica and cavity heuristics, Bayes-optimal inference, phase-transition analysis.
- **Pairs with:** the online-PCA paper above, same sequential-recovery-threshold flavor.

### 4. Explaining Near-Zero Hessian Eigenvalues Through Approximate Symmetries in Neural Networks
Marcel Kühn, Bernd Rosenow. arXiv:2607.07845, 2026-07-08. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07845

- **The move:** It identifies the vast bulk of near-zero Hessian eigenvalues as weakly lifted pseudo-Goldstone modes of the network's continuous parametrization symmetries, a physics import that pays rent.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Everyone explains the Hessian's few large eigenvalues; this explains the many tiny ones with a mechanism. In deep linear nets the symmetries are exact and give flat directions with explicitly constructed eigenvectors; ReLU is treated as a symmetry-breaking perturbation, and high-curvature directions live orthogonal to the symmetry subspace while the bulk lies inside it, checked on a student-teacher model and on CIFAR-10. Remove the Goldstone framing and the result collapses, the sign of a real import.
- **Cost:** ~2 hrs, medium, needs Hessian geometry, Lie-group and parametrization symmetries; a little Goldstone-mode intuition helps.
- **Pairs with:** "A Defense of the Quadratic Model" above and the singular-learning line in the log.

### 5. The exact information-based complexity of smooth convex minimization (timeless)
Yoel Drori. arXiv:1606.01424, 2016-06-04 (J. Complexity). https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.01424

- **The move:** It constructs a new smooth convex worst-case function whose information-based complexity lower bound exactly matches the Optimized Gradient Method's worst-case rate, proving first-order smooth convex minimization is exactly solved with no remaining constant-factor gap.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the load-bearing lower-bound half of the performance-estimation-problem program: it certifies OGM as exactly optimal, closing the classical Nesterov gap with a tight constant, not an order. The proof technique, a novel construction of the hard function, is clean enough to teach and is cited far less than its importance warrants. A reference-chase gem for anyone who thought first-order complexity was settled in the 1980s.
- **Cost:** ~2 hrs, medium-hard, needs smooth convex optimization, Nesterov acceleration, comfort with min-max arguments.
- **Pairs with:** Kim and Fessler's Optimized Gradient Method, the matching upper bound, and the broader PEP literature.

## C. Target trial emulation

### 1. The Role of Assignment in Defining and Identifying Causal Effects in Randomized Trials
Issa J. Dahabreh, Lawson Ung, Miguel A. Hernán, Yu-Han Chiu. Epidemiology, 2026 (online ahead of print). https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000002034

- **The move:** The per-protocol effect is the effect of assignment-plus-adherence, not the effect of the treatment strategy itself, and the two come apart whenever assignment acts on the outcome outside of treatment.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It builds explicit causal structures where the per-protocol estimand and the treatment-strategy estimand have different identifying functionals, and shows identifying either one requires assignment information even under randomization, unless you buy an exclusion restriction and no confounding by assignment. It names blinding as exactly the mechanism that breaks the exclusion restriction, so the trial's own machinery can invalidate the interpretation people want. This is the conceptual spine under every per-protocol and clone-censor-weight emulation, argued by counterexample.
- **Cost:** ~60-90 min, medium-hard, needs counterfactuals and DAGs, comfort with the estimand-versus-estimator distinction.
- **Pairs with:** Lu, Esen, Ross, "Toward a Clearer Definition of the Per-Protocol Effect," Epidemiology 2026 (https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000002035), the companion published alongside it.

### 2. Estimating the effect of pravastatin vs usual care under full adherence in the ALLHAT Lipid-Lowering Trial
Rienna G. Russo, Anna Siefkas, Barry R. Davis, Eric B. Rimm, Issa J. Dahabreh, Miguel A. Hernán, Goodarz Danaei. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026, 195(7). https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag049

- **The move:** A pragmatic trial that found no effect of assignment to pravastatin on death yields a clearly protective effect once you estimate the per-protocol full-adherence effect, and the framing flips the conclusion.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A clean worked application of entry 1 on a real usual-care-controlled trial where a third of the treatment arm was non-adherent by year 5. Adjusting for adherence-related factors moves the 5-year risk difference to -3.0% (RR 0.79), robust across treatment-strategy definitions, and the usual-care comparator is the hard case that makes the lesson concrete. Same data, two estimands, two answers, with a defensible reason to care about the second.
- **Cost:** ~45 min, medium, needs basic IP-weighting for adherence and per-protocol reasoning.
- **Pairs with:** entry 1 above, its theoretical counterpart.

### 3. On the Statistical Limitations of Landmark Analysis for Addressing Immortal Time Bias
Fei Wan. Statistics in Medicine, 2026, 45(18-19):e70679. https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.70679

- **The move:** Landmark analysis, the go-to fix for immortal time bias, does not recover the causal effect; it attenuates toward the null because the reference group silently mixes never-treated and late-treated subjects.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Under potential outcomes and piecewise proportional hazards, Wan derives closed-form expressions for naive, exclusion, and landmark estimators and pins down exactly why landmark bias appears and how it scales with true effect, treatment rate, and event rate. The alarming result is that the attenuation alone can make a landmark analysis less powerful than a randomized trial of equal size, so a small effect from landmarking may be bias wearing the costume of a null. Single author, one clean derivation, a directly usable warning against a widespread practice.
- **Cost:** ~60 min, medium-hard, needs survival analysis and hazard-model algebra.
- **Pairs with:** Suissa's prevalent new-user antibiotics paper in the log, a design-side counterpoint.

### 4. Extending Inferences from a Randomized Trial to Trial-eligible and Treatment-candidate Target Populations
Jessica C. Young, Issa J. Dahabreh, Stefan James, David Erlinge, Ole Fröbert, Anita Berglund, Rebecca Rylance, Miguel A. Hernán, Anthony A. Matthews. Epidemiology, 2026, 37(5). https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000001994

- **The move:** It separates two target populations people routinely conflate, those eligible for the trial versus those who are actual treatment candidates in routine care, and transports the VALIDATE-SWEDEHEART result to each.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The trial-eligible versus treatment-candidate distinction is a genuine reframe of who the target is, operationalized by standardizing trial outcome models to registry-characterized populations. Risk ratios stay close to the index trial while absolute risks shift by about a percentage point, which is the honest lesson about what generalizes and what does not. A concrete template for the transportability step that emulation usually hand-waves.
- **Cost:** ~45 min, medium, needs standardization and IP-weighting, transportability assumptions.
- **Pairs with:** the SWEDEHEART/REDUCE-AMI prospective benchmarking paper in the log, same registry ecosystem.

### 5. Vaccine Efficacy Against Naturally Asymptomatic Infections: A Novel Estimand for Quantifying Vaccine Effects
Elizabeth Rogawski McQuade, Razieh Nabi, Allison Codi, Natalie Dean, Marc Lipsitch, David Benkeser. Epidemiology, 2026 (online ahead of print). https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000002021

- **The move:** The naive vaccinated-versus-unvaccinated contrast for asymptomatic infection conflates two opposing effects, preventing infection and converting symptomatic to asymptomatic, so a good vaccine can look harmful; a principal-stratification estimand fixes it.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A crisp case of estimand specification surfacing a subtle bias, then formalizing VENAI, efficacy among those who would naturally be asymptomatic, as the interpretable analogue of standard efficacy. It gives identification assumptions, bounds that avoid cross-world independence, and a sensitivity analysis, then applies it to the mRNA-1273 trial where the corrected estimate looks nothing like the naive one. Even outside vaccines it is a teachable example of how a sloppy contrast hides a compositional shift.
- **Cost:** ~60-90 min, hard, needs principal stratification, potential outcomes, bounding arguments.
- **Pairs with:** Jia and Lipsitch, "Defining and Estimating Outcomes Directly Averted by a Vaccination Program," Epidemiology 2026 (https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000001953).

## D. Information geometry

### 1. Spectral duality structures and the Fisher-Rao geometry of reset distributions
Juan Antonio Vega Coso. arXiv:2608.15805 (math.PR), 2026-08-16. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15805

- **The move:** Under the square-root embedding, the reset-neutral separatrix of a Markov process with geometric resetting becomes a totally geodesic subsphere, and the dimension of the reset-response space equals the number of active spectral-duality orbits.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A sleeper that uses Fisher-Rao geometry to answer a concrete dynamical question, does adding resetting always help or hurt and with what sign, rather than to decorate it. In dimension one the geodesic-subsphere structure forces fixed-sign response; in higher dimensions it constructs explicit counterexamples to the naive global sign principle. Strip out the Fisher-Rao structure and the sign-classification theorem collapses, which is the rubric's ideal of geometry doing real work.
- **Cost:** ~2-3 hrs, moderate-to-hard, needs Markov resetting, spectral theory of generators, basic Fisher-Rao and simplex geometry.
- **Pairs with:** the same author's separatrix-structure companion, arXiv:2607.10717.

### 2. Optimal feedback control under stepwise equilibration and partial observation
Francesco Mottes, Michael P. Brenner. arXiv:2607.21523 (cond-mat.stat-mech), 2026-07-23. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21523

- **The move:** The minimum work of a measurement-driven controller splits exactly into a positive thermodynamic-length transport cost and a negative information-extraction term, and the cost of writing and resetting the measurement record upper-bounds the beneficial cycles.
- **Why it is worth your time:** An honest negative result. Even with perfect measurements the record-keeping cost forces net work non-negative, so information cannot be turned into a free lunch. They solve the Bellman recursion analytically for a harmonic trap and show optimal protocols spend their information budget early and hit endpoint constraints near the deadline. Thermodynamic length, the info-geometric metric on thermodynamic state space, is what makes the decomposition and the bound clean.
- **Cost:** ~3 hrs, hard, needs stochastic thermodynamics, optimal control and Bellman equations, thermodynamic length.
- **Pairs with:** "Measuring thermodynamic length" (Crooks) in the log, the metric backbone.

### 3. Information Geometry meets Functional ANOVA: An Exact Fisher-Information Decomposition, with an Application to Radio Luminosity Functions
Marko Imbrišak, Krešimir Tisanić. arXiv:2608.05374 (astro-ph.IM), 2026-08-05. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05374

- **The move:** Score contributions of main effects and interactions are Hilbert-space vectors whose Gram matrix reproduces the Fisher metric exactly, giving an additive breakdown of the full Fisher information matrix into main-effect, interaction, and cross-channel blocks, the parameter-information analogue of Sobol indices.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A clean, teachable reframe: Fisher information is literally the Gram matrix of ANOVA score channels, and the identity is exact rather than approximate. The decomposition localizes which parts of the observation constrain which parameters and exposes compensatory sloppy directions directly, tying information geometry to the sloppiness literature. The astrophysics is just a demonstration; the derivation stands alone.
- **Cost:** ~1.5-2 hrs, moderate, needs Fisher information, functional ANOVA and Sobol decomposition, basic RKHS.
- **Pairs with:** "Efficient coding drives systems towards criticality and sloppiness" in the log.

### 4. Weak Information Geometry: Riemannian Structures from Distributional Inference Functions and Stein Discrepancies
Rodrigo Labouriau. arXiv:2607.11246 (math.ST), 2026-07-13. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11246

- **The move:** Any inference instrument with full-rank sensitivity S and positive-definite variability V induces a Godambe metric G = Sᵀ V⁻¹ S, a genuine Riemannian metric that reduces to Fisher-Rao for the score but extends to undominated families and parameter-dependent support where Fisher-Rao is undefined.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A principled generalization of Fisher-Rao built from estimating functions and Stein discrepancies rather than log-likelihood gradients, representing laws as tempered distributions. Weak inferential separation shows up geometrically as block-diagonality of the metric, a satisfying structural payoff. It quietly enlarges the class of models that have an information geometry at all, which is exactly the object that survives when the likelihood-based construction breaks.
- **Cost:** ~2.5 hrs, hard, needs estimating functions and Godambe information, Stein discrepancies, RKHS, some functional analysis.
- **Pairs with:** Amari-Nagaoka dual-connection machinery, reframed here from the estimating-function side.

### 5. An extended Čencov characterization of the information metric (timeless)
L. L. Campbell. Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society 98(1):135-141, 1986. https://www.ams.org/journals/proc/1986-098-01/S0002-9939-1986-0848890-5/

- **The move:** Čencov's uniqueness theorem, that the Fisher metric is the only Markov-invariant metric, is extended off the probability simplex to the full positive cone of unnormalized measures, with a short differential-geometry proof that drops Čencov's category-theoretic apparatus.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The under-cited companion to Čencov that most people cite Čencov for without reading. Working on the positive cone clarifies why the Fisher metric is forced and makes the invariance argument transparent and teachable in a few pages. It is the cleanest entry point to the why-this-metric-and-no-other question, and it still gets reference-chased by current foundational work such as the Čencov-Petz line.
- **Cost:** ~1 hr, moderate, needs Riemannian metrics, Markov maps and congruent embeddings, comfort with the simplex.
- **Pairs with:** Rao (1945) in the log as the metric's origin, with Campbell supplying the uniqueness.

## Threads

**Thread 1: Inference is a zero-sum game, and its cost has a geometry.**
Papers: Bayesian 1 (concentration game), Bayesian 5 (Grünwald-Dawid), Information geometry 2 (feedback control). Balsubramani makes Bayesian updating the equalizer strategy of a game whose value decomposes into an information term and a retempering-transport term; Grünwald and Dawid proved twenty years ago that MaxEnt and robust Bayes are the same game with a generalized entropy and its divergence. Mottes and Brenner arrive at the same shape from thermodynamics: minimum control work splits into a positive thermodynamic-length transport cost and a negative information-extraction term. Three fields, one decomposition, transport plus information, with the divergence in the statistics papers and the thermodynamic metric in the physics one playing the same structural role.

**Thread 2: The naive contrast computes the wrong estimand.**
Papers: Target trial 3 (landmark), Target trial 5 (VENAI), Target trial 1 (assignment), Bayesian 2 (Bessel debiasing). Wan shows landmark analysis silently mixes never-treated and late-treated subjects and attenuates toward the null; Rogawski McQuade and colleagues show the naive asymptomatic-infection contrast blends prevention and symptom conversion so a good vaccine can look harmful; Dahabreh and colleagues show the per-protocol contrast is not the treatment-strategy effect people read it as. Lu, Lee, and Nicholls make the same point in computation: exp of an unbiased loss estimate does not target the Gibbs posterior you named. In every case the estimator is honest about something, just not the thing on the label, and the fix is to write the estimand down first.

**Thread 3: Count the learnable directions, by geometry or by probability.**
Papers: Learning 1 (online PCA), Learning 3 (extensive-width), Information geometry 1 (reset separatrix), Learning 4 (Hessian symmetries). Dey finds the online-PCA recovery threshold sits at n/(d log d) and the critical window is governed by a single Gaussian; Nguyen and Barbier make effective width the count of currently learnable teacher features, recovered one at a time through sharp transitions. Vega Coso counts reset-response directions as active spectral-duality orbits and reads them off a geodesic subsphere, while Kühn and Rosenow count the near-zero Hessian directions as symmetry Goldstone modes. The tension is method: probability and replica heuristics on the learning side, exact Fisher-Rao and Lie-symmetry geometry on the other, converging on the same question of how many directions are alive.

**Thread 4: What forces the metric, and what survives when likelihood breaks.**
Papers: Information geometry 5 (Campbell-Čencov), Information geometry 4 (Godambe metric), Information geometry 3 (Fisher as Gram matrix), Bayesian 3 (possibilistic IM). Campbell says the Fisher metric is the unique Markov-invariant choice, even on the positive cone; Imbrišak and Tisanić show that same metric is exactly the Gram matrix of ANOVA score channels. Labouriau then asks what happens where the score does not exist and builds a Godambe metric from estimating functions that reduces to Fisher-Rao but extends past it. Martin, Prim, and Williams push the same instinct outside additive probability entirely, replacing the prior with a possibilistic calculus that keeps reliability where the Bayesian construction strains.

## Synthesis

The week's spine is duality. Balsubramani, Grünwald-Dawid, and Mottes-Brenner independently write the value of an inference or control problem as a transport cost against an information gain, which suggests the game-theoretic and thermodynamic pictures of learning are one picture waiting for a shared statement. Against that constructive optimism sit four negative results, three in causal inference and one in Bayesian computation, that all say the same thing: the quantity you compute by default answers a question you did not ask, and only writing down the estimand first repairs it. The geometry papers supply the discipline that makes both moods precise. Campbell explains why the Fisher metric is not a choice, Labouriau shows what to do when the likelihood that defines it is gone, and Imbrišak-Tisanić make the metric something you can decompose and read. The sharp-threshold papers add the dynamical counterpart, that learnability arrives in countable discrete jumps rather than smoothly, whether you count directions by replica heuristics or by geodesic subspheres. Two questions worth sitting with. First, if regret, robust Bayes, and minimum control work all decompose into transport plus information, is thermodynamic length the same object as the statistical divergence in the Bayesian games, or only its analogue. Second, the negative-result cluster and the count-the-directions cluster meet on identifiability: a landmark reference group and a near-zero Hessian mode are both directions along which the data cannot tell you what you wanted, and it is worth asking whether the geometry that counts learnable features could also count the estimands an emulation can actually identify.

# Weekly Reading List — 08-12-26

A fragility-and-invariance week. One current runs through everything: the property you assume your setup already has, a consistent posterior, an identifiable model, a well-defined estimand, is usually the exception rather than the default, and it fails for natural reasons, not pathological ones. Against that, a second current keeps finding the one clean object that governs a messy procedure, a limiting kernel, an h-transform, an algebraic invariant, a metric correction, and shows that either the object or the correction to it is where the content lives. The geometry section leans thermodynamic this week, and the learning-theory section is unusually dynamical. ## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Seeing the Forest for the Trees: The Gaussian Process Limit of BART
Cory McCartan, Melody Huang. arXiv:2607.28844, 2026-07-30. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28844

- The move: BART is secretly a Gaussian process. Send the number of trees to infinity and the ensemble collapses to a GP with an explicit kernel you can actually analyze.
- Why it is worth your time: this closes the gap between a workhorse nobody fully understood and a clean limiting object. The payoff is not decorative, the GP view yields random-tree features that attain minimax ridge rates depending only logarithmically on dimension, and it explains BART's empirical success without hand-waved contraction arguments. It builds the muscle of hunting for the tractable limit hiding inside a messy sampler, the same instinct the NNGP literature rewards.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate, needs RKHS and GP kernels and additive models.
- Pairs with: finalist A3, another Bayesian object revealed to be a familiar stochastic-analysis construction.

### 2. A Simple Example of Bayesian Nonparametric Inconsistency
Antonio R. Linero. arXiv:2607.28958, 2026-07-31. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28958

- The move: a negative result with a sharp threshold, put a prior on the Dirichlet process concentration parameter and the tail of that single hyperprior decides whether the entire model is consistent.
- Why it is worth your time: exponential-or-heavier tails on the concentration parameter kill posterior consistency, lighter tails save it, and the example is transparent enough to teach from directly. It unifies a consistency result and an inconsistency result under one visible mechanism and trains the reflex that full support buys you nothing, that a hyperprior tail you never think about is load-bearing. This is the modern, hand-computable descendant of the classic below.
- Cost: 30 min, moderate, needs Dirichlet-process basics.
- Pairs with: finalist A5, the sixty-year-old inconsistency example this descends from directly.

### 3. Embedding martingale diffusions as binary posteriors in sequential inference
Steven Campbell, Karl Kristian Engelund. arXiv:2607.20373, 2026-07-22. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20373

- The move: every bounded martingale diffusion is a Bayesian posterior. Any time-homogeneous martingale diffusion on [0,1] with absorbing endpoints is exactly the posterior belief of some binary sequential experiment, and the converse pins down when observations identify the hidden state.
- Why it is worth your time: a genuine two-way structural identity, not an analogy. The forward direction realizes any win-martingale as a posterior, and the converse is a Riccati condition equivalent to a Doob h-transform, with the classical Shiryaev-Wonham filter recovered when the harmonic function is not spatially harmonic. It builds the cross-domain muscle linking h-transforms and harmonic functions to sequential updating, and it reads belief dynamics as a diffusion.
- Cost: 60 min, hard, needs stochastic calculus, diffusions, martingales, and Doob transforms.
- Pairs with: optimal stopping and the Shiryaev change-point filter, and finalist B4, both reading an inference process as a named dynamical object.

### 4. Exchangeable Testing Against an Unknown Benchmark
Alexander Gnedin. arXiv:2608.04838, 2026-08-05. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04838

- The move: a de Finetti construction built purely from ranks. Compare each new point to a latent benchmark and the exchangeable sequence, the predictive law, and the de Finetti measure all fall out combinatorially.
- Why it is worth your time: the posterior of the running rank is a closed combinatorial form, the normalized rank converges to a latent strength variable with a Beta-tilted density, and predictive probabilities come as clean multiplicative formulas. It is a concrete, hand-computable window into how exchangeability and a latent mixing variable generate each other, which is exactly the foundations muscle the abstract de Finetti theorem never exercises. Read it as an operational rank rule that manufactures its own mixing measure.
- Cost: 35 min, moderate, needs exchangeability and de Finetti basics.
- Pairs with: Kingman's paintbox and partition structures, and anytime-valid testing by betting.

### 5. On the Asymptotic Behavior of Bayes' Estimates in the Discrete Case (older classic)
David A. Freedman. Annals of Mathematical Statistics 34(4):1386-1403, 1963. https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703871

- The move: the original proof that a Bayesian can be perfectly coherent and still converge to the wrong answer. Even in the countable discrete case, a reasonable prior with mass near the truth can drive the posterior to a ridiculous estimate.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the load-bearing ancestor of every posterior-consistency and BNP-inconsistency result, including finalist A2. Freedman shows that in the space of priors, consistency is the exception in a precise topological sense, which reframes "the posterior concentrates" from a theorem into a fragile assumption. Reading it next to Linero shows how little the failure mode has changed in six decades, and it builds the sleeper-spotting habit of returning to the quiet paper whose one idea still runs the field.
- Cost: 50 min, hard, needs measure-theoretic probability and comfort with the topology of priors.
- Pairs with: finalist A2, the same failure brought back to the Dirichlet-process setting, and Diaconis-Freedman 1986 in the log.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. Partial Differential Equation Barriers to Identifiability in Infinite Mixture Models
Dung Le, Nicola Bariletto, Alessandro Rinaldo, Nhat Ho. arXiv:2608.08597, 2026-08-09. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08597

- The move: non-identifiability of a mixture is exactly the condition that the kernel is annihilated by a nontrivial differential or difference-differential operator over parameter space.
- Why it is worth your time: the reframe is clean, whether infinitely many mixing measures collapse to the same density becomes a question about the null space of an operator acting on the kernel family, which they run through Gaussian, Student-t, Gamma, Beta, and general exponential families and pair with a minimax lower bound for the non-identifiable cases. It replaces case-by-case identifiability folklore with one operator-theoretic criterion and tells you which kernel classes stay safe. It builds the instinct that an estimation obstruction is often a hidden analytic degeneracy, not a sample-size problem.
- Cost: 2 hours, moderate-hard, needs mixture models, method of moments, and comfort with PDE and operator language.
- Pairs with: finalist B2 and Teicher's finite-mixture identifiability, all about the degeneracy of the parameter-to-model map.

### 2. Langevin dynamics along the zero set of real-analytic potentials
Martin Larsson. arXiv:2608.09840, 2026-08-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09840

- The move: in the large-beta limit, Langevin diffusion on a real-analytic loss collapses onto a hierarchy of Dirichlet forms indexed by local learning coefficient, and the limiting motion provably drifts toward the more singular strata.
- Why it is worth your time: this gives singular learning theory an honest dynamical mechanism rather than a free-energy accounting identity. The stratification of the zero set by real log canonical threshold falls out of a Dirichlet-form convergence, and the diffusion prefers higher-codimension singular pieces, a rigorous handle on why noisy gradient methods settle into degenerate, generalizing minima. It reads as probability rather than physics, which is refreshing, and it sharpens how you think about SGD noise as a selector among degenerate solutions.
- Cost: 3 hours, hard, needs Dirichlet forms, real-analytic stratifications, and basic singular learning theory.
- Pairs with: finalist B1 on the same param-to-model degeneracy, and the Watanabe free-energy side in the log's singular-learning entries.

### 3. The Phase Transition in Online PCA Depends on n/d log(d), not n/d
Apratim Dey. arXiv:2607.23914, 2026-07-27. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23914

- The move: Oja-type streaming PCA does not recover the spike at the constant aspect ratio n/d that batch PCA needs. The true threshold sits at n/(d log d), so streaming pays an exact logarithmic tax.
- Why it is worth your time: a crisp, memorable negative result. The n/d intuition imported from batch spiked covariance is simply wrong for the online algorithm, and the correction is a clean log(d) with a characterized critical-window law. It is the kind of sharp separation between two regimes people assumed matched that recalibrates priors, and it teaches you to read a sample-complexity threshold skeptically when the algorithm, not just the statistics, is the binding constraint.
- Cost: 90 min, moderate, needs spiked covariance and BBP, stochastic approximation, and Oja's method.
- Pairs with: Baik-Ben Arous-Peche, and finalist B5, another "the threshold is not where you think" story.

### 4. A Bifurcation Theory Framework for Gradient Descent on the Edge of Stability
Eric Gan. arXiv:2606.15551, 2026-06-14. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15551

- The move: edge-of-stability training is a flip, or period-doubling, bifurcation in the direction normal to the minimizer manifold, and that framing yields a genuine convergence proof to the manifold at the 2/eta threshold.
- Why it is worth your time: the edge-of-stability literature is crowded and mostly descriptive, and this one imports the right dynamical-systems object, a flip bifurcation on a normally hyperbolic manifold, and gets provable convergence that subsumes earlier product-stability conditions as a special case. For a reader fluent in dynamical systems it is the cleanest bridge from "sharpness hovers at 2/eta" to a named codimension-one bifurcation. It teaches you to see progressive sharpening and the 2-periodic oscillation as the generic normal-form behavior it is.
- Cost: 2 hours, moderate, needs bifurcation theory, center-manifold and normal-hyperbolicity ideas, and gradient-descent dynamics.
- Pairs with: finalist A3, the other paper this week reading an iterative process as a named dynamical object, and the continuous-time edge-of-stability flows.

### 5. The sample complexity of multi-reference alignment (older sleeper)
Amelia Perry, Jonathan Weed, Afonso S. Bandeira, Philippe Rigollet, Amit Singer. arXiv:1707.00943, 2017; SIAM J. Math. Data Sci. 2018. https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00943

- The move: recovering a signal under random group shifts needs about 1/SNR samples at high SNR but jumps to about 1/SNR^3 in the low-SNR regime, and the cubic exponent is set by the lowest moment that survives averaging over the group.
- Why it is worth your time: a decades-old cryo-EM observation, why resolution cost blows up cubically in noise, gets its exact information-theoretic explanation via which invariant first identifies the signal. It is the load-bearing sleeper under a current line of method-of-moments and invariant-feature work, and the SNR^3 law is a beautiful case of sample complexity dictated by an algebraic invariant rather than dimension. Remove the group-averaging structure and the result collapses, which is exactly the cross-domain-import test passed cleanly.
- Cost: 2 hours, moderate-hard, needs method of moments, group actions and invariants, and information-theoretic lower bounds.
- Pairs with: finalist B3 on thresholds landing where intuition does not expect, and finalist D4, another rate or metric set by a cumulant.

## C. Target trial emulation

### 1. On the Statistical Limitations of Landmark Analysis for Addressing Immortal Time Bias
Fei Wan. Statistics in Medicine 45(18-19):e70679, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.70679

- The move: the standard fix for immortal time bias is itself biased. A closed-form analysis shows landmark analysis attenuates toward the null and can lose more power than a randomized trial of the same size.
- Why it is worth your time: under piecewise proportional hazards and potential outcomes, Wan derives exact expressions for the naive, exclusion, and landmark estimators and shows the landmark reference group is a contaminated mixture of never-treated and late-treated subjects, so it cannot recover the target effect. The attenuation vanishes only under the null, which means a small landmark effect may be bias, not a true null. It kills the reflexive "we ran a landmark analysis so immortal time is handled" move and points to time-dependent modeling as the principled route. According to PubMed, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.70679).
- Cost: 45 min, moderate-hard, needs survival analysis, hazard algebra, and potential outcomes.
- Pairs with: finalist C5, the clone-censor-weight construction that is the identifiable alternative to the biased fix.

### 2. Vaccine Efficacy Against Naturally Asymptomatic Infections: A Novel Estimand for Quantifying Vaccine Effects
Elizabeth Rogawski McQuade, Razieh Nabi, Allison Codi, Natalie Dean, Marc Lipsitch, David Benkeser. Epidemiology, 2026-07-22. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000002021

- The move: a routinely reported quantity, the vaccine effect on asymptomatic infection, is secretly two effects glued together, and the naive contrast can make a good vaccine look harmful.
- Why it is worth your time: using principal stratification they define VENAI, the efficacy among people who would have been asymptomatic anyway, isolating true prevention from the mechanical symptomatic-to-asymptomatic conversion effect that pollutes the standard estimand. They give identification assumptions, cross-world-free bounds, and a sensitivity analysis, then re-analyze the mRNA-1273 trial, where VENAI is markedly higher than the naive number. It is a clean case of estimand specification flipping the interpretation, and the register is the one this section rewards. According to PubMed, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000002021).
- Cost: 60 min, hard, needs principal stratification, cross-world independence, and bounds.
- Pairs with: finalist B5 in spirit, an effect made identifiable only through the right invariant or stratum, and the without-matching VE estimand paper in the log.

### 3. A Framework for Causal Estimand Selection Under Positivity Violations
Martha Barnard, Jared D. Huling, Julian Wolfson. Biometrics 82(1):ujag014, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1093/biomtc/ujag014

- The move: reframe overlap trouble as an explicit bias-decomposition choice, the statistical bias of the estimator versus estimand mismatch, the deviation from the population you actually care about.
- Why it is worth your time: instead of silently letting overlap weighting redefine your target population, they make the tradeoff a stated design decision with two design-based metrics and a selection procedure that encodes an analyst's preference for preserving the original population versus reducing bias. It is the constructive counterpart to the standard "overlap weighting targets an uninterpretable population" critique, and it is directly relevant to any emulation with poor overlap. The right-heart-catheterization example makes the abstract tradeoff concrete. According to PubMed, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomtc/ujag014).
- Cost: 60 min, hard, needs inverse probability weighting, propensity scores, and estimand theory.
- Pairs with: finalist C2 on choosing the estimand deliberately, and the metformin-versus-sulfonylureas overlap paper in the log.

### 4. An Operational Target Trial Emulation Framework for Causal Inference Using Electronic Health Record Data
Yanfei Wang, Yongqiu Li, Tuo Lin, Yi Guo. NPJ Digital Medicine 9(1), 2026. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-02563-z

- The move: draw a hard line between trial specification and its realization in EHR data, and ask when healthcare-driven data generation makes the emulation non-identifiable regardless of how good the design is.
- Why it is worth your time: most target-trial guidance assumes the data can support the protocol, and this one interrogates when it cannot, because the measurement and visit process that generates EHR data is itself outcome- and treatment-dependent. It gives an operational vocabulary for "the design is fine but the estimand is not identifiable here," which is exactly the failure mode routine emulations paper over. Short and pointed, and it belongs next to the identifiability results in the other three sections. According to PubMed, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-02563-z).
- Cost: 30 min, moderate, needs target-trial basics and informative-visit or measurement bias.
- Pairs with: finalist B1 and finalist A2, the same "the thing you assumed you had is not identifiable or not consistent" pattern in different fields.

### 5. How to Estimate the Effect of Treatment Duration on Survival Outcomes Using Observational Data (foundational)
Miguel A. Hernan. BMJ 360:k182, 2018. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.k182

- The move: the canonical three-step clone-censor-weight recipe, built on the deceptively simple insight that dead people do not deviate from their assigned strategy.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the paper the entire clone-censor-weight literature quietly rests on, yet it is written as a four-page how-to you can hand a student, assign by intended rather than achieved duration, clone, censor at deviation, then reweight by inverse probability of censoring. It is the clean constructive resolution to finalist C1's landmark critique, the identifiable way to compare sustained strategies without manufacturing immortal time. Read the two together to see a bias diagnosed and then dissolved. According to PubMed, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.k182).
- Cost: 20 min, low, needs inverse probability weighting and censoring.
- Pairs with: finalist C1, the negative result it answers, and the grace-period and dynamic-regime emulations that use it.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. Weak Information Geometry: Riemannian Structures from Distributional Inference Functions and Stein Discrepancies
Rodrigo Labouriau. arXiv:2607.11246, 2026-07-13 (rev 2026-08-04). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11246

- The move: build a metric geometry from estimating functions rather than likelihoods, any instrument with full-rank sensitivity induces the Godambe information as a metric, and Fisher-Rao is the special case dominated in Loewner order.
- Why it is worth your time: this widens the domain of information geometry instead of redecorating it, and the geometry is not optional, the flagship examples are models with no likelihood at all, including a Cantor-distributed location model and parameter-dependent support where no Fisher-Rao manifold exists. Weak inferential separation becomes block-diagonality of the Godambe metric, and in a stochastic-heat-equation example the stabilization rate is the spectral gap of the discrete Laplacian, so the geometry becomes a diagnostic with teeth. It builds the muscle of asking what the metric is when there is no likelihood, which is where most information-geometry intuition quietly breaks.
- Cost: 2.5 hours, hard, needs estimating equations and Godambe information, Stein discrepancy, and tempered distributions.
- Pairs with: Barndorff-Nielsen observed-versus-expected geometry, and the estimating-function tradition as the missing inference bridge.

### 2. Ergotropic and passive contributions on the phase-space information geometry of Gaussian states
Ivan Medina, Camila Raupp, Pedro B. Melo, Diogo O. Soares-Pinto. arXiv:2607.09855, 2026-07-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09855

- The move: the Wigner-Fisher metric of a Gaussian state splits exactly into a passive part, the Wigner-entropy rate of the passive state, and an ergotropic part measuring how displacement and squeezing move the state through phase space, so extractable work provably constrains the geometric line element.
- Why it is worth your time: a genuine cross-import that pays rent, ergotropy is a thermodynamic quantity and here it reappears as a named piece of a Fisher metric, not a metaphor for one. The concrete payoff is an ergotropic Mpemba effect traced to the anomalous geometric evolution of squeezed thermal states. It sharpens the instinct that a thermodynamic resource decomposition and a metric decomposition are frequently the same equation read two ways.
- Cost: 2 hours, medium-hard, needs Gaussian and continuous-variable quantum states, the Wigner function, ergotropy, and quantum Fisher information.
- Pairs with: finalist D5, the classical fluctuation-geometry ancestor of "thermodynamics constrains the metric," and the thermodynamic-length lineage in the log.

### 3. Information Geometry meets Functional ANOVA: An Exact Fisher-Information Decomposition, with an Application to Radio Luminosity Functions
Marko Imbrisak, Kresimir Tisanic. arXiv:2608.05374, 2026-08-05. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05374

- The move: the Fisher information matrix decomposes exactly and additively along Hoeffding-Sobol, or functional ANOVA, channels, main effects, interactions, and cross-channel terms, as a channel-resolved pullback of the ambient Fisher metric.
- Why it is worth your time: one clean idea executed cleanly and freshly, a Sobol variance accounting applied to parameter information instead of output variance, with additivity as a theorem rather than an approximation. It localizes where in the data each parameter is constrained and exposes the compensating sloppy channel combinations directly as metric blocks, a sharper handle on sloppiness than eigenvalue-staring. The radio-luminosity-function application near the turnover shows the decomposition earning its keep rather than illustrating it.
- Cost: 90 min, medium, needs Fisher information and Sobol or ANOVA variance decomposition.
- Pairs with: finalist D4 and the sloppy-model manifold literature, both about reading structure off the Fisher metric.

### 4. A Tractable Pseudo-Metric on Non-Parametric Exponential Statistical Manifolds via SPD Geometry
Amit Vishwakarma, K. S. Subrahamanian Moosath. arXiv:2607.11092, 2026-07-13. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11092

- The move: embed an exponential family into symmetric positive definite matrices via the expected outer product of augmented sufficient statistics, and the pulled-back affine-invariant metric differs from Fisher-Rao by an explicit third-cumulant term that vanishes exactly for Gaussians.
- Why it is worth your time: the correction term is the gem, it names precisely how Fisher-Rao and the SPD-embedding metric disagree and pins the disagreement to skewness, recovering the Calvo-Oller Gaussian case as the point where the correction dies. The SPD geometry does real work, converting an intractable infinite-dimensional geodesic problem into a closed-form, moment-computable distance, and it hands back an affine-invariant, bandwidth-free two-sample test as a byproduct. It builds the habit of asking which cumulant a given metric is blind to.
- Cost: 2 hours, medium-hard, needs exponential families, the SPD affine-invariant metric, and cumulants.
- Pairs with: finalist D3 on decomposing the Fisher metric, and finalist B5, another quantity set by which moment survives.

### 5. Riemannian geometry in thermodynamic fluctuation theory (older classic)
George Ruppeiner. Reviews of Modern Physics 67(3):605-659, 1995; erratum 68:313, 1996. https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.67.605

- The move: the entropy Hessian is a Riemannian metric on thermodynamic state space, and its scalar curvature is not decoration, its magnitude measures the correlation volume and diverges at criticality with the correlation length.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the load-bearing ancestor of the entire 2024-2026 thermodynamic-geometry industry, square-well fluids, QCD crossover, black-hole chemistry, that keeps reinventing curvature-sign and R-crossing arguments. Reading the original shows the covariance and consistency reasoning those follow-ups assume, and why curvature equals interaction strength rather than being a coincidence. It is the best single antidote to treating "curvature diverges at the critical point" as folklore, and it sits directly under this week's ergotropic Gaussian result.
- Cost: long, 4 hours full or 90 min skim, medium, needs fluctuation theory, basic Riemannian geometry, and Gaussian statistics.
- Pairs with: finalist D2, the quantum-thermodynamic descendant, and the criticality-and-sloppiness thread that connects to efficient coding near criticality in the log.

## Threads

### Thread 1: The property you assume you have is the exception, not the default
Linero's Dirichlet-process inconsistency (A2), Freedman 1963 (A5), the PDE barriers to mixture identifiability (B1), and the operational EHR emulation framework (C4) are one argument in four registers. A consistent posterior, an identifiable mixing measure, and an identifiable causal estimand are all things practitioners assume their setup already has, and each paper shows the property fails for natural, non-pathological reasons. The mechanisms are different and that is the interesting part, a hyperprior tail (A2), the topology of prior space (A5), a differential operator's null space (B1), and an outcome-dependent measurement process (C4). The tension worth holding, A2 and A5 say the failure is about the prior you chose and is in principle fixable by choosing better, B1 says the obstruction is baked into the kernel family and no choice removes it, and C4 says it lives in a data-generating process you may not even control.

### Thread 2: The clean object, and its correction
Two impulses run against each other here. A1 shows BART is a Gaussian process and A3 shows a martingale diffusion is a posterior, both finding that a messy procedure is secretly a clean, familiar object. D1 (Godambe generalizes Fisher-Rao), D3 (the Fisher metric is a sum of ANOVA channels), D4 (Fisher-Rao differs from the SPD metric by a skewness term), and B5 (the sample-complexity rate is set by the lowest surviving moment) go the other way, the content is not the base object but the correction or the generalization the base object misses. The through-line is the remove-the-structure test, delete the limit, the h-transform, the estimating function, the cumulant, or the invariant and the result collapses. The split is a real disagreement about where meaning sits, in the clean limit or in the residual the clean limit throws away.

### Thread 3: Dynamics as a selection principle, and the price of the path
Larsson's Langevin diffusion drifting to singular strata (B2), online PCA paying a log tax for being sequential (B3), gradient descent undergoing a flip bifurcation at the edge of stability (B4), the ergotropic decomposition constraining geometric motion by extractable work (D2), and Ruppeiner's curvature diverging at criticality (D5) are all about where a process on a manifold goes and what the path costs. B2 gives a mechanism for why noisy gradient methods prefer degenerate minima, B4 names the normal-form behavior of edge-of-stability training, and B3 prices the cost of streaming. The geometry pair reads motion thermodynamically, and the deepest cross-link is that B2 and D5 both tie a manifold's singular or critical geometry to where the dynamics concentrate, degenerate strata in one, the critical point in the other.

### Thread 4: Name the estimand, or the number answers no question
The landmark critique (C1), VENAI (C2), the positivity-driven estimand selection (C3), and the clone-censor-weight recipe (C5) are the target-trial section's insistence that a reported number is not a causal target until you say which one. C1 shows a landmark hazard contrast estimates a contaminated mixture, C2 shows a naive vaccine effect is two effects that can cancel, C3 makes the population you target an explicit design choice, and C5 gives the constructive fix that makes a duration effect identifiable at all. Gnedin's rank construction (A4) is the Bayesian flank, an estimand, the probability of beating a latent benchmark, defined operationally from the ground up. The disagreement with Thread 1 is instructive, that thread says identifiability often fails, this one says the prior question is usually whether the target was ever well defined.

## Synthesis

This week the useful content of a method is either a fragility you did not know it had or the one object that governs it once you strip everything else away. The fragility cluster (A2, A5, B1, C4) argues that consistency and identifiability are special, not generic, and that they fail through mechanisms as ordinary as a hyperprior tail or a clinic visit schedule, so the honest default is to prove you have the property rather than assume it. The invariance cluster (A1, A3, D1, D3, D4, B5) argues the complementary point, that behind a messy estimator there is usually a clean limit, a generalized metric, or an algebraic invariant, and the discipline is to find it and then ask whether the interesting part is the object or the correction the object discards. The two clusters share a test, remove the load-bearing structure and the result should collapse, and the papers that pass it are the ones worth your time. The dynamical section (B2, B3, B4, with D2 and D5 on the geometry side) supplies the third theme, that a process on a landscape is a selection principle, drifting toward degenerate strata, paying a measurable tax for going online, or bifurcating at a sharp threshold, and that where it settles and what the path costs are often the same equation read from two ends. Where the week genuinely disagrees is temperament. The estimand and invariance papers trust structure, name the target or the invariant and the rest follows, while the fragility and converse papers trust nothing until they have proved where it breaks. Ruppeiner (D5) and Larsson (B2) sit at the most interesting seam, both connecting the singular or critical geometry of a manifold to where the dynamics pile up, which is exactly the register the criticality and control-energy questions live in. Two questions worth sitting with. First, if noisy gradient dynamics provably prefer the more singular strata (B2) and thermodynamic curvature diverges where correlations do (D5), is there a single statement that reads degeneracy-seeking and criticality-seeking as the same drift. Second, if the interesting content of an information metric is repeatedly the correction term, the skewness in D4, the ergotropic piece in D2, the sloppy channel in D3, does an aging or degrading system show up as one whose correction terms grow until the base geometry stops describing it.

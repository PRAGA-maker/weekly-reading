## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Seeing the Forest for the Trees: The Gaussian Process Limit of BART
Cory McCartan, Melody Huang. arXiv:2607.28844, stat.ME, 30 Jul 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28844

- The move: as the number of trees grows, BART converges to a Gaussian process with an explicit kernel, so its performance is an RKHS property, not tree magic.
- Why it is worth your time: prior BART theory chased posterior contraction rates that blow up with dimension. This flips the frame. Send the trees to infinity, read off the limiting kernel and its RKHS, and BART's behavior becomes a statement about a known function space. The random tree features give a usable finite approximation with minimax rates. It is a clean reframe of a method most people treat as a black box, and it builds the muscle of taking a nuisance hyperparameter to its limit to expose structure.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate, needs comfort with RKHS and GP priors.
- Pairs with: Lo (1984) below, and any kernel view of ensembles.

### 2. Calibrated Bayesian Inference for Stochastic Intervention Effects
Tyler M. Schmidt, Nathan B. Wikle. arXiv:2608.02924, stat.ME, 3 Aug 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02924

- The move: a post hoc correction on posterior samples that leaves the prior and sampler untouched yet delivers asymptotically efficient estimates and frequentist-valid intervals for stochastic intervention effects.
- Why it is worth your time: nonparametric Bayes gives flexible causal estimation but its posterior uncertainty need not be calibrated for the causal functional. Rather than rebuild the model, they patch the output and prove the patched posterior is efficient. Stochastic interventions also weaken the positivity requirement, which is where deterministic-treatment estimands strain. This is the calibration-versus-coherence tension handled with a scalpel.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate to hard, needs semiparametric efficiency and influence functions.
- Pairs with: the target trial estimand papers in section C, and martingale posteriors below.

### 3. Generalised Robust Bayes for Joint Inference of Model and Contamination
Masahiro Fujisawa, Masaki Adachi, Takuo Matsubara. arXiv:2607.25665, stat.ME, 28 Jul 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25665

- The move: Hölder-Bayes, a generalized posterior over both the model parameter and the contamination proportion, so robustness stops being merely qualitative and starts naming which observations are anomalous.
- Why it is worth your time: standard generalized Bayes swaps the log likelihood for a robust divergence and buys insensitivity to contamination, but it cannot tell you how much contamination there was or where. Applying the Hölder divergence to a scaled density turns the contamination fraction into an inferred quantity with global bias-robustness guarantees. It closes a real gap in the robust Bayes program and states its boundedness conditions plainly.
- Cost: 35 min, moderate, needs divergence-based inference and influence-function robustness.
- Pairs with: model criticism and prior-data conflict work.

### 4. Inference on counterfactual distributions using martingale posteriors
Gregor Steiner, Mark Steel. arXiv:2607.24143, stat.ME, 27 Jul 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24143

- The move: estimate entire counterfactual outcome distributions, not average effects, by running the martingale posterior of Fong et al. on a Dirichlet process mixture predictive.
- Why it is worth your time: average treatment effects hide the shape of an effect distribution. Building the target from a predictive recursion means uncertainty on any derived functional, quantiles, tails, distributional contrasts, comes out coherently without committing to a parametric outcome model. It is a concrete demonstration that the predictive-first view of Bayes buys you causal quantities the estimand-first view makes awkward.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate, needs predictive resampling and DP mixtures.
- Pairs with: the target trial estimand papers, and section D's interventional score geometry.

### 5. On a Class of Bayesian Nonparametric Estimates: I. Density Estimates
Albert Y. Lo. Annals of Statistics 12(1), 351-357, 1984. https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176346412

- The move: the paper that made the Dirichlet process mixture a density estimator, convolving a DP with a kernel and giving the posterior an explicit mixture form.
- Why it is worth your time: three of the four picks above stand on this. Martingale-posterior counterfactuals use DP mixtures as the underlying model, the BART limit lives in the same nonparametric-prior world, and the modern generalized-Bayes machinery inherits Lo's tractability instinct. Read the original to see how much of current practice was already load-bearing in seven pages. This is the timeless entry this week; it beats padding the recent list with a weaker paper.
- Cost: 30 min, moderate, classic notation but short.
- Pairs with: Ferguson (1973) and the normalized-random-measure literature.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. Dead Directions: Geometric Singular Learning
Tejas Pradeep Shirodkar. arXiv:2606.05957, cs.LG, 4 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05957

- The move: one primitive, the dead direction, a unit vector along which the Fisher metric degenerates, and a proof that its KL order is recoverable as the decay rate of directional Fisher curvature in the original coordinates.
- Why it is worth your time: singular learning theory computes Bayesian invariants after resolving singularities, information geometry works in original coordinates under a non-degeneracy that overparameterized models violate constantly. This bridges the two vocabularies with a single geometric object and shows they name the same vector. The payoff is that you can read the local learning coefficient's ingredient without blowing up coordinates. It is the cleanest cross-domain import of the week; remove the geometry and there is no result.
- Cost: 50 min, hard, needs singular learning theory and Fisher geometry.
- Pairs with: section D throughout, especially the weak information geometry and Ricci curvature picks.

### 2. Zero Generalization Error Theorem for Random Interpolators via Algebraic Geometry
Naoki Yoshida, Isao Ishikawa, Masaaki Imaizumi. arXiv:2512.06347, cs.LG, 6 Dec 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06347

- The move: in a teacher-student setup, once the sample count passes a threshold the generalization error of interpolators hits exactly zero, and the proof runs through algebraic geometry rather than any property of SGD.
- Why it is worth your time: the dominant story credits SGD's implicit bias for good interpolators. This argues the model itself is responsible, since even randomly sampled interpolators generalize past a sample threshold, and it proves the exact-zero claim with algebraic-geometry machinery. It is a direct, load-bearing tension with the implicit-bias literature, and the kind of surprising sharp result that changes what you think is doing the work.
- Cost: 50 min, hard, needs algebraic geometry and teacher-student risk analysis.
- Pairs with: the volume hypothesis debate and Dead Directions above.

### 3. Approximate Message Passing with Random Initialization for Phase Retrieval
Yuchen Chen, Yandi Shen, Xingyu Xu. arXiv:2608.01654, math.ST, 3 Aug 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01654

- The move: an exact trajectory analysis of AMP from a random start for noiseless phase retrieval, showing random initialization reaches the weak-recovery threshold delta = 1/2 despite an initial overlap of order d to the minus one half.
- Why it is worth your time: classical fixed-time state evolution breaks when the initial overlap is vanishing and recovery needs a growing number of iterations. They prove a Gaussian decomposition of the AMP path and control the error over the whole recovery horizon, pinning the threshold. This is a genuine sample-and-iteration complexity result for a canonical nonconvex problem, done without the usual spectral warm start.
- Cost: 55 min, hard, needs high-dimensional asymptotics and state evolution.
- Pairs with: spectral initialization results and the geometry of nonconvex recovery.

### 4. Relative Positions Generalize, Absolute Positions Memorize: An Implicit-Bias Account of Length Generalization in Attention
Subham Singh, Ashutosh Mishra, Subha Raut. arXiv:2607.18759, cs.LG, 21 Jul 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18759

- The move: an optimization explanation, not an expressivity one, for why rotary encodings extrapolate to longer sequences and learned absolute encodings do not.
- Why it is worth your time: the usual accounts ask whether a length-generalizing solution exists. This asks which of the fitting solutions gradient descent actually selects, and proves that rotary encodings make the attention logit a function of relative offset alone, so the selected solution transfers. It is learning theory sitting on top of a real transformer phenomenon, which is exactly the sweet spot for this domain, and it reframes a widely cited empirical regularity as an implicit-bias fact.
- Cost: 35 min, moderate, needs attention mechanics and implicit-bias arguments.
- Pairs with: implicit-bias-of-gradient-descent results and positional-encoding studies.

### 5. Stochastic Saddle Avoidance Beyond Unit Excitation and Smoothness: A Pathwise Lyapunov-Perron Framework
Junwen Qiu, Bohao Ma, Andre Milzarek. arXiv:2608.03001, math.OC, 4 Aug 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03001

- The move: an almost-sure saddle-avoidance theorem that drops the unit-excitation assumption, so noise that vanishes near stationarity or lives in a low-dimensional data-dependent subspace no longer breaks the argument.
- Why it is worth your time: unit excitation, positive noise in every direction, is convenient and false for exactly the regimes we train in, interpolation models and finite sums. Removing it via a pathwise Lyapunov-Perron construction makes saddle avoidance a statement about the noise structure that actually occurs. It is an optimization-geometry result that earns its generality by matching real gradient noise rather than a textbook idealization.
- Cost: 50 min, hard, needs stochastic approximation and stable-manifold arguments.
- Pairs with: edge-of-stability and SGD-noise-geometry work.

## C. Target trial emulation

Discovered through PubMed. DOI links resolve to the journal of record.

### 1. Vaccine Efficacy Against Naturally Asymptomatic Infections: A Novel Estimand for Quantifying Vaccine Effects
Elizabeth Rogawski McQuade, Razieh Nabi, Allison Codi, Natalie Dean, Marc Lipsitch, David Benkeser. Epidemiology, 22 Jul 2026. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000002021

- The move: the naive asymptomatic-infection efficacy compares apples to a mixture, since it folds together preventing asymptomatic infection and converting symptomatic cases to asymptomatic, and when the second effect is strong a good vaccine looks harmful.
- Why it is worth your time: they build a principal-stratification estimand, VENAI, for efficacy among those who would naturally be asymptomatic, which excludes the conversion effect and behaves like a proper analogue of the standard efficacy measures. They give identification assumptions, assumption-free bounds, and a sensitivity analysis, then apply it to the mRNA-1273 trial where VENAI comes out much higher than the naive number. This is estimand hygiene surfacing a bias that routine reporting buries.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate, needs principal stratification.
- Pairs with: the directly-averted estimand below, and section D's interventional score geometry.

### 2. Non-random selection with and without bias due to selecting on an exposure
Chanelle J. Howe, Han-Chih T. Hsieh, Jason R. Gantenberg, Arman Oganisian, Charles B. Eaton, Haidong Lu. Epidemiology, 7 Jul 2026. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000002018

- The move: selecting on an exposure level does not always bias the effect estimate, and whether it does is decided by the causal structure and the chosen estimand, not by the mere fact of non-random selection.
- Why it is worth your time: the literature drills when selection bias occurs and underteaches when it does not, which leaves people over-correcting and mis-arguing with reviewers. Working through diagrams and simulations, they map the settings where selecting on exposure is safe, with code. It builds the discipline of asking which estimand you want before declaring a design broken. A small paper that sharpens a reflex most analysts get wrong.
- Cost: 30 min, easy to moderate, needs causal diagrams.
- Pairs with: the plasmode-simulation caution below.

### 3. A Cautionary Note for Plasmode Simulation Studies in the Setting of Causal Inference
Pamela A. Shaw, Susan Gruber, Brian D. Williamson, Rishi Desai, Susan M. Shortreed, Chloe Krakauer, Jennifer C. Nelson, Mark J. van der Laan. Statistics in Medicine, Aug 2026. https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.70676

- The move: one of the two common plasmode simulation frameworks can make certain estimators appear overly biased, and the artifact survives at large sample sizes and even on randomized-trial data.
- Why it is worth your time: plasmode simulation is how method comparisons get their credibility in pharmacoepidemiology, so a framework that fabricates bias in a good estimator quietly corrupts the evidence base for which method to trust. They give a theoretical result and simulations pinning the failure to how the resampled data and the probabilistic model are combined, then say which framework preserves the properties you need. This is a negative result about the tools we use to validate tools.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate, needs simulation design and estimator evaluation.
- Pairs with: the selection-bias teaching note above.

### 4. Causal Effects on Nonterminal Event Time With Application to Antibiotic Usage and Future Resistance
Tamir Zehavi, Uri Obolski, Michal Chowers, Daniel Nevo. Statistics in Medicine, Jul 2026. https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.70650

- The move: existing principal-stratification estimands for semi-competing risks throw away patients for whom the effect is well defined and clinically interesting, so they define a larger stratum, infected-or-survivors, and target the feasible-infection causal effect on it.
- Why it is worth your time: comparing future antibiotic resistance across treatments is hard because some patients only survive under one arm, and the standard survivor-average trick shrinks the population past the point of interest. Widening the principal stratum and identifying the effect through two coupled illness-death models with a cross-world correlation parameter is a real estimand-design move, not a plug-in. It teaches how to pick the subpopulation the science cares about instead of the one the math finds convenient.
- Cost: 45 min, hard, needs principal stratification and illness-death models.
- Pairs with: competing-risks and semi-competing-risks methodology.

### 5. Defining and Estimating Outcomes Directly Averted by a Vaccination Program when Rollout Occurs Over Time
Katherine M. Jia, Christopher B. Boyer, Alyssa Bilinski, Marc Lipsitch. Epidemiology, 19 Jan 2026. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000001953

- The move: the popular hazard-difference estimator for deaths directly averted by vaccination is biased even in a randomized trial, because it ignores the faster depletion of susceptibles among the unvaccinated.
- Why it is worth your time: a lot of pandemic impact accounting used exactly this estimator, and the paper shows it overstates averted outcomes whenever the vaccine works, with the overstatement large for averted infections at high reproduction number and high efficacy. They define the directly-averted estimand cleanly, give an unbiased estimator in a one-stage trial, and extend it to avertible outcomes. A surprising-in-an-RCT result is a strong gem, and this one has direct policy reach.
- Cost: 35 min, moderate, needs survival estimands and infectious-disease dynamics.
- Pairs with: the VENAI estimand above.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. Weak Information Geometry: Riemannian Structures from Distributional Inference Functions and Stein Discrepancies
Rodrigo Labouriau. arXiv:2607.11246, math.ST, 13 Jul 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11246

- The move: any inference instrument with full-rank sensitivity, a Stein representation, a weak regular inference function, a positive Schwartz kernel, induces a Godambe-information Riemannian metric, and Fisher-Rao is the special case where the instrument is the score.
- Why it is worth your time: the class of models you can treat as Riemannian manifolds is far larger than Fisher-Rao once you work in tempered distributions and separate the law from the instrument that probes it. The Godambe form G = S^T V^{-1} S becomes the metric, which quietly geometrizes estimating-equation and Stein-discrepancy inference. It is a reframe that widens the whole information-geometry toolkit past its usual boundary.
- Cost: 50 min, hard, needs Godambe information and distribution theory.
- Pairs with: Dead Directions in section B, and estimating-equation inference.

### 2. Quantization of Ricci Curvature in Information Geometry
Carlos C. Rodriguez. arXiv:2603.10054, cs.IT, 9 Mar 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10054

- The move: a 20-year-old conjecture resolved, the volume-averaged Fisher Ricci scalar is quantized to positive half-integers for tree and complete-graph Bayesian networks, false in general, with explicit loop counterexamples.
- Why it is worth your time: it proves the quantization via a universal Beta-function cancellation, then honestly kills it in the loopy case, and extends to Gaussian DAGs where a sign dichotomy appears, discrete networks curve positively and Gaussian ones form solvable Lie groups with negative curvature. The mix of a clean positive theorem, a clean disproof, and a structural dichotomy is exactly the honest framing this list rewards. Curvature here is doing real classification work, not decoration.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate to hard, needs Fisher-Rao curvature and graphical models.
- Pairs with: singular-learning geometry and the correlation-matrix curvature result below.

### 3. Exact Likelihood and Sampling for Riemannian Gaussian Distributions on Correlation Matrices
Kisung You. arXiv:2608.02593, stat.ME, 3 Aug 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02593

- The move: build a proper Riemannian Gaussian on full-rank correlation matrices under quotient-affine geometry, and find that the normalizing constant varies with the center, so exact maximum likelihood and Frechet estimation aim at different population targets.
- Why it is worth your time: correlation matrices are a quotient, and honoring both quotient distance and quotient volume forces a normalizer that is not constant, which is where the surprise lives. The consequence, that two estimators everyone treats as interchangeable have different estimands, is a real statistical bite coming straight out of curvature. It also delivers exact score equations and chart-based sampling, so it is usable, not just cautionary.
- Cost: 45 min, hard, needs Riemannian geometry of SPD and correlation manifolds.
- Pairs with: the Ricci-quantization paper above, and any Frechet-mean methodology.

### 4. Interventional Score Geometry for Causal Inference
Mojtaba Eslami. arXiv:2607.21914, stat.ME, 24 Jul 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21914

- The move: the score field of the observational density cannot identify causal direction, so define an interventional score on the submanifold that a hard intervention restricts you to, and read causal influence off its variation.
- Why it is worth your time: observationally equivalent structural models share the same score geometry, which is a clean statement of why geometry-from-p alone fails at causation. The fix is to treat do(X = xi) as a restriction to the slice x_k = xi and define the score on the remaining free coordinates, turning intervention into a geometric operation. It is the bridge this week between the geometry section and the target trial section, and it makes the estimand's geometry explicit.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate to hard, needs score-based modeling and causal do-calculus.
- Pairs with: the martingale-posterior counterfactuals in section A, and the target trial estimands in section C.

### 5. On Conservative Statistical Riemann Surfaces
Hanwen Liu. arXiv:2605.09133, math-ph, 9 May 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09133

- The move: a correspondence between information geometry and gauge theory, where a conservative statistical structure on a surface forces the Chebyshev 1-form to be harmonic and the traceless Amari-Chentsov tensor to descend to a holomorphic cubic differential.
- Why it is worth your time: it imports Higgs-bundle and Tzitzeica-equation machinery, then shows conservative statistical structures are generated by solutions to that equation, generalizing the Labourie-Loftin correspondence. The borrowed structure is load-bearing; strip the gauge theory and there is no moduli statement left. It is the most ambitious cross-pollination on the list and a reminder that the Amari-Chentsov tensor is a geometric object with a life outside statistics.
- Cost: 60 min, very hard, needs Riemann surfaces, Higgs bundles, and affine differential geometry.
- Pairs with: the weak-information-geometry reframe above, and geometric views of exponential families.

## Threads

### Thread 1: A degenerate geometry is the signal, not the failure
Dead Directions (B1), Quantization of Ricci Curvature (D2), Weak Information Geometry (D1), and the correlation-matrix Riemannian Gaussian (D3) all take a spot where the classical Fisher setup breaks and read information off the break. Dead Directions makes a rank-deficient Fisher metric the carrier of the learning coefficient. Weak Information Geometry replaces the score with a broader instrument when the Fisher case is too narrow. The correlation-matrix paper lets center-dependent curvature split two estimators apart. The throughline: non-degeneracy was always an assumption of convenience, and dropping it is where the content is.

### Thread 2: Intervention is a restriction, and the estimand has a geometry
Interventional Score Geometry (D4), martingale-posterior counterfactuals (A4), calibrated stochastic-intervention inference (A2), and the two vaccine estimands (C1, C5) all treat an intervention as an operation on a distribution and insist on getting the target object right before estimating. Eslami restricts to a submanifold, Steiner and Steel push a predictive forward to a counterfactual distribution, Schmidt and Wikle patch a posterior to a valid causal interval, and the vaccine papers name the exact contrast the naive estimator confuses. The tension worth holding: score geometry says direction is unidentifiable from observation alone, while the target trial papers show even a randomized comparison can point at the wrong estimand.

### Thread 3: The feared bias is conditional
Non-random selection with and without bias (C2), the plasmode-simulation caution (C3), and the zero-generalization-error theorem (B2) each attack a reflex. Selection on exposure is assumed to bias and often does not. Plasmode simulation is assumed to validate methods and can manufacture bias. Interpolation is assumed to demand implicit-bias rescue and, past a sample threshold, generalizes on its own. Read together they argue for checking the structure before invoking the standard worry, and for distrusting the machinery that certifies our worries.

### Thread 4: Nonparametric predictive Bayes is the quiet engine
Lo (1984) (A5), martingale-posterior counterfactuals (A4), the BART Gaussian-process limit (A1), and Hölder-Bayes robustness (A3) all run on flexible nonparametric priors and predictive recursions. Lo built the DP mixture density estimator, the counterfactual paper runs a martingale posterior on exactly that object, BART turns out to be a GP in the limit, and generalized Bayes inherits the same tractability instinct while adding robustness. The recent work is less a departure than a compounding of a forty-year-old move.

## Synthesis

The strongest current this week is that degeneracy is informative. A Fisher metric that loses rank, a curvature that quantizes then fails on loops, a normalizer that refuses to be constant, an interpolator that should overfit and does not: in each case the classical regularity assumption was hiding the interesting object. That runs directly against the habit of treating singular or non-standard regimes as pathologies to regularize away, and it rhymes with how singular learning theory earns its keep. The target trial papers make the same point in a different key. A comparison that looks clean, naive asymptomatic efficacy, directly-averted deaths, selection on exposure, carries a bias that only a careful estimand exposes, and sometimes the fix is to widen the population or restrict the distribution rather than adjust an estimator. Where the papers disagree is on what does the work in generalization: the algebraic-geometry theorem says the model, the length-generalization paper says the optimizer's implicit bias, and both are proven, not asserted, so the disagreement is real and localizable. Two questions worth sitting with. First, if interventions are restrictions to submanifolds, is there a single geometric account that covers the target trial estimand, the counterfactual distribution, and the interventional score, or do they only rhyme? Second, when is a degenerate Fisher direction a nuisance to quotient out, as in the correlation-matrix paper, versus the carrier of the learning coefficient, as in Dead Directions, and what distinguishes the two cases before you compute. The connection to aging as a dynamical system is genuine but faint here; the sharper link this week is between optimization geometry and identification, where control over which solution or which estimand you land on is the whole game.

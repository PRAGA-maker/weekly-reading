# Weekly Reading List, 08-26-26

The spine this week is specification: which of the structure you carefully wrote down actually survives contact with the data, and which of it was decoration. Bayesian and target trial both hand you negative results about wasted specification effort (elaborate prior dependence washes out; the naive per-protocol contrast is not the estimand you named). Geometry and learning theory both reason about the shape of a solution set rather than a trajectory, and one classic in each domain is doing the load-bearing work the recent papers lean on. Network access confirmed against arXiv, PubMed, and journal DOIs. TTE picks verified via PubMed metadata; the two paywalled classics (Dawid, Wootters) verified via Semantic Scholar records.

## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Post-Processing Posterior Predictive P-values
Nils Lid Hjort, Fredrik A. Dahl, Gunnhildur Högnadóttir Steinbakk. arXiv:2605.24169 (stat.ME), 2026-05-22. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24169

- **The move:** Takes the posterior predictive p-value, a diagnostic everyone uses and quietly knows is not uniform under the model, and manufactures a calibrated version that is, through a double-simulation scheme.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The ppp's non-uniformity is a folk fact people wave away; this makes the correction operational and, more usefully, turns the calibrated value into a common ruler you can lay across different priors, competing models, and parametric-versus-nonparametric specifications. It builds the model-criticism reflex of distrusting a diagnostic until you know its null distribution, then the craft of building that null. Hjort's involvement means the asymptotics are done, not gestured at.
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate, needs posterior predictive checks and simulation-based calibration.
- **Pairs with:** the Dawid classic below, which frames calibration as a demand a Bayesian imposes on themselves rather than an external audit.

### 2. Posterior Ramifications of Prior Dependence Structures
Luke Hagar, Nathaniel T. Stevens. arXiv:2312.06437 (stat.ME), 2023-12-11. https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06437

- **The move:** A clean negative result. Carefully elicited copula dependence in a multivariate prior cannot survive into the posterior asymptotically; the data wash the dependence structure out.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the rare paper that tells you when not to do work. It exposes the mismatch between the effort practitioners pour into rich prior dependence and the vanishing footprint that effort leaves on inference, and it says precisely when the elicitation is load-bearing versus decorative. A quiet 2023 paper that never went viral, whose punchline sharpens your sense of what a prior actually controls once data arrive.
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate, needs multivariate priors and basic large-sample posterior behavior.
- **Pairs with:** the Polson martingale pick below, the mirror image: there you must specify more than you think, here less of what you specified matters.

### 3. The Well-Calibrated Bayesian
A. Philip Dawid. Journal of the American Statistical Association 77(379):605-610, 1982. https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1982.10477856

- **The move:** Proves a coherent Bayesian expects to be well-calibrated on their own forecasts, then follows the theorem to its self-undermining edge: no forecaster can be calibrated against every sequence.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Everyone cites this and almost no one reads it. The reframe, calibration as an internal consistency demand a Bayesian imposes on themselves rather than a frequentist audit imposed from outside, seeds the entire modern calibration and game-theoretic-probability literature. The twist at the close, that coherence and universal calibration are incompatible, is exactly the honest limit the current Bayesian workflow discourse keeps rediscovering forty years later.
- **Cost:** 45 min, easy to moderate, no machinery, just careful reading.
- **Pairs with:** Foster and Vohra on calibration attainability, the descendants of Dawid's closing remark.

### 4. Martingale Posterior Predictive Coherence: Hausdorff Moment Hierarchy
Nicholas G. Polson, Daniel Zantedeschi. arXiv:2603.00661 (stat.ME), 2026-02-28. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00661

- **The move:** For exchangeable Bernoulli data the map from posterior mean to the k-step-ahead predictive is set-valued for k at least 2. The first moment does not pin down multi-step prediction; you need the full terminal law.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It locates a precise foundational gap in the martingale-posterior program by routing it through the classical Hausdorff moment problem: predictive coherence is a statement about all moments, and stopping at the mean leaves a genuine set of admissible futures. A teachable result that connects de Finetti-style exchangeability to a named piece of classical analysis and clarifies what specifying the predictive actually costs.
- **Cost:** 90 min, moderate to hard, needs exchangeability, moment problems, and the martingale posterior framework.
- **Pairs with:** Fong, Holmes, and Walker's martingale posterior paper, the framework this stress-tests.

### 5. The Dirichlet Process as Sampling Distribution
Luis E. Nieto-Barajas. arXiv:2607.26185 (stat.ME), 2026-07-28. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26185

- **The move:** Flips the Dirichlet process's job. Instead of a prior over unknown measures, treat the DP itself as the data-generating law and do inference on its centering measure and precision.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A genuine role-inversion: the object every Bayesian nonparametrician uses as a prior becomes a likelihood, which forces you to confront the DP's sampling properties, a thing rarely examined head-on. Worth it for the perspective shift alone, the kind of reframe that makes you re-derive familiar facts and notice which ones secretly depended on the prior-role framing.
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate, needs Dirichlet process basics.
- **Pairs with:** Ferguson's original 1973 DP paper, read against the grain here.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. The Sharp SAT/UNSAT Phase Transition in Random Ellipsoid Fitting
Theodor Misiakiewicz, Garrett G. Wen. arXiv:2608.10184 (math.ST/math.PR), 2026-08-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10184

- **The move:** Proves the ellipsoid-fitting conjecture with a razor-sharp SDP-feasibility threshold at exactly n roughly d squared over 4.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Can a centered ellipsoid pass through n random Gaussian points has resisted a sharp constant for a decade; here the transition is pinned exactly, with a genuinely different toolkit on each side, Gaussian comparison plus exact constraint correction for feasibility, projected-Gordon escape for infeasibility. This is the how-few-measurements-suffice muscle at its cleanest, and the two-sided asymmetry of the proof is itself the lesson. A feasibility threshold that actually arises, not a pathological construction.
- **Cost:** 3 hours across two sittings, hard, needs SDP duality, Gaussian comparison inequalities, random matrix bulk arguments.
- **Pairs with:** the Gardner classic below, the same reasoning about the shrinking volume of a solution set, thirty-eight years earlier.

### 2. Zeroth-Order Optimization at the Edge of Stability
Minhak Song, Liang Zhang, Bingcong Li, Niao He, Michael Muehlebach, Sewoong Oh. arXiv:2604.14669 (cs.LG), 2026-04-16. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14669

- **The move:** First-order edge of stability is governed by the top Hessian eigenvalue; zeroth-order stability is secretly governed by the entire Hessian spectrum, the trace.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A crisp surprise with a mechanism. Because ZO gradient estimates inject isotropic probing noise, mean-square stability picks up every eigenvalue, so large ZO step sizes implicitly regularize the Hessian trace rather than the top eigenvalue, a different implicit bias than everyone assumes by analogy to SGD. It rewards the optimization-geometry-explains-a-real-phenomenon muscle and lands as ZO fine-tuning of large models comes into vogue. Honest framing: they give the practical two-parameter stability bound.
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate, needs the quadratic-model edge-of-stability picture and mean-square stochastic stability.
- **Pairs with:** "A Defense of the Quadratic Model" (2607.21716, already in the log), the first-order baseline this departs from.

### 3. Dropout and Random Gradient Masking Are Asymptotically Equivalent in Large ResNets
Javier Maass, Lénaïc Chizat. arXiv:2607.16761 (stat.ML), 2026-07-18. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16761

- **The move:** Two mechanistically opposite tricks, masking activations (dropout) and masking gradients, collapse to the same limiting feature-learning dynamics at large depth and width.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The reframe pays rent: the usual dropout story about co-adaptation and forward-pass corruption is shown to be inessential, because random gradient masking leaves the forward pass untouched and injects only unbiased update noise yet reaches the identical mean-field limit. It is an X-is-secretly-Y result that quietly demotes the standard folk explanation for why dropout works. Chizat's mean-field machinery is load-bearing: remove the large-depth ResNet scaling and the equivalence dissolves.
- **Cost:** 2 hours, moderate to hard, needs mean-field feature-learning limits of deep nets.
- **Pairs with:** Bordelon and Pehlevan on feature-learning infinite-width limits, for the scaling that makes this go through.

### 4. How Many Samples Are Needed to Determine Causal Direction? Sharp Minimax Bounds for Bivariate LiNGAM
Jikai Jin. arXiv:2608.15840 (math.ST/stat.ML), 2026-08-16. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15840

- **The move:** A sharp local-minimax law for calling the arrow direction that separates identification coming from non-Gaussianity from identification coming from covariance and scale.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Instead of another LiNGAM-is-identifiable existence statement, it quantifies exactly how hard the identification is and which structural signal, edge strength, distance from Gaussianity, or scale uncertainty, is actually carrying it, including regimes where non-Gaussian dependence is nearly useless and covariance does the work. That decomposition is the taste: identifiability with a sample-complexity price tag and a mechanism for where the information lives. The two-variable object keeps the derivation teachable.
- **Cost:** 90 min, moderate, needs Le Cam or Fano minimax lower-bound technique and the LiNGAM setup.
- **Pairs with:** Shimizu et al.'s original LiNGAM identifiability paper, the existence result this sharpens, and the Perényi vaccine-waning pick in TTE, another where-does-the-signal-live decomposition.

### 5. The Space of Interactions in Neural Network Models
Elizabeth Gardner. Journal of Physics A: Mathematical and General 21(1):257-270, 1988. https://doi.org/10.1088/0305-4470/21/1/030

- **The move:** Computes the volume of weight space that stores p random patterns and finds a sharp capacity phase transition at alpha equals p over N, the origin of phase transitions in learning.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Nearly every modern result on this list, capacity thresholds, storage and interpolation transitions, the whole replica lineage behind today's high-dimensional learning phase diagrams, descends from this four-page calculation, yet the ML crowd routinely reinvents it uncited. It is the archetype of the taste you are hunting: reason about the geometry of the solution set, its shrinking volume, rather than about a single optimizer's trajectory, and the transition falls out. Reading it makes the ellipsoid-fitting pick above feel like the same family.
- **Cost:** 90 min, moderate, short but dense, the replica-symmetric volume computation takes patience.
- **Pairs with:** Gardner and Derrida's companion "Optimal storage properties of neural network models," same volume, same year.

## C. Target trial emulation

### 1. What Phases of the Drug Development Framework Can Epidemiological Studies Emulate?
Stephen Burgess, Benjamin Woolf, Emily Bassett, David Robertson, Sofía Villar, Jeremy A. Labrecque. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag194

- **The move:** Reframes "which trial are you emulating" as "which phase of drug development are you emulating," and argues natural experiments including Mendelian randomization are best read as early-phase target trials.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It imports the phase-1-through-4 ladder into the target-trial vocabulary and makes a genuinely useful distinction: a quasi-random comparison that answers a fuzzier proof-of-concept question can be preferable to a policy-relevant comparison riddled with confounding. The estimand from an early-phase emulation is a means to an end, not the end itself. It gives you a clean typology for when emulation should chase relevance and when it should chase internal validity. Per PubMed, DOI [10.1093/aje/kwag194](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag194).
- **Cost:** 40 min, low math, needs to know what MR and per-protocol effects are.
- **Pairs with:** "The Ideal Trial" (already in the log), the relevance-versus-feasibility tradeoff from a different angle.

### 2. Observational Studies Analyzed Like Randomized Experiments: An Application to Postmenopausal Hormone Therapy and Coronary Heart Disease
Miguel A. Hernán, Alvaro Alonso, Roger Logan, Francine Grodstein, Karin B. Michels, Walter C. Willett, JoAnn E. Manson, James M. Robins. Epidemiology 19(6):766-779, 2008. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0b013e3181875e61

- **The move:** The load-bearing sleeper. The paper that first emulated a trial as a nested sequence of trials and thereby dissolved the Nurses' Health Study versus Women's Health Initiative hormone-therapy contradiction.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is where the modern machinery actually comes from: conceptualize eligible women as initiators or noninitiators across a sequence of emulated trials, reproduce the WHI intention-to-treat estimate from observational data, then inverse-probability-weight for adherence. It shows the famous discrepancy was largely time since menopause and length of follow-up, the framing, not the data, produced the conflict. Everyone cites Hernán 2016 and 2022; far fewer read the 2008 paper that made them possible. Per PubMed, DOI [10.1097/EDE.0b013e3181875e61](https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0b013e3181875e61).
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate, needs intention-to-treat versus per-protocol and IPW basics.
- **Pairs with:** the Danaei statins emulation (in the log), the second worked example of the same template.

### 3. Per-Protocol Analyses of Pragmatic Trials
Miguel A. Hernán, James M. Robins. New England Journal of Medicine 377(14):1391-1398, 2017. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsm1605385

- **The move:** The clean, non-technical derivation of why the naive per-protocol analysis, censor at deviation and compare compliers, is biased, and what to do instead.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It is the reference that separates the intention-to-treat estimand from the per-protocol estimand and shows that recovering the latter requires IP-weighting or g-estimation for post-randomization confounding, the exact reasoning underneath clone-censor-weight. Short, example-driven, and the conceptual spine of this month's fresh per-protocol papers. Load-bearing pedagogy you can hand a student. Per PubMed, DOI [10.1056/NEJMsm1605385](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsm1605385).
- **Cost:** 30 min, low, no prerequisite beyond trial basics.
- **Pairs with:** Dahabreh and Hernán, "The Role of Assignment in Defining and Identifying Causal Effects" (already in the log).

### 4. Confusion in Evaluating Research Quality Comes from Lack of Clarity: The Case for Explicit Research Questions
Matthew P. Fox, Amelia Wesselink, Katrina L. Kezios, Tara E. Jenson, Marcia Pescador-Jimenez. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag201

- **The move:** Uses a public "correlation is not causation" broadside as a foil to argue that most methodological confusion is a question-specification failure, not a design flaw.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It insists description, prediction, and causation are three distinct estimands with different assumptions and evaluation criteria, and that naming which one you are after, an estimand before an estimator, is what makes an observational study critiquable or defensible at all. This is the specify-the-causal-question register applied as a rebuttal to a real critique, and it is honest about the limits of what design alone can buy. Per PubMed, DOI [10.1093/aje/kwag201](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag201).
- **Cost:** 30 min, low, no math, no prerequisite.
- **Pairs with:** "Clarifying Causal Questions: Anatomy of a Causal Estimand" (in the log), and the Bayesian specification thread this week.

### 5. A Simple and Powerful Test of Vaccine Waning
Gellért Perényi, Matias Janvin, Mats J. Stensrud. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag118

- **The move:** Reframes "is efficacy waning" as a formal test of whether an individual-level treatment effect is constant over time, sidestepping the uninterpretable classical efficacy-over-time curve.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Existing causal waning estimands can only be bounded, and the bounds are usually too wide to conclude anything; this trades the point estimand for a hypothesis test that is valid under interpretable assumptions and far more powerful, computable from summary data alone. The payoff is a surprising result: reanalyzing the BNT162b2 trial, it rejects no-waning where a prior analysis could not. A clean case of choosing the right target to make a question answerable. Per PubMed, DOI [10.1093/aje/kwag118](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag118).
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate to hard, needs potential outcomes and comfort with bounds under monotonicity.
- **Pairs with:** "Vaccine Efficacy Against Naturally Asymptomatic Infections" (in the log), the same group's estimand-first mindset.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. Thermodynamic Geometry of Friction on Graphs: Resistance, Commute Times, and Optimal Transport
Jordan R. Sawchuk, David A. Sivak. arXiv:2601.01273 (cond-mat.stat-mech), 2026-01-03 (v3 2026-07-30). https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01273

- **The move:** The thermodynamic friction metric, the commute-time embedding, the resistance distance, and discrete L-2 Wasserstein transport are literally the same object on a graph.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the cross-domain-import muscle at its best. Four communities, linear-response thermodynamics, random walks, circuit theory, optimal transport, have been computing one metric under four names. Sivak's group does not just note the analogy; they show resistance-distance algebra computes the dissipation metric, so cost of a slow protocol becomes Joule heating routing probability through dynamical bottlenecks. Remove the geometry and there is no result, the whole paper is the identification. The cleanest instance of geometry paying rent this week.
- **Cost:** 2 hours, moderate, needs near-equilibrium linear-response thermodynamics and graph Laplacians, no heavy differential geometry.
- **Pairs with:** Crooks, "Measuring thermodynamic length" (in the log), the graph-native sequel.

### 2. Thermodynamic Geometry Through Second Order Phase Transitions
Omer M. Basri, Oren Raz. arXiv:2512.01936 (cond-mat.stat-mech), 2025-12-01. https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01936

- **The move:** The friction metric diverges at a critical point, yet the thermodynamic length across the transition stays finite, and the optimal protocol sometimes deliberately drives through the critical point rather than around it.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The naive picture, metric blows up so avoid the transition, is wrong. Using Widom scaling per universality class, they show the geodesic length integrand's divergence is integrable in some classes, so the minimal-dissipation path can cross criticality even when a single-phase detour exists. That is a genuinely counterintuitive optimal-control claim falling straight out of geometry plus scaling theory, and it warns you against reading a metric singularity as a barrier. Honest about which universality classes it holds in.
- **Cost:** 3 hours, moderate to hard, needs critical exponents and Widom scaling plus thermodynamic length.
- **Pairs with:** the Sawchuk friction pick above, static equivalence there versus dynamics through a singularity here.

### 3. Gating Enables Curvature: A Geometric Expressivity Gap in Attention
Satwik Bathula, Anand A. Joshi. arXiv:2604.14702 (cs.LG/stat.ML), 2026-04-16. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14702

- **The move:** Ungated attention is confined to flat statistical manifolds by its affine structure; multiplicative gating is exactly what unlocks positively curved Fisher-Rao geometry, a provable expressivity gap.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The reframe survives the decorated-method audit cleanly. Modeling attention outputs as Gaussian mean-parameters and reading off the induced Fisher-Rao curvature, they prove ungated layers cannot reach curved manifolds and gated ones can, then confirm gated models help precisely on nonlinear-decision-boundary tasks and not on linear ones, so the geometry makes a falsifiable prediction that holds. The depth-amplification of curvature under composition is a teachable mechanism most geometry-of-transformers papers only gesture at. A rare gated-attention paper where Fisher does the actual work.
- **Cost:** 90 min, moderate, needs exponential-family and Fisher-Rao basics plus comfort with attention.
- **Pairs with:** the Gardner and ellipsoid picks in learning theory, curvature and feasibility of the representable set from a different door.

### 4. Statistical Distance and Hilbert Space
William K. Wootters. Physical Review D 23(2):357-362, 1981. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.23.357

- **The move:** The statistical distinguishability of two quantum states, defined purely by measurement fluctuations, that is, the Fisher-Rao metric, equals the Hilbert-space angle between their rays.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The load-bearing sleeper. Everyone cites Braunstein-Caves and quantum Fisher information for metrology; this is the paper where the identity statistical distance equals geometric angle was first forced out of the structure of quantum measurement, and it silently underwrites half the quantum-Fisher-at-criticality papers flooding arXiv this month. Six pages, no machinery, one idea that reframes what a Hilbert-space inner product means operationally. Read it and the recent quantum-geometry transport papers stop looking like vocabulary.
- **Cost:** 60 min, easy to moderate, needs intro quantum mechanics and the idea of Fisher information.
- **Pairs with:** the Sun-Nagaosa pick below, the 1981 seed and the 2026 many-body payoff.

### 5. Quantum Information Geometry of Multicomponent Superconducting Fluctuation Transport
Zi-Ting Sun, Ying-Ming Xie, Naoto Nagaosa. arXiv:2606.15928 (cond-mat.supr-con), 2026-06-14. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15928

- **The move:** Longitudinal paraconductivity is the quantum Fisher information of superconducting fluctuation modes, and the fluctuational anomalous Hall effect is the mean Uhlmann curvature, two measurable transport coefficients read directly off the quantum-geometric tensor of the Cooper-pair propagator.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Cross-domain rent paid in a real material system. Starting from time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau, they identify the fluctuating-pair density matrix and show standard transport quantities are geometric invariants of it, then derive a geometric bound between longitudinal and Hall responses with no analogue in normal electronic transport. That bound is the tell: the geometry is not reinterpreting known physics, it is predicting a constraint you could not have written down otherwise. Nagaosa doing information geometry is worth the attention on its own.
- **Cost:** 3 hours, hard, needs Ginzburg-Landau superconducting fluctuations and mixed-state Uhlmann geometry.
- **Pairs with:** the Wootters classic above for the metric's origin.

## Threads

**1. Which specified structure is load-bearing.** Hagar-Stevens (Bayesian 2), Polson-Zantedeschi (Bayesian 4), Burgess (TTE 1), Fox (TTE 4), Dawid (Bayesian 3). Every one of these is about the gap between what you write down and what actually controls the answer. Hagar shows elaborate prior dependence washes out asymptotically, so the elicitation effort was decorative; Polson shows the opposite failure, that the posterior mean underspecifies multi-step prediction and you must commit to the full terminal law. Fox and Burgess make the same point in causal language: name the estimand and the phase of question before the estimator, or the critique lands on the wrong target. The tension between Hagar and Polson is the useful part: specification is neither always wasted nor always sufficient, and knowing which regime you are in is the skill.

**2. Geometry of the solution set, not the trajectory.** Gardner (learning 5), Misiakiewicz-Wen (learning 1), Sawchuk-Sivak (geometry 1), Wootters (geometry 4), Basri-Raz (geometry 2). These reason about the shape, volume, or metric of a feasible or representable set rather than about an optimizer's path through it. Gardner counts the shrinking volume of weight space to get a capacity transition; ellipsoid fitting pins a feasibility threshold the same way; Sawchuk and Wootters each show a single distance is four or two things at once. Basri supplies the counterexample that keeps you honest: a metric can diverge while the length it induces stays finite, so reading a singularity in the geometry as a barrier in the problem is a mistake. Geometry reveals structure, but only if you check what it actually measures.

**3. Where the information lives.** Jin (learning 4), Song (learning 2), Perényi (TTE 5), Bathula-Joshi (geometry 3). Each decomposes a nominal quantity into the component that carries the operative signal. Jin splits causal-direction identification into a non-Gaussianity part and a covariance-scale part and finds regimes where each dominates; Song splits optimization stability into top-eigenvalue versus full-trace and shows zeroth-order noise switches which one governs; Perényi trades an unbounded waning estimand for a test that concentrates the available power; Bathula isolates gating as the single ingredient that unlocks curvature. Same object, different load-bearing component, and identifying which one changes the problem's character.

## Synthesis

The through-line this week is that specification and identification are two names for the same discipline: deciding what you are actually asking before you compute, and knowing which part of your apparatus carries the answer. Bayesian and target trial converge on it from opposite traditions, one warning that carefully elicited prior dependence evaporates, the other that the naive per-protocol contrast was never the estimand you named. Learning theory and information geometry converge on a second version, that the honest object of study is often the shape of a set (of solutions, of representable manifolds, of feasible protocols) rather than a trajectory or a single number. The papers disagree productively about how much structure survives: Hagar says less than you think, Polson says you must commit to more, Basri says the geometry itself can mislead you about where the barriers are. Two connect directly to control energy and criticality, Basri's protocols that drive through a critical point rather than around it and Sawchuk's dissipation-as-routing metric, which is the kind of thing worth holding next to control-energy views of aging as a dynamical system: minimal-cost paths sometimes go through the singularity, not around it. The Wootters-to-Nagaosa arc is a reminder that a forty-five-year-old six-page identity can still be the load-bearing lemma under a flood of current papers, which is the strongest argument for reference chasing over recency. Two questions worth sitting with. First, when is a metric singularity a real obstacle and when is it an integrable artifact of the coordinates, and is there a general criterion beyond the case-by-case scaling arguments Basri gives. Second, if identification signal can be decomposed into a component that carries it (Jin's non-Gaussianity versus covariance, Song's trace versus top eigenvalue), can the same decomposition be run on a target trial emulation to say which part of the design is buying identification and which part is decorative, the causal analogue of Hagar's washout result.

# Weekly Reading List, 09-09-26

A week organized around one idea seen from four sides: updating is optimization on a dual manifold, and the Fisher metric is the accountant that prices what the update costs. Bayesian and target trial both deliver clean deflationary results this week, and the strongest threads run from a learning-theory degeneracy to a Bayesian identification failure to an epidemiology assumption that turns out to be optional. Network access confirmed against arXiv and PubMed. TTE picks verified via PubMed metadata; DOI links included.

## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Learning rate selection via weighted Fisher divergence
Takahiro Onizuka. arXiv:2606.26478 (stat.ME), 2026-06-25. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26478

- **The move:** The generalized-Bayes learning rate is chosen in closed form by minimizing a weighted Fisher divergence between the general posterior's sandwich-variance Gaussian limit and the ideal normal, and the standard information-matching rate falls out as a special case that is provably never smaller.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The learning-rate-under-misspecification problem is usually solved by bootstrap loops or SafeBayes-style calibration, and this collapses it to a single closed-form quantity you can compute once. The result that the popular Lyddon-Holmes-Walker information-matching rate is recovered as a corollary and sits as an upper bound is the kind of "your method is the ceiling of mine" statement worth internalizing. Single author, low citation, directly on the loss-based-updating thread you have been building.
- **Cost:** 45 min, moderate, needs Gibbs posteriors and sandwich asymptotics.
- **Pairs with:** "When Is Generalized Bayes Bayesian?" (2602.01573) in the log, and entry 3 below, its geometric cousin.

### 2. Nonparametric Bayesian Inference for Partially Identified Discrete Response Models
Elie Tamer, Christopher D. Walker. arXiv:2608.25814 (econ.EM), 2026-08-26. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25814

- **The move:** Put a Gaussian-process prior on the conditional choice probabilities and ordinary posterior sampling on the reduced form pushes forward to Bayesian inference on the identified set, with no moment conversion and no covariate discretization.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Partial identification is exactly where Bayesian inference gets accused of letting the prior do the identifying, and this puts the prior on the reduced form instead and derives posterior consistency for the true identified set. The GP conjugacy is load bearing: remove it and the closed-form set inference collapses, which is the test for whether an import pays rent. Coming from a leading partial-identification econometrician, the framing about what the prior is and is not doing is careful rather than hand-waved.
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate to hard, needs partial identification basics and GP priors.
- **Pairs with:** entry 4 in the target trial section, Greenland and Cole on when identification conditions actually bind.

### 3. A Generalization of Amari's Bayesian Duality
Mohammad Emtiyaz Khan, Thomas Möllenhoff. arXiv:2609.09126 (cs.AI), 2026-09-08. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09126

- **The move:** Amari's old Bayesian duality is an instance of a convex duality of Bayes' rule itself, and the generalization links exact Bayes, natural-gradient variational updates, and information geometry under one dual object.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is a real reframe from the two people who built the Bayesian-learning-rule program: Bayes' rule as the dual of a convex problem, with the dual variables carrying the geometry. It reconnects a 1980s information-geometry result to modern approximate inference, which is the sort of bridge that generates follow-on work rather than a single result. Short and idea dense, not benchmark driven.
- **Cost:** 40 min for the idea, longer to chase the geometry; hard, needs exponential families, convex duality, information geometry.
- **Pairs with:** Zellner below and "The Dually Flat Geometry of Planning as Inference" (entry D2), the same duality worn as physics and as RL.

### 4. Posterior concentration and adaptation of the mixing measure in Dirichlet process mixtures
Filippo Ascolani. arXiv:2606.29109 (math.ST), 2026-06-27. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29109

- **The move:** For a DP mixture of a true finite mixture the posterior adapts to the correct number of components and contracts near-optimally on the mixing measure, yet resolving that measure below n^(-1/2) provably requires the number of active components to grow like log n.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It states a genuine tension cleanly: the model recovers the right K, but the object you actually sample from must keep spawning clusters at a logarithmic rate, and O(log n) truncation is both necessary and sufficient to mimic the exact posterior. That turns the folklore complaint that "the DP uses too many clusters" into a precise, teachable rate. Single-author theory that names the load-bearing lemma rather than burying it.
- **Cost:** 60 min, hard, needs posterior contraction machinery and mixture identifiability.
- **Pairs with:** "Asymptotic regimes for MLE in the Ewens-Pitman model" (2606.13554) in the log, and entry B3 in learning theory below on singular model selection.

### 5. Optimal Information Processing and Bayes's Theorem (timeless)
Arnold Zellner, with discussion by Jaynes, Hill, Kullback, Bernardo. The American Statistician 42(4):278-280, 1988. https://doi.org/10.1080/00031305.1988.10475585

- **The move:** Bayes' theorem is derived as the solution to an optimization problem, the information-processing rule that outputs exactly the information it was given and is therefore 100% efficient, rather than posited as an axiom.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the ancestor of the whole "Bayes as free-energy minimization" and generalized-variational-inference literature, and reading it makes entry 3 above look inevitable rather than novel. Six pages plus a discussion by four giants, so you get the immediate objections in the same sitting, including where the efficiency criterion is doing quiet work. Under-read relative to how often its idea is now silently assumed.
- **Cost:** 30 min, easy to moderate, needs entropy and KL basics.
- **Pairs with:** entry 3 above, its direct modern continuation.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. The Multiple Timescales of Gradient Descent on the Edge of Stability: A Perturbative Derivation of the Central Flow
Raphaël Berthier. arXiv:2609.01034 (cs.LG), 2026-09-01. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01034

- **The move:** The empirically fit central-flow model of edge of stability falls straight out of a textbook singular-perturbation expansion of gradient descent, cleanly separating fast oscillation along the sharp direction, intermediate self-stabilization, and slow drift along constrained minimizers.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The central flow was a striking but phenomenological object, and this shows it is a classical multiple-scales problem, which is a real "this mess is secretly a standard method" reframe. The ε to 0 constrained-gradient-flow limit gives a rigorous handle on why sharpness stays pinned rather than a fitted curve. It is the rare edge-of-stability paper you could derive on a whiteboard.
- **Cost:** ~2 hrs, moderate, needs gradient flow, method of multiple scales, basic Hessian dynamics.
- **Pairs with:** the bifurcation-theory edge-of-stability entry (2606.15551) in the log, a different mathematics for the same phenomenon.

### 2. Minimax Optimal Early-Stopped Gradient Descent for Gaussian Mixture Classification
Alex Buna, Shirley Xiaoqi Liu, Patrick Rebeschini. arXiv:2608.06250 (stat.ML), 2026-08-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06250

- **The move:** Early stopping beats interpolation not by a constant but by an exponential sample factor, so the max-margin implicit bias of gradient descent is provably the wrong bias here.
- **Why it is worth your time:** "Interpolation can require exponentially more samples than early stopping" is exactly the sharp separation that punctures the benign-overfitting default, stated for a model that actually arises rather than a pathology. A new calibration trick converts excess logistic risk to zero-one risk under label noise without the usual square-root penalty, which is what buys minimax optimality. This is a mechanism-level claim about when the implicit bias of GD is actively harmful, not another upper bound.
- **Cost:** 2-3 hrs, moderately hard, needs minimax rates, logistic and margin dynamics, the Gaussian mixture model.
- **Pairs with:** the Soudry-style implicit-bias-of-logistic-GD line, whose limits it marks.

### 3. Asymptotics for Model Selection in Probabilistic Principal Component Analysis
Mathias Drton, Andrew McCormack, Daniel Windisch. arXiv:2608.23513 (math.ST), 2026-08-24. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23513

- **The move:** Singular learning theory fixes BIC's over-penalization for choosing the number of PCA components, because the Fisher information degenerates at the boundary between component counts, and the resulting sBIC is consistent.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is singular learning theory applied to a regime that genuinely arises, rank-deficient Fisher information at a model boundary, not one of the pathological constructions the field is prone to. The correction is a real, usable model-selection rule with a guarantee, and it extends to the PPCA-versus-factor-analysis choice. A clean example of standard asymptotics breaking and the right replacement being written down.
- **Cost:** ~2 hrs, moderately hard, needs BIC and marginal likelihood, real-log-canonical thresholds, PPCA.
- **Pairs with:** "Dead Directions: Geometric Singular Learning" (2606.05957) in the log, and entry A4 above on where the Fisher information collapses in a Bayesian mixture.

### 4. Lehmer Transform
Masoud Ataei. arXiv:2609.05790 (math.ST), 2026-09-05. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05790

- **The move:** Encode a positive dataset as one analytic curve of consecutive power-sum ratios whose logarithm is a free-energy increment in an exponentially tilted ensemble, then prove identifiability breaks exactly at geometric growth.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The idiosyncratic-sleeper pick. It imports statistical-mechanics free-energy machinery to build a location-statistic curve, and its rigidity theorem is a crisp negative result: past geometric growth, resonant log-periodic families of distinct laws collapse onto the same curve, a hard limit on recovery. Multiplicative-noise deconvolution by pointwise division falls out as a surprising byproduct. High variance, exactly the kind of under-cited cross-domain idea this list exists to surface.
- **Cost:** 90 min, moderate but unusual notation, needs power sums and moment problems, exponential tilting, a little complex analysis.
- **Pairs with:** classical moment-problem identifiability, and entry C3 below in information geometry on free energy as geometry.

### 5. Risk Equivalence between RKHS Regression and Sequence Models for Lipschitz Spectral Algorithms
Yicheng Li, Yuqian Cheng, Zhuo Chen, Qian Lin. arXiv:2609.08817 (math.ST), 2026-09-08. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08817

- **The move:** Any Lipschitz spectral kernel estimator, ridge, gradient flow, stable GD, has asymptotically the same risk as the Gaussian sequence model with the matching filter, so hard kernel analysis reduces to a diagonal toy.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A clean reduction that recovers minimax rates and, more notably, the exact Pinsker constants for RKHS regression across classical and high-dimensional regimes. Collapsing a whole family of spectral algorithms onto one transparent sequence-model calculation is the kind of unification that makes future proofs cheap. Sharp constants, not just rates, which is where most kernel theory stops.
- **Cost:** 2.5 hrs, hard, needs RKHS and kernel ridge, the Gaussian sequence model, Pinsker's theorem.
- **Pairs with:** classical nonparametric minimax theory, and the geometry-of-estimation lineage in section D.

## C. Target trial emulation

### 1. Negatives about positivity and consistency as conditions for causal inference
Sander Greenland, Stephen R. Cole. American Journal of Epidemiology 195(9):2416-2422, 2026. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag093)

- **The move:** Two assumptions every emulation recites, positivity and consistency, are demoted, positivity to a technical requirement of specific estimators rather than of causal inference, and consistency to a demand for a well-defined outcome variable.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Greenland argues that dwelling on positivity distracts from the real practical threat, large-sample approximation failure that reports rarely check, and that consistency conflates construct ambiguity with coarsening. This is a direct, contrarian counterweight to the checklist way the field teaches the target-trial assumptions, from the person who named half the concepts. It will change how you read the assumptions paragraph of every emulation, and it reframes what "identified" is buying you.
- **Cost:** 45 min, moderate, needs potential-outcomes vocabulary but no math.
- **Pairs with:** entry A2 above on partial identification, the Bayesian version of the same worry about when identification conditions bind.

### 2. Quantifying the impact of immortal time bias: empirical evidence from meta-analyses
Min Seo Kim, Dong Keon Yon, Seung Won Lee, ... John P. A. Ioannidis. Journal of the Royal Society of Medicine 118(9):293-303, 2025. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/01410768251366880)

- **The move:** Instead of a toy simulation, harvest 25 real meta-analysis topics covering 182 studies and measure how much immortal time bias actually moves published effect sizes.
- **Why it is worth your time:** 44% of studies were affected; biased studies gave effect sizes 29% larger in favor of the intervention on average, and in a quarter of topics dropping them flipped statistical significance. It converts a bias everyone gestures at into a citable number, with the Ioannidis meta-research fingerprint on the design. Read it against entry 3, which argues the scary numbers are partly an artifact of which studies get chosen.
- **Cost:** 40 min, easy to moderate, needs forest plots and heterogeneity.
- **Pairs with:** entry 3 below, its direct antagonist.

### 3. Immortal time bias tends to be more pronounced in methodological studies than in empirical studies: a metaepidemiological study
Xia Zhang, Jiayue Xu, Qiao He, ... Wen Wang, Xin Sun. Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 187:111936, 2025. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2025.111936)

- **The move:** The dramatic before-and-after corrections you see in immortal-time methods papers may be worst-case demonstrations, not the typical damage in applied studies.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Comparing 46 empirical against 21 methodological studies, conclusion flipping from Mantel-Byar or landmark correction was roughly twice as common in the methods papers, which suggests the teaching literature selects vivid cases. It is a quietly self-critical result about how the field advertises its own biases. Sitting it next to entry 2 gives you the actual open question, how bad is immortal time bias in the wild, as a matched pair rather than a slogan.
- **Cost:** 35 min, moderate, needs Mantel-Byar and landmark methods.
- **Pairs with:** entry 2 above, the reason to hold both in mind.

### 4. Demystifying Clone-Censor-Weighting to Studying Treatment Initiation Windows
Michael Webster-Clark, Yi Li, Sophie Dell'Aniello, Robert W. Platt. Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety 34(10):e70223, 2025. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/pds.70223)

- **The move:** A hands-on clone-censor-weight tutorial that is honest about the uncomfortable part, that the estimand for "initiate within 30 versus 90 days" shifts with the population's actual timing, so you may not be estimating what you think.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It runs CCW end to end on public synthetic Medicare data, reproducible in a way most tutorials are not, and foregrounds that comparing initiation windows produces contrasts that vary substantially across study populations. The candor about estimand instability is the teachable lesson, not the mechanics. The most concrete recent piece for someone who wants to run clone-censor-weight rather than admire it.
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate, needs survival analysis and inverse-probability-of-censoring weights.
- **Pairs with:** "How to Estimate the Effect of Treatment Duration on Survival Outcomes" (bmj.k182) in the log, the same grace-period problem by other means.

### 5. Double Negative Control Inference in Test-Negative Design Studies of Vaccine Effectiveness
Kendrick Qijun Li, Xu Shi, Wang Miao, Eric Tchetgen Tchetgen. Journal of the American Statistical Association 119(547):1859-1870, 2023. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2023.2220935)

- **The move:** The test-negative design's folklore that it self-corrects for healthcare-seeking behavior is false, because conditioning on being tested is a collider, and a pair of negative controls can actually identify vaccine effectiveness in the target population.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It separates two distinct biases baked into the design, residual healthcare-seeking confounding and collider-stratification selection bias, then shows proximal machinery with negative-control exposure and outcome variables repairs both, with a Michigan COVID application. More statistically demanding than the rest of this section and correspondingly under-read outside methods circles, a genuine sleeper for anyone taking test-negative estimates at face value.
- **Cost:** ~2 hrs, hard, needs proximal causal inference and bridge functions.
- **Pairs with:** "Comparison of the Test-negative Design and Cohort Design With Explicit Target Trial Emulation" (EDE 1709) in the log, the empirical companion to this identification theory.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. The Loss Floor of Denoising Score Matching: Fisher Geometry from Schrödinger Bridges
Avinash Raju, Kai Zhang. arXiv:2608.23916 (cs.LG), 2026-08-24. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23916

- **The move:** The irreducible excess in denoising-score-matching loss is exactly the trace of the Fisher-Rao metric of the conditional endpoint family, integrated along the diffusion trajectory.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It explains a real practical mystery, why raw DSM losses across noise schedules are literally incomparable, as a difference in additive geometric floors rather than model quality. The decomposition into data-determined information flow times schedule-determined weights is teachable and immediately usable when you tune a diffusion model. The geometry is load bearing: kill the Fisher-Rao term and the floor is unexplained.
- **Cost:** 75 min, moderate, needs diffusion models and score matching.
- **Pairs with:** "Attention Implements the Fisher Geometry of Exponential Families" in the log, and entry B5 above on the geometry of estimation risk.

### 2. The Dually Flat Geometry of Planning as Inference
Nikola Milosevic, Asaki Kataoka, Nicolas Hinrichs, Kenji Doya, Nico Scherf. arXiv:2609.04005 (cs.AI), 2026-09-03. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04005

- **The move:** Achievable visitation measures form a dually flat manifold with log-policies and visitation probabilities as dual affine charts, so planning as inference generalizes to nonlinear reward functionals and the TD error becomes a marginal-utility reading.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The dual-flat structure does real work, solving each nonlinear planning objective in a single natural-gradient step rather than decorating RL with the word manifold. It is a rare reinforcement-learning paper where the Amari machinery is the engine. The reinterpretation of TD error as marginal utility is a fresh teaching angle you can carry back to standard value iteration.
- **Cost:** 75 min, moderate, needs RL basics and dually flat geometry.
- **Pairs with:** entry A3 above and Zellner (A5), the same duality as convex Bayes.

### 3. Thermodynamic geometry of friction on graphs: Resistance, commute times, and optimal transport
Jordan R. Sawchuk, David A. Sivak. arXiv:2601.01273 (cond-mat.stat-mech), 2026-01-03. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01273

- **The move:** The thermodynamic friction metric of a slowly driven Markov chain is the commute-time embedding and the resistance distance, and linear-response thermodynamic length is a discrete optimal-transport cost.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A genuine four-way equivalence, thermodynamics to random walks to electrical networks to discrete optimal transport, where each identification is exact rather than analogical. It lets you import Laplacian and resistance intuition straight into dissipation calculations. A clean sleeper from the Sivak group that makes the abstract friction tensor concrete.
- **Cost:** 60 min, moderate, needs Markov chains and graph Laplacians.
- **Pairs with:** entry 5 below, its continuous-time ancestor.

### 4. The Thermodynamic Geometry of Conditional Control
J. G. G. de Oliveira Jr., M. E. R. Filippetto, T. A. B. Pinto Silva, A. C. S. Costa, R. M. Angelo. arXiv:2607.19177 (quant-ph), 2026-07-21. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19177

- **The move:** Informational measures alone cannot predict the thermodynamic value of conditional control, since informationally identical ensembles can differ, and a geometric spectral-rearrangement vector paired with energy gaps is the minimal descriptor that closes the gap.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The rare honest negative result in this corner of the field: it shows exactly where information geometry under-determines thermodynamics and supplies the missing geometric object rather than papering over it. A useful corrective to the reflex that information equals free energy. The minimality argument, that no purely informational quantity suffices, is the sharp part.
- **Cost:** 75 min, harder, needs quantum thermodynamics and feedback control.
- **Pairs with:** entry B2 in learning theory, another result that marks the limits of a comfortable default.

### 5. Thermodynamic metrics and optimal paths (timeless)
David A. Sivak, Gavin E. Crooks. Physical Review Letters 108, 190602, 2012. arXiv:1201.4166. https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.4166

- **The move:** A generalized friction tensor turns thermodynamic state space into a Riemannian manifold whose geodesics are the minimum-dissipation finite-time protocols.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the foundation the recent thermodynamic-geometry surge builds on, including entries 3 and 4 above, yet it is short and startlingly readable. The friction-tensor-as-metric move is the archetype of the geometry proving the optimality result rather than describing it after the fact. You have Crooks 2007 on thermodynamic length and Ruppeiner in the log, but not this friction-metric paper that made finite-time optimal protocols geometric.
- **Cost:** 40 min, moderate, needs linear-response statistical mechanics.
- **Pairs with:** entries 3 and 4 above, its direct lineage.

## Threads

### 1. Updating is optimization on a dual manifold
Khan and Möllenhoff (A3), Zellner (A5), and the dually flat planning paper (D2). All three say the same thing in different accents: an update, whether Bayes' rule, an information-processing rule, or a planning step, is the stationary point of a convex or variational problem on a dually flat manifold, with the dual coordinates carrying the geometry. Zellner posits Bayes as the uniquely 100%-efficient rule; the modern two relax that to generalized and approximate updates and ask what the geometry then licenses. The tension worth holding is whether efficiency is a derivation or a choice.

### 2. Where identification degenerates
Drton and coauthors on singular PPCA model selection (B3), Ascolani on DP mixtures (A4), Tamer and Walker on partial identification (A2), and Greenland and Cole on positivity and consistency (C1). Each domain hits the point where standard regularity breaks: the Fisher information goes rank deficient at a model boundary, the mixing measure cannot be resolved without unbounded clusters, the parameter is a set rather than a point, or positivity simply fails. The shared lesson is that the right response is a new object, sBIC, a growing truncation, an identified set, a demoted assumption, not a patched version of the old point estimate. This is the strongest cross-domain link this week: a learning-theory degeneracy, a Bayesian nonparametric one, and an epidemiological one are the same phenomenon.

### 3. Geometry as the thermodynamic accountant
Raju and Zhang on the DSM loss floor (D1), the friction-on-graphs equivalence (D3), Sivak and Crooks (D5), Onizuka on learning-rate selection via Fisher divergence (A1), and the free-energy curve behind the Lehmer transform (B4). The Fisher-Rao metric keeps showing up as the price of something irreducible: the dissipation of a finite-time protocol, the floor of a training loss, the right tempering of a posterior, the limit of what a dataset curve can recover. The test each passes is the same, remove the geometry and the bound has nothing to stand on.

### 4. Negative results that discipline a practice
Greenland and Cole (C1), Kim and Ioannidis (C2) against Zhang and coauthors (C3), Buna and coauthors on early stopping (B2), and the conditional-control paper (D4). Each kills or deflates a default: that positivity is always required, that interpolation is benign, that information determines thermodynamic value. The cleanest disagreement on the whole list is internal to target trial, C2 measuring immortal time bias as large and common while C3 argues the alarming demonstrations are a selection artifact of the methods literature. Read as a pair they define the open question rather than answer it.

## Synthesis

The spine of the week is that a quantity you routinely compute is often the value of an optimization you did not know you were solving, and the Fisher metric is what prices it. Bayes' rule, a planning step, and a natural-gradient update turn out to be one dual object (A3, A5, D2), and the same metric that sets a minimum-dissipation protocol also sets a diffusion model's loss floor and a posterior's learning rate (D1, D5, A1). The second, quieter theme is degeneracy: the most interesting results this week live exactly where the usual regularity fails, and they succeed by naming a new object instead of forcing the old one (B3, A4, A2, C1). The negative results are the ones most likely to change how you work. Early stopping beating interpolation by an exponential factor (B2) says the implicit bias of gradient descent is sometimes the wrong bias, and the conditional-control paper (D4) says information geometry can under-determine the physics it is supposed to explain, both worth more than a positive bound. Target trial disagrees with itself in a productive way, with C2 and C3 unable to settle how much immortal time bias actually costs, which is more honest than either alone. Where these connect to ongoing work: the singular-learning and identification thread bears directly on when a dynamical or causal model is even estimable, and the thermodynamic-geometry cluster is the natural language for control energy and dissipation in systems driven away from equilibrium. Two questions worth sitting with. First, if updating is optimization on a dual manifold, what is the estimand-level analogue, is a target trial's causal question also a stationary point of something, and would that make its identification failures geometric. Second, when the Fisher information degenerates, is the right move always to grow the model (sBIC, log-n clusters) or sometimes to accept the set and stop pretending there is a point.

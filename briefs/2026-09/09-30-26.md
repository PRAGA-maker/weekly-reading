## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. When Noise Estimation Hides Basis Misspecification in Repeated Bayesian Inverse Problems
Rares Dimitrie Grozavescu, Mark Girolami. arXiv:2609.37762 (stat.ME/math.ST/stat.ML), 2026-09-29. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37762

- **The move:** Estimating the observation-noise variance silently absorbs out-of-basis signal, so posterior coverage collapses while every standard diagnostic still reads nominal.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the good kind of negative result. The MLE noise estimate eats the energy the restricted prior cannot represent, so "learn the noise level" defeats the very check you would use to catch misspecification. They give a fix with exact finite-sample behavior, John's sphericity statistic on residual spectra across repeated problems, and are honest that it cannot see isotropic out-of-basis variation, which really is indistinguishable from a noise-scale change. Builds the instinct for when a nuisance parameter is laundering model error into apparent good fit.
- **Cost:** 40 min, moderate, needs linear Gaussian inverse problems and posterior coverage.
- **Pairs with:** the logged uniform-parametrization checks and the posterior-predictive-check learning-rate degeneracy papers, same theme of diagnostics gone blind under a hidden reparametrization.

### 2. Competitive Optimality in Testing by Betting via Bell-Cover Randomization
Aaditya Ramdas. arXiv:2609.35714 (math.ST/cs.GT/cs.IT/stat.ME), 2026-09-28. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35714

- **The move:** Import Bell and Cover's 1980 log-optimal-portfolio game into testing: multiply the numeraire e-variable by a Uniform(0,2) draw and it beats any other e-variable head-to-head with probability at least one half.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A clean import where removing the borrowed structure collapses the result. It recasts "which e-value is best" as a two-player wealth game, shows the numeraire e-variable is the competitively optimal player, that randomization helps only it, and states plainly that this buys sharp size at most alpha/2 at the cost of expected log-wealth and power. Sharpens what "optimal e-value" can even mean and builds the game-theoretic-statistics muscle.
- **Cost:** 45 min, moderate to hard, needs e-values, e-processes, and Ville's inequality.
- **Pairs with:** finalist A3, its direct ancestor, and the logged testing-by-betting and measure-valued-martingale lineage.

### 3. Competitive Optimality of Logarithmic Investment
Robert M. Bell, Thomas M. Cover. Mathematics of Operations Research 5(2):161-166, 1980. DOI 10.1287/moor.5.2.161. https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.5.2.161

- **The move:** The Kelly log-optimal portfolio, pre-multiplied by a Uniform[0,2] variable, wins a two-person zero-sum "who has more money" game against any competing randomized strategy. The growth-optimal policy is also the competitively optimal one.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the borrowed structure under A2, and a short, surprising, teach-from-it classic that statisticians mostly know secondhand through information theory. Reading it first makes the modern e-value competitive-optimality results feel inevitable rather than clever, and it is the cleanest illustration that "grows fastest in the long run" and "beats a single opponent right now" can be the same object. Reference-chased from the Ramdas paper; genuinely under-cited in the statistics literature.
- **Cost:** 30 min, easy to moderate, needs only expected-log-wealth and Kelly intuition.
- **Pairs with:** A2, and Cover's growth-rate work generally.

### 4. Semiparametric Bernstein-von Mises Theorems from Stein's Method
Paul Rosa. arXiv:2609.37939 (math.ST), 2026-09-29. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37939

- **The move:** Prove semiparametric BvM without the usual perturbation machinery by recasting the prior's influence as the prior-weighted divergence of a vector field over the model, through Stein's method with an information-geometric frame.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A derivation you could lecture from. It replaces "control how the prior tilts the posterior" with explicit local scalar quantities (functional, prior, chosen vector field), delivers quantitative Gaussian-approximation bounds, and shows functional corrections can kill posterior bias where standard BvM fails. The Stein's-method-into-Bayesian-asymptotics import is the real bet, and it trains reading semiparametric efficiency through geometry rather than empirical-process bookkeeping.
- **Cost:** 60 min, hard, needs semiparametric BvM and comfort with Stein's method and information geometry.
- **Pairs with:** the information geometry section below, and the logged "On the geometry of Bayesian inference."

### 5. Population-Level Decision Curve Analysis May Mislead the Evaluation of Prediction Model Usefulness under Subgroup Utility Heterogeneity
Junfeng Wang, Kim Zhipei Wang, Ben Van Calster, Nan van Geloven, Ewout Steyerberg, Laure Wynants. arXiv:2609.34521 (stat.ME), 2026-09-28. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34521

- **The move:** Population-level net benefit secretly sums quantities living on different implicit utility scales, so the standard decision-curve verdict can point opposite to true population utility.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A decision-theoretic negative result that names its failure mode exactly. It characterizes an "inconsistency region," widest when subgroups are similarly sized but value true positives differently, and gives the tell: opposite-signed subgroup net-benefit changes. It kills a common practice, reading one population DCA curve, and replaces it with subgroup-level DCA plus a robustness check. Same disease as improper pooling and Simpson-style reversals, but in utility space.
- **Cost:** 35 min, easy to moderate, needs the net-benefit expected-utility framing.
- **Pairs with:** the logged "When Is Generalized Bayes Bayesian" and the causal-decision-theory papers, all about whether the object you optimize is a coherent utility.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. Benign Overfitting Does Not Occur in Diffusion Models
Tyler Farghly, Benjamin Dupuis, Alain Durmus, Umut Simsekli. arXiv:2607.02671 (stat.ML), 2026-07-02. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02671

- **The move:** An impossibility theorem. Unless sample size grows exponentially in dimension, a diffusion model cannot interpolate and generalize at once, because score matching has no covariance-alignment escape hatch that ordinary regression enjoys.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The benign-overfitting and double-descent folklore got quietly assumed to carry over to diffusion; this shows it does not, and pins the failure on a concrete structural difference. Regression benefits from target-empirical covariance alignment, score matching admits none, so overfitting is irreparably harmful, and generalization is governed instead by time-smoothness of the score and early stopping. Builds the habit of asking which mechanism made benign overfitting benign, then checking whether it survives a change of loss.
- **Cost:** 45 min, hard, needs high-dimensional regression and basic score-matching.
- **Pairs with:** Bartlett, Long, Lugosi, Tsigler, "Benign Overfitting in Linear Regression" (PNAS 2020), the foil it dismantles.

### 2. The Zero Pattern of a Design Matrix Drives Multiple Descent in Over-parameterized Regression
Kevin Han Huang, Haoyu Ye, Somak Laha, Morgane Austern. arXiv:2607.24041 (stat.ML/math.ST), 2026-07-27 (v2 2026-09-10). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24041

- **The move:** Multiple descent in ridgeless regression is controlled by the combinatorial zero-pattern of the design matrix, read off via maximum matchings and the Dulmage-Mendelsohn decomposition of a bipartite graph.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The cleanest kind of import: the graph structure is not decoration, it is exactly what predicts where the variance goes singular and a new descent peak appears. Remove the combinatorics and the result collapses, which is the taste test for a borrowed tool paying rent. It reframes an empirical curve as a statement about matchings in a sparsity graph, with deterministic equivalents in the vanishing-ridge regime to back it.
- **Cost:** 40 min, moderate to hard, needs deterministic-equivalent tools for ridge regression.
- **Pairs with:** Hastie, Montanari, Rosset, Tibshirani, "Surprises in High-Dimensional Ridgeless Least Squares" (Ann. Stat. 2022).

### 3. How Deep Are Deep GPs, Really? A Sharp Threshold and a Non-Gaussian Limit for Compositional GPs
Mark Kozdoba, Shie Mannor. arXiv:2606.08218 (stat.ML), 2026-06-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08218

- **The move:** For RBF-kernel deep GPs there is a critical bandwidth r_c(d) of order sqrt(d): above it the deep composition degenerates, below it the prior converges to a non-degenerate, non-Gaussian limit with genuine coordinate dependence.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It answers "when does stacking GPs buy you anything" with a sharp threshold, not a vibe, and the interesting side is below the threshold, where the limit is non-Gaussian and multimodal. It trains spotting when a modeling abstraction, depth, is justified versus when it silently collapses to something trivial, and it is a rare case where the degenerate boundary is the informative object. Clean enough to lecture from.
- **Cost:** 35 min, moderate, needs Gaussian processes and distributional limits.
- **Pairs with:** Duvenaud et al, "Avoiding Pathologies in Very Deep Networks" (AISTATS 2014), the degeneracy warning this sharpens.

### 4. The Multiple Timescales of Gradient Descent on the Edge of Stability: A Perturbative Derivation of the Central Flow
Raphael Berthier. arXiv:2609.01034 (stat.ML/math.OC), 2026-09-01. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01034

- **The move:** Derives the empirically-fitted central-flow model of edge-of-stability training rigorously, as the leading term of a multiple-scales perturbation expansion in a loss splitting f = g + eps*h, with self-stabilization as the higher-order correction.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The central-flow picture has been mostly an empirical fit; this puts it on a singular-perturbation footing and separates three timescales cleanly: fast oscillation along the sharp direction, intermediate self-stabilization, slow drift along minimizers. It even explains why fluctuations persist when several eigenvalues sit at the stability edge. A classical applied-math technique nailing a live deep-learning phenomenon, and a derivation you could teach from.
- **Cost:** 60 min, hard, needs edge of stability and multiple-scales methods.
- **Pairs with:** Cohen et al, "Understanding Optimization in Deep Learning via Central Flows" (2024), the empirical model this grounds.

### 5. Benign Loss Landscapes Can Coexist with Worst-Case Hardness
Zach Furman, Stephan Waldchen, Yangda Bei, Liam Hodgkinson. arXiv:2609.13057 (stat.ML), 2026-09-11. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13057

- **The move:** In tree tensor networks every minimum-norm local minimum is global, yet gradient descent still cannot learn some polynomial-size targets in polynomial time, and the obstruction is high-order degenerate saddles from rank-deficiency, not bad minima.
- **Why it is worth your time:** It separates two things people conflate, "no bad local minima" and "easy to optimize," and shows the real bottleneck lives in degenerate-saddle geometry, with parity as the case study tying landscape structure to genuine computational hardness. A useful antidote to the reflex that a benign landscape means you are safe, and a clean instance of a degenerate regime being the informative object.
- **Cost:** 40 min, moderate to hard, needs tensor-network basics, saddle geometry, a little complexity intuition.
- **Pairs with:** the logged "Dead Directions: Geometric Singular Learning" (arXiv:2606.05957) on rank-deficiency as the source of degenerate structure.

## C. Target trial emulation

Target trial picks were discovered and verified through the PubMed connector; DOI links are included for each. arXiv stat.ME was unreachable this session for causal preprints, but the methods frontier here lives in these journals anyway, so coverage is not hurt. The slate skews 2025-2026 by design; the recent Epidemiology, AJE, and JASA crop is unusually on-target this cycle, and the American Statistician teaching paper doubles as the under-cited sleeper.

### 1. Comparing causal estimands from sequential nested versus single-point target trials: a simulation study
Catherine Wiener, Chase Latour, Kathleen E. Hurwitz, Xiaojuan Li, Catherine R. Lesko, Alexander Breskin, M. Alan Brookhart. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026-09-25. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag227 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42788856/)

- **The move:** The two most common ways to set time zero, one sequence of nested trials versus a single-point new-user trial, are not two estimators of one estimand. They target different estimands, and the paper quantifies the gap by simulation.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This makes the "sensitivity to the target trial choice" question concrete. It builds the muscle of reading a design as an estimand commitment rather than an analysis convenience, so a null and a signal from the same data can both be correct answers to different questions. Serious methods lineup (Brookhart, Breskin, Lesko) earns it the slot.
- **Cost:** 40 min, moderate, needs new-user design, IPTW, and what "time zero" does.
- **Note:** online-first, abstract not yet posted; the claim is grounded in the title, author list, and that it is a full simulation article rather than a letter.
- **Pairs with:** the logged review of methods for misalignment of eligibility, follow-up start, and treatment assignment.

### 2. Causal Identification Conditions for the Effect of Treatment in the Treated: Illustration Using the Northwest Germany Stroke Registry
Catherine Wiener, Paul N. Zivich, Tobias Kurth, Michele Jonsson-Funk, Alexander Breskin, Klaus Berger, Stephen R. Cole. Epidemiology 37(1):57-66, 2026-01. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000001924 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41086382/)

- **The move:** When positivity fails only on one side, some strata never get treated, the ATT is still identified under strictly weaker conditions than the ATE, so switching estimand rescues a consistent answer the ATE cannot give.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The applied punchline is a sign flip. IPTW for the ATE says tPA is harmful; SMR-weighted ATT says protective, and simulations show ATE bias grows with the nonpositivity while the ATT stays clean. It trains the instinct to choose the estimand your data can support instead of defaulting to the ATE and eating the bias. Clean teaching derivation with runnable framing.
- **Cost:** 35 min, moderate, needs propensity scores, IPTW versus SMR weighting, positivity.
- **Pairs with:** C5, since both are about the estimand being a consequential choice rather than a given.

### 3. An Example to Illustrate Randomized Trial Estimands and Estimators
Linda J. Harrison, Sean S. Brummel. The American Statistician 79(3):383-392, 2025. https://doi.org/10.1080/00031305.2025.2468399 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40801068/)

- **The move:** Takes the ICH E9(R1) five intercurrent-event strategies (treatment-policy, composite, while-on-treatment, hypothetical, principal stratum) and writes each in potential-outcome notation with a matching estimator, on one worked treatment-discontinuation example.
- **Why it is worth your time:** This is the Rosetta Stone between the regulator's estimand vocabulary and the Hernan-Robins per-protocol and g-computation machinery, and it names exactly when two strategies coincide. Under-cited because it sits in a teaching journal, not a methods flagship, but it is the cleanest single artifact for seeing that "per-protocol effect" is several distinct estimands wearing one label.
- **Cost:** 45 min, low to moderate, needs potential outcomes and basic g-computation.
- **Pairs with:** the logged per-protocol-effect and Role-of-Assignment papers.

### 4. Nonparametric Causal Inference for Optogenetics: Sequential Excursion Effects for Dynamic Regimes
Gabriel Loewinger, Alexander W. Levis, Francisco Pereira. Journal of the American Statistical Association, 2026-03-16. https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2026.2635071 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41878320/)

- **The move:** Imports "excursion effects" from mobile-health micro-randomized trials into neuroscience to build a taxonomy of identifiable dynamic-regime effects that survive deterministic positivity violations, with IPW and multiply-robust estimators and explicit identifiability conditions.
- **Why it is worth your time:** A cross-domain import that pays rent for target trial work. The recurring failure mode in clone-censor-weight emulations of sustained or dynamic strategies is positivity breaking down over follow-up; the excursion-effect construction defines contrasts that stay identifiable precisely there, instead of forcing an estimand the data cannot carry. Builds the muscle of redefining the target to match the support.
- **Cost:** 1.5 hr, hard, needs MSMs, dynamic treatment regimes, IPW, influence functions.
- **Pairs with:** history-restricted MSMs and sequentially randomized experiments; the identifiability-under-violation framing rhymes with partial identification.

### 5. Everything All at Once: On Choosing an Estimand for Multi-component Environmental Exposures
Kara E. Rudolph, Shodai Inose, Nicholas T. Williams, Ivan Diaz, Lucia Calderon, Jacqueline M. Torres, Marianthi-Anna Kioumourtzoglou. Epidemiology 37(6):807-818, 2026-07-22. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000002022 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42484456/)

- **The move:** Define the estimand as a data-supported shift (modified treatment policy) on a continuous multi-component exposure, explicitly diagnose where the shift would force extrapolation, and shrink the shift to stay inside the observed support before estimating nonparametrically.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The transferable idea is letting positivity and data support dictate the estimand, not the reverse. Even if you never touch exposure mixtures, the recipe (assess extrapolation, modify the intervention to what the data can identify, then estimate with machine learning and step-by-step code) is the discipline a rigorous emulation of a continuous or dynamic dose needs. Honest about its own failure mode, the register worth prizing.
- **Cost:** 1 hr, moderate to hard, needs shift interventions, positivity, some TMLE or one-step intuition.
- **Pairs with:** C2, both on the estimand as a choice with consequences.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. Information geometry of emergent symmetry quotients and their weak unfoldings
Arnaud Coatanhay, Angelique Dremeau. arXiv:2609.29260 (math.ST/math.DG), 2026-09-24. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29260

- **The move:** When a model limit collapses two parameters into one identifiable only up to reflection, the Fisher metric degenerates in the signed chart but stays regular in the invariant chart, and the leftover geometry is a strictly negatively curved quotient with a two-jet displacement law.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Rare in using the geometry to explain an asymptotic pathology rather than the reverse. Three regimes key on tau_n = sqrt(n) eps_n^2 (signed LAN, a critical curved Gaussian experiment, and a quotient regime with n^{-1/4} scaling), and the difference is pinned to which Amari connection you use, with holonomy jumping from SO(2) to GL+(2,R). Builds the muscle for reading identifiability loss and non-standard rates as curvature, not noise, and the CIR-OU example lets you compute a quotient Fisher metric.
- **Cost:** 90 min, hard, needs local asymptotic theory and affine connections.
- **Pairs with:** Efron's "Defining the Curvature of a Statistical Problem" (logged) as the classical counterpoint on statistical curvature and second-order efficiency.

### 2. A two-point approach to the inverse problem in information geometry
Florio M. Ciaglia, Giuseppe Marmo, Marco Pacelli, Luca Schiavone, Alessandro Zampini. arXiv:2608.16714 (math.DG/math-ph), 2026-08-17. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16714

- **The move:** Run the usual construction backwards. Instead of building a metric-affine manifold from a contrast function, recover the contrast and pre-contrast functions directly from a given metric and connection, through a two-point contrast bi-form.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Almost everyone treats the divergence as primary and the geometry as derived; this reverses the arrow and solves the inverse problem for general metric-affine manifolds with no curvature or torsion restriction, then reduces homotopically to the statistical-manifold cases. The clean derivation you can teach from, with semisimple Lie groups under Cartan-Schouten connections and Berger-metric spheres as explicit examples. It trains the habit of asking which object in a framework is actually fundamental.
- **Cost:** 2 hr, hard, needs connections and torsion plus Eguchi's contrast-function formalism.
- **Pairs with:** the logged "Reformulation Invariance and the Axiomatic Foundations of Inference" for the same what-is-primary question.

### 3. The Dually Flat Geometry of Planning as Inference
Nikola Milosevic, Asaki Kataoka, Nicolas Hinrichs, Kenji Doya, Nico Scherf. arXiv:2609.04005 (cs.AI), 2026-09-03. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04005

- **The move:** The set of achievable state-visitation measures in RL is a dually flat statistical manifold, with visitation probabilities and log-policies as the two dual coordinate systems linked by conditional entropy.
- **Why it is worth your time:** The geometry pays rent: each optimization becomes one natural-gradient step, the TD error reappears as a marginal-utility estimate, and planning-as-inference extends from linear rewards to nonlinear functionals of the visitation pattern. Remove the dually flat structure and the unification collapses, so this is a genuine import rather than a manifold-flavored restatement of soft RL. Good for building intuition on when a Bregman dual-coordinate view actually buys you an algorithm.
- **Cost:** 75 min, medium, needs MaxEnt RL basics and dually flat or Bregman geometry.
- **Pairs with:** the logged "Attention Implements the Fisher Geometry of Exponential Families" for another exponential-family-does-real-work reframe.

### 4. Stochastic Processes as Non-Metric Geodesics in Information Geometry
T. Koide, A. van de Venn. arXiv:2609.08870 (cond-mat.stat-mech/math-ph), 2026-09-08. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08870

- **The move:** Continuous stochastic processes correspond to geodesics on Gaussian statistical manifolds carrying a non-metric connection, and the physical restoring force can be charged either to scalar curvature or purely to non-metricity, exactly the gravity-theory ambiguity.
- **Why it is worth your time:** Importing non-metricity from gravitational theory is a real cross-domain move and it earns its keep: expectation-parameter geodesics split into three geometric classes, one generating bridge processes that reduce to Ornstein-Uhlenbeck or Brownian motion, and slow-driving work-minimizing protocols land exactly on the non-metric geodesics. It sharpens the sense that "geodesic" is connection-dependent, not metric-dependent, a distinction most information-geometry writing blurs.
- **Cost:** 90 min, hard, needs stochastic processes and affine connections with non-metricity and torsion.
- **Pairs with:** D3 and the logged thermodynamic-length line, for the same slow-driving optimal-protocol story from the dissipation side.

### 5. G-Exponential Families through Probability Coordinates
Manuela-Simona Cojocea. arXiv:2609.03208 (math.ST), 2026-09-02. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03208

- **The move:** Build generalized exponential families by transporting a plain exponential family on the unit interval through a probability-coordinate chart, so heavy tails and robustness come from the geometry of the chart rather than from algebraic deformation.
- **Why it is worth your time:** One sharp idea executed cleanly, and a pointed contrast with the Tsallis-Kaniadakis deformation tradition: every member is tail-equivalent to the chart density, and robustness (bounded sufficient statistics, bounded influence, sane estimation even under infinite means) falls out of geometric compactification. An under-cited single-author sleeper that teaches you to get heavy-tail behavior and robustness for free from a coordinate change instead of a new algebra. Work through the Cauchy-chart example.
- **Cost:** 60 min, medium, needs exponential-family basics and a little robust-statistics vocabulary.
- **Pairs with:** the logged "Exponential families from a single KL identity" for the minimalist-construction mood.

## Threads

**1. Negative results that kill a default (Bayesian A1, A5; Learning B1, B5; TTE C2).** The strongest current in the list. Each names a comfortable default and the specific mechanism by which it fails: learning the noise level blinds your coverage diagnostics (A1), reading one population decision curve inverts the verdict under subgroup utility heterogeneity (A5), benign overfitting does not transfer from regression to diffusion because score matching lacks covariance alignment (B1), a benign landscape is still hard because the obstruction is degenerate saddles not bad minima (B5), and the ATE eats a bias the ATT sidesteps under one-sided positivity (C2). The throughline is that "it usually works" is not a mechanism, and each paper supplies the mechanism that bounds where it stops working.

**2. Geometry as the load-bearing object, and a fight over what is primary (Bayesian A4; all of D; Learning B2).** Half the list reads behavior off the geometry of the model rather than off the sampler or the optimizer: semiparametric bias as a vector-field divergence (A4), identifiability loss as curvature and holonomy (D1), planning as a dually flat manifold (D3), stochastic processes as non-metric geodesics (D4), even multiple descent as matchings in a sparsity graph (B2). The tension worth sitting with is D2 against the rest: everyone else treats the divergence as primary and the geometry as derived, while D2 inverts the arrow and reconstructs the contrast function from a given metric and connection. If D2 is right about what is fundamental, several of these "read it off the geometry" moves are reading off a derived object.

**3. The estimand or abstraction is dictated by support, not convenience (TTE C1, C2, C4, C5; Learning B3).** Target trial emulation this week is one sustained argument that the target should be chosen to fit what the data can identify: design as an estimand commitment (C1), switch to the ATT when positivity is one-sided (C2), define excursion effects that survive deterministic positivity violations (C4), shrink an exposure shift to stay in support (C5). B3 is the learning-theory echo: depth in a GP buys you something only below a critical bandwidth, and above it the abstraction silently collapses. Same discipline in two literatures, ask when the structure you are assuming is actually supported before you estimate through it.

**4. An import pays rent only when removing it collapses the result (Bayesian A2, A3; Information D3, D4; Learning B2).** Four papers pass the test the spec cares about. Testing by betting is exactly the Kelly-Cover growth game and nothing less (A2, A3), planning is exactly a dually flat manifold (D3), a stochastic process is exactly a non-metric geodesic (D4), multiple descent is exactly a matching structure (B2). In each, delete the borrowed machinery and the theorem has no backbone. Read against thread 2, this is the constructive counterweight to the decoration worry: the geometry and the game are load-bearing here precisely because the result does not survive without them.

## Synthesis

This week's list is unusually coherent: two forces run through nearly all twenty papers. The first is the disciplined negative result, a default named and then broken with a mechanism rather than a counterexample, and it shows up identically in Bayesian diagnostics, diffusion generalization, loss-landscape hardness, decision curves, and estimand choice. The second is geometry doing real work, with the sharpest internal disagreement of the week sitting inside information geometry itself: the inverse-problem paper argues the divergence is not primary at all, which quietly destabilizes the other picks that read statistical behavior off a metric they assumed was fundamental. The target trial section and the deep-GP threshold paper converge from opposite fields on one rule, choose the target to match the support, do not force an estimand or an abstraction the data cannot carry, and this is the most portable lesson in the list. The Bell-Cover classic is the sleeper worth the detour, because it makes the modern e-value competitive-optimality results feel inevitable and reframes "grows fastest" and "wins right now" as one object. Where these papers disagree is on whether structure is discovered or imposed: the edge-of-stability and multiple-descent papers find geometry already governing a real phenomenon, while the planning-as-inference and non-metric-geodesic papers impose a geometry and check that it pays rent. Two questions worth carrying: if a degenerate regime is so often the informative object (B3, B5, D1), is degeneracy a pathology to avoid or the place to look first, and does the support-limited-estimand discipline of target trials have an exact analogue for when a learned representation is identifiable at all. The list rewards reading the threads against each other, not just the entries down the page.

# Weekly Reading List, 09-23-26

A geometry-heavy week. The strongest recent work is about what a constraint does to a problem: the sharpness ceiling that shapes the slow flow at the edge of stability, the privacy budget that turns Fisher efficiency into a set-valued object, the initiation window that dissolves a supposedly identified causal contrast. Two de Finetti revivals anchor the Bayesian section, one categorical and idealized, one empirical and damning. Network access was fine: arXiv API, category listings, and PubMed all reachable.

## A. Bayesian statistics

### 1. Statistical models as natural transformations: meaningfulness, coherence and priors as states in Markov categories
Francesco Vaccarino. arXiv:2609.22929, 2026-09-19. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22929

- The move: a statistical model is exactly a natural transformation between functors into the Kleisli category of the Giry monad, and once you accept that, meaningfulness, Kolmogorov consistency, exchangeability, and Jeffreys priors all become naturality conditions you can check or fail.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the categorical foundations paper that actually earns the machinery. Tjur's criterion for a meaningful parameter turns out to force indexing by the support and then coincide with naturality; a prior is coherent when it is natural over a class of design maps, and coherence over injections is precisely the exchangeability the categorical de Finetti theorem needs. The payoff line is that ridge regression is the Bayesian inversion of the Gaussian linear model under a prior coherent over insertions but never over injections, which tells you something real about when hierarchical pooling is and is not consistent. It also delivers a negative result: Jeffreys' general rule is not coherent, while the location-scale version is.
- Cost: 90 min, hard, needs comfort with categorical probability (Markov categories, the Giry monad) and Baues-Wirsching cohomology.
- Pairs with: the Dutch-books paper below, as the idealized coherence structure whose empirical violation that paper measures.

### 2. Posterior concentration and adaptation of the mixing measure in Dirichlet process mixtures
Filippo Ascolani. arXiv:2606.29109, 2026-06-27. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29109

- The move: a phase transition in the DP mixture posterior, resolving the mixing measure to any precision finer than n^{-1/2} requires a number of components that grows logarithmically in n, and this exactly explains the well-known logarithmic drift in the number of clusters.
- Why it is worth your time: this connects two things people usually treat separately, the Wasserstein contraction rate of the mixing measure and the notorious cluster-count inconsistency of the DP. The result is sharp: the mass beyond the K-th stick vanishes at n^{-1/2} up to sub-polynomial factors, the number of clusters grows like log n as in the prior, but the fraction of points outside the K largest clusters vanishes polynomially. The practical corollary is clean, any truncation with at least K sticks recovers the optimal rate, but you need order log n sticks to reproduce the exact posterior's clustering. This is the paper to cite the next time someone claims DP cluster counts are just a nuisance.
- Cost: 60 min, moderate to hard, needs posterior contraction rate machinery and stick-breaking asymptotics.
- Pairs with: singular learning theory below, another case where the effective dimension the posterior actually uses grows slowly and predictably.

### 3. Convex order and preservation of convexity for Bayesian posterior updates
Erhan Bayraktar, Yuqiong Wang. arXiv:2609.05065, 2026-09-04. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05065

- The move: fixing the current value of a posterior statistic, the same statistic after more data is larger in convex order when the current value rests on fewer observations, and convexity in the current statistic is preserved through updating.
- Why it is worth your time: one sharp structural idea about how a Bayesian posterior responds to future information, stated for one-parameter exponential families and extended to continuous time through Levy processes when the family is infinitely divisible. The two properties, convex-order monotonicity and preservation of convexity, are exactly the tools you need for time-monotonicity in dynamic Bayesian decision and optimal stopping problems, where informal "more data means more responsive" arguments usually get waved through. Short, self-contained, and the kind of lemma you will reach for later.
- Cost: 35 min, moderate, needs stochastic orders and exponential-family conjugacy.
- Pairs with: Dawid's prequential view below and dynamic treatment-timing questions in the target trial section.

### 4. Dutch Books for Language Models
Isaiah Andrews, Suproteem Sarkar. arXiv:2609.02797, 2026-09-02. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02797

- The move: elicit event probabilities from a language model, then solve a linear program for the largest guaranteed arbitrage profit against those probabilities, giving a label-free, outcome-free measure of incoherence built directly on de Finetti.
- Why it is worth your time: this is coherence theory used as an instrument, not decoration. Because the Dutch-book profit needs no realized outcomes, you can audit forecasts whose events have not resolved, which is the point. The empirical findings are sharp: incoherence rises with richer logical relationships between events, and irrelevant context can inflate it by an order of magnitude. It reads as the empirical counterweight to the categorical foundations paper above, both are about coherence, but one constructs the ideal and the other measures how far a deployed system falls from it. Andrews is a serious econometrician and the writing is honest about what the measure does and does not capture.
- Cost: 40 min, easy to moderate, needs only de Finetti coherence and linear programming.
- Pairs with: prediction-market and proper-scoring-rule literature, and the calibration thread running through this list.

### 5. Present Position and Potential Developments: Some Personal Views. Statistical Theory: The Prequential Approach
A. Philip Dawid. Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series A, 147(2):278-292, 1984. https://doi.org/10.2307/2981683

- The move: judge a probability forecaster only by the sequence of forecasts it actually made against the outcomes that actually occurred, and most questions of calibration and validity become questions about that one realized sequence rather than about the model.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the under-read source of nearly everything predictive in modern Bayes, martingale posteriors, conformal validity, calibration-by-betting, and the "coherence without a true model" stance the two papers above both inhabit. Read it now because the field has looped back to it, the prequential principle is exactly the move that lets you evaluate a forecaster without committing to a data-generating truth. It rewards reading in the original: the framing is sharper and less hedged than the textbook summaries.
- Cost: 60 min, moderate, discursive rather than technical.
- Pairs with: the Dutch-books paper, which is a prequential audit in all but name.

## B. Learning theory and high-dimensional optimization

### 1. The Multiple Timescales of Gradient Descent on the Edge of Stability: A Perturbative Derivation of the Central Flow
Raphael Berthier. arXiv:2609.01034, 2026-09-01. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01034

- The move: the empirically accurate but heuristic central flow of Cohen et al. becomes the rigorous leading-order term of a singular-perturbation expansion, once you write the loss as g + epsilon h and send epsilon to zero.
- Why it is worth your time: this replaces a plausible fit with a derivation you can trust and extend. Three timescales fall out of the method of multiple scales, fast oscillation along the sharpest direction, intermediate self-stabilization, and the slow central flow that is gradient flow of h constrained to minimizers of g with sharpness at most 2/eta. The self-stabilization mechanism shows up at next order, and Berthier pushes past prior analyses to the multi-eigenvalue case and explains why the fluctuations persist rather than decay. This is the cleanest current account of why the sharpness ceiling, not the loss alone, dictates the trajectory.
- Cost: 75 min, hard, needs singular perturbation theory and comfort with edge-of-stability phenomenology.
- Pairs with: the finite-time thermodynamics model-selection paper below and the information-geometry-at-EoS work, all of which say curvature sets the effective dynamics.

### 2. High-Dimensional Learning Dynamics of Attention-Indexed Models
Yizhou Xu, Margarita Sagitova, Lenka Zdeborova, Florent Krzakala. arXiv:2609.03858, 2026-09-03. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03858

- The move: a solvable high-dimensional limit for attention, in which the population landscape collapses to a finite set of trace order parameters while online SGD is governed by an infinite moment hierarchy that is exponentially well approximated by a finite truncation.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the statistical-physics-of-learning program reaching attention with a real sharp result, not a metaphor. Attention parameterization itself acts as an implicit bias: optimizing the raw d-by-d matrix S can stay trapped in an uninformative state, tied attention S = W W^T triggers automatic symmetry breaking and recovers signal in order d^2 log d samples, and untied attention exposes a fast-slow mechanism where the pre-activation mean moves first and the overlaps follow. The d^2 log d sample threshold is the kind of crisp count this list exists to surface. From the Zdeborova and Krzakala group, so the exactness is load-bearing.
- Cost: 75 min, hard, needs high-dimensional dynamics and order-parameter methods.
- Pairs with: singular learning and the finite-time thermodynamics paper, on when the effective number of degrees of freedom is far smaller than the parameter count.

### 3. Minimax Optimal Early-Stopped Gradient Descent for Gaussian Mixture Classification
Alex Buna, Shirley Xiaoqi Liu, Patrick Rebeschini. arXiv:2608.06250, 2026-08-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06250

- The move: in a Gaussian mixture with label-flipping noise, gradient descent stopped at an oracle time hits minimax-optimal zero-one risk, while the max-margin interpolator that GD converges to in direction can need exponentially more samples for the same risk.
- Why it is worth your time: a genuine separation between early stopping and interpolation, with matching upper and lower bounds rather than a one-sided bound. The technical heart is a new calibration result that converts excess logistic risk into excess zero-one risk under the misspecification induced by label flips, and crucially removes the square-root rate that standard conversions carry. The lower bound for linear interpolators is what gives the result teeth: interpolation is not merely suboptimal here, it is exponentially wasteful. This is the sharp modern statement of the implicit-regularization-of-early-stopping story.
- Cost: 60 min, hard, needs margin theory and minimax lower-bound technique.
- Pairs with: Yao, Rosasco and Caponnetto below, the classical early-stopping-as-regularization result this sharpens.

### 4. Non-Equilibrium Model Selection via Finite-Time Thermodynamics
Masayuki Ohzeki. arXiv:2606.16399, 2026-06-15. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16399

- The move: replace the equilibrium posterior in WBIC with an effective ensemble generated by learning dynamics under a resource constraint, and singular learning theory hands back a resource-dependent real log canonical threshold.
- Why it is worth your time: a short, high-idea-density paper that connects three things usually kept apart, singular learning theory, finite-time thermodynamics, and MDL. The equilibrium information criteria WAIC and WBIC assume you sampled the posterior; real training does not, and this paper asks what the correct complexity term is when the ensemble is the one your dynamics actually produced in bounded time. The resulting finite-time singular complexity is computable when the effective potential is analytic. It is terse and demands you fill gaps, but the reframe, model selection as a finite-time thermodynamic quantity, is worth the effort.
- Cost: 40 min, hard, needs singular learning theory (RLCT) and finite-time thermodynamics.
- Pairs with: the central-flow paper above and the information-geometry-of-thermodynamics work, on cost incurred by moving through a curved space in finite time.

### 5. On Early Stopping in Gradient Descent Learning
Yuan Yao, Lorenzo Rosasco, Andrea Caponnetto. Constructive Approximation, 26:289-315, 2007. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00365-006-0663-2

- The move: in reproducing-kernel gradient descent, the iteration number is a regularization parameter, and stopping at the right time gives the same bias-variance tradeoff and optimal rates as explicit Tikhonov penalties.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the paper that made "early stopping is regularization" a theorem rather than folklore, and it is the direct ancestor of the minimax early-stopping result above. Reading it now clarifies exactly what is new in the modern separations: the classical account gives optimal rates in a well-specified RKHS, while the recent work shows early stopping can beat interpolation by exponential margins under misspecification. The stopping-rule-as-inverse-regularization view still organizes the whole subject.
- Cost: 60 min, moderate, needs RKHS and spectral filtering.
- Pairs with: the Gaussian-mixture early-stopping paper, its sharp descendant.

## C. Target trial emulation

### 1. Structural Limitations of Early-Late Treatment Comparisons Under Target Trial Emulation: A Simulation Study
Atsushi Takayama, Shiro Tanaka, Koji Kawakami. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026-08-28. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag208

- The move: clone-censor-weighting applied to early-versus-late initiation comparisons does not deliver a well-defined causal estimand, estimated risk ratios swing with the initiation window and even reverse direction in settings where the true marginal contrast is exactly 1.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the negative result the field needs. Early-versus-late comparisons are widely sold as the clean TTE fix for immortal time bias, and this structural simulation shows the window-defined strategy contrast is shaped by risk accumulated before initiation, not by treatment. The direction reversals are not explained by weight instability or overlap failure, so you cannot diagnose the problem with the usual checks. Read it before designing or refereeing another E-L emulation. The register is exactly the target-trial tradition, careful about what the estimand actually is rather than how to estimate it.
- Cost: 40 min, moderate, needs clone-censor-weighting and immortal time bias.
- Pairs with: the vaccine-waning test below, another case where the classical estimand is not what people think it is.

### 2. A simple and powerful test of vaccine waning
Gellert Perenyi, Matias Janvin, Mats J. Stensrud. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026-09-03. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag118

- The move: instead of trying to estimate a waning estimand whose bounds are too wide to be useful, test the sharper null that the individual-level treatment effect is constant over time, which is valid under interpretable assumptions and far more powerful.
- Why it is worth your time: a clean example of choosing a better question. Formal waning estimands can only be bounded under weak assumptions, and the bounds rarely resolve whether waning is present; reframing as a test of constant effect recovers real power, and two of the three test statistics need only summary data from published trials. Applied to BNT162b2, the test rejects no-waning where a prior analysis could not. Stensrud's group is consistently sharp on estimand definition, and this is a strong instance of trading an unidentifiable magnitude for an identifiable qualitative claim.
- Cost: 45 min, moderate, needs causal estimands for time-varying effects and basic testing theory.
- Pairs with: the E-L structural paper above, and the convex-order posterior result in the Bayesian section on effects that change with time.

### 3. What phases of the drug development framework can epidemiological studies emulate?
Stephen Burgess, Benjamin Woolf, Emily Bassett, David Robertson, Sofia Villar, Jeremy A. Labrecque. American Journal of Epidemiology, 2026-08-12. https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag194

- The move: map emulations onto the drug-development phases, a late-phase target trial estimates a directly actionable effect, whereas a natural experiment is better understood as an early-phase target trial that gives proof of concept but an estimand that may not translate to practice.
- Why it is worth your time: a reframe that clarifies a recurring confusion, when a valid-but-inapplicable comparison beats an applicable-but-biased one. Placing Mendelian randomization and other quasi-random designs as early-phase trials tells you what to ask of them and, more importantly, what not to. Burgess and Labrecque are exactly the right authors for this, and the piece is honest that the framework buys clarity, not identification. It changes how you read the next natural-experiment paper that overclaims practical relevance.
- Cost: 30 min, easy to moderate, discursive.
- Pairs with: the estimand-choice paper below, on matching the target to what the design can support.

### 4. Everything All at Once: On Choosing an Estimand for Multi-component Environmental Exposures
Kara E. Rudolph, Shodai Inose, Nicholas T. Williams, Ivan Diaz, Lucia Calderon, Jacqueline M. Torres, Marianthi-Anna Kioumourtzoglou. Epidemiology, 2026-07-22. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000002022

- The move: define the exposure-mixture effect as a data-supported shift in the multivariate exposure, chosen to minimize extrapolation, and estimate it fully nonparametrically so no parametric assumption about the mixture-outcome surface is smuggled in.
- Why it is worth your time: mixtures are where causal estimands usually get quietly replaced by regression coefficients, and this paper takes the estimand seriously first, then estimates it. The shift can act on one or several components, encode interactions, and move components by different amounts, with an explicit extrapolation check that modifies the shift to stay inside the data. Diaz's nonparametric-efficiency machinery does the estimation, and the pesticide-and-hypertension application is a real longitudinal case, not a toy. This is the template for asking a well-posed question about a continuous multivariate exposure.
- Cost: 50 min, moderate to hard, needs modified treatment policies and nonparametric efficiency.
- Pairs with: the phase-framing paper above, both about choosing an estimand the design can actually support.

### 5. Observational studies analyzed like randomized experiments: an application to postmenopausal hormone therapy and coronary heart disease
Miguel A. Hernan, Alvaro Alonso, Roger Logan, Francine Grodstein, Karin B. Michels, Walter C. Willett, Joann E. Manson, James M. Robins. Epidemiology, 19(6):766-779, 2008. https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0b013e3181875e61

- The move: conceptualize the Nurses' Health Study as a sequence of nested trials with an intention-to-treat analysis, and the hormone-therapy result that contradicted the Women's Health Initiative for years reconciles with it, once time since menopause and length of follow-up are matched.
- Why it is worth your time: this is the paper where target trial emulation is born in practice, well before the vocabulary settled. The famous WHI-versus-observational discrepancy in coronary risk turns out to be largely an artifact of estimand and eligibility, not confounding, and the sequence-of-trials construction plus inverse-probability adjustment for adherence is the exact template every emulation now follows. Read it as the origin of the E-L cautions in this section: the structural fixes people now stress-test all trace back here. Under-read relative to its influence because everyone cites the later framing papers instead.
- Cost: 55 min, moderate, needs ITADE-style ITT versus per-protocol reasoning and IPW.
- Pairs with: the E-L structural-limitations paper, which probes exactly the initiation-window machinery this paper introduced.

## D. Information geometry

### 1. A Generalization of Amari's Bayesian Duality
Mohammad Emtiyaz Khan, Thomas Mollenhoff. arXiv:2609.09126, 2026-09-08. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09126

- The move: Amari's little-cited Bayesian duality is really a convex duality of Bayes' rule, and reading it that way generalizes it and connects it to modern natural-gradient learning.
- Why it is worth your time: Khan has spent years arguing that Bayesian updating is natural-gradient ascent in the dual (expectation) coordinates, and here he traces that idea back to an Amari result most people skipped and extends it. The reframe is the value, Bayes' rule as a convex-duality operation on the statistical manifold, which is what licenses treating posterior computation and optimization as the same geometric object. Short and pointed. It is the cleanest bridge in this list between the Bayesian and information-geometry sections.
- Cost: 35 min, moderate, needs dual coordinates and convex duality.
- Pairs with: the message-passing information-geometry paper below and the categorical-coherence paper in the Bayesian section.

### 2. Stochastic Processes as Non-Metric Geodesics in Information Geometry
T. Koide, A. van de Venn. arXiv:2609.08870, 2026-09-08. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08870

- The move: geodesics of the alpha-connections on the Gaussian statistical manifold correspond one-to-one with a class of constant-noise stochastic processes, and the work-minimizing slow-driving protocol in stochastic thermodynamics is exactly one of these expectation geodesics.
- Why it is worth your time: this is cross-pollination that pays rent. The correspondence sorts Gaussian geodesics into three geometric classes, and the boundary-connecting class gives an explicit generalized bridge process that reduces to Ornstein-Uhlenbeck or Brownian motion in operational limits, with the physical restoring force fixing the connection parameter alpha. The striking part is that the same non-metricity that shows up in the geometrical-trinity-of-gravity analogy plays an active role in setting the optimal thermodynamic protocol. Remove the geometry and there is no statement left, which is the test a decorated paper fails.
- Cost: 60 min, hard, needs alpha-connections, non-metric affine geometry, and stochastic thermodynamics.
- Pairs with: the finite-time thermodynamics model-selection paper in the learning-theory section, on the geometric cost of finite-time driving.

### 3. Exact Asymptotic Efficiency under zCDP: Diameter-Constrained Information Geometry
T. Tony Cai, Yicheng Li. arXiv:2609.16895, 2026-09-15. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16895

- The move: under zero-concentrated differential privacy, the object governing efficiency is not the Fisher information but a diameter-constrained information region, a set of achievable information matrices, and the exact local minimax risk is an inverse-information variational functional over that set.
- Why it is worth your time: this is a real reframe of efficiency theory, from a single Fisher information matrix to a set-valued geometry, forced by the privacy constraint. The efficient estimator becomes loss-dependent, it releases the empirical mean of a nearly optimal bounded statistic with Gaussian noise and locally inverts the moment map, which is a genuine departure from the classical picture where the efficient estimator does not depend on the loss. Closed-form constants come out for Gaussian means, categorical vectors, and regression, and the whole theory transfers to Gaussian DP by a parameter rescaling. Tony Cai, so the asymptotics are exact rather than order-of.
- Cost: 75 min, hard, needs local asymptotic minimax theory and differential privacy.
- Pairs with: the early-stopping and constrained-optimization papers, all about how a constraint reshapes the effective geometry and the optimal procedure.

### 4. Preventing Model Collapse: A Fisher-Rao Perspective on the Dynamics of Training with Synthetic Data
Matteo Marchi, Joao Pedro Silvestre, Bahman Gharesifard, Paulo Tabuada. arXiv:2609.18878, 2026-09-16. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18878

- The move: analyze recursive training on synthetic data under the Fisher-Rao metric on the probability simplex rather than the Euclidean metric, and the minimum human-to-synthetic data ratio that prevents model collapse stops being vacuous in high dimension.
- Why it is worth your time: the borrowed structure does the work. A prior lower bound on the required human-data fraction relies on the Euclidean metric in R^n and goes vacuous as dimension grows, which is useless precisely where model collapse matters. Switching to the Fisher-Rao geometry of categorical distributions yields contraction and invariance bounds that stay meaningful in high dimension and give a different, non-trivial required ratio. This is a timely question answered with the right geometry, and it is a clean case study in why the metric on the simplex is not a cosmetic choice.
- Cost: 50 min, moderate to hard, needs Fisher-Rao geometry and contraction analysis.
- Pairs with: the diffusion-and-collapse literature, and the message-passing paper below on inference dynamics on the simplex.

### 5. Information Geometry of Message Passing
Mykola Lukashchuk, Kyrylo Yemets, Alex Ledbetter, Ismail Senoz. arXiv:2608.15922, 2026-08-16. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15922

- The move: the natural-gradient stationary condition of variational inference has an edge-local form on a Forney factor graph, at a stationary point the natural parameter of an edge is the sum of two natural-gradient projections of the exact belief-propagation messages.
- Why it is worth your time: this locates the geometry inside the algorithm. Starting from the Bethe free energy and constraining a chosen edge marginal to an exponential family, the message a factor sends becomes the natural-gradient projection of the exact BP log-message at the receiving marginal, which is strictly more faithful than variational message passing's averaging under neighboring beliefs. The two coincide only when edge uncertainty vanishes, and the geometric version wins exactly where uncertainty persists, along partially observed latent chains and in streaming parameter estimation, showing up as better calibration. It is a concrete, buildable scheme, not a slogan about manifolds.
- Cost: 50 min, moderate to hard, needs factor graphs, Bethe free energy, and natural-gradient VI.
- Pairs with: Khan and Mollenhoff above, the same natural-gradient-as-Bayes idea seen from the graph side.

## Threads

### Thread 1: Curvature sets the dynamics
Berthier (B-learning 1), Ohzeki (B-learning 4), Koide and van de Venn (D2), and Marchi et al. (D4). The throughline is that the metric or the sharpness ceiling, not the loss or the estimator alone, dictates what actually happens. Berthier's slow flow lives on the sharpness-2/eta constraint surface, Ohzeki's complexity term is a finite-time thermodynamic quantity, Koide and van de Venn's optimal protocol is a geodesic, and Marchi et al. show a Euclidean analysis of the same dynamics is vacuous where the Fisher-Rao one is sharp. Tension: the learning-theory papers treat curvature as an obstacle the dynamics must survive, while the geometry papers treat it as the structure that defines the optimal path. Both are right, which is the interesting part.

### Thread 2: de Finetti, twice
Vaccarino (A1), Andrews and Sarkar (A4), Dawid (A5), and behind them the coherence framing generally. Vaccarino builds the idealized object, coherence as naturality, exchangeability as a condition on priors, and derives when it holds. Andrews and Sarkar measure how badly a deployed system violates it, with an arbitrage number. Dawid supplies the stance that makes both make sense, judge a forecaster by its realized sequence, not its model. The tension worth sitting with: Vaccarino's coherent priors form a polytope of exactly characterized dimension, a tidy geometric object, while the empirical incoherence Andrews and Sarkar find grows with logical structure and context, suggesting the coherent set is precisely where real systems are not.

### Thread 3: The estimand is the hard part
Takayama et al. (C1), Perenyi et al. (C2), Burgess et al. (C3), Rudolph et al. (C4), and Hernan et al. (C5). Every one of these is about defining the target rather than estimating it. Takayama et al. show a supposedly identified contrast is window-dependent and can reverse sign; Perenyi et al. trade an unidentifiable magnitude for an identifiable test; Burgess et al. sort designs by which estimand they can support; Rudolph et al. choose a mixture shift the data actually support; Hernan et al. reconcile a decade-long controversy by fixing eligibility and time zero. Connect this to A4 and A5: an incoherent forecast and an ill-posed estimand are the same failure, a quantity treated as meaningful that the structure does not support.

### Thread 4: Constraints make efficiency geometry-dependent
Cai and Li (D3), Buna et al. (B-learning 3), and Lukashchuk et al. (D5). A privacy budget turns Fisher efficiency into an optimization over a set of information matrices with a loss-dependent optimal estimator; an early-stopping budget makes the stopping time a regularizer with rates that beat interpolation; a computational budget on inference makes the natural-gradient projection local on a graph. In each case a constraint that looks like a nuisance reshapes the effective geometry and the optimal procedure stops being the unconstrained one. The classical picture, one efficient estimator independent of loss, is the special case where no constraint binds.

## Synthesis

The week's center of gravity is the claim that constraints and curvature, not likelihoods and estimators, are what determine outcomes. Cai and Li make it exact for private estimation, Berthier makes it exact for edge-of-stability training, and Marchi et al. show the same dynamics is either trivial or informative depending only on which metric you put on the simplex. The target trial section says the identical thing in causal language: Takayama et al. and Hernan et al. both show that getting the estimand and the eligibility structure right is the whole game, and that a well-defined question can dissolve a controversy or expose a bias that no amount of estimation would fix. The two de Finetti papers sharpen the tension the list keeps circling, Vaccarino characterizes exactly where coherence lives, and Andrews and Sarkar show deployed systems live somewhere else. Where the papers disagree is on whether geometry is friend or obstacle: the optimization work treats curvature as something to survive, the information-geometry work treats it as the object that defines the optimum, and finite-time thermodynamics (Ohzeki, Koide and van de Venn) sits between them by pricing the cost of moving through a curved space in bounded time. For ongoing work on aging as a dynamical system and control energy near criticality, Thread 1 is the one to sit with: if the slow dynamics of a high-dimensional system are gradient flow constrained to a sharpness surface, then the control-energy and criticality questions are questions about that surface's geometry, and the finite-time-thermodynamics papers give a first vocabulary for the cost of driving along it. Two questions worth holding: is the set-valued efficiency object of Cai and Li a special case of a general "constraint region" that also contains the early-stopping and edge-of-stability constraints, and is the incoherence Andrews and Sarkar measure the empirical shadow of an estimand that, like the E-L contrast, was never well-defined to begin with.
